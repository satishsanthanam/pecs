Intravenous immune globulin (IVIG)

🏠 Home / Blood disorders / Blood donation and transfusion

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Intravenous immune globulin (IVIG) is a blood product administered intravenously, containing antibodies (immunoglobulins) pooled from thousands of healthy human plasma donors.
  • Primary Function: It provides passive immunity and modulates the immune system, used to treat a wide range of immune deficiencies and autoimmune disorders.
  • Mechanism: IVIG works by supplying functional antibodies, neutralizing pathogenic antibodies, blocking Fc receptors, and modulating cytokine production.
  • Source: The immunoglobulins are purified from human plasma, ensuring a broad spectrum of antibodies against various infectious agents.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Recurrent Infections: Patients with primary immunodeficiency often present with frequent, severe, or unusual infections (e.g., pneumonia, sinusitis, sepsis) prompting IVIG consideration.
  • Muscle Weakness & Neuropathy: Symptoms like progressive muscle weakness, tingling, numbness, or paralysis (e.g., in Guillain-Barré Syndrome, Myasthenia Gravis, CIDP) are key indicators for IVIG therapy.
  • Bleeding & Bruising: Unexplained petechiae, purpura, or mucosal bleeding due to low platelet counts (e.g., Idiopathic Thrombocytopenic Purpura - ITP) may necessitate IVIG.
  • Systemic Inflammation: Signs of widespread inflammation, fever, rash, and organ dysfunction (e.g., Kawasaki disease) can be indications for IVIG.
  • Chronic Fatigue & Malaise: Persistent fatigue, general malaise, and poor quality of life often accompany underlying immune dysregulation treatable with IVIG.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-infusion Assessment: Always assess renal function, hydration status, and baseline vital signs before initiating IVIG to mitigate risks of renal impairment and adverse reactions.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate hydration before, during, and after infusion to prevent renal tubular necrosis and reduce the incidence of headache and other infusion-related side effects.
  • Infusion Rate: Start the infusion at a slow rate and gradually increase, closely monitoring the patient for any signs of adverse reactions, especially during the first 15-30 minutes.
  • Contraindications: Exercise extreme caution or avoid in patients with severe IgA deficiency who have known anti-IgA antibodies, due to the high risk of anaphylaxis.
  • Thrombotic Risk: Be vigilant for signs of thrombosis (e.g., stroke, myocardial infarction, DVT, PE), particularly in elderly patients, those with pre-existing cardiovascular disease, or hypercoagulable states.
  • Aseptic Meningitis: Monitor for symptoms of aseptic meningitis (severe headache, nuchal rigidity, photophobia) which can occur hours to days post-infusion.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-infusion Hydration: Encourage increased oral fluid intake for 24-48 hours prior to infusion to optimize hydration and reduce the risk of renal complications and headaches.
  • During Infusion: Maintain adequate fluid intake, either orally or intravenously, throughout the infusion period as tolerated by the patient.
  • Post-infusion Hydration: Continue to encourage generous fluid intake for at least 24-48 hours after the infusion to aid in drug excretion and prevent delayed side effects.
  • No Specific Food Restrictions: Generally, there are no specific food restrictions directly related to IVIG administration; patients can maintain their usual diet unless otherwise advised for managing side effects like nausea.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Patient Monitoring: Continuously monitor vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature) before, during, and after infusion, especially during the initial phase.
  • Adverse Reaction Management: Be prepared to manage infusion-related reactions such as headache, fever, chills, rash, and more severe reactions like anaphylaxis. Have emergency medications readily available.
  • Product Handling: Ensure proper storage, preparation, and administration of the IVIG product according to manufacturer guidelines and institutional policies. Do not shake the vial.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document the product name, lot number, expiration date, dose, infusion rate, patient's response, and any adverse events.
  • Patient Education: Educate the patient and family about potential side effects, signs to watch for, and when to seek immediate medical attention.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Therapy: IVIG dosing and frequency must be highly individualized based on the patient's specific diagnosis, weight, clinical response, and trough IgG levels (for immunodeficiency).
  • Risk-Benefit Analysis: A thorough risk-benefit assessment is crucial, particularly in patients with pre-existing renal impairment, cardiovascular disease, or a history of thrombotic events.
  • Pre-medication Strategy: Consider pre-medication with antihistamines, NSAIDs, or corticosteroids for patients prone to infusion-related reactions or those with a history of mild reactions.
  • Renal Function Monitoring: Regular monitoring of serum creatinine and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is essential, especially in patients at risk for acute kidney injury.
  • Thrombosis Prophylaxis: For high-risk patients, consider prophylactic measures against thrombosis, such as low-dose aspirin or anticoagulants, in consultation with a hematologist.
  • Alternative Therapies: Evaluate the need for IVIG versus subcutaneous immune globulin (SCIG) based on patient preference, compliance, and clinical indication.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Understanding Indications: Familiarize yourself with the various clinical indications for IVIG, differentiating between primary immunodeficiencies and autoimmune conditions.
  • Adverse Event Recognition: Learn to identify the signs and symptoms of common and severe adverse reactions to IVIG, including infusion-related reactions, aseptic meningitis, and thrombotic events.
  • Infusion Protocol Adherence: Master the institutional protocols for IVIG administration, including proper venipuncture, aseptic technique, pump programming, and rate adjustments.
  • Patient Assessment Skills: Develop strong assessment skills to monitor patient hydration, vital signs, and neurological status throughout the IVIG therapy.
  • Patient Education & Support: Be prepared to educate patients about their treatment, potential side effects, and provide emotional support during what can be a lengthy infusion process.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Understand the critical importance of accurate and comprehensive documentation for IVIG administration, including lot numbers and patient responses.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pharmacology: IVIG consists primarily of intact immunoglobulin G (IgG) molecules, with a broad range of antibody specificities, and a typical half-life of 21-28 days.
  • Key Indications: Primary Humoral Immunodeficiency, Idiopathic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP), Kawasaki Disease, Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS), Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy (CIDP), Multifocal Motor Neuropathy (MMN), Myasthenia Gravis.
  • Adverse Effects Profile: Common adverse effects include headache, fever, chills, nausea, rash. Serious adverse effects include acute renal failure, thrombotic events (stroke, MI, DVT, PE), aseptic meningitis, hemolytic anemia, and anaphylaxis.
  • Product Variability: Different IVIG products may vary in their excipients (e.g., sucrose, maltose, albumin), osmolality, and IgA content, which can influence tolerability and risk profiles.
  • Monitoring Parameters: Baseline and periodic monitoring of renal function (creatinine, BUN), urine output, fluid balance, and potentially serum osmolality and IgG trough levels.
  • Mechanism of Action (Detailed): Involves Fc receptor blockade, anti-idiotypic antibody neutralization, complement modulation, cytokine regulation, and effects on B and T cell activation.
