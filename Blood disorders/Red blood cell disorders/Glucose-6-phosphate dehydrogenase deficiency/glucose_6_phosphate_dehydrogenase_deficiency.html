Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency

🏠 Home / Blood disorders / Red blood cell disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Genetic Disorder: Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency is an inherited X-linked recessive condition.
  • Enzyme Function: G6PD is vital for red blood cell (RBC) metabolism, protecting them from oxidative damage.
  • Hemolytic Anemia Risk: Individuals with G6PD deficiency are prone to hemolytic anemia when exposed to certain oxidative triggers.
  • Geographic Prevalence: More common in populations of African, Mediterranean, and Asian descent.
  • Common Triggers: Includes specific medications (e.g., some antimalarials, sulfonamides), fava beans, and infections.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Acute Hemolysis: Symptoms typically manifest after exposure to an oxidative stressor.
  • Jaundice: Yellowing of the skin and eyes due to increased bilirubin from RBC breakdown.
  • Dark Urine: Often described as 'tea-colored' or dark, resulting from hemoglobinuria.
  • Pallor: Noticeable paleness of the skin and mucous membranes due to anemia.
  • Fatigue and Weakness: General tiredness, lethargy, and reduced physical stamina.
  • Shortness of Breath: Dyspnea, particularly with exertion, indicating reduced oxygen-carrying capacity.
  • Tachycardia: An abnormally rapid heart rate.
  • Splenomegaly: Enlargement of the spleen, which may occur during hemolytic episodes.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Trigger Avoidance: Strictly avoid fava beans and a specific list of medications known to induce hemolysis (e.g., primaquine, dapsone, sulfonamides, nitrofurantoin, high-dose aspirin).
  • Medication Disclosure: Always inform all healthcare providers about G6PD deficiency before receiving any new prescription or over-the-counter medication.
  • Infection Management: Promptly treat infections, as they can act as significant oxidative stressors.
  • Newborn Screening: Essential in at-risk populations to prevent severe neonatal jaundice and potential kernicterus.
  • Hydration Status: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially during periods of illness or potential oxidative stress.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fava Bean Restriction: Absolute avoidance of fava beans (broad beans) and any food products containing them.
  • Legume Caution: While fava beans are the primary concern, some individuals may exhibit sensitivity to other legumes; exercise caution.
  • Oxidative Food Awareness: Be mindful of other potential dietary oxidative stressors, although fava beans are the most potent and well-documented.
  • Fluid Intake: Encourage consistent and adequate hydration, particularly during illness or suspected hemolytic episodes.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Hemolytic Crisis Recognition: Educate patients and caregivers on recognizing the signs of an acute hemolytic crisis, such as sudden jaundice, dark urine, and severe fatigue.
  • Medical Identification: Advise carrying a medical alert card or wearing a medical identification bracelet indicating G6PD deficiency.
  • Family Screening: Recommend screening for family members, especially male relatives, due to the X-linked genetic inheritance pattern.
  • Unidentified Substances: Exercise extreme caution with traditional remedies, herbal supplements, or unprescribed medications that may contain undisclosed oxidative agents.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Confirm diagnosis with a quantitative G6PD enzyme assay, ideally performed outside of an acute hemolytic episode.
  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education to patients regarding trigger avoidance and early symptom recognition.
  • Medication Guidance: Furnish a detailed list of contraindicated medications and advise on safe alternatives.
  • Clinical Monitoring: Monitor for signs of anemia and jaundice, especially in neonates and during acute hemolytic events.
  • Genetic Counseling: Offer genetic counseling to affected individuals and their families to understand inheritance patterns and risks.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Teaching Role: Nurses are crucial in educating patients and families about G6PD deficiency, trigger avoidance, and symptom management.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Diligently review medication orders for any drugs contraindicated in G6PD deficiency.
  • Neonatal Care: Understand the significance of G6PD screening in neonates presenting with unexplained hyperbilirubinemia.
  • Transfusion Preparedness: Be prepared for potential blood transfusions in cases of severe hemolytic anemia.
  • Cultural Competence: Recognize the higher prevalence in specific ethnic groups and tailor patient education and care accordingly.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Enzyme Deficiency: Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) is the rate-limiting enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP).
  • NADPH Production: G6PD catalyzes the first step in the PPP, producing NADPH, which is essential for reducing glutathione.
  • Oxidative Stress Mechanism: Insufficient NADPH leads to impaired reduction of oxidized glutathione, allowing reactive oxygen species to accumulate and cause oxidative damage to hemoglobin and red blood cell membranes.
  • Heinz Bodies: Oxidized hemoglobin precipitates within red blood cells, forming Heinz bodies, which lead to increased cellular rigidity and premature destruction by the spleen.
  • X-linked Inheritance: The G6PD gene is located on the X chromosome, resulting in a higher incidence and severity in males; females are typically carriers but can be symptomatic due to unfavorable X-inactivation.
  • Variant Spectrum: Numerous G6PD variants exist globally, exhibiting a wide range of enzyme activity levels and clinical phenotypes (e.g., G6PD A-, G6PD Mediterranean).
