Lymphoma

🏠 Home / Blood disorders / Lymphoma and Hodgkin disease

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Lymphoma is a cancer that begins in infection-fighting cells of the immune system called lymphocytes. These cells are in the lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, bone marrow, and other parts of the body.
  • Types: The two main types are Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) and Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), each with distinct characteristics and treatment approaches.
  • Lymphatic System Role: The lymphatic system is a vital part of the immune system, responsible for fighting disease and maintaining fluid balance. Lymphoma disrupts these critical functions.
  • Cellular Origin: Lymphoma involves the abnormal proliferation of lymphocytes, which are a type of white blood cell, leading to the formation of tumors.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Painless Swelling: Enlarged lymph nodes in the neck, armpit, or groin that are typically painless.
  • Unexplained Fever: Persistent fever without an identifiable infection.
  • Drenching Night Sweats: Profuse sweating during sleep, often soaking clothes and bedding.
  • Unintended Weight Loss: Significant loss of body weight (more than 10% in six months) without dieting or increased physical activity.
  • Persistent Fatigue: Extreme tiredness that does not improve with rest.
  • Generalized Itching: Pruritus (itching) that can be severe and widespread.
  • Respiratory Symptoms: Cough, shortness of breath, or chest pain if lymph nodes in the chest are enlarged.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Prompt Evaluation: Seek immediate medical attention for any persistent or concerning symptoms, especially swollen lymph nodes, fever, night sweats, or unexplained weight loss.
  • Infection Risk: Patients undergoing treatment for lymphoma often have compromised immune systems; strict infection prevention measures are crucial.
  • Vaccination Status: Discuss all vaccinations with the healthcare team before and during treatment, as live vaccines may be contraindicated.
  • Fertility Preservation: For patients of reproductive age, discuss fertility preservation options with the oncology team prior to starting chemotherapy or radiation.
  • Secondary Malignancies: Long-term survivors require ongoing monitoring for potential late effects of treatment, including secondary cancers.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Managing Nausea: Consume small, frequent meals of bland foods to help manage treatment-induced nausea and maintain caloric intake.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain excellent fluid intake, especially during chemotherapy, to prevent dehydration and support kidney function.
  • Neutropenic Diet: During periods of low white blood cell counts (neutropenia), a neutropenic diet may be recommended to minimize exposure to bacteria from food.
  • Soft Foods for Mucositis: If oral mucositis develops, opt for soft, non-acidic, and cool foods to minimize discomfort and ensure nutritional intake.
  • Avoid Raw/Undercooked Foods: Strictly avoid raw or undercooked meats, fish, eggs, and unpasteurized dairy products to reduce the risk of foodborne infections.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Treatment Adherence: Strict adherence to the prescribed treatment regimen, including medication schedules and appointments, is paramount for optimal outcomes.
  • Side Effect Reporting: Immediately report any new or worsening side effects to the healthcare team for timely management and intervention.
  • Support System Utilization: Encourage patients to lean on family, friends, and support groups for emotional and practical assistance throughout their journey.
  • Infection Prevention: Emphasize meticulous hand hygiene, avoidance of large crowds, and prompt reporting of any signs of infection (e.g., fever, chills).
  • Follow-up Care: Stress the importance of regular follow-up appointments and surveillance scans post-treatment to monitor for recurrence or late complications.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Definitive Diagnosis: A lymph node biopsy is essential for definitive diagnosis and classification of lymphoma, guiding subsequent treatment decisions.
  • Staging Importance: Accurate staging (e.g., Ann Arbor system) is critical to determine the extent of the disease and inform the most appropriate treatment strategy.
  • Multimodal Treatment: Treatment often involves a combination of chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, and in some cases, stem cell transplantation.
  • Prognosis Variability: Prognosis varies significantly depending on the specific type of lymphoma, stage at diagnosis, and individual patient factors.
  • Personalized Approach: Treatment plans are highly individualized, considering the patient's overall health, lymphoma subtype, and genetic markers.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education on lymphoma, treatment regimens, potential side effects, and self-care strategies.
  • Symptom Management: Proactively assess and manage symptoms such as pain, fatigue, nausea, and B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss).
  • Chemotherapy Administration: Ensure safe and accurate administration of chemotherapeutic agents, adhering to protocols for handling, infusion, and waste disposal.
  • Psychosocial Support: Offer emotional support and resources to address patient and family anxiety, depression, and coping mechanisms.
  • Central Line Care: Meticulously manage central venous access devices (e.g., PICC lines, ports) to prevent infection and maintain patency.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathology: Hodgkin lymphoma is characterized by Reed-Sternberg cells; Non-Hodgkin lymphoma encompasses a diverse group of B-cell and T-cell lymphomas.
  • Staging System: The Ann Arbor staging system is widely used to classify the extent of lymphoma, from Stage I (localized) to Stage IV (widespread).
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Key diagnostic tests include excisional lymph node biopsy, bone marrow biopsy, PET/CT scans, and sometimes lumbar puncture.
  • Immunophenotyping: Flow cytometry and immunohistochemistry are crucial for identifying specific cell surface markers (e.g., CD20, CD30) to classify lymphoma subtypes.
  • Common Regimens: Standard chemotherapy regimens include ABVD for Hodgkin lymphoma and R-CHOP (Rituximab, Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Vincristine, Prednisone) for aggressive B-cell Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
