Hairy cell leukemia

🏠 Home / Blood disorders / Leukemia

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Hairy cell leukemia (HCL) is a rare, slow-growing cancer of the blood and bone marrow, characterized by an accumulation of abnormal B lymphocytes with hair-like projections.
  • Impact: It leads to pancytopenia (low counts of all blood cells), often resulting in anemia, increased infection risk, and bleeding tendencies.
  • Key Feature: The abnormal cells infiltrate the bone marrow and spleen, causing splenomegaly (enlarged spleen) and impairing normal blood cell production.
  • Prognosis: HCL is generally treatable, with many patients achieving long-term remission, though it often requires ongoing monitoring.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Fatigue and Weakness: Primarily due to anemia (low red blood cell count).
  • Frequent Infections: Resulting from neutropenia (low white blood cell count), making patients susceptible to bacterial, viral, and fungal infections.
  • Easy Bruising or Bleeding: Caused by thrombocytopenia (low platelet count), manifesting as petechiae, purpura, or prolonged bleeding.
  • Abdominal Fullness or Discomfort: Often due to an enlarged spleen (splenomegaly), which can also cause early satiety.
  • Unexplained Weight Loss: A general systemic symptom that can occur with various malignancies.
  • Fever and Night Sweats: Less common but can be present, indicative of systemic inflammation or infection.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Infection Prevention: Meticulous hand hygiene, avoiding sick individuals, and prompt reporting of any signs of infection (e.g., fever, chills) are critical due to immunosuppression.
  • Bleeding Risk Management: Avoid medications that increase bleeding risk (e.g., NSAIDs, aspirin) unless specifically prescribed. Use soft toothbrushes and electric razors.
  • Vaccination Status: Discuss all vaccinations with your physician; live attenuated vaccines are generally contraindicated, and inactivated vaccines may have reduced efficacy.
  • Avoid Crowded Environments: Especially during periods of severe neutropenia or active treatment, to minimize exposure to pathogens.
  • Regular Monitoring: Adhere strictly to scheduled blood tests and follow-up appointments to monitor disease progression and treatment response.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Neutropenic Diet: If severely neutropenic, follow strict food safety guidelines: thoroughly cook all meats, avoid raw or undercooked eggs, unpasteurized dairy, and unwashed raw fruits/vegetables.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially during treatment, to support kidney function and overall well-being.
  • Nutritional Support: Focus on nutrient-dense foods to combat fatigue and maintain weight. Small, frequent meals may be better tolerated than large ones.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Especially if liver function is compromised or during specific chemotherapy regimens.
  • Iron-Rich Foods: Incorporate iron-rich foods (e.g., lean meats, fortified cereals) to help manage anemia, if not contraindicated.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Fever Reporting: Any temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher must be reported to the healthcare team immediately, as it can indicate a serious infection.
  • Monitor for Bleeding: Watch for new or worsening bruising, petechiae, nosebleeds, gum bleeding, or blood in urine/stools.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including prophylactic antibiotics or antivirals, exactly as directed.
  • Avoid Injury: Be cautious with activities that carry a risk of injury or trauma, especially if platelet counts are low.
  • Report New Symptoms: Any new or worsening symptoms, such as increasing abdominal discomfort, persistent fatigue, or recurrent infections, should be communicated promptly.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Diagnosis relies on peripheral blood smear showing 'hairy cells,' bone marrow biopsy, and flow cytometry demonstrating characteristic immunophenotype (CD11c, CD25, CD103, CD123 positivity).
  • BRAF V600E Mutation: This mutation is highly prevalent in HCL and can be a diagnostic and therapeutic target.
  • Treatment Strategy: Purine analogs (cladribine or pentostatin) are highly effective first-line treatments, often inducing long-lasting remissions. Rituximab may be added or used in relapsed cases.
  • Watchful Waiting: Asymptomatic patients with stable disease and minimal cytopenias may be candidates for a 'watch and wait' approach.
  • Long-term Follow-up: Essential for monitoring for relapse, managing treatment-related side effects, and screening for secondary malignancies.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology: Understand HCL as a clonal B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder originating from a mature B-lymphocyte that has undergone somatic hypermutation.
  • Nursing Interventions: Focus on comprehensive patient education regarding infection prevention, bleeding precautions, fatigue management, and medication adherence.
  • Chemotherapy Administration: Safe handling and administration of purine analogs, monitoring for acute and long-term side effects (e.g., myelosuppression, immunosuppression).
  • Blood Product Transfusions: Understand indications for red blood cell and platelet transfusions and monitor for transfusion reactions.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide emotional support and resources for patients coping with a chronic cancer diagnosis and its impact on quality of life.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Code: C91.4 - Hairy cell leukemia.
  • Key Immunophenotype Markers: CD11c, CD25, CD103, CD123 (strong co-expression).
  • Genetic Marker: BRAF V600E mutation (present in >90% of cases).
  • First-Line Treatment: Cladribine (2-CdA) or Pentostatin (DCF), both purine analogs.
  • Relapsed/Refractory Treatment: Rituximab, Vemurafenib (for BRAF V600E positive), Moxetumomab pasudotox.
  • Complications: Opportunistic infections, myelosuppression, secondary malignancies, autoimmune phenomena.
