Pulmonary embolism (blood clot in the lung)

🏠 Home / Blood disorders / Blood clots in the legs or lungs

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Pulmonary embolism (PE) is a sudden blockage in a lung artery, usually caused by a blood clot that travels from a deep vein in the leg (deep vein thrombosis or DVT) to the lungs.
  • Severity: It is a life-threatening medical emergency requiring immediate attention.
  • Primary Cause: Most PEs originate from DVT, where a clot breaks off and travels through the bloodstream to the pulmonary arteries.
  • Risk Factors: Include prolonged immobility (e.g., long flights, bed rest), major surgery, cancer, pregnancy, oral contraceptives, inherited clotting disorders, and obesity.
  • Prompt Action: Early diagnosis and treatment are crucial to prevent severe complications and mortality.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sudden Shortness of Breath: Often appears suddenly and worsens with exertion.
  • Chest Pain: Typically sharp or stabbing, often worse with deep breaths, coughing, or bending.
  • Cough: May be dry or produce blood-streaked sputum.
  • Rapid Heart Rate (Tachycardia): An unusually fast heartbeat.
  • Lightheadedness or Dizziness: Can indicate reduced oxygen supply to the brain.
  • Sweating: Unexplained or excessive perspiration.
  • Anxiety: A feeling of impending doom or severe apprehension.
  • Symptoms of DVT: Swelling, pain, tenderness, redness, or warmth in one leg (often the calf or thigh).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • DVT Prevention: Implement strategies to prevent deep vein thrombosis, such as early ambulation after surgery, regular movement during long periods of sitting, and use of compression stockings or pneumatic compression devices for high-risk individuals.
  • Anticoagulant Adherence: If prescribed anticoagulant medications, take them exactly as directed and do not miss doses without consulting your physician.
  • Travel Precautions: During long journeys, stand up and walk around every hour or two, perform leg exercises while seated, and stay well-hydrated.
  • Medical History Disclosure: Always inform healthcare providers about any personal or family history of blood clots or clotting disorders.
  • Warning Signs: Seek immediate medical attention for any sudden onset of shortness of breath, chest pain, or symptoms suggestive of DVT.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Vitamin K Intake (for Warfarin users): Maintain a consistent intake of Vitamin K-rich foods (e.g., leafy green vegetables) if on warfarin, as fluctuations can affect its efficacy. Do not drastically change your diet without medical advice.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration, which can contribute to increased blood viscosity and clot formation.
  • Alcohol Consumption: Limit or avoid excessive alcohol intake, as it can interact with anticoagulant medications and increase bleeding risk.
  • Supplement Caution: Discuss all herbal supplements, vitamins, and over-the-counter medications with your physician, as some can interfere with blood clotting or anticoagulant therapy (e.g., Omega-3 fatty acids, garlic, ginger).
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a generally healthy, balanced diet to support overall cardiovascular health.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Activation: Call emergency services immediately if a patient exhibits sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, or signs of circulatory collapse.
  • Patient Positioning: Help the patient into a comfortable position, often semi-Fowler's, to ease breathing.
  • Vital Sign Monitoring: Continuously monitor vital signs, including respiratory rate, heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation.
  • Reassurance: Keep the patient calm and provide reassurance, as anxiety can worsen respiratory distress.
  • Medication History: Be prepared to provide a complete list of the patient's current medications, allergies, and medical history to emergency personnel.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prompt Diagnosis: Suspect PE in patients presenting with acute dyspnea and chest pain, especially with risk factors. Confirm diagnosis with imaging like CT pulmonary angiography.
  • Anticoagulation: Initiate anticoagulation promptly with heparin (LMWH or unfractionated) or direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) as the cornerstone of treatment.
  • Risk Stratification: Assess the patient's hemodynamic stability and risk of adverse outcomes to guide management, including consideration for thrombolysis or embolectomy in high-risk cases.
  • Long-term Management: Determine the appropriate duration of anticoagulation based on the PE's cause (provoked vs. unprovoked) and individual bleeding/recurrence risk.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients thoroughly on medication adherence, signs of bleeding, and symptoms of recurrence.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Prioritization: Focus on ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation) immediately upon suspicion of PE. Administer oxygen as prescribed.
  • Assessment: Conduct a thorough respiratory and cardiovascular assessment, noting any signs of hypoxemia, tachypnea, tachycardia, or hypotension.
  • Interventions: Establish IV access, prepare for anticoagulant administration, and monitor for signs of bleeding or adverse reactions to medications.
  • Patient Education: Instruct patients on the importance of medication adherence, recognizing signs of bleeding, and symptoms that warrant immediate medical attention.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide emotional support to patients and families, addressing anxiety related to the acute and potentially life-threatening nature of PE.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Often a consequence of Virchow's Triad: venous stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability, leading to DVT and subsequent embolization.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA) is the gold standard; V/Q scan for patients with contraindications to CTPA; D-dimer assay for ruling out PE in low-probability patients.
  • Pharmacology: Anticoagulants include unfractionated heparin, low molecular weight heparins (LMWH), warfarin, and direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) such such as rivaroxaban, apixaban, dabigatran, and edoxaban.
  • Interventional Procedures: Catheter-directed thrombolysis, systemic thrombolysis, surgical pulmonary embolectomy, and inferior vena cava (IVC) filter placement for select patients.
  • Risk Assessment Tools: Wells' score for PE probability, PERC rule (Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria), and Geneva score for clinical probability assessment.
