Taking medicines to prevent HIV before exposure

🏠 Home / Understanding medicines / Medicines for specific health problems

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding PrEP: Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) involves taking specific antiretroviral medications to prevent HIV infection in individuals who are HIV-negative but at substantial risk.
  • High Effectiveness: When taken consistently as prescribed, PrEP is highly effective (over 99%) at preventing sexual HIV transmission and highly effective for injection drug use.
  • Target Population: Recommended for individuals with ongoing risk factors, including those with an HIV-positive partner, multiple partners, history of STIs, or injection drug use.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sexual Risk Factors: Engaging in anal or vaginal sex without condoms with partners of unknown HIV status or who are HIV-positive and not virally suppressed.
  • Partner Status: Having a sexual partner who is HIV-positive with a detectable viral load.
  • Recent STIs: A history of recent bacterial sexually transmitted infections (e.g., syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia).
  • Injection Drug Use: Sharing needles or other injection equipment.
  • Frequent PEP Use: Repeated or frequent use of Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) in the past.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Confirmed HIV-Negative Status: PrEP is only for individuals confirmed to be HIV-negative; taking PrEP while HIV-positive can lead to drug resistance.
  • Kidney Function Monitoring: Baseline and ongoing monitoring of kidney function (creatinine clearance) is essential, as PrEP medications can affect renal health.
  • Hepatitis B Status: If positive for Hepatitis B, discontinuing PrEP can lead to a severe flare-up of Hepatitis B, requiring careful management.
  • Drug Interaction Review: Discuss all other medications, supplements, and herbal remedies with a healthcare provider to identify potential interactions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Medication Intake: Most PrEP regimens can be taken with or without food, but consistency in timing is crucial for maintaining protective drug levels.
  • Hydration Importance: Maintaining adequate hydration is important, especially given the potential for kidney function monitoring associated with PrEP medications.
  • General Guidance: No specific food or drink restrictions are typically required with PrEP, but always follow your prescriber's specific instructions.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Adherence is Key: Missing doses significantly reduces PrEP's effectiveness; consistent daily or on-demand dosing as prescribed is vital.
  • Regular Testing: Requires regular HIV testing (typically every 3 months) to ensure continued HIV-negative status and monitor for breakthrough infections.
  • STI Prevention: PrEP only prevents HIV; it does not protect against other sexually transmitted infections (STIs) like syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, or herpes.
  • Potential Side Effects: Common side effects can include nausea, diarrhea, headache, and fatigue, usually mild and transient. Report persistent or severe symptoms.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough medical evaluation, including HIV testing, STI screening, and kidney function tests, is mandatory before initiating PrEP.
  • Personalized Regimen: The choice of PrEP regimen (daily oral, on-demand, or injectable) should be individualized based on risk factors, preferences, and adherence potential.
  • Ongoing Support: Regular follow-up appointments are crucial for monitoring adherence, side effects, kidney function, and continued HIV/STI screening.
  • Holistic Sexual Health: PrEP is part of a comprehensive HIV prevention strategy that includes condom use, regular STI testing, and harm reduction for injection drug use.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a critical role in educating patients about PrEP benefits, proper dosing, potential side effects, and the importance of adherence.
  • Monitoring & Screening: Assisting with scheduling and explaining the necessity of regular HIV, STI, and kidney function tests.
  • Adherence Support: Providing counseling and strategies to support consistent medication taking and addressing barriers to adherence.
  • Resource Navigation: Connecting patients to resources for sexual health, mental health, and substance use support.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Approved Medications: Oral PrEP typically involves emtricitabine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (FTC/TDF) or emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide (FTC/TAF). Injectable cabotegravir is also approved.
  • Mechanism of Action: These antiretroviral drugs inhibit enzymes (reverse transcriptase or integrase) essential for HIV replication, preventing the virus from establishing an infection.
  • Key Studies: Landmark trials like iPrEx, PROUD, IPERGAY, and DISCOVER demonstrated the efficacy and safety of oral PrEP. HPTN 083 and HPTN 084 supported injectable cabotegravir.
  • Clinical Guidelines: PrEP prescribing and monitoring are guided by recommendations from organizations such as the CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) and WHO (World Health Organization).
