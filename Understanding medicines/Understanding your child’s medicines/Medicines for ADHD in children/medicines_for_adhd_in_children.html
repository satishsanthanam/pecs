Medicines for ADHD in children

🏠 Home / Understanding medicines / Understanding your child’s medicines

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of Medication: ADHD medications are prescribed to help manage core symptoms such as inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity, thereby improving focus, self-control, and overall functioning in children.
  • Main Categories: Medications primarily fall into two categories: stimulants (e.g., methylphenidate, amphetamines) and non-stimulants (e.g., atomoxetine, guanfacine, clonidine). Each works differently to affect brain chemicals.
  • Individualized Treatment: The choice and dosage of medication are highly individualized, based on the child's specific symptoms, age, weight, medical history, and response to treatment.
  • Not a Cure: These medicines do not cure ADHD but help control symptoms while they are active in the body, allowing children to better utilize behavioral therapies and academic support.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Inattention: Difficulty sustaining attention in tasks or play activities, often making careless mistakes, appearing not to listen, or struggling to follow instructions.
  • Hyperactivity: Fidgeting, squirming, inability to stay seated, excessive running or climbing, difficulty playing quietly, and often talking excessively.
  • Impulsivity: Blurting out answers before questions are completed, difficulty waiting their turn, interrupting or intruding on others, and engaging in risky behaviors without considering consequences.
  • Academic & Social Challenges: Difficulties in school performance, strained peer relationships, and challenges in following rules or routines at home.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Cardiac Screening: A thorough cardiac history and physical examination, including blood pressure and heart rate, are essential before initiating stimulant medication due to potential cardiovascular effects.
  • Growth Monitoring: Regular monitoring of height and weight is crucial, as some medications can cause appetite suppression and impact growth over time.
  • Psychiatric Monitoring: Watch for new or worsening psychiatric symptoms such as anxiety, depression, agitation, or suicidal ideation, especially with non-stimulant medications.
  • Potential for Misuse/Diversion: Store medications securely to prevent accidental ingestion by others or intentional misuse/diversion, particularly with stimulant medications.
  • Drug Interactions: Inform the prescribing physician about all other medications, supplements, and herbal remedies the child is taking to avoid adverse drug interactions.
  • Abrupt Discontinuation: Do not stop medication abruptly without consulting the physician, as some non-stimulants may require gradual tapering to avoid withdrawal symptoms or symptom rebound.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Managing Appetite Suppression: Offer high-calorie, nutrient-dense meals and snacks when appetite is best (e.g., early morning before medication, or in the evening after medication effects wear off).
  • Consistent Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake throughout the day, as some medications can cause dry mouth.
  • Caffeine Avoidance: Limit or avoid caffeine-containing beverages and foods (soda, energy drinks, chocolate) as caffeine can exacerbate stimulant side effects like jitters, anxiety, and sleep disturbances.
  • Acidic Foods & Juices: Avoid consuming highly acidic foods or juices (e.g., orange juice, vitamin C supplements) close to the time of taking certain amphetamine-based medications, as they can interfere with absorption.
  • Meal Timing: Administer medication as directed, often with or after meals to minimize stomach upset, unless otherwise specified by the physician.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Strict Adherence: Administer medication exactly as prescribed by the physician regarding dosage, frequency, and timing. Do not adjust doses without medical advice.
  • Safe Storage: Keep all medications in a secure, locked location, out of reach of children and others, to prevent accidental ingestion or unauthorized access.
  • Prompt Reporting of Side Effects: Immediately report any new or concerning side effects (e.g., severe mood changes, chest pain, significant sleep problems, tics) to the prescribing physician.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments for medication adjustments, symptom monitoring, and overall health assessments.
  • Do Not Share: Never share ADHD medication with others, as it is prescribed specifically for the individual child and can be harmful if taken by someone else.
  • School Communication: Inform the school nurse and relevant school personnel about the child's medication regimen and any potential side effects that may impact school performance or behavior.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Treatment: Medication is most effective when integrated into a comprehensive treatment plan that includes behavioral therapy, parent training, and educational support.
  • Titration Process: Finding the optimal medication and dose often involves a careful titration process, starting with a low dose and gradually increasing it while monitoring for efficacy and side effects.
  • Ongoing Assessment: Regular clinical assessments are vital to monitor the child's response to medication, evaluate for side effects, and make necessary adjustments to the treatment plan over time.
  • Open Communication: Maintain open and honest communication with your child's healthcare provider about all aspects of their health, behavior, and any concerns you may have.
  • Long-term Management: ADHD is a chronic condition, and medication management may be a long-term strategy, requiring periodic re-evaluation as the child grows and develops.
  • Educate Yourself: Understand the specific medication prescribed, including its benefits, potential side effects, and how to administer it correctly.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Medication Administration: Verify the 'five rights' of medication administration (right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time) and educate parents on proper home administration techniques.
  • Side Effect Monitoring: Systematically assess for common side effects such as appetite suppression, insomnia, headache, stomachache, irritability, and cardiovascular changes (HR, BP).
  • Patient Education: Provide clear, age-appropriate education to children and their caregivers about the medication's purpose, expected effects, and importance of adherence.
  • Growth & Development: Monitor and chart height and weight regularly, reporting any significant deviations from growth curves to the healthcare provider.
  • School Nurse Collaboration: Work closely with school nurses to ensure safe medication storage and administration during school hours, and to monitor for medication effects or side effects in the school setting.
  • Diversion Awareness: Educate families on the risks of medication diversion and strategies for secure storage and disposal of unused medication.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pharmacokinetics: Understand the onset of action, peak effect, and duration of different formulations (immediate-release, extended-release) for various stimulant and non-stimulant medications.
  • Mechanism of Action: Stimulants primarily increase dopamine and norepinephrine in the synaptic cleft; non-stimulants have varied mechanisms, e.g., norepinephrine reuptake inhibition (atomoxetine) or alpha-2 adrenergic agonism (guanfacine, clonidine).
  • Common Adverse Effects: Anorexia, insomnia, headache, abdominal pain, irritability, increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, and potential for tics.
  • Contraindications: Absolute contraindications for stimulants include symptomatic cardiovascular disease, moderate to severe hypertension, hyperthyroidism, glaucoma, and history of severe agitation or drug abuse.
  • Black Box Warnings: Be aware of black box warnings, particularly for stimulants regarding potential for abuse and dependence, and for atomoxetine regarding increased risk of suicidal ideation in children and adolescents.
  • Drug-Drug Interactions: Significant interactions can occur with MAOIs, tricyclic antidepressants, SSRIs, antihypertensives, and certain antacids; always review the full drug interaction profile.
