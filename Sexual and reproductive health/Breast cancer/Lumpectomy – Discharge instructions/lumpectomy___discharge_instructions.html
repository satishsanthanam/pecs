Lumpectomy – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Breast cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Wound Care: Keep your incision clean and dry. You may shower 24-48 hours after surgery, but avoid scrubbing the incision site. Pat dry gently.
  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medication as directed to manage discomfort. Do not wait for pain to become severe before taking medication.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting (nothing over 5-10 pounds), strenuous activities, and repetitive arm movements on the affected side for 2-4 weeks, or as advised by your surgeon.
  • Drain Care (if applicable): If you have a surgical drain, you will receive specific instructions on how to empty it, measure output, and care for the site. Keep a record of the drain output.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Ensure you attend all scheduled post-operative appointments with your surgeon and oncology team to monitor your recovery and discuss further treatment plans.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Expected Discomfort: It is normal to experience mild to moderate pain, soreness, bruising, and swelling around the incision site and breast.
  • Numbness or Tingling: You may experience temporary numbness, tingling, or altered sensation in the breast, armpit, or inner arm due to nerve irritation or damage.
  • Signs of Infection: Watch for increased redness, warmth, swelling, pus-like drainage from the incision, foul odor, or a fever greater than 100.4°F (38°C).
  • Hematoma or Seroma: Report any sudden, significant swelling, firmness, or a new collection of fluid under the skin at the surgical site, which could indicate a hematoma (blood collection) or seroma (fluid collection).
  • Lymphedema Concerns: Be aware of persistent swelling, tightness, or heaviness in the arm, hand, or chest wall on the affected side, which could be an early sign of lymphedema.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Incision Protection: Do not soak the incision in a bathtub, hot tub, or swimming pool until it is fully healed and approved by your surgeon to prevent infection.
  • Arm Movement: Perform gentle arm and shoulder exercises as instructed by your physical therapist or nurse to prevent stiffness and improve range of motion, but avoid overexertion.
  • Clothing: Wear loose-fitting, comfortable clothing and a supportive, non-underwire bra (if advised) to avoid pressure and irritation on the surgical site.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive while taking narcotic pain medication or if your arm movement is restricted to the point of impairing your ability to safely operate a vehicle.
  • Sexual Activity: Discuss with your surgeon when it is safe to resume sexual activity, as comfort levels and physical limitations may vary during recovery.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths, diluted juices) to stay hydrated, especially if you are taking pain medications that can cause constipation.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate fiber-rich foods such as fruits, vegetables, and whole grains into your diet to prevent constipation, a common side effect of pain medication and reduced activity.
  • Light, Bland Meals: Start with light, easily digestible meals after surgery, gradually returning to your regular diet as tolerated. Avoid heavy, greasy, or spicy foods initially.
  • Avoid Alcohol and Caffeine: Limit or avoid alcohol and excessive caffeine intake during your initial recovery period, as they can interfere with healing and medication effectiveness.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein for tissue repair and vitamins for overall healing and immune support.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contacts: Ensure you have the surgeon's office number, after-hours contact, and emergency services number readily available.
  • Medication Assistance: Help the patient adhere to their medication schedule, especially for pain relief, and monitor for any adverse reactions.
  • Observation for Complications: Closely monitor the incision site for any signs of infection (redness, warmth, discharge) or excessive swelling/bleeding, and report immediately.
  • Support with Daily Activities: Assist the patient with tasks that involve lifting, reaching, or strenuous arm movements to prevent strain on the surgical site.
  • Emotional Support: Provide emotional support and encourage open communication about feelings, concerns, or any discomfort the patient may be experiencing.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Instructions: Strict adherence to post-operative wound care, activity restrictions, and medication regimens is paramount for optimal healing and complication prevention.
  • Emotional Well-being: A lumpectomy can have significant emotional and psychological impacts. Encourage patients to seek support from family, friends, support groups, or mental health professionals.
  • Rehabilitation: Early, gentle range-of-motion exercises are crucial to prevent shoulder stiffness and cording, but always follow specific physical therapy recommendations.
  • Long-term Surveillance: Emphasize the importance of ongoing breast cancer surveillance, including regular clinical breast exams, mammograms, and adherence to adjuvant therapy plans.
  • Open Communication: Patients should feel empowered to communicate any concerns, unexpected symptoms, or questions directly to their healthcare team without hesitation.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Systematically review and reinforce all discharge instructions, including wound care, pain management, activity limitations, and signs of complications.
  • Vital Signs Monitoring: Assess vital signs regularly, particularly temperature, to detect early signs of infection or other post-operative complications.
  • Drain Management Education: Provide clear, hands-on demonstration and return demonstration for surgical drain care, including emptying, measuring output, and securing the drain.
  • Pain Assessment and Intervention: Utilize a pain scale to assess pain levels accurately and ensure effective administration of analgesics, evaluating their efficacy and side effects.
  • Psychosocial Support: Assess the patient's emotional state, provide active listening, and offer resources for psychological support and breast cancer support groups.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Surgical Site Infection (SSI): Common pathogens, risk factors (e.g., obesity, diabetes), and prophylactic antibiotic use.
  • Seroma/Hematoma Formation: Pathophysiology, clinical presentation, and management strategies including aspiration or re-intervention.
  • Lymphedema Prophylaxis: Early identification of risk factors, education on arm precautions, and referral to lymphedema therapists.
  • Adjuvant Therapy Considerations: Discussion of post-lumpectomy radiation therapy, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, and targeted therapy based on pathology results.
  • Oncoplastic Breast Surgery: Techniques combining tumor resection with immediate breast reconstruction or reshaping to optimize cosmetic outcomes and patient satisfaction.
