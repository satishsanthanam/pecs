Sentinel lymph node biopsy for breast cancer

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Breast cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of SLNB: Sentinel lymph node biopsy is a surgical procedure to determine if breast cancer cells have spread from the primary tumor to the lymph nodes under the arm (axilla).
  • Minimally Invasive Approach: It aims to identify and remove only the 'sentinel' lymph node(s) – the first nodes to which cancer cells are most likely to spread – rather than a full axillary dissection.
  • Staging and Treatment Guidance: The results are crucial for accurately staging breast cancer and guiding subsequent treatment decisions, including chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and further surgery.
  • Procedure Overview: Involves injecting a radioactive tracer, a blue dye, or both near the tumor to locate the sentinel node(s) which are then surgically removed and examined by a pathologist.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Palpable Breast Lump: A new or changing lump or mass in the breast, often the primary indicator for breast cancer diagnosis.
  • Changes in Breast Skin: Dimpling, puckering, redness, scaling, or thickening of the skin on the breast.
  • Nipple Abnormalities: Nipple retraction, discharge (especially bloody or clear), or changes in nipple appearance.
  • Axillary Swelling or Lump: Swelling or a palpable lump in the armpit, which may suggest lymph node involvement.
  • Persistent Breast Pain: While less common, persistent pain in the breast or nipple area can sometimes be a symptom.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Allergy Disclosure: Inform your medical team about any known allergies, particularly to dyes (e.g., isosulfan blue, methylene blue) or radioactive substances.
  • Medication Review: Discuss all current medications, including over-the-counter drugs, supplements, and especially blood thinners (anticoagulants), which may need to be adjusted or temporarily stopped before surgery.
  • Pregnancy and Lactation Status: It is vital to inform your doctor if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, as the radioactive tracer and blue dye may pose risks.
  • Prior Surgical History: Disclose any previous breast surgeries, axillary surgeries, or radiation therapy to the breast/axilla, as these can alter lymphatic drainage patterns.
  • Infection Prevention: Follow all pre-operative skin preparation instructions to minimize the risk of surgical site infection.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to the 'nil per os' (NPO) guidelines provided by your surgical team, typically requiring no food or drink for 6-8 hours before the procedure.
  • Hydration Before Fasting: Ensure adequate hydration in the days leading up to the procedure, stopping at the specified fasting time.
  • Post-operative Fluid Reintroduction: Begin with clear liquids after surgery, gradually advancing to light, easily digestible foods as tolerated and as advised by your care team.
  • Avoid Alcohol and Smoking: Refrain from alcohol consumption and smoking for at least several weeks before and after surgery to promote optimal healing and reduce complications.
  • Nutrient-Rich Recovery Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein, vitamins, and minerals post-surgery to support tissue repair and recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Post-operative Discomfort: Expect some pain, tenderness, and bruising at the surgical site and potentially in the armpit; manage with prescribed pain medication.
  • Skin and Urine Discoloration: If blue dye is used, your skin (especially near the injection site) and urine may temporarily appear blue or green for a day or two.
  • Lymphedema Monitoring: Be vigilant for signs of lymphedema, such as swelling, heaviness, or tightness in the arm or hand on the affected side, and report any concerns immediately.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and repetitive arm movements on the affected side for a period as advised by your surgeon to aid healing.
  • Incision Care: Keep the surgical incision clean and dry, following specific dressing change instructions to prevent infection and promote healing.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Precision in Staging: SLNB offers a precise method for assessing nodal involvement, crucial for accurate breast cancer staging and prognosis.
  • Reduced Morbidity: By avoiding full axillary dissection in node-negative patients, SLNB significantly reduces the risk of long-term complications like lymphedema and nerve damage.
  • Tailored Treatment Plans: The pathological findings from the sentinel node directly inform decisions regarding adjuvant therapies, including chemotherapy, radiation, and endocrine therapy.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Optimal management involves a collaborative effort between surgeons, oncologists, radiation oncologists, and pathologists to ensure comprehensive care.
  • Patient Selection: Careful patient selection is paramount; SLNB is most appropriate for clinically node-negative breast cancer patients.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Patient Education: Educate patients thoroughly on the procedure, potential side effects (e.g., blue discoloration, radiation exposure), and post-operative expectations.
  • Assessment for Contraindications: Conduct a comprehensive pre-operative assessment, including allergy history, medication review, and pregnancy status.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, surgical site for bleeding/infection, pain levels, and early signs of lymphedema or nerve injury.
  • Wound Care and Drain Management: Provide clear instructions for incision care, dressing changes, and management of any surgical drains, including output measurement.
  • Psychosocial Support: Offer empathetic support and resources to patients coping with a cancer diagnosis and surgical intervention, addressing fears and anxieties.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Radioactive Tracer: Commonly Technetium-99m (Tc-99m) sulfur colloid or albumin colloid, injected peritumorally or intradermally.
  • Blue Dyes: Isosulfan blue (Lymphazurin) or methylene blue, used to visually identify lymphatic channels and nodes.
  • Gamma Probe: A handheld device used intraoperatively to detect radioactivity and locate the sentinel lymph node(s).
  • Lymphoscintigraphy: A nuclear medicine imaging technique performed pre-operatively to map lymphatic drainage and identify sentinel nodes.
  • Frozen Section Analysis: Rapid pathological examination of the sentinel node during surgery to determine immediate nodal status.
  • Immunohistochemistry (IHC): Advanced pathology technique used for definitive detection of micrometastases or isolated tumor cells in the sentinel node.
