Breast cancer

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Breast cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Prevalence: Breast cancer is the most common cancer among women worldwide, though it can also affect men.
  • Early Detection: Early detection through screening and self-awareness significantly improves prognosis and treatment outcomes.
  • Risk Factors: Factors include genetics (BRCA1/2 mutations), age, dense breast tissue, obesity, alcohol consumption, and certain hormone therapies.
  • Types: Common types include invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC) and invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC), with various less common forms.
  • Impact: Diagnosis and treatment can profoundly impact physical, emotional, and sexual health, requiring comprehensive support.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • New Lump or Mass: A new lump or thickening in the breast or underarm that feels different from the surrounding tissue.
  • Skin Changes: Dimpling, puckering, redness, scaling, or thickening of the breast skin.
  • Nipple Changes: Nipple inversion, discharge (other than breast milk), or scaling of the nipple or breast skin.
  • Breast Pain: Persistent breast or nipple pain that does not resolve with the menstrual cycle.
  • Swelling: Swelling of all or part of the breast, even if no distinct lump is felt.
  • Lymph Node Changes: Swelling of lymph nodes around the collarbone or under the arm.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Regular Screening: Adhere to recommended mammography schedules based on age and risk factors, typically starting at age 40 or 50.
  • Clinical Breast Exams: Undergo regular clinical breast exams by a healthcare professional as part of routine check-ups.
  • Breast Self-Awareness: Be familiar with the normal look and feel of your breasts and report any changes promptly to a doctor.
  • Genetic Counseling: Consider genetic counseling and testing if there is a strong family history of breast or ovarian cancer.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: Maintain a healthy weight, limit alcohol intake, engage in regular physical activity, and avoid smoking.
  • Hormone Therapy Review: Discuss the risks and benefits of hormone replacement therapy with your physician, especially if you have risk factors.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Plant-Based Focus: Emphasize a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes to support overall health and potentially reduce recurrence risk.
  • Limit Processed Foods: Reduce intake of highly processed foods, sugary drinks, and red and processed meats.
  • Healthy Fats: Incorporate healthy fats from sources like avocados, nuts, seeds, and olive oil.
  • Maintain Healthy Weight: Achieve and maintain a healthy body weight, as obesity is a known risk factor for breast cancer and recurrence.
  • Hydration During Treatment: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially during chemotherapy or radiation, to manage side effects and support recovery.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Limit or avoid alcohol consumption, as even moderate intake can increase breast cancer risk.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Evaluation: Any new or persistent breast changes must be evaluated by a healthcare provider without delay.
  • Adherence to Treatment: Strictly follow prescribed treatment plans, including medications, chemotherapy, radiation, and surgical aftercare.
  • Side Effect Management: Report all treatment side effects to your medical team for appropriate management and support.
  • Psychosocial Support: Seek support from family, friends, support groups, or mental health professionals to cope with the emotional impact.
  • Fertility Preservation: Discuss fertility preservation options with your oncologist before starting treatment, if applicable.
  • Sexual Health: Address concerns about sexual health and intimacy with your healthcare provider, as treatment can impact these areas.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Breast cancer care requires a team approach involving surgeons, oncologists, radiation oncologists, pathologists, and support staff.
  • Personalized Treatment: Treatment plans are highly individualized, based on cancer type, stage, hormone receptor status, HER2 status, and patient preferences.
  • Genetic Testing: Recommend genetic testing for patients with a strong family history or early-onset breast cancer to guide treatment and screening for relatives.
  • Clinical Trials: Discuss participation in clinical trials for eligible patients to access innovative treatments.
  • Survivorship Care: Emphasize the importance of long-term follow-up care, including surveillance for recurrence and management of late effects of treatment.
  • Shared Decision-Making: Encourage patients to actively participate in treatment decisions, ensuring their values and goals are respected.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients on breast self-awareness, screening guidelines, and the importance of adherence to treatment.
  • Pre- and Post-Operative Care: Provide comprehensive care for surgical patients, including pain management, wound care, and lymphedema prevention.
  • Chemotherapy & Radiation Support: Monitor for and manage side effects of chemotherapy and radiation therapy, including nausea, fatigue, skin reactions, and myelosuppression.
  • Psychosocial Assessment: Assess patients for psychological distress, body image concerns, and sexual health issues, providing appropriate referrals.
  • Medication Management: Educate patients on hormone therapy, targeted therapy, and other medications, including potential side effects and adherence.
  • Resource Navigation: Assist patients in accessing support groups, financial aid, and other community resources.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Mammography: X-ray imaging of the breast used for screening and diagnosis.
  • Biopsy: Removal of tissue for microscopic examination (e.g., core needle biopsy, excisional biopsy).
  • TNM Staging: Classification system (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) used to describe the extent of cancer.
  • Hormone Receptor Status: Testing for estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) expression, guiding hormone therapy.
  • HER2 Status: Testing for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2, guiding targeted therapy.
  • Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy: Surgical procedure to identify and remove the first lymph nodes to which cancer cells are likely to spread.
  • Adjuvant Therapy: Treatment given after primary treatment (e.g., surgery) to reduce the risk of recurrence.
  • Neoadjuvant Therapy: Treatment given before primary treatment (e.g., surgery) to shrink the tumor.
  • Lymphedema: Swelling caused by lymph fluid buildup, often a side effect of lymph node removal or radiation.
