Mastectomy – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Breast cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Recovery: Your recovery at home is a crucial phase requiring careful attention to wound care, pain management, and activity restrictions.
  • Wound Care Essentials: Keep your surgical incision clean and dry. Follow specific instructions for dressing changes provided by your healthcare team.
  • Pain Management Strategy: Take prescribed pain medication as directed to manage discomfort. Do not wait for pain to become severe before taking medication.
  • Activity Limitations: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, and sudden arm movements on the affected side to promote healing and prevent complications.
  • Follow-Up Appointments: Ensure you attend all scheduled post-operative appointments to monitor your healing progress and address any concerns.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Signs of Infection: Report any increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus-like drainage from the incision, or a fever above 100.4°F (38°C) immediately.
  • Excessive Swelling or Bruising: Monitor for significant or rapidly increasing swelling, bruising, or a hard lump under the skin, which could indicate a hematoma or seroma.
  • Uncontrolled Pain: Contact your doctor if your pain is not adequately managed by prescribed medications or if it suddenly worsens.
  • New Arm Swelling: Be vigilant for any swelling, heaviness, or discomfort in the arm or hand on the side of the surgery, as this could be an early sign of lymphedema.
  • Numbness or Tingling: While some numbness is common, report any severe or persistent numbness, tingling, or weakness in the arm or hand.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Arm and Shoulder Exercises: Begin gentle range-of-motion exercises as instructed by your physical therapist or nurse to prevent stiffness and promote lymphatic drainage.
  • Avoid Heavy Lifting: Refrain from lifting anything heavier than 5-10 pounds (e.g., a gallon of milk) with the affected arm for at least 4-6 weeks, or as advised.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive while taking opioid pain medication or until you can comfortably and safely operate the vehicle, including emergency braking and steering.
  • Surgical Drain Care: If you have surgical drains, meticulously follow instructions for emptying, measuring output, and caring for the drain sites to prevent infection and ensure proper function.
  • Protect the Affected Arm: Avoid blood pressure cuffs, injections, or blood draws on the arm on the side of the mastectomy to reduce the risk of lymphedema.
  • Sun Protection: Protect your incision site from direct sun exposure for at least one year to prevent hyperpigmentation and promote optimal scar healing.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids, such as water and clear broths, to aid in recovery and prevent dehydration, especially if you are taking pain medications.
  • Fiber-Rich Diet: Incorporate high-fiber foods like fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to prevent constipation, which is a common side effect of opioid pain medications.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a well-balanced diet rich in protein, vitamins, and minerals to support wound healing and overall recovery.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol, especially while taking pain medications, as it can interact with medications and impair judgment.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: If you experience nausea or decreased appetite, try eating smaller, more frequent meals throughout the day.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assist with Daily Activities: Be prepared to assist the patient with personal care, dressing, and light household tasks, especially in the initial weeks post-surgery.
  • Medication Management: Help the patient adhere to their medication schedule, ensuring pain relief and preventing complications.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be aware of the warning signs of infection, excessive swelling, or uncontrolled pain, and know when to contact the healthcare provider.
  • Emotional Support: Provide emotional comfort and support, acknowledging the physical and emotional challenges of recovery from mastectomy.
  • Transportation Assistance: Arrange transportation for follow-up appointments and other necessary outings, as the patient may have driving restrictions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Instructions: Strict adherence to all post-operative instructions is paramount for optimal healing and minimizing complications.
  • Proactive Pain Management: Do not hesitate to utilize prescribed analgesics to maintain comfort, as effective pain control facilitates early mobilization and recovery.
  • Gradual Activity Progression: Gradually increase your activity level as tolerated, but always prioritize rest and avoid overexertion to prevent strain on the surgical site.
  • Emotional Well-being: It is normal to experience a range of emotions during this time; seek support from family, friends, or professional counselors if needed.
  • Open Communication: Maintain open communication with your healthcare team. Report any unusual symptoms or concerns promptly to ensure timely intervention.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Systematically reinforce teaching on wound care, drain management, pain assessment, and medication administration during discharge.
  • Lymphedema Risk Assessment: Educate patients on lymphedema prevention strategies, including skin care, avoiding trauma to the affected arm, and recognizing early signs.
  • Psychosocial Support Integration: Assess for body image concerns, anxiety, or depression, and provide resources for support groups or mental health services.
  • Safe Discharge Planning: Ensure the patient and caregiver demonstrate understanding of all discharge instructions, including emergency contact information and follow-up schedule.
  • Home Environment Assessment: Consider potential barriers in the home environment that may impede recovery and provide adaptive strategies or referrals as needed.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Surgical Site Infection (SSI) Prevention: Emphasize sterile technique for dressing changes and meticulous hand hygiene to minimize bacterial contamination.
  • Lymphedema Prophylaxis: Early initiation of therapeutic exercises and patient education on risk factor modification are critical for preventing post-mastectomy lymphedema.
  • Neuropathic Pain Management: Be aware of potential neuropathic pain (e.g., post-mastectomy pain syndrome) and discuss appropriate pharmacological or non-pharmacological interventions.
  • Reconstructive Options Discussion: Provide information on breast reconstruction options, including timing and types, for future consideration by the patient.
  • Oncological Surveillance: Stress the importance of ongoing oncological follow-up, including regular screenings and appointments, as part of comprehensive cancer care.
