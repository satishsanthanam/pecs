# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Mastectomy is a surgical procedure involving the removal of the entire breast, often performed to treat or prevent breast cancer.
* **Types of Mastectomy:** Includes total (simple), modified radical, skin-sparing, and nipple-sparing mastectomies, chosen based on cancer stage, size, and location.
* **Indications:** Primarily for invasive breast cancer, ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS), or as a prophylactic measure for individuals at very high genetic risk (e.g., BRCA1/2 mutations).
* **Reconstruction Options:** Patients often have the option for immediate or delayed breast reconstruction using implants or autologous tissue.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Palpable Breast Lump:** A new, often painless lump or mass in the breast, which may be firm or irregular.
* **Skin Changes:** Dimpling, puckering, redness, scaling, or thickening of the breast skin, sometimes resembling an orange peel (peau d'orange).
* **Nipple Abnormalities:** Nipple retraction, inversion, discharge (especially bloody or clear), or changes in nipple skin (e.g., scaling, crusting).
* **Axillary Lymph Node Swelling:** Enlargement or tenderness of lymph nodes in the armpit, indicating potential spread of cancer.
* **Persistent Breast Pain:** While less common, persistent or localized breast pain that does not resolve can be a symptom.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Pre-operative Medication Review:** Discontinue anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents, and certain supplements (e.g., Vitamin E, fish oil) as directed by the surgeon to minimize bleeding risk.
* **Infection Prevention:** Maintain strict hand hygiene and follow wound care instructions meticulously post-operatively to prevent surgical site infections.
* **Lymphedema Risk Management:** Avoid blood pressure measurements, injections, or blood draws in the affected arm; protect the arm from injury, sunburn, and infection.
* **Activity Restrictions:** Limit heavy lifting, strenuous activities, and overhead arm movements for several weeks post-surgery to allow for proper healing and prevent complications.
* **Drain Care:** Monitor and record drain output regularly, ensuring drains are properly secured and emptied as instructed to prevent fluid accumulation (seroma).


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Pre-operative Fasting:** Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines for food and fluids for the specified duration before surgery to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
* **Post-operative Hydration:** Begin with clear liquids and gradually advance to a regular diet as tolerated, ensuring adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration.
* **Constipation Management:** Increase fiber intake and maintain hydration to counteract potential constipation, a common side effect of pain medications.
* **Nutrient-Rich Diet:** Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein, vitamins, and minerals to support wound healing and recovery.
* **Avoid Alcohol and Caffeine:** Limit or avoid alcohol and excessive caffeine intake during the immediate post-operative period, as they can interfere with healing and hydration.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Signs of Infection:** Immediately report fever (above 100.4°F or 38°C), increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or purulent discharge from the surgical site.
* **Excessive Bleeding:** Notify the medical team if there is significant or persistent bleeding from the incision or around the drains.
* **Sudden Swelling or Pain:** Report any sudden, severe swelling or pain in the breast area or arm that is not relieved by prescribed pain medication.
* **Changes in Drain Output:** Contact your healthcare provider if drain output abruptly stops, significantly increases, or changes in color or consistency.
* **Symptoms of Lymphedema:** Seek medical attention for persistent swelling, heaviness, tightness, or aching in the arm or hand on the affected side.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Shared Decision-Making:** Engage in thorough discussions with your surgical oncologist about all treatment options, including breast conservation versus mastectomy, and reconstruction choices.
* **Multidisciplinary Approach:** Mastectomy is often part of a comprehensive treatment plan that may include chemotherapy, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, or targeted therapy.
* **Psychosocial Support:** Acknowledge the significant emotional and psychological impact of mastectomy; consider counseling, support groups, or psychiatric evaluation as needed.
* **Long-term Surveillance:** Adhere to a personalized follow-up schedule, including clinical exams and imaging, to monitor for recurrence or new primary cancers.
* **Rehabilitation:** Early engagement in physical therapy and occupational therapy is crucial for restoring arm and shoulder mobility and preventing lymphedema.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pre-operative Education:** Provide comprehensive teaching on the surgical procedure, pain management expectations, drain care, and early mobilization exercises.
* **Post-operative Assessment:** Regularly assess vital signs, surgical site for signs of infection or hematoma, drain patency and output, and neurovascular status of the ipsilateral arm.
* **Pain Management:** Administer prescribed analgesics, assess pain levels frequently, and educate patients on non-pharmacological pain relief strategies.
* **Patient Mobility & Positioning:** Assist with early ambulation and proper positioning to prevent complications like deep vein thrombosis and promote comfort.
* **Lymphedema Prevention Education:** Instruct patients on arm precautions, skin care, and the importance of reporting early signs of lymphedema.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **ICD-10 Codes:** C50.x (Malignant neoplasm of breast), Z90.1x (Acquired absence of breast and nipple), Z85.3 (Personal history of malignant neoplasm of breast).
* **CPT Codes:** 19303 (Mastectomy, simple, complete), 19307 (Mastectomy, modified radical), 19305 (Mastectomy, radical).
* **Pathology Report Elements:** Tumor size, type, grade, lymph node status, receptor status (ER/PR/HER2), surgical margins, Ki-67 proliferation index.
* **Oncotype DX/MammaPrint:** Gene expression profiling tests used to assess recurrence risk and guide adjuvant chemotherapy decisions for certain breast cancers.
* **Lymphedema Staging:** Clinical classification of lymphedema severity (e.g., Stage 0-III) guiding management strategies.
