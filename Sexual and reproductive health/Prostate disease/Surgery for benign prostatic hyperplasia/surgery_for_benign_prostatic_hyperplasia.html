Surgery for benign prostatic hyperplasia

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Prostate disease

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Surgical Purpose: Procedures for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) aim to relieve urinary obstruction caused by an enlarged prostate, improving urine flow and reducing associated symptoms.
  • Primary Goal: To remove or ablate prostate tissue that is compressing the urethra, thereby restoring normal voiding function.
  • Common Procedures: Includes Transurethral Resection of the Prostate (TURP), various laser therapies (e.g., HoLEP, PVP), and open prostatectomy for very large glands.
  • Indications for Surgery: Typically considered when medical therapies fail, or for complications such as recurrent urinary tract infections, bladder stones, renal impairment, or refractory urinary retention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms (LUTS): Significant bother from urinary frequency, urgency, nocturia, weak or intermittent stream, straining to void, and a feeling of incomplete bladder emptying.
  • Acute Urinary Retention: Sudden inability to urpass urine, often requiring emergency catheterization.
  • Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs): Due to urinary stasis and incomplete bladder emptying.
  • Bladder Stones: Formation of calculi in the bladder as a result of chronic urinary obstruction.
  • Gross Hematuria: Visible blood in the urine, especially if recurrent and not attributed to other causes.
  • Renal Insufficiency: Impaired kidney function or hydronephrosis secondary to long-standing bladder outlet obstruction.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Anticoagulant Management: Strict adherence to pre-operative protocols for discontinuing or bridging blood-thinning medications to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Infection Prophylaxis: Administration of pre-operative antibiotics to reduce the risk of post-surgical infection.
  • Anesthesia Assessment: Comprehensive pre-anesthetic evaluation to identify and manage co-morbidities that may affect surgical risk.
  • Post-Operative Bleeding: Close monitoring for hematuria, clot retention, and signs of significant blood loss post-procedure.
  • TURP Syndrome Awareness: Vigilance for symptoms of hyponatremia (e.g., confusion, nausea, bradycardia) during and after Transurethral Resection of the Prostate.
  • Urinary Incontinence Counseling: Informing patients about the potential for temporary or, rarely, permanent urinary incontinence post-surgery.
  • Retrograde Ejaculation: Educating patients that retrograde ejaculation is a common and expected side effect of most BPH surgeries.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Operative Fasting: Strict adherence to NPO (nil per os) guidelines for food and drink, typically 6-8 hours prior to surgery, as instructed by the surgical team.
  • Post-Operative Hydration: Encourage increased oral fluid intake post-surgery to help flush the bladder, prevent clot formation, and maintain urinary flow.
  • Avoid Bladder Irritants: Temporarily restrict consumption of caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods post-operatively, as these can irritate the bladder and worsen urinary symptoms.
  • High-Fiber Diet: Promote a diet rich in fiber to prevent constipation and straining during bowel movements, which can increase intra-abdominal pressure and potentially lead to bleeding.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Post-Operative Monitoring: Closely observe the patient for signs of excessive bleeding (bright red urine, large clots), fever, severe pain, or inability to urinate.
  • Catheter Care Instructions: If discharged with a urinary catheter, ensure clear understanding of drainage bag management, hygiene, and signs of catheter blockage or infection.
  • Activity Restrictions Enforcement: Remind the patient to avoid heavy lifting, strenuous activities, and prolonged sitting for several weeks as advised by the surgeon.
  • Medication Adherence Support: Assist in ensuring the patient takes all prescribed pain relievers, antibiotics, and other medications as directed.
  • Emergency Contact Preparedness: Know when and how to contact the surgical team or seek emergency medical attention for concerning symptoms.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Treatment: The choice of surgical procedure is highly individualized, based on prostate size, symptom severity, patient co-morbidities, and personal preferences.
  • Risk-Benefit Discussion: A thorough discussion of potential benefits, such as significant symptom improvement, must be balanced against potential risks and side effects.
  • Comprehensive Pre-Operative Assessment: Essential to optimize patient health before surgery, including cardiac risk assessment and medication review.
  • Post-Operative Follow-up: Crucial for monitoring recovery, assessing long-term outcomes, and managing any persistent or new symptoms.
  • Expectation Management: Patients should be counselled on realistic outcomes, including the common occurrence of retrograde ejaculation and the potential for temporary urinary symptoms.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-Operative Education: Provide detailed patient teaching on the specific surgical procedure, expected post-operative course, pain management strategies, and discharge instructions.
  • Post-Operative Assessment: Conduct frequent assessments of vital signs, urine output, characteristics of urine (color, presence of clots), catheter patency, and pain levels.
  • Fluid Balance Management: Accurately monitor intake and output, especially with continuous bladder irrigation (CBI), to prevent fluid overload or dehydration.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics and evaluate their effectiveness, utilizing non-pharmacological interventions as appropriate.
  • Patient Mobility & Activity: Assist with early ambulation post-surgery to prevent complications like deep vein thrombosis and promote recovery.
  • Discharge Planning: Reinforce all discharge instructions, including wound care (if applicable), activity restrictions, medication regimen, and signs/symptoms requiring medical attention.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • TURP (Transurethral Resection of the Prostate): Endoscopic removal of prostate tissue using an electrosurgical loop; considered a gold standard for moderate-sized prostates.
  • HoLEP (Holmium Laser Enucleation of the Prostate): Laser-based procedure that enucleates the adenomatous prostate tissue, suitable for all prostate sizes, with lower bleeding risk.
  • PVP (Photoselective Vaporization of the Prostate): Uses a high-power laser (e.g., GreenLight) to vaporize prostate tissue, often associated with less bleeding.
  • Open Prostatectomy: Surgical removal of the prostate through an abdominal incision, typically reserved for very large prostates (>100 grams) or concomitant bladder pathology.
  • AUA Symptom Score: American Urological Association Symptom Index, a validated questionnaire used to quantify the severity of BPH symptoms.
  • Continuous Bladder Irrigation (CBI): Post-operative flushing of the bladder with sterile fluid to prevent clot formation and maintain catheter patency.
  • Retrograde Ejaculation: Condition where semen enters the bladder instead of exiting through the urethra during orgasm, a common consequence of BPH surgery.
