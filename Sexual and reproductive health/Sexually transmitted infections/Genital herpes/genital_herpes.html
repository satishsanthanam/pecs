Genital herpes

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Sexually transmitted infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Viral Infection: Genital herpes is caused by the herpes simplex virus (HSV), primarily HSV-2, but also HSV-1.
  • Lifelong Condition: Once infected, the virus remains dormant in the body, leading to potential recurrent outbreaks.
  • Transmission Routes: Primarily transmitted through skin-to-skin contact during sexual activity, even in the absence of visible sores.
  • Asymptomatic Shedding: The virus can be shed and transmitted when no symptoms or lesions are present.
  • No Cure, Manageable: There is currently no cure for herpes, but antiviral medications can effectively manage symptoms and reduce the frequency of outbreaks.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Primary Outbreak Lesions: Characterized by painful blisters or sores on the genitals, buttocks, inner thighs, or perianal area.
  • Flu-like Symptoms: Initial outbreaks may be accompanied by fever, body aches, swollen lymph nodes, and headache.
  • Recurrent Outbreaks: Typically milder and shorter in duration than the primary episode, often localized to the initial site.
  • Prodromal Symptoms: Many individuals experience tingling, itching, or pain in the affected area hours to days before lesions appear.
  • Dysuria: Pain or discomfort during urination can occur if sores are near the urethra.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Sexual Contact During Outbreaks: Refrain from all sexual activity when sores or prodromal symptoms are present to prevent transmission.
  • Consistent Condom Use: Use condoms consistently and correctly during all sexual encounters to reduce, though not eliminate, transmission risk.
  • Partner Disclosure: Openly discuss your herpes status with sexual partners to ensure informed consent and shared decision-making regarding risk reduction.
  • Antiviral Suppressive Therapy: Daily suppressive antiviral medication can significantly reduce the frequency of outbreaks and lower the risk of transmission to partners.
  • Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water after touching any lesions to prevent autoinoculation or spread to other body parts.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake, especially during outbreaks, to support overall health and healing processes.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in vitamins and minerals to bolster immune system function.
  • Lysine-Arginine Balance: Some individuals report that consuming foods high in lysine (e.g., dairy, fish, chicken) and limiting foods high in arginine (e.g., nuts, chocolate, seeds) may help manage outbreaks, though scientific evidence is mixed.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit acidic foods or beverages if they cause discomfort during urination, particularly with active genital lesions.
  • Immune-Boosting Foods: Incorporate foods rich in Vitamin C (citrus fruits, berries) and Zinc (lean meats, legumes) to potentially support immune response.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Neonatal Herpes Risk: Pregnant individuals with genital herpes must inform their healthcare provider due to the risk of transmission to the infant during vaginal delivery.
  • Cesarean Section Indication: A C-section may be recommended if active lesions or prodromal symptoms are present at the onset of labor to prevent neonatal herpes.
  • Immunocompromised Patients: Outbreaks can be more severe, prolonged, and atypical in individuals with weakened immune systems.
  • Ocular Herpes Prevention: Avoid touching eyes after touching lesions to prevent the spread of the virus to the eyes, which can cause serious vision impairment.
  • Psychosocial Support: Be aware of the potential for psychological distress, stigma, and impact on relationships; encourage seeking support from healthcare providers or support groups.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis & Counseling: Prompt diagnosis allows for timely initiation of antiviral therapy and comprehensive counseling on transmission, recurrence, and management strategies.
  • Antiviral Treatment Regimens: Prescribe appropriate antiviral medications (e.g., acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir) for episodic treatment of outbreaks or daily suppressive therapy.
  • STI Co-screening: Recommend screening for other sexually transmitted infections, as herpes can increase susceptibility to HIV acquisition.
  • Pregnancy Management: Closely monitor pregnant patients with herpes, providing guidance on antiviral prophylaxis in late pregnancy and delivery mode decisions.
  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Emphasize the importance of consistent medication adherence, safe sexual practices, and recognizing prodromal symptoms.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education & Empowerment: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients about herpes, dispelling myths, and empowering them to manage their condition effectively.
  • Emotional & Psychosocial Support: Provide empathetic care, addressing patient concerns regarding stigma, relationships, and quality of life impacts.
  • Medication Adherence Counseling: Instruct patients on the correct use, dosage, and potential side effects of antiviral medications for both episodic and suppressive therapy.
  • Lesion Care & Hygiene: Educate patients on proper hygiene for lesions to prevent secondary bacterial infections and promote healing.
  • Risk Reduction Strategies: Reinforce the importance of condom use, partner communication, and avoiding sexual contact during active outbreaks.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: Caused by Herpes Simplex Virus type 1 (HSV-1) or type 2 (HSV-2), both members of the Alphaherpesvirinae subfamily.
  • Pathophysiology: HSV establishes latency in sensory nerve ganglia (e.g., sacral ganglia for genital herpes), reactivating periodically to cause mucocutaneous lesions.
  • Diagnosis: Primarily clinical, confirmed by viral culture, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for HSV DNA, or type-specific serologic tests for HSV antibodies (IgG).
  • Complications: Aseptic meningitis, sacral radiculopathy, disseminated herpes in immunocompromised individuals, and neonatal herpes.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Syphilis chancres, chancroid, lymphogranuloma venereum, Behçet's disease, candidiasis, and contact dermatitis.
