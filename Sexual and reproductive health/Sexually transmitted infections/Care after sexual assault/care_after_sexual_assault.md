# 🩺 Medical Content

<div class='awareness-section'>
  <h2>💡 What You Need to Know

  
    * **Immediate Safety:** Prioritize your safety and get to a secure location immediately after the assault.

    * **Medical Attention is Crucial:** Seek medical care as soon as possible, even if you don't have visible injuries. This is vital for addressing potential physical injuries, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and pregnancy risks.

    * **Evidence Preservation:** If you are considering reporting the assault, try to avoid showering, changing clothes, or cleaning the area until a forensic medical examination can be performed.

    * **Support Systems:** Reach out to trusted friends, family, or a crisis hotline for emotional support. You do not have to go through this alone.

    * **Legal Options:** You have the right to report the assault to law enforcement, but this is your decision. Medical care can proceed regardless of your choice to report.

  



<div class='symptoms-section'>
  <h2>🤒 Associated Symptoms

  
    * **Physical Injuries:** Abrasions, bruises, lacerations, internal injuries, or pain in various body areas.

    * **Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs):** Potential exposure to infections like chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, HIV, and hepatitis B/C, which may initially be asymptomatic or present with discharge, pain, or sores.

    * **Unwanted Pregnancy:** Risk of pregnancy, especially if contraception was not used or failed during the assault.

    * **Emotional and Psychological Trauma:** Acute stress reactions, anxiety, fear, shock, dissociation, difficulty sleeping, nightmares, flashbacks, and feelings of shame or guilt.

    * **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Nausea, vomiting, or appetite changes due to shock, stress, or potential medication side effects.

  



<div class='precautions-section'>
  <h2>🛡️ Crucial Precautions

  
    * **Personal Safety First:** Ensure you are in a safe environment and away from the perpetrator.

    * **Avoid Self-Blame:** Understand that sexual assault is never the victim's fault.

    * **Do Not Isolate:** Seek support from trusted individuals or professional crisis counselors. Isolation can worsen emotional distress.

    * **Forensic Evidence:** If considering legal action, avoid actions that could compromise evidence (e.g., showering, changing clothes, douching) until after a forensic examination.

    * **Medication Adherence:** If prescribed Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV or emergency contraception, adhere strictly to the prescribed regimen for maximum effectiveness.

  



<div class='dietary-section'>
  <h2>🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  
    * **Hydration:** Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially if experiencing shock, stress, or nausea, to prevent dehydration.

    * **Gentle Nutrition:** Opt for bland, easily digestible foods if experiencing nausea or stomach upset due to stress or medication side effects.

    * **Avoid Alcohol and Illicit Substances:** Refrain from consuming alcohol or recreational drugs, as these can impair judgment, hinder emotional processing, and interact negatively with prescribed medications.

    * **Medication Interactions:** Be aware of any specific dietary instructions provided with prescribed medications (e.g., PEP, emergency contraception) to ensure optimal absorption and minimize side effects.

    * **Post-Sedation Care:** If any medical procedures required sedation, follow specific instructions regarding reintroduction of fluids and food, typically starting with clear liquids.

  



<div class='warning-box'>
  <h2>⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  
    * **Provide a Safe Space:** Ensure the survivor is in a secure and private environment, free from judgment or further harm.

    * **Listen Actively and Believe:** Offer empathetic listening without questioning the survivor's account or blaming them. Validate their feelings.

    * **Respect Autonomy:** Support the survivor's decisions regarding medical care, reporting, and next steps. Do not pressure them into actions they are not ready for.

    * **Assist with Resources:** Help connect the survivor with medical professionals, sexual assault crisis centers, legal aid, and mental health services.

    * **Maintain Confidentiality:** Respect the survivor's privacy and do not share their experience without explicit permission.

  



<div class='doctor-advice'>
  <h2>🩺 Physician's Perspective

  
    * **Trauma-Informed Care:** Approach every interaction with sensitivity, respect, and an understanding of the potential psychological impact of trauma.

    * **Comprehensive Medical Evaluation:** Conduct a thorough physical examination to identify and treat injuries, and assess for potential STI exposure and pregnancy risk.

    * **Prophylactic Treatment:** Offer and administer Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV, prophylactic antibiotics for other STIs, and emergency contraception as indicated and desired by the patient.

    * **Forensic Evidence Collection:** If the patient consents, perform a meticulous forensic medical examination (SANE exam) to collect evidence, ensuring proper chain of custody.

    * **Referral and Follow-up:** Provide referrals to mental health specialists, sexual assault advocacy services, and ensure appropriate follow-up for STI testing and treatment.

  



<div class='student-nurse-index'>
  <h2>🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  
    * **Patient Advocacy:** Act as a strong advocate for the survivor, ensuring their rights are protected and their needs are met throughout the medical and legal processes.

    * **Trauma-Informed Communication:** Utilize non-judgmental language, explain all procedures clearly, and obtain explicit consent for every step of care.

    * **Emotional Support:** Provide immediate emotional support, comfort, and reassurance, recognizing the profound psychological distress the survivor may be experiencing.

    * **Documentation:** Maintain accurate, objective, and detailed documentation of findings, interventions, and patient statements, adhering to legal and ethical guidelines.

    * **Resource Navigation:** Educate the survivor about available community resources, including crisis hotlines, support groups, and legal aid, and assist with connections.

  



<div class='technical-index'>
  <h2>🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  
    * **SANE (Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner):** A registered nurse with specialized training in forensic examination and care of sexual assault survivors.

    * **PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis):** Antiretroviral medications taken after potential exposure to HIV to prevent infection. Must be started within 72 hours.

    * **EC (Emergency Contraception):** Medications or devices used to prevent pregnancy after unprotected sexual intercourse or assault.

    * **Forensic Evidence Collection:** The systematic process of gathering and preserving physical evidence from a sexual assault for potential legal proceedings.

    * **Trauma-Informed Care:** An approach to care that recognizes the widespread impact of trauma and integrates knowledge about trauma into policies, procedures, and practices.

    * **Chain of Custody:** The chronological documentation or paper trail showing the seizure, custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of physical or electronic evidence.
