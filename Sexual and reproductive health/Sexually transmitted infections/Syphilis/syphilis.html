Syphilis

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Sexually transmitted infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Syphilis is a systemic sexually transmitted infection (STI) caused by the bacterium Treponema pallidum.
  • Transmission: Primarily through direct contact with a syphilitic sore (chancre) during vaginal, anal, or oral sexual activity. Can also be transmitted congenitally from mother to child.
  • Stages: The infection progresses through primary, secondary, latent, and tertiary stages, each with distinct clinical manifestations.
  • Curability: Syphilis is curable with antibiotics, especially when treated in its early stages. Untreated, it can lead to severe and irreversible health complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Primary Syphilis: Characterized by a single, painless sore (chancre) at the site of infection, typically appearing 10-90 days after exposure. Often goes unnoticed due to its location or lack of pain.
  • Secondary Syphilis: Develops weeks after the chancre heals, presenting with a non-itchy skin rash (often on palms and soles), swollen lymph nodes, fever, fatigue, muscle aches, and patchy hair loss.
  • Latent Syphilis: A period with no visible signs or symptoms, but the infection remains in the body. This stage can last for many years.
  • Tertiary Syphilis: Occurs years after initial infection if untreated, leading to severe complications affecting the heart, brain, nerves, bones, and other organs (e.g., neurosyphilis, cardiovascular syphilis, gummatous syphilis).
  • Congenital Syphilis: Transmitted from an infected mother to her fetus, potentially causing miscarriage, stillbirth, or severe health problems in the infant, including bone deformities, anemia, jaundice, and neurological issues.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Safe Sexual Practices: Consistent and correct use of barrier methods (e.g., condoms) during all sexual activity significantly reduces the risk of syphilis transmission.
  • Regular Screening: Individuals with multiple partners, those with new partners, or those at higher risk should undergo regular STI screening, including syphilis testing.
  • Partner Notification: Informing all recent sexual partners about a syphilis diagnosis is crucial for their testing and treatment, preventing further spread of the infection.
  • Treatment Adherence: Complete the full course of prescribed antibiotics exactly as directed by a healthcare provider, even if symptoms disappear, to ensure complete eradication of the infection.
  • Abstinence During Treatment: Avoid all sexual contact until treatment is completed and a healthcare provider confirms the infection is cleared and sores have healed.
  • Maternal Screening: Pregnant individuals should be screened for syphilis early in pregnancy and again in the third trimester and at delivery if at high risk, to prevent congenital syphilis.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially during antibiotic treatment, to support kidney function and overall well-being.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in vitamins and minerals to support the immune system and aid in recovery during and after treatment.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Avoid alcohol consumption, particularly during antibiotic treatment, as it can interfere with medication efficacy or exacerbate potential side effects.
  • Avoidance of Irritants: Limit intake of highly processed foods, excessive sugar, and caffeine that may contribute to general inflammation or discomfort during recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Medical Consultation: Seek prompt medical evaluation if any symptoms suggestive of syphilis appear or after unprotected sexual contact with a partner diagnosed with syphilis.
  • Partner Management: Ensure all recent sexual partners are notified, tested, and treated to break the chain of transmission. Confidentiality should be maintained during this process.
  • Follow-up Testing: Adhere to recommended follow-up blood tests after treatment to confirm cure and monitor for potential re-infection.
  • Counselling: Engage in counselling regarding safe sex practices, risk reduction strategies, and the importance of ongoing STI prevention.
  • No Self-Treatment: Do not attempt to self-diagnose or self-treat syphilis; proper medical diagnosis and prescription from a healthcare professional are essential.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Imperative: Early and accurate diagnosis using serological tests (e.g., RPR, VDRL for screening; FTA-ABS, TP-PA for confirmation) is paramount for effective treatment and preventing disease progression.
  • Treatment Protocol: Penicillin G remains the gold standard for syphilis treatment, with dosage and duration varying by stage of infection. Alternative antibiotics are available for penicillin-allergic patients.
  • Public Health Mandate: Syphilis is a reportable disease; healthcare providers have a responsibility to report cases to public health authorities for surveillance and partner services.
  • Neurosyphilis & Ocular Syphilis: Maintain a high index of suspicion for these severe manifestations, especially in HIV-positive individuals, as they require specific, more intensive treatment regimens.
  • Congenital Syphilis Prevention: Emphasize universal prenatal screening and timely treatment of infected pregnant individuals to eliminate congenital syphilis and its devastating effects.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a critical role in educating patients about syphilis transmission, symptoms, treatment adherence, and prevention strategies, including safe sex practices.
  • Screening Protocols: Understanding and implementing screening guidelines for at-risk populations, including pregnant individuals, men who have sex with men (MSM), and individuals with other STIs.
  • Contact Tracing: Nurses often assist public health teams in confidential partner notification and contact tracing efforts to control outbreaks and prevent further spread.
  • Jarisch-Herxheimer Reaction: Educate patients about this acute febrile reaction that can occur after initial syphilis treatment and how to manage its transient symptoms.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide empathetic support and address stigma associated with STI diagnoses, encouraging open communication, adherence to care, and mental well-being.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Causative Agent: Treponema pallidum (a spirochete bacterium).
  • Diagnostic Tests: Non-treponemal tests (Rapid Plasma Reagin [RPR], Venereal Disease Research Laboratory [VDRL]) for screening; Treponemal tests (Fluorescent Treponemal Antibody Absorption [FTA-ABS], T. pallidum Particle Agglutination [TP-PA], Enzyme Immunoassay [EIA], Chemiluminescence Immunoassay [CIA]) for confirmation.
  • Treatment (Early Syphilis): Benzathine penicillin G, 2.4 million units intramuscularly in a single dose.
  • Treatment (Late Latent/Tertiary Syphilis): Benzathine penicillin G, 2.4 million units intramuscularly weekly for three doses.
  • Treatment (Neurosyphilis): Aqueous crystalline penicillin G, 18-24 million units intravenously daily for 10-14 days.
  • Reporting: Syphilis is a nationally notifiable condition in many countries, requiring reporting to public health authorities.
