Hysteroscopy

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Gynecologic surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Hysteroscopy is a minimally invasive procedure used to visualize the inside of the uterus (endometrial cavity) using a thin, lighted telescope (hysteroscope) inserted through the vagina and cervix.
  • Purpose: It is performed for both diagnostic purposes (to identify uterine abnormalities) and operative purposes (to treat certain conditions directly).
  • Common Indications: Frequently indicated for abnormal uterine bleeding, infertility evaluation, recurrent miscarriage, removal of polyps or fibroids, and evaluation of uterine anomalies.
  • Anesthesia: Can be performed under local anesthesia, conscious sedation, or general anesthesia, depending on the complexity of the procedure and patient preference.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Abnormal Uterine Bleeding: Irregular, heavy, prolonged, or post-menopausal bleeding often prompts a hysteroscopy to identify underlying causes like polyps, fibroids, or hyperplasia.
  • Infertility or Recurrent Miscarriage: Evaluation for intrauterine adhesions (Asherman's syndrome), uterine septa, or submucosal fibroids that may impede conception or pregnancy maintenance.
  • Pelvic Pain: Chronic pelvic pain, especially if associated with menstrual irregularities, can be investigated for intrauterine pathology.
  • Difficulty with IUD Removal: When an intrauterine device (IUD) string is not visible, hysteroscopy can aid in its localization and removal.
  • Suspected Uterine Anomalies: Imaging findings (e.g., ultrasound, MRI) suggesting congenital uterine malformations may lead to hysteroscopy for definitive diagnosis and potential correction.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pregnancy Exclusion: Hysteroscopy is contraindicated in confirmed or suspected pregnancy. A pregnancy test is mandatory prior to the procedure.
  • Active Pelvic Infection: The procedure should be postponed if there is an active pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) or other acute genital tract infection to prevent spread.
  • Anticoagulant Management: Patients on blood thinners (anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents) must discuss medication adjustments with their physician well in advance to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Allergies: Inform the medical team of any known allergies, especially to latex, iodine, or medications used for anesthesia.
  • Fluid Management: Close monitoring of distension media fluid balance is critical during operative hysteroscopy to prevent fluid overload, especially with hypotonic solutions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fasting for Anesthesia: If general anesthesia or deep sedation is planned, strict fasting from food and liquids (typically 6-8 hours for food, 2 hours for clear liquids) is required prior to the procedure.
  • Light Meal for Local Anesthesia: If only local anesthesia or minimal sedation is used, a light meal may be permitted several hours before, but always confirm with the healthcare provider.
  • Post-Procedure Hydration: After sedation or general anesthesia, begin with clear liquids and gradually advance to a light diet as tolerated to prevent nausea and vomiting.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from alcohol consumption for at least 24-48 hours post-procedure, especially if sedatives were administered, as it can interact with medications and impair recovery.
  • Hydration Prior: Ensure adequate hydration in the days leading up to the procedure, unless otherwise instructed, to support overall well-being.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Transportation Requirement: If sedation or general anesthesia is administered, a responsible adult must accompany the patient to and from the facility and remain with them for a specified period (e.g., 24 hours) post-procedure.
  • Post-Procedure Discomfort: Expect some cramping, mild pain, and light vaginal bleeding or spotting for a few days. Over-the-counter pain relievers may be recommended.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting, douching, tampons, and sexual intercourse for a period (typically 1-2 weeks) as advised by the physician to prevent infection and promote healing.
  • Signs of Complication: Seek immediate medical attention for severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding (soaking more than one pad per hour), foul-smelling vaginal discharge, fever (above 100.4°F or 38°C), or chills.
  • Follow-up Appointment: Ensure you understand and attend all scheduled follow-up appointments to review results and monitor recovery.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Approach: Each hysteroscopy is tailored to the patient's specific symptoms, medical history, and diagnostic findings, ensuring the most appropriate intervention.
  • Risk-Benefit Discussion: A thorough discussion of potential risks (e.g., infection, bleeding, uterine perforation, fluid overload) versus the benefits of diagnosis and treatment is crucial before consent.
  • Pre-Procedure Evaluation: Comprehensive pre-operative assessment, including imaging (ultrasound) and sometimes endometrial biopsy, helps in planning the procedure and anticipating findings.
  • Minimally Invasive Advantage: Hysteroscopy offers a direct visual assessment and often allows for immediate treatment of intrauterine pathologies, reducing the need for more invasive surgery.
  • Post-Procedure Expectations: Patients should be fully informed about expected recovery, potential discomfort, and when to resume normal activities, emphasizing the importance of adhering to post-operative instructions.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients about the hysteroscopy procedure, pre-operative preparations, and post-operative care, including pain management and warning signs.
  • Pre-operative Assessment: Conducting comprehensive patient assessments, including vital signs, allergy status, medication reconciliation, and confirming NPO status if applicable.
  • Intraoperative Support: Assisting the physician with instrument setup, managing distension media, monitoring fluid balance, and ensuring patient comfort and safety during the procedure.
  • Post-Anesthesia Care: Monitoring vital signs, assessing pain levels, managing nausea, and observing for signs of complications in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU).
  • Discharge Planning: Providing clear verbal and written discharge instructions, ensuring the patient understands activity restrictions, medication schedules, and when to seek emergency care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Hysteroscope Types: Diagnostic hysteroscopes (smaller diameter, typically 2-5mm) for visualization; Operative hysteroscopes (larger, 5-10mm) with working channels for instruments.
  • Distension Media: Solutions used to distend the uterine cavity for visualization, including saline (isotonic, used with mechanical instruments) and glycine (hypotonic, used with electrosurgical instruments).
  • Energy Sources: Electrosurgery (monopolar or bipolar) and mechanical instruments (scissors, graspers, morcellators) are used for operative hysteroscopy.
  • Complications: Potential complications include uterine perforation, hemorrhage, infection, fluid overload (especially with hypotonic media), gas embolism, and cervical laceration.
  • Indications for Operative Hysteroscopy: Resection of submucosal fibroids, polypectomy, lysis of intrauterine adhesions, metroplasty for uterine septa, and removal of retained products of conception.
