# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Immediate Safety:** Prioritize getting to a safe location away from the perpetrator. Your safety is paramount.
* **Medical Attention is Crucial:** Seek medical care as soon as possible, even if there are no visible injuries. This allows for assessment of physical harm, STI prevention, and emergency contraception.
* **Evidence Preservation:** If you are considering reporting the assault, try not to shower, bathe, change clothes, brush teeth, or clean the area of assault before a medical forensic exam. Place any clothing worn during the assault in a paper bag.
* **Forensic Exam (SANE):** A Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner (SANE) can perform a comprehensive medical forensic exam to collect evidence, provide medical care, and offer support. This exam is voluntary.
* **Emotional Support:** Reach out to a trusted friend, family member, or a sexual assault crisis hotline for immediate emotional support. You do not have to go through this alone.
* **Your Choices Matter:** All decisions regarding medical care, evidence collection, and reporting are yours to make. You have the right to choose what feels right for you.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Physical Injuries:** Bruises, lacerations, internal trauma, pain in various body areas, headaches, or gastrointestinal distress.
* **Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs):** Risk of contracting STIs such as chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, herpes, or HIV.
* **Unwanted Pregnancy:** Risk of pregnancy if emergency contraception is not administered or is ineffective.
* **Acute Stress Reaction:** Immediate psychological responses including shock, numbness, fear, anxiety, confusion, difficulty concentrating, or dissociation.
* **Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD):** Long-term symptoms such as intrusive thoughts, flashbacks, nightmares, avoidance of reminders, hypervigilance, and altered mood or reactivity.
* **Depression and Anxiety:** Persistent feelings of sadness, hopelessness, loss of interest, panic attacks, or generalized anxiety.
* **Sleep Disturbances:** Insomnia, difficulty falling or staying asleep, or restless sleep.
* **Eating Disorders:** Changes in appetite, overeating, or undereating as coping mechanisms.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Safety Planning:** Develop a safety plan if you feel at risk from the perpetrator or others. This may involve changing routines, securing your living space, or seeking temporary shelter.
* **STI and Pregnancy Prophylaxis:** Discuss post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV and other STIs, as well as emergency contraception (EC), with a healthcare provider within the recommended timeframes.
* **Avoid Self-Blame:** Understand that sexual assault is never the victim's fault. The responsibility lies solely with the perpetrator.
* **Substance Avoidance:** Refrain from using alcohol or illicit drugs as coping mechanisms, as they can hinder emotional processing and recovery.
* **Monitor Mental Health:** Be vigilant for signs of worsening psychological distress, such as severe depression, suicidal ideation, or increasing anxiety, and seek professional help immediately.
* **Establish Support Systems:** Connect with trusted friends, family, support groups, or a therapist to build a strong network for emotional healing.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Maintain Hydration:** Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially water, to support overall physiological function and help manage stress responses.
* **Nutrient-Rich Foods:** Focus on consuming balanced, nutrient-dense meals to support energy levels and mood stability, counteracting potential appetite loss or stress-related eating patterns.
* **Avoid Stimulants and Depressants:** Limit caffeine and sugar intake, which can exacerbate anxiety. Strictly avoid alcohol and recreational drugs, as they impair judgment and hinder emotional recovery.
* **Small, Frequent Meals:** If appetite is suppressed due to trauma, try consuming smaller, more frequent, and easily digestible meals throughout the day.
* **Mindful Eating:** Practice mindful eating to reconnect with your body and promote a sense of control, especially if eating patterns have been disrupted.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Believe and Validate:** Always believe the survivor's account and validate their feelings. Avoid questioning their story or implying blame.
* **Respect Autonomy:** Support the survivor's decisions regarding medical care, reporting, and legal actions without pressure or judgment.
* **Offer Practical Support:** Assist with practical needs such as transportation to medical facilities, finding safe housing, or connecting with advocacy services.
* **Listen Actively:** Provide a safe space for the survivor to share their experience if they choose, listening without interruption or offering unsolicited advice.
* **Avoid Judgmental Language:** Refrain from asking 'why' questions (e.g., 'Why were you there?', 'Why didn't you fight back?') as these can be re-traumatizing.
* **Encourage Professional Help:** Gently encourage seeking medical attention and mental health support, emphasizing that these resources are available to help them heal.
* **Self-Care for Attendants:** Recognize the emotional toll of supporting a survivor and ensure you also seek support if needed to prevent burnout.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Trauma-Informed Care:** Approach every interaction with sensitivity, respect, and an understanding of the potential psychological impact of trauma.
* **Comprehensive Medical Evaluation:** Conduct a thorough physical examination to identify and treat any injuries, including a genital exam if indicated and consented to.
* **Forensic Evidence Collection:** Offer and explain the option of a Sexual Assault Forensic Exam (SAFE) by a SANE nurse, ensuring the patient understands the process and their right to decline.
* **Prophylactic Treatment:** Administer or prescribe post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV and other STIs, and offer emergency contraception (EC) within the appropriate timeframes.
* **STI Screening and Treatment:** Screen for common STIs and provide empiric treatment as per guidelines, with follow-up testing as necessary.
* **Mental Health Referrals:** Provide immediate referrals to mental health professionals specializing in trauma, victim advocacy services, and local support groups.
* **Documentation:** Maintain meticulous, objective, and non-judgmental documentation of findings, interventions, and patient statements, using the patient's own words where appropriate.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **SANE Nurse Specialization:** Understand the specialized role of a Sexual Assault Nurse Examiner (SANE) in providing compassionate, evidence-based care and forensic evidence collection.
* **Trauma-Informed Approach:** Apply principles of trauma-informed care, including safety, trustworthiness, peer support, collaboration, empowerment, and cultural sensitivity, in all patient interactions.
* **Patient Advocacy:** Act as a strong advocate for the survivor, ensuring their rights are protected, their choices are respected, and they have access to all available resources.
* **Confidentiality and Privacy:** Maintain strict confidentiality and privacy throughout the care process, creating a safe and secure environment for the patient.
* **Resource Navigation:** Be proficient in identifying and connecting survivors with local and national resources, including crisis hotlines, victim advocacy centers, legal aid, and mental health services.
* **Documentation Best Practices:** Learn to document findings accurately, objectively, and comprehensively, using clear, non-judgmental language, which is critical for both medical care and potential legal proceedings.
* **Self-Care for Nurses:** Recognize the potential for secondary trauma or compassion fatigue and actively engage in self-care strategies and debriefing.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Sexual Assault Forensic Exam (SAFE):** A specialized medical examination performed by a SANE nurse to collect forensic evidence, assess injuries, and provide medical care following sexual assault.
* **Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP):** A course of antiretroviral drugs taken within 72 hours of potential HIV exposure to prevent infection, and antibiotics for other STIs.
* **Emergency Contraception (EC):** Medications (e.g., levonorgestrel, ulipristal acetate) or IUD insertion used to prevent pregnancy after unprotected intercourse, most effective when taken as soon as possible.
* **Trauma-Informed Care (TIC):** An organizational framework that involves understanding, recognizing, and responding to the effects of all types of trauma. Emphasizes physical, psychological, and emotional safety for both providers and survivors.
* **Rape, Abuse & Incest National Network (RAINN):** The largest anti-sexual violence organization in the U.S., operating the National Sexual Assault Hotline and providing resources.
* **Acute Stress Disorder (ASD):** A trauma- and stressor-related disorder that can occur within one month of exposure to a traumatic event, characterized by dissociative symptoms, re-experiencing, avoidance, and arousal.
* **Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD):** A psychiatric disorder that may occur in people who have experienced or witnessed a traumatic event, characterized by intrusive memories, avoidance, negative changes in thinking and mood, and changes in arousal and reactivity.
* **Victim Advocacy Services:** Non-profit organizations and programs that provide support, information, and assistance to victims of crime, including sexual assault.
