# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition**: Emergency contraception (EC) is a method used to prevent pregnancy after unprotected sexual intercourse or contraceptive failure.
* **Types Available**: Includes oral pills (levonorgestrel, ulipristal acetate) and the copper intrauterine device (Cu-IUD).
* **Timing is Crucial**: Oral EC is most effective when taken as soon as possible after unprotected sex, typically within 72 to 120 hours depending on the specific type. The Cu-IUD can be inserted up to 5 days.
* **Mechanism of Action**: Primarily works by delaying or inhibiting ovulation; it does not terminate an existing pregnancy.
* **Not Routine Contraception**: EC is intended for emergency use only and should not be used as a regular method of birth control.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Nausea and Vomiting**: Common side effects, particularly with levonorgestrel, which can be mitigated by taking EC with food.
* **Abdominal Discomfort**: Mild cramping or lower abdominal pain may occur.
* **Headache and Dizziness**: Temporary head pain or lightheadedness are reported side effects.
* **Breast Tenderness**: Hormonal changes can lead to temporary breast sensitivity.
* **Menstrual Irregularities**: Changes in the timing, flow, or duration of the next menstrual period are common.
* **Indications for Use**: Unprotected sexual intercourse, condom breakage, missed birth control pills, dislodged contraceptive patch/ring, or sexual assault.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Existing Pregnancy**: EC is ineffective if pregnancy is already established and should not be used. A pregnancy test may be indicated if there's uncertainty.
* **Severe Vomiting**: If vomiting occurs within 2-3 hours of taking oral EC, a repeat dose or antiemetic may be necessary, or an alternative method like a copper IUD should be considered.
* **Drug Interactions**: Certain medications (e.g., enzyme-inducing anticonvulsants, St. John's Wort) can reduce the effectiveness of oral EC. Always disclose all medications to a healthcare provider.
* **Weight and BMI**: Ulipristal acetate may be more effective than levonorgestrel for individuals with a higher body mass index (BMI).
* **STI Protection**: EC does not protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs); barrier methods like condoms are essential for STI prevention.
* **Repeated Use**: Frequent use of EC is not recommended as it is less effective than regular contraception and may lead to more side effects.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Take with Food**: Oral emergency contraception can be taken with a meal or snack to help reduce the incidence of nausea and stomach upset.
* **Hydration**: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially if experiencing nausea or vomiting, to prevent dehydration.
* **Avoid Alcohol**: While not a direct contraindication, alcohol consumption can exacerbate nausea or dizziness, which are potential side effects of EC.
* **No Specific Restrictions**: Generally, there are no specific food or fluid restrictions required when taking emergency contraception.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Follow-up for Missed Period**: If your next menstrual period is more than a week late after taking EC, or if it is unusually light or heavy, take a pregnancy test.
* **Persistent Symptoms**: Seek medical attention if you experience severe abdominal pain, unusual vaginal bleeding, or other concerning symptoms after taking EC.
* **Future Contraception**: Discuss long-term, reliable contraception options with a healthcare provider to prevent future emergency situations.
* **STI Screening**: Consider screening for sexually transmitted infections if unprotected sex occurred, especially if the partner's STI status is unknown.
* **Sexual Assault Support**: If EC is needed due to sexual assault, seek immediate medical care, counseling, and legal support services.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Timely Intervention**: Emphasize the critical importance of administering or prescribing EC as soon as possible after unprotected intercourse to maximize efficacy.
* **Comprehensive Counseling**: Provide thorough counseling on all available EC methods, their mechanisms of action, potential side effects, and the importance of ongoing contraception.
* **Rule Out Existing Pregnancy**: Always confirm the absence of an existing pregnancy before prescribing or inserting EC, particularly a copper IUD.
* **Dual Protection**: Counsel patients on the necessity of dual protection, using EC for pregnancy prevention and barrier methods for STI prevention.
* **Individualized Care**: Tailor EC recommendations based on the patient's medical history, current medications, BMI, and the time elapsed since unprotected intercourse.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Patient Education**: Educate patients on the correct administration of oral EC, expected side effects, and what to anticipate regarding their next menstrual cycle.
* **Assessment for Contraindications**: Conduct a thorough assessment for any contraindications, such as known pregnancy, and potential drug interactions.
* **Emotional Support**: Provide empathetic and non-judgmental support, especially for patients who have experienced sexual assault or contraceptive failure.
* **Follow-up Instructions**: Clearly instruct patients on when to perform a pregnancy test and when to seek further medical evaluation.
* **Contraceptive Planning**: Initiate discussions about effective, long-term contraception methods to empower patients in preventing future unintended pregnancies.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Levonorgestrel (LNG)**: A progestin-only pill that primarily inhibits or delays ovulation. Most effective within 72 hours, with some efficacy up to 120 hours.
* **Ulipristal Acetate (UPA)**: A selective progesterone receptor modulator, generally more effective than LNG, especially when taken up to 120 hours after unprotected intercourse, and potentially in individuals with higher BMI.
* **Copper Intrauterine Device (Cu-IUD)**: The most effective method of EC, offering over 99% efficacy when inserted within 5 days of unprotected sex, and provides ongoing contraception for up to 10 years.
* **Efficacy Rates**: Efficacy varies by method and timing; LNG reduces pregnancy risk by 75-89%, UPA by 85%, and Cu-IUD by >99%.
* **Mechanism of Action (Detailed)**: EC primarily prevents ovulation, fertilization, or implantation; it does not induce abortion of an established pregnancy.
* **WHO Guidelines**: Refer to World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines for comprehensive recommendations on the provision and access to emergency contraception.
