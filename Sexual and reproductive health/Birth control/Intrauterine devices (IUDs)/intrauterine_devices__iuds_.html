Intrauterine devices (IUDs)

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Birth control

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARC): IUDs are highly effective, reversible birth control methods that can last for several years.
  • Two Main Types: There are hormonal IUDs (releasing progestin, e.g., levonorgestrel) and non-hormonal IUDs (copper).
  • Mechanism of Action: Hormonal IUDs primarily thicken cervical mucus, thin the uterine lining, and may suppress ovulation. Copper IUDs create a local inflammatory reaction toxic to sperm and eggs, preventing fertilization.
  • Effectiveness and Duration: Both types are over 99% effective. Hormonal IUDs typically last 3-8 years, while copper IUDs can last up to 10-12 years.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Indications for Consideration: Desire for highly effective, long-term contraception; management of heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) or severe menstrual pain (dysmenorrhea) for hormonal IUDs.
  • Expected Post-Insertion: Mild to moderate cramping and spotting are common immediately after insertion.
  • Hormonal IUD Effects: May lead to lighter periods, irregular spotting, or amenorrhea (absence of periods) over time.
  • Copper IUD Effects: May cause heavier, longer, or more painful periods, especially in the first few months after insertion.
  • Signs of Complication: Persistent severe pain, fever, unusual or foul-smelling vaginal discharge, or changes in bleeding patterns that are concerning should prompt medical evaluation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Contraindications: Pregnancy, active pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), current sexually transmitted infection (STI), unexplained vaginal bleeding, certain uterine anomalies, or specific cancers (e.g., cervical, endometrial).
  • STI Screening: It is crucial to screen for and treat any STIs before IUD insertion to minimize the risk of PID.
  • Uterine Perforation Risk: A rare but serious risk during insertion, where the IUD punctures the uterine wall.
  • Expulsion Risk: The IUD can partially or completely come out, especially in the first year. Regular string checks are important.
  • Ectopic Pregnancy: While IUDs significantly reduce the overall risk of pregnancy, if a pregnancy does occur with an IUD in place, there is a higher chance it could be ectopic.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Insertion Hydration: Ensure adequate hydration before the procedure to help with comfort and reduce lightheadedness.
  • Light Meal Before: A light meal is generally recommended before insertion to prevent nausea or dizziness, especially if pain medication is taken.
  • No Specific Dietary Restrictions: There are no specific food or fluid restrictions directly related to IUD insertion or use.
  • Post-Procedure Comfort: Avoid heavy meals or alcohol if pain medication has been prescribed, as this may exacerbate gastrointestinal side effects.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Complications: Watch for severe abdominal pain, fever, chills, unusual or foul-smelling vaginal discharge, or heavy, prolonged bleeding.
  • Check IUD Strings: Learn how to regularly check for the IUD strings to ensure the device is in place. Contact your provider if you cannot feel them or if they feel longer/shorter.
  • No STI Protection: IUDs do not protect against sexually transmitted infections. Consistent use of barrier methods (e.g., condoms) is necessary for STI prevention.
  • Signs of Pregnancy: If you suspect you might be pregnant (e.g., missed period, breast tenderness) while an IUD is in place, contact your healthcare provider immediately.
  • Follow-Up Appointments: Adhere to scheduled follow-up appointments to ensure proper placement and address any concerns.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Shared Decision-Making: A thorough discussion of all contraceptive options, including benefits, risks, and patient preferences, is essential for informed consent.
  • Comprehensive History: A detailed medical and sexual history, including STI risk assessment, is crucial before recommending an IUD.
  • Pre-Insertion Counseling: Patients should be fully counseled on the insertion procedure, expected discomfort, potential side effects, and signs of complications.
  • Individualized Choice: The choice between hormonal and copper IUDs should be individualized based on patient health status, desire for menstrual cycle control, and specific contraindications.
  • Post-Insertion Support: Provide clear instructions for post-insertion care, symptom management, and when to seek urgent medical attention.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients about IUD types, mechanism of action, insertion process, expected side effects, and complication recognition.
  • Pain Management: Provide comfort measures and administer analgesia as prescribed before and after insertion to manage cramping.
  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Monitor for signs of vasovagal reaction (dizziness, pallor, bradycardia) immediately after insertion.
  • String Check Instruction: Teach patients how to perform regular IUD string checks and what to do if strings are missing or feel different.
  • STI Prevention Counseling: Reinforce that IUDs do not protect against STIs and counsel on barrier methods.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pearl Index: IUDs have a Pearl Index of <1.0 pregnancies per 100 woman-years, indicating high effectiveness.
  • Hormonal IUD (LNG-IUD): Releases levonorgestrel, causing endometrial atrophy, increased cervical mucus viscosity, and impaired sperm motility/viability. Systemic absorption is minimal.
  • Copper IUD (Cu-IUD): Induces a sterile inflammatory reaction in the endometrium, creating a spermicidal environment and preventing implantation. Does not release hormones.
  • Contraindication Categories: Classified by WHO Medical Eligibility Criteria (MEC) into categories 1-4, guiding safe use based on medical conditions.
  • Management of Expulsion: If expulsion occurs, the IUD must be removed, and alternative contraception or re-insertion (if appropriate) should be discussed.
  • Management of Perforation: Requires surgical removal of the IUD, often laparoscopically, and careful monitoring for complications.
