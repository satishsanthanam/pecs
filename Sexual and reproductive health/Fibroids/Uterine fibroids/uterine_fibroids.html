Uterine fibroids

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Fibroids

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Uterine fibroids, also known as leiomyomas, are non-cancerous (benign) growths that develop in the muscular wall of the uterus.
  • Prevalence: They are very common, affecting a significant percentage of women, often during their reproductive years.
  • Types and Location: Fibroids can vary in size and number, growing inside the uterine cavity (submucosal), within the uterine wall (intramural), or on the outer surface of the uterus (subserosal).
  • Hormonal Influence: Their growth is often linked to estrogen and progesterone levels, tending to shrink after menopause.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Heavy Menstrual Bleeding: Prolonged or excessively heavy periods (menorrhagia) leading to anemia.
  • Pelvic Pain and Pressure: Chronic discomfort, a feeling of fullness, or pressure in the lower abdomen.
  • Urinary Symptoms: Frequent urination or difficulty emptying the bladder due to pressure on the bladder.
  • Bowel Issues: Constipation or difficulty with bowel movements if fibroids press on the rectum.
  • Backache or Leg Pain: Pressure on nerves can cause pain radiating to the back or legs.
  • Painful Intercourse: Dyspareunia, especially with certain fibroid locations.
  • Reproductive Challenges: In some cases, fibroids can contribute to infertility or recurrent miscarriages.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Regular Gynecological Exams: Essential for early detection and monitoring of fibroid growth and symptom progression.
  • Symptom Tracking: Maintain a detailed record of menstrual cycles, bleeding patterns, pain levels, and any new or worsening symptoms.
  • Informed Treatment Decisions: Thoroughly discuss all medical and surgical treatment options, including risks, benefits, and impact on fertility, with your healthcare provider.
  • Anemia Management: If experiencing heavy bleeding, monitor for symptoms of anemia (fatigue, dizziness) and discuss iron supplementation with your doctor.
  • Post-Treatment Monitoring: Adhere to follow-up appointments to assess treatment effectiveness and monitor for potential recurrence or complications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Iron-Rich Foods: Increase intake of iron-rich foods (e.g., lean red meat, poultry, fish, beans, spinach, fortified cereals) to counteract anemia from heavy bleeding.
  • Fiber-Rich Diet: Consume plenty of fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to help manage constipation and promote overall digestive health.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake, especially if experiencing heavy menstrual flow, to prevent dehydration.
  • Limit Red Meat and Processed Foods: Some studies suggest a potential link between high consumption of red meat and processed foods with fibroid growth; focus on a whole-food diet.
  • Vitamin D Intake: Discuss vitamin D levels with your physician, as some research indicates a potential role of vitamin D deficiency in fibroid development.
  • Cruciferous Vegetables: Include vegetables like broccoli, cabbage, and kale, which contain compounds that may help with estrogen metabolism.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Sudden Severe Pain: Seek immediate medical attention for sudden, sharp, or severe pelvic pain, especially if accompanied by fever or heavy bleeding, as it could indicate fibroid degeneration or torsion.
  • Pregnancy Considerations: If pregnant with fibroids, close monitoring by an obstetrician is crucial due to potential complications like pain, premature labor, or issues during delivery.
  • Anemia Management: Be vigilant for signs of severe anemia (extreme fatigue, shortness of breath, pallor) and follow medical advice for iron replacement or other interventions.
  • Understanding Treatment Risks: Fully comprehend the potential side effects and recovery periods associated with medical therapies (e.g., GnRH agonists) and surgical procedures (e.g., myomectomy, hysterectomy).
  • Post-Menopausal Fibroids: While fibroids typically shrink after menopause, any new growth or significant increase in size post-menopause warrants thorough investigation to rule out other conditions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Approach: Management of uterine fibroids is highly individualized, considering symptom severity, fibroid size and location, patient age, desire for future fertility, and overall health.
  • Diagnostic Clarity: Ultrasound is the primary diagnostic tool, often supplemented by MRI for detailed mapping, especially prior to surgical intervention.
  • Medical Management: Options include hormonal contraceptives, GnRH agonists (to temporarily shrink fibroids), tranexamic acid (for heavy bleeding), and selective progesterone receptor modulators (SPRMs).
  • Minimally Invasive Procedures: Uterine artery embolization (UAE) and radiofrequency ablation (RFA) are effective for many patients seeking less invasive options.
  • Surgical Interventions: Myomectomy (removal of fibroids while preserving the uterus) is an option for women desiring future fertility, while hysterectomy (removal of the uterus) offers a definitive cure for symptomatic fibroids.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients about fibroid symptoms, diagnostic procedures, treatment options, and self-care strategies.
  • Symptom Assessment: Thorough assessment of menstrual history, pain characteristics, and impact on daily life is crucial for care planning.
  • Anemia Monitoring: Regular assessment of vital signs, fatigue levels, and laboratory results (e.g., hemoglobin, hematocrit) to manage anemia.
  • Pre- and Post-operative Care: Provide comprehensive care for patients undergoing myomectomy or hysterectomy, including pain management, wound care, mobility assistance, and discharge teaching.
  • Emotional Support: Offer empathetic support and resources to address patient concerns regarding fertility, body image, and quality of life affected by fibroids.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Leiomyoma: The medical term for a uterine fibroid, indicating a benign tumor of smooth muscle.
  • Menorrhagia: Abnormally heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding, a common symptom of uterine fibroids.
  • Dyspareunia: Painful sexual intercourse, which can be caused by fibroids depending on their size and location.
  • Myomectomy: A surgical procedure to remove uterine fibroids while preserving the uterus, often chosen by women who wish to retain fertility.
  • Hysterectomy: Surgical removal of the uterus, which is a definitive treatment for uterine fibroids.
  • Uterine Artery Embolization (UAE): A minimally invasive procedure that blocks the blood supply to fibroids, causing them to shrink.
  • Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone (GnRH) Agonists: Medications that temporarily induce a menopausal state, shrinking fibroids by reducing estrogen levels.
