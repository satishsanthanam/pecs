Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) and premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD)

🏠 Home / Sexual and reproductive health / Menstrual cycle

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding PMS: Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) encompasses a range of physical and emotional symptoms that occur in the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and resolve with menstruation.
  • Understanding PMDD: Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD) is a more severe and debilitating form of PMS, characterized by pronounced mood disturbances that significantly impair daily functioning.
  • Cyclical Nature: Both conditions are strictly cyclical, with symptoms appearing consistently in the week or two before menstruation and subsiding shortly after its onset.
  • Prevalence: PMS affects up to 75% of menstruating individuals, while PMDD affects about 3-8%.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Mood Swings & Irritability (PMS/PMDD): Rapid shifts in mood, increased irritability, anger, or interpersonal conflicts.
  • Depression & Anxiety (PMDD predominant): Profound sadness, hopelessness, anxiety, tension, or panic attacks.
  • Physical Discomforts (PMS/PMDD): Bloating, breast tenderness, headaches, muscle aches, joint pain, and fatigue.
  • Cognitive Symptoms (PMS/PMDD): Difficulty concentrating, forgetfulness, and feeling overwhelmed.
  • Sleep Disturbances (PMS/PMDD): Insomnia or hypersomnia.
  • Appetite Changes (PMS/PMDD): Food cravings or overeating.
  • Loss of Interest (PMDD specific): Markedly diminished interest in usual activities.
  • Suicidal Ideation (Severe PMDD): In severe cases, thoughts of self-harm or suicide may occur, requiring immediate medical attention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Symptom Tracking: Maintain a detailed symptom diary for at least two consecutive menstrual cycles to accurately identify patterns and severity, crucial for diagnosis.
  • Avoid Self-Diagnosis: Seek professional medical evaluation to differentiate PMS/PMDD from other mood disorders or underlying medical conditions.
  • Medication Adherence: If prescribed, strictly follow the regimen for selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) or hormonal therapies, understanding their onset of action and potential side effects.
  • Mental Health Monitoring: Be vigilant for worsening mood symptoms, especially thoughts of self-harm or suicide in PMDD, and seek immediate psychiatric consultation.
  • Lifestyle Adjustments: Prioritize stress reduction techniques, regular exercise, and adequate sleep to mitigate symptom severity.
  • Substance Avoidance: Limit or avoid alcohol and caffeine, as they can exacerbate mood swings and anxiety.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Increase Complex Carbohydrates: Incorporate whole grains, fruits, and vegetables to help stabilize blood sugar and mood.
  • Reduce Sodium Intake: Limit processed foods and high-sodium items to minimize fluid retention and bloating.
  • Limit Caffeine: Restrict coffee, tea, and energy drinks, as caffeine can worsen anxiety, irritability, and sleep disturbances.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Alcohol can disrupt sleep, dehydrate, and exacerbate mood symptoms, particularly depression.
  • Adequate Calcium & Vitamin D: Ensure sufficient intake through dairy, fortified foods, or supplements, as these may help reduce some PMS symptoms.
  • Magnesium-Rich Foods: Include dark leafy greens, nuts, seeds, and legumes, which may help alleviate muscle cramps and mood swings.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eating smaller, more frequent meals can help maintain stable blood sugar levels and prevent cravings.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Educate Support System: Inform family members or partners about PMS/PMDD to foster understanding and support during symptomatic periods.
  • Crisis Plan for PMDD: For individuals with PMDD, establish a clear crisis plan, including contact information for mental health professionals or emergency services, especially if suicidal ideation is present.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Schedule regular appointments with healthcare providers to monitor symptom management and adjust treatment plans as needed.
  • Avoid Stigmatization: Recognize that PMS and PMDD are legitimate medical conditions, not simply 'moodiness,' and advocate for appropriate care.
  • Mindfulness & Relaxation: Encourage practices like meditation, deep breathing, or yoga to manage stress and emotional symptoms.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Thorough Diagnostic Workup: A comprehensive history, physical exam, and symptom charting over 2-3 cycles are essential to confirm diagnosis and rule out other conditions like major depressive disorder or thyroid dysfunction.
  • First-Line Pharmacotherapy: Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) are often the first-line treatment for PMDD, prescribed either continuously or during the luteal phase.
  • Hormonal Interventions: Combined oral contraceptives (COCs) with drospirenone may be considered for PMS/PMDD, particularly those with a continuous or extended-cycle regimen.
  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT): Recommend CBT as an effective non-pharmacological intervention, especially for managing emotional symptoms and coping strategies.
  • Referral to Specialists: Consider referral to a psychiatrist or gynecologist specializing in reproductive mental health for complex or refractory cases.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Emphasize that treatment must be tailored to the individual's specific symptoms, severity, and response to therapy.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education on Cycle Tracking: Instruct patients on how to accurately track their menstrual cycle and associated symptoms, emphasizing consistency and detail.
  • Assessment for Suicidal Risk: Systematically screen patients with PMDD for suicidal ideation and implement appropriate safety protocols and referrals.
  • Medication Counseling: Provide clear instructions on SSRI administration, potential side effects (e.g., nausea, sexual dysfunction), and the importance of consistent use.
  • Lifestyle Modification Support: Educate patients on the benefits of dietary changes, regular exercise, and stress reduction techniques, offering practical tips.
  • Empathy and Validation: Offer empathetic support, validating the patient's experience and distress, which can significantly improve adherence to treatment.
  • Interdisciplinary Collaboration: Understand the importance of collaborating with physicians, mental health professionals, and dietitians for holistic patient care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria for PMDD: Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, outlines specific criteria for PMDD, including at least five symptoms, with one being a core mood symptom, present in the final week before menses, improving within a few days after onset, and minimal or absent in the week post-menses.
  • Luteal Phase Dysregulation: The underlying pathophysiology is thought to involve an abnormal response to normal fluctuations of ovarian steroids (estrogen and progesterone) during the luteal phase, affecting neurotransmitter systems, particularly serotonin.
  • Serotonin Dysregulation: Evidence suggests that individuals with PMS/PMDD may have altered serotonin receptor sensitivity or serotonin transporter function.
  • First-Line Pharmacotherapy: Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) such as fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, and citalopram are FDA-approved for PMDD.
  • GnRH Agonists: Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonists (e.g., leuprolide) can induce a temporary medical menopause, effectively eliminating ovarian hormone fluctuations, used in severe, refractory PMDD, often with add-back therapy.
  • Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine): Some studies suggest a modest benefit for PMS symptoms, though evidence is mixed.
  • Calcium Carbonate: Supplementation has shown some efficacy in reducing physical and emotional PMS symptoms.
