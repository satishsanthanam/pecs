Delirium (confusion)

🏠 Home / Brain and nerves / Memory loss and dementia

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Acute Onset: Delirium is an acute, fluctuating disturbance in attention and cognition, developing over hours to days.
  • Reversible Condition: It is often a reversible condition if the underlying cause is promptly identified and treated.
  • Key Features: Characterized by impaired consciousness, disorientation, and perceptual disturbances (e.g., hallucinations).
  • Common Triggers: Frequently triggered by acute illness, infection, medication side effects, surgery, or metabolic imbalances.
  • Vulnerability: Older adults, individuals with pre-existing cognitive impairment, and those with multiple comorbidities are at higher risk.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sudden Change in Mental Status: An abrupt and noticeable shift from baseline cognitive function.
  • Fluctuating Attention: Difficulty focusing, easily distracted, and periods of lucidity interspersed with confusion.
  • Disorientation: Inability to recognize time, place, or familiar people.
  • Perceptual Disturbances: Visual or auditory hallucinations, misinterpretations, or delusions.
  • Sleep-Wake Cycle Disturbances: Insomnia, daytime somnolence, or reversal of the normal sleep pattern (e.g., 'sundowning').
  • Psychomotor Changes: Can manifest as hyperactive (agitation, restlessness) or hypoactive (lethargy, withdrawal, reduced movement).
  • Memory Impairment: Difficulty recalling recent events or conversations.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Identify and Treat Underlying Causes: Promptly investigate and manage infections, metabolic derangements, dehydration, pain, or medication side effects.
  • Medication Review: Scrutinize all medications, especially anticholinergics, sedatives, opioids, and polypharmacy, for potential contributions to delirium.
  • Environmental Modifications: Maintain a calm, well-lit environment, provide clocks and calendars, and ensure the presence of familiar objects or people.
  • Ensure Hydration and Nutrition: Actively encourage fluid intake and provide adequate nutritional support to prevent dehydration and malnutrition.
  • Promote Early Mobilization: Encourage ambulation and activity as tolerated to prevent deconditioning and improve cognitive function.
  • Avoid Physical Restraints: Physical restraints can exacerbate agitation and confusion, increasing the risk of injury.
  • Pain Management: Effectively assess and manage pain to reduce distress and a common trigger for delirium.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Crucial to prevent dehydration, a significant precipitating factor for delirium; encourage regular sips of water or other fluids.
  • Nutritional Support: Ensure regular, balanced meals are offered and consumed to maintain energy levels and prevent malnutrition.
  • Electrolyte Monitoring: Closely monitor and correct any electrolyte imbalances (e.g., hyponatremia, hypernatremia) that can severely impact brain function.
  • Avoid Alcohol and Illicit Substances: These substances can directly induce or worsen delirium and should be strictly avoided.
  • Medication Timing: Administer medications that affect fluid balance or cognition as prescribed, ensuring they do not inadvertently contribute to dehydration or oversedation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Maintain Calm Demeanor: Approach the individual gently, speak in a clear, slow, and reassuring tone.
  • Frequent Reorientation: Regularly remind the person of the time, place, and situation using simple, consistent language.
  • Establish Routine: Maintain a consistent daily schedule for meals, sleep, and activities to provide predictability.
  • Ensure Safety: Remove potential hazards from the environment to prevent falls or injury, especially during periods of agitation.
  • Facilitate Familiarity: Encourage the presence of familiar family members or caregivers and bring familiar items (e.g., photos, blankets) to the bedside.
  • Report Changes: Immediately report any sudden or significant changes in behavior, mental status, or physical condition to the medical team.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Paramount: Prompt recognition and investigation of underlying causes are critical for improving patient outcomes and preventing complications.
  • Multifactorial Approach: Delirium often has multiple contributing factors, necessitating a comprehensive assessment and individualized management plan.
  • Judicious Pharmacotherapy: Antipsychotics should be used cautiously and at the lowest effective dose for severe agitation or psychosis, avoiding benzodiazepines unless for withdrawal syndromes.
  • Non-Pharmacological First-Line: Prioritize environmental, supportive, and reorientation strategies as the primary interventions.
  • Prognosis and Long-Term Impact: Delirium is associated with increased morbidity, mortality, longer hospital stays, and a higher risk of long-term cognitive decline.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Utilize Assessment Tools: Employ validated screening tools like the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) or 4AT to identify delirium quickly and accurately.
  • Proactive Risk Factor Identification: Screen all patients for delirium risk factors upon admission and implement preventive strategies.
  • Environmental Management: Implement strategies such as consistent lighting, noise reduction, and providing visual cues (clocks, calendars) to optimize the patient's environment.
  • Therapeutic Communication: Practice clear, concise, and empathetic communication, using reorientation and validation techniques.
  • Meticulous Documentation: Document changes in mental status, precipitating factors, interventions, and patient responses thoroughly.
  • Interdisciplinary Collaboration: Work closely with physicians, pharmacists, occupational therapists, physical therapists, and family to provide holistic, patient-centered care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, provides specific diagnostic criteria for delirium.
  • ICD-10/11 Codes: Relevant codes include F05.0 (Delirium not superimposed on dementia) and F05.1 (Delirium superimposed on dementia).
  • Neurotransmitter Imbalance: Often linked to cholinergic deficiency and dopaminergic excess, among other neurotransmitter dysregulations.
  • Inflammatory Hypothesis: Systemic inflammation can lead to neuroinflammation, disrupting brain function and contributing to delirium.
  • Risk Factors (PREDICTORS Mnemonic): Pain, Restraint, Environment, Drugs, Infection, Cognition, Sleep disturbances are common risk factors.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Crucial to differentiate delirium from dementia, depression, and other acute psychiatric conditions.
