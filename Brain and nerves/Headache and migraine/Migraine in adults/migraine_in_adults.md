# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Migraine is a primary headache disorder characterized by recurrent attacks of moderate to severe head pain, often throbbing, unilateral, and associated with other symptoms.
* **Phases of Migraine:** Attacks typically progress through prodrome (pre-headache symptoms), aura (neurological symptoms preceding or accompanying headache), headache phase, and postdrome (post-headache symptoms).
* **Impact:** Migraine significantly impairs quality of life, work productivity, and daily activities, often requiring acute treatment and sometimes preventive therapy.
* **Triggers:** Common triggers include stress, hormonal changes, certain foods, sleep deprivation, weather changes, and sensory stimuli (bright lights, loud noises, strong smells).


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Head Pain Characteristics:** Typically unilateral (though can be bilateral), throbbing or pulsating, moderate to severe intensity, aggravated by physical activity.
* **Nausea and Vomiting:** Common gastrointestinal symptoms accompanying the headache phase.
* **Sensory Sensitivities:** Photophobia (increased sensitivity to light), phonophobia (increased sensitivity to sound), and osmophobia (increased sensitivity to smell) are hallmark features.
* **Migraine Aura:** Transient neurological symptoms, most commonly visual (e.g., flashing lights, zigzag lines, blind spots), but can also be sensory (numbness, tingling) or speech-related.
* **Other Symptoms:** Fatigue, irritability, difficulty concentrating, neck stiffness, and mood changes can occur during prodrome and postdrome phases.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Trigger Identification:** Maintain a headache diary to identify and avoid personal migraine triggers, which can significantly reduce attack frequency.
* **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Be vigilant about the frequency of acute pain medication use; overuse can lead to chronic daily headache.
* **Emergency Care:** Seek immediate medical attention for sudden, severe headache, headache accompanied by fever, stiff neck, confusion, weakness, vision changes, or numbness, as these may indicate a more serious condition.
* **Contraindications for Acute Treatments:** Triptans are contraindicated in patients with uncontrolled hypertension, ischemic heart disease, stroke, or certain peripheral vascular diseases.
* **Gradual Withdrawal:** If discontinuing preventive medications, do so under medical supervision to avoid rebound symptoms.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Identify Dietary Triggers:** Common food triggers include aged cheeses, processed meats, chocolate, caffeine (both excess and withdrawal), alcohol (especially red wine), artificial sweeteners, and MSG.
* **Maintain Regular Meals:** Skipping meals can lead to blood sugar fluctuations, which may trigger migraines in susceptible individuals.
* **Hydration:** Ensure adequate fluid intake throughout the day, as dehydration is a known migraine trigger.
* **Caffeine Management:** Maintain a consistent caffeine intake or gradually reduce it if it's a suspected trigger; avoid sudden withdrawal.
* **Nutritional Supplements:** Discuss with a physician about potential benefits of supplements like magnesium, riboflavin (Vitamin B2), and CoQ10, which may help some individuals.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Support During Attacks:** Assist the individual in finding a quiet, dark, and cool environment to rest during a migraine attack.
* **Medication Assistance:** Help administer prescribed acute medications promptly at the onset of symptoms, as early treatment is often most effective.
* **Monitor Symptoms:** Observe for any worsening symptoms, new neurological deficits, or signs of medication side effects, and report them to a healthcare professional.
* **Emotional Support:** Provide reassurance and understanding, as migraine attacks can be debilitating and emotionally distressing.
* **Avoid Triggers:** Help minimize exposure to known triggers in the environment, such as strong perfumes, loud noises, or bright lights.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Accurate Diagnosis:** A thorough history and neurological examination are crucial to differentiate migraine from other headache types and rule out secondary causes.
* **Personalized Treatment Plan:** Treatment involves both acute (abortive) medications for attacks and preventive medications to reduce frequency and severity, tailored to the individual's needs.
* **Pharmacological Options:** Acute treatments include NSAIDs, triptans, gepants, and ditans. Preventive options include beta-blockers, tricyclic antidepressants, anticonvulsants, and CGRP monoclonal antibodies.
* **Non-Pharmacological Strategies:** Recommend lifestyle modifications, stress management techniques (e.g., biofeedback, cognitive behavioral therapy), and regular exercise.
* **Regular Follow-up:** Ongoing assessment of treatment efficacy, side effects, and adjustment of the management plan is essential for optimal migraine control.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Patient Education:** Educate patients on migraine pathophysiology, trigger identification, proper medication use (timing, dosage, side effects), and non-pharmacological coping strategies.
* **Pain Assessment:** Utilize validated pain scales and symptom checklists to accurately assess migraine severity, associated symptoms, and impact on daily life.
* **Medication Administration:** Ensure correct administration of acute and preventive medications, monitoring for therapeutic effects and adverse reactions.
* **Environmental Management:** Create a therapeutic environment for patients experiencing an acute attack, minimizing sensory stimuli (light, sound, smell).
* **Documentation:** Meticulously document headache characteristics, triggers, medication use, treatment response, and patient education provided.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **ICD-10 Codes:** G43.0 (Migraine without aura), G43.1 (Migraine with aura), G43.8 (Other migraine), G43.9 (Migraine, unspecified).
* **Diagnostic Criteria:** International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition (ICHD-3) provides the gold standard for migraine diagnosis.
* **Key Pharmacological Classes:** Triptans (e.g., sumatriptan, zolmitriptan), CGRP inhibitors (e.g., erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab), gepants (e.g., ubrogepant, rimegepant), beta-blockers (e.g., propranolol), tricyclic antidepressants (e.g., amitriptyline), anticonvulsants (e.g., topiramate).
* **Pathophysiology:** Involves activation of the trigeminal vascular system, cortical spreading depression, and neuroinflammation, with genetic predispositions.
* **Differential Diagnosis:** Must differentiate from tension-type headache, cluster headache, medication overuse headache, and secondary headaches (e.g., intracranial hemorrhage, tumor, giant cell arteritis).
