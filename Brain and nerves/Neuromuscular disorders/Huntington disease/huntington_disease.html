Huntington disease

🏠 Home / Brain and nerves / Neuromuscular disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Genetic Basis: Huntington disease (HD) is an inherited neurodegenerative disorder caused by a mutation in the HTT gene.
  • Progressive Decline: Characterized by a gradual and relentless deterioration of motor control, cognitive abilities, and psychiatric health.
  • Autosomal Dominant: A single copy of the mutated gene is sufficient to cause the disease, meaning each child of an affected parent has a 50% chance of inheriting it.
  • Variable Onset: Symptoms typically manifest in mid-life (30s-50s), but can appear earlier (juvenile HD) or later in life.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Motor Dysfunction: Involuntary, jerky movements (chorea), dystonia, impaired gait, difficulty with balance, and problems with speech (dysarthria) and swallowing (dysphagia).
  • Cognitive Impairment: Progressive decline in memory, executive functions (planning, problem-solving), judgment, and slowed thought processing.
  • Psychiatric Disturbances: Common manifestations include depression, anxiety, irritability, obsessive-compulsive behaviors, apathy, and sometimes psychosis.
  • Weight Loss: Unexplained and significant weight loss is often observed, even with adequate caloric intake, due to increased metabolic demands and difficulty eating.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Implement safety measures such as removing tripping hazards, installing grab bars, and using assistive devices to mitigate the risk of falls due to motor instability.
  • Aspiration Risk Management: Closely monitor for signs of dysphagia and modify food and liquid consistencies as recommended by a speech-language pathologist to prevent aspiration pneumonia.
  • Medication Adherence & Monitoring: Ensure strict adherence to prescribed medications for symptom management (e.g., chorea, psychiatric symptoms) and monitor for potential side effects.
  • Genetic Counseling: Provide comprehensive genetic counseling to affected individuals and at-risk family members to facilitate informed decision-making regarding testing and family planning.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Modified Food Textures: Adapt food textures (e.g., soft, pureed) and liquid consistencies (e.g., thickened liquids) to ensure safe swallowing and prevent choking or aspiration.
  • High Calorie & Nutrient-Dense Diet: Encourage frequent, small, high-calorie, and nutrient-dense meals and snacks to combat weight loss and meet increased metabolic demands.
  • Hydration Strategies: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially when using thickened liquids, to prevent dehydration.
  • Feeding Assistance: Provide assistance with feeding as motor skills decline to ensure sufficient intake and maintain nutritional status.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Environmental Safety: Maintain a safe and clutter-free environment to prevent injuries from falls or uncontrolled movements.
  • Communication Support: Be patient and allow ample time for communication; utilize alternative communication methods if speech becomes severely impaired.
  • Behavioral Observation: Closely observe and document any changes in mood, behavior, or cognitive function, reporting them promptly to the healthcare team.
  • Swallowing Protocol Adherence: Strictly follow prescribed dietary and swallowing protocols to minimize the risk of aspiration during meals and fluid intake.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Diagnosis is primarily clinical, supported by a family history and confirmed by genetic testing for the CAG repeat expansion in the HTT gene.
  • Symptomatic Treatment: Management is largely symptomatic, focusing on medications to control chorea (e.g., tetrabenazine, deutetrabenazine) and psychiatric symptoms (e.g., antidepressants, antipsychotics).
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Emphasize a comprehensive, multidisciplinary care team including neurologists, psychiatrists, physical, occupational, and speech therapists, and dietitians.
  • Prognosis & Support: Provide realistic expectations regarding disease progression and offer robust psychological and social support services for patients and their families.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Holistic Assessment: Conduct thorough assessments of motor function, cognitive status, mood, nutritional intake, and swallowing ability to guide care planning.
  • Individualized Care Plans: Develop and implement individualized care plans addressing mobility, communication, nutrition, safety, and emotional well-being.
  • Patient & Family Education: Educate patients and families about disease progression, medication management, safety measures, and available support groups and resources.
  • Ethical Considerations: Understand the complex ethical issues surrounding genetic testing, predictive testing, and end-of-life care planning in HD.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Genetic Mutation: Caused by an expanded CAG trinucleotide repeat in exon 1 of the HTT gene on chromosome 4p16.3.
  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by selective neuronal degeneration, particularly in the striatum (caudate and putamen) and cerebral cortex, leading to neurotransmitter imbalances.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Clinical presentation of characteristic motor, cognitive, and/or psychiatric symptoms, confirmed by genetic testing showing ≥36 CAG repeats.
  • Pharmacological Interventions: Tetrabenazine and deutetrabenazine are FDA-approved for chorea; other medications target psychiatric symptoms (e.g., SSRIs, antipsychotics).
