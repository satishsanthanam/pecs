Neck fracture

🏠 Home / Brain and nerves / Spinal cord injury

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Cervical Spine Fracture: A neck fracture involves a break in one or more of the seven bones (vertebrae) that form the cervical spine, located in the neck.
  • Traumatic Origin: Most commonly results from high-impact trauma such as motor vehicle accidents, falls, diving accidents, or sports injuries.
  • Spinal Cord Risk: A significant concern is potential damage to the spinal cord, which can lead to severe neurological deficits, including paralysis.
  • Stability is Key: The stability of the fracture dictates the risk of further injury and the approach to treatment.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Neck Pain: Intense pain in the neck, often exacerbated by movement or palpation.
  • Muscle Spasms: Involuntary tightening and cramping of neck and shoulder muscles.
  • Neurological Deficits: Numbness, tingling, weakness, or paralysis in the arms, legs, or torso, indicating potential spinal cord involvement.
  • Headache and Tenderness: Pain radiating to the head, along with localized tenderness and bruising around the fracture site.
  • Difficulty Swallowing/Breathing: In severe cases, swelling or instability can affect the airway or esophagus.
  • Altered Sensation: Loss of sensation or abnormal sensations below the level of injury.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Immobilization: Any suspected neck fracture requires immediate and rigid immobilization of the cervical spine using a cervical collar and a rigid backboard.
  • Avoid Movement: Do not attempt to move the patient's head or neck; any movement can worsen spinal cord injury.
  • Log-Roll Technique: If repositioning is necessary, use a log-roll technique with multiple personnel to maintain spinal alignment.
  • Monitor ABCs: Continuously assess and maintain the patient's airway, breathing, and circulation, as respiratory compromise can occur.
  • Neurological Assessment: Perform frequent neurological checks to detect any changes in motor function, sensation, or reflexes.
  • Pain Management: Administer appropriate analgesia while being mindful of potential altered mental status.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Initial NPO Status: Patients with suspected or confirmed neck fractures are typically kept NPO (nil per os) due to the high likelihood of requiring surgery or the risk of aspiration.
  • Gradual Reintroduction: Once medically cleared and stable, a gradual reintroduction of clear liquids, followed by a soft diet, will be initiated as tolerated.
  • Nutrient-Dense Foods: Focus on a diet rich in protein, vitamins (especially C and D), and minerals (calcium) to support bone healing and tissue repair.
  • Adequate Hydration: Ensure sufficient fluid intake to prevent constipation, especially if on pain medication, and to support overall recovery.
  • Swallowing Assessment: For patients with high cervical injuries, a swallowing assessment may be necessary before oral intake to prevent aspiration.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Strict Immobilization Adherence: Ensure the patient maintains strict cervical spine immobilization as prescribed, whether with a collar, halo vest, or other device.
  • Neurological Monitoring: Regularly observe for any changes in the patient's strength, sensation, or ability to move, and report immediately.
  • Assistance with ADLs: Provide comprehensive assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs) to prevent unnecessary movement and ensure patient comfort and hygiene.
  • Pressure Injury Prevention: Implement rigorous skin care protocols, especially around immobilization devices, to prevent pressure injuries.
  • Psychological Support: Offer emotional support and clear communication to the patient and family, addressing anxieties related to the injury and recovery.
  • Medication Management: Administer prescribed medications for pain, muscle spasms, and other symptoms, monitoring for side effects.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Rapid Diagnosis: Prompt and accurate diagnosis using imaging (X-ray, CT scan, MRI) is crucial to determine fracture type, stability, and spinal cord involvement.
  • Treatment Strategy: Management ranges from conservative (immobilization) to surgical intervention, depending on fracture stability, neurological status, and patient factors.
  • Neurological Preservation: The primary goal is to prevent further neurological injury and optimize recovery of existing function.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Effective care requires collaboration among emergency physicians, neurosurgeons, orthopedic surgeons, neurologists, and rehabilitation specialists.
  • Long-Term Rehabilitation: Comprehensive physical and occupational therapy is essential for maximizing functional recovery and adapting to any residual deficits.
  • Risk of Complications: Be vigilant for complications such as spinal shock, neurogenic shock, deep vein thrombosis, and pulmonary embolism.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Spinal Immobilization Techniques: Master the application and assessment of cervical collars, head blocks, and backboards for safe patient transfer.
  • Comprehensive Neurological Assessment: Understand and perform detailed neurological assessments, including motor strength, sensory testing, and reflex checks, correlating findings with dermatomes and myotomes.
  • Halo Vest Care: Learn the principles of halo vest application, pin site care, and patient education regarding mobility and hygiene with the device.
  • Bladder and Bowel Management: Anticipate and manage potential neurogenic bladder and bowel dysfunction, implementing appropriate catheterization and bowel regimens.
  • Respiratory Management: Monitor for signs of respiratory compromise, especially with high cervical injuries, and be prepared for airway management.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on the importance of adherence to immobilization, signs of complications, and rehabilitation expectations.
