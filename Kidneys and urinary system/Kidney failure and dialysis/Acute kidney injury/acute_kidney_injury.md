# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Rapid Decline:** Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden and often reversible decrease in kidney function, leading to the accumulation of waste products in the blood.
* **Three Categories:** AKI is broadly classified into pre-renal (reduced blood flow to kidneys), intrinsic (direct damage to kidney tissue), and post-renal (obstruction of urine outflow).
* **Critical Impact:** Even mild AKI can increase the risk of chronic kidney disease (CKD), cardiovascular events, and mortality.
* **Early Detection:** Prompt identification and management of the underlying cause are crucial for preventing progression and improving outcomes.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Decreased Urine Output:** Oliguria (reduced urine production) or anuria (no urine production) is a common and significant sign.
* **Fluid Retention:** Swelling in the legs, ankles, or around the eyes (edema), and shortness of breath due to fluid in the lungs.
* **General Malaise:** Fatigue, weakness, and a general feeling of being unwell.
* **Gastrointestinal Issues:** Nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, and metallic taste in the mouth.
* **Neurological Changes:** Confusion, disorientation, seizures, or coma in severe cases due to uremic encephalopathy.
* **Cardiac Manifestations:** Irregular heartbeats (arrhythmias) due to electrolyte imbalances, particularly hyperkalemia.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Nephrotoxic Avoidance:** Exercise extreme caution with or avoid medications known to harm kidneys, such as NSAIDs, certain antibiotics (e.g., aminoglycosides), and contrast dyes, especially in at-risk individuals.
* **Hydration Management:** Maintain adequate hydration, particularly during illness, but avoid overhydration in established AKI with oliguria.
* **Underlying Condition Control:** Meticulously manage chronic conditions like diabetes, hypertension, and heart failure, which are major risk factors for AKI.
* **Blood Pressure Monitoring:** Ensure blood pressure is within target ranges, avoiding both hypotension (which can reduce kidney perfusion) and severe hypertension.
* **Early Intervention:** Seek immediate medical attention for symptoms suggestive of AKI or any sudden changes in urine output.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Fluid Restriction:** If urine output is low, fluid intake will be strictly limited to prevent fluid overload and pulmonary edema.
* **Sodium Restriction:** Limit salt intake to control blood pressure and reduce fluid retention.
* **Potassium Restriction:** Avoid high-potassium foods (e.g., bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes) to prevent dangerous hyperkalemia.
* **Phosphate Restriction:** Limit foods high in phosphorus (e.g., dairy products, nuts, processed foods) to prevent hyperphosphatemia.
* **Protein Modification:** Protein intake may be restricted to reduce the burden on the kidneys, but adequate protein is essential for healing and preventing malnutrition.
* **Calorie Adequacy:** Ensure sufficient calorie intake from carbohydrates and fats to prevent the body from breaking down its own protein for energy.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Medication Adherence:** Administer all prescribed medications exactly as directed, paying close attention to dosage adjustments for kidney function.
* **Daily Weight Monitoring:** Track daily body weight to assess fluid status; a sudden increase can indicate fluid retention.
* **Input and Output Charting:** Maintain meticulous records of all fluid intake and urine output to monitor kidney function and guide fluid management.
* **Vital Sign Surveillance:** Regularly monitor blood pressure, heart rate, and respiratory rate for signs of complications or worsening condition.
* **Symptom Reporting:** Immediately report any new or worsening symptoms, such as increased swelling, shortness of breath, confusion, or changes in urine color/frequency.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Identify Etiology:** The primary goal is to rapidly identify and address the underlying cause of AKI, whether it's hypovolemia, nephrotoxic exposure, or obstruction.
* **Supportive Care:** Management focuses on supportive measures, including fluid and electrolyte balance, blood pressure control, and nutritional support.
* **Medication Review:** A thorough review of all current medications is essential to discontinue nephrotoxic agents and adjust dosages for impaired renal function.
* **Renal Replacement Therapy:** For severe AKI with life-threatening complications (e.g., refractory hyperkalemia, severe fluid overload, uremic encephalopathy), renal replacement therapy (dialysis) may be necessary.
* **Long-term Follow-up:** Patients recovering from AKI require close monitoring for potential progression to chronic kidney disease and cardiovascular complications.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **AKI Staging:** Understand and apply the KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) criteria for staging AKI based on serum creatinine and urine output.
* **Fluid Balance Assessment:** Master techniques for accurate fluid intake and output measurement, physical assessment for fluid overload/depletion, and interpretation of daily weights.
* **Electrolyte Monitoring:** Recognize the signs and symptoms of common electrolyte imbalances in AKI, such as hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and hyperphosphatemia.
* **Medication Administration:** Be proficient in administering renally-dosed medications and understanding the implications of drug accumulation in kidney dysfunction.
* **Patient Education:** Educate patients and families on dietary restrictions, fluid management, symptom recognition, and the importance of follow-up care.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pathophysiology:** AKI mechanisms include pre-renal azotemia (e.g., hypovolemia, heart failure), intrinsic renal injury (e.g., acute tubular necrosis, interstitial nephritis, glomerulonephritis), and post-renal obstruction (e.g., BPH, kidney stones).
* **Diagnostic Markers:** Key indicators include serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), urine output, fractional excretion of sodium (FENa), and novel biomarkers like neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) or kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1).
* **Imaging Modalities:** Renal ultrasound is crucial to rule out post-renal obstruction and assess kidney size; CT scans or MRI may be used for specific etiologies.
* **Pharmacological Interventions:** Diuretics (e.g., furosemide) for fluid overload, vasopressors (e.g., norepinephrine) for pre-renal AKI with hypotension, and medications to correct electrolyte imbalances (e.g., calcium gluconate for hyperkalemia).
* **Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT):** Indications for RRT include refractory fluid overload, severe hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, uremic encephalopathy, and pericarditis. Modalities include intermittent hemodialysis (IHD), continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and peritoneal dialysis.
