How to care for a peritoneal dialysis catheter

🏠 Home / Kidneys and urinary system / Kidney failure and dialysis

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Infection Prevention: Meticulous daily care of your peritoneal dialysis (PD) catheter is crucial to prevent serious infections like peritonitis and exit-site infections.
  • Daily Inspection: Regularly inspect the catheter exit site for any signs of redness, swelling, pain, or unusual drainage.
  • Hand Hygiene: Always perform thorough handwashing with soap and water or an alcohol-based hand rub before touching your catheter or supplies.
  • Sterile Technique: Adhere strictly to the prescribed sterile or clean technique during all dressing changes and connection procedures to minimize contamination risk.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Exit-Site Redness & Tenderness: Localized inflammation, warmth, or pain around the catheter exit site.
  • Pus or Drainage: Any purulent, cloudy, or foul-smelling discharge from the catheter exit site.
  • Fever & Chills: Systemic signs indicating a potential infection, such as peritonitis or a more widespread exit-site infection.
  • Abdominal Pain: New or worsening abdominal discomfort, especially diffuse pain, which can be a primary symptom of peritonitis.
  • Cloudy Dialysate: The most significant indicator of peritonitis; dialysate fluid appearing turbid or opaque instead of clear after an exchange.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Handwashing: Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water for at least 20 seconds before and after any interaction with the catheter or dialysis supplies.
  • Aseptic Dressing Changes: Follow the exact aseptic technique provided by your healthcare team for all exit-site dressing changes.
  • Protect the Catheter: Secure the catheter to prevent pulling, tugging, or accidental dislodgement, which can cause trauma or infection.
  • Avoid Submersion: Do not take baths, swim in pools, lakes, or oceans unless explicitly cleared by your dialysis team, due to high infection risk.
  • Showering Guidelines: Follow specific instructions for showering, which may involve using a waterproof dressing or avoiding direct water spray on the exit site.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Protein Intake: Ensure sufficient protein consumption to compensate for protein losses during peritoneal dialysis, supporting tissue repair and immune function.
  • Fluid Management: Adhere to prescribed fluid restrictions or targets to prevent fluid overload or dehydration, which can impact overall health and wound healing.
  • Sodium & Potassium Control: Follow dietary guidelines for sodium and potassium to manage blood pressure and electrolyte balance, supporting overall well-being.
  • Blood Glucose Monitoring: For diabetic patients, strict blood glucose control is vital, as elevated glucose levels can impair wound healing and increase infection susceptibility.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Recognize Infection Signs: Attendants must be trained to recognize and report any signs of infection (redness, pain, fever, cloudy dialysate) immediately.
  • Emergency Contact Information: Keep emergency contact numbers for the dialysis unit or healthcare provider readily accessible.
  • Assist with Hygiene: Help ensure the patient maintains strict hand hygiene and a clean environment, especially during dialysis exchanges.
  • Catheter Protection: Assist the patient in protecting the catheter from accidental trauma or dislodgement, especially during movement or activities.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adhere to Protocols: Strict adherence to all prescribed catheter care protocols, including dressing change frequency and technique, is non-negotiable for patient safety.
  • Prompt Reporting of Changes: Any changes in the exit site appearance, dialysate clarity, or patient symptoms must be reported to the dialysis team without delay.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Consistent attendance at scheduled clinic visits is essential for catheter assessment, education reinforcement, and overall health monitoring.
  • Medication Compliance: Ensure all prescribed medications, including prophylactic or therapeutic antibiotics, are taken exactly as directed to prevent or treat infections effectively.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Patient Education: Nurses are responsible for providing thorough, individualized education on catheter care, infection prevention, and complication recognition.
  • Aseptic Technique Demonstration: Master and consistently demonstrate strict aseptic technique during all catheter-related procedures, serving as a role model for patients.
  • Exit-Site Assessment & Documentation: Regularly assess the catheter exit site for signs of infection, inflammation, or skin breakdown, and document findings meticulously.
  • Troubleshooting & Support: Be prepared to troubleshoot common issues related to catheter care and provide empathetic emotional support to patients and their families.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Peritonitis: Inflammation of the peritoneum, typically caused by bacterial or fungal contamination of the dialysate, a severe complication of PD.
  • Exit-Site Infection: A localized infection at the point where the PD catheter exits the skin, characterized by redness, pain, and purulent discharge.
  • Tunnel Infection: An infection along the subcutaneous path of the PD catheter, which can extend from the exit site and potentially lead to peritonitis.
  • Aseptic Technique: A set of practices performed to prevent contamination by microorganisms, fundamental for safe peritoneal dialysis catheter care.
