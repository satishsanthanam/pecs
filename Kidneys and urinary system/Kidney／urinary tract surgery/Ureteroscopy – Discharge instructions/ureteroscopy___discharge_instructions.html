Ureteroscopy – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Kidneys and urinary system / Kidney/urinary tract surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Ureteroscopy is a minimally invasive procedure to remove kidney or ureteral stones, or to diagnose and treat other urinary tract issues.
  • Post-Procedure Expectations: It is normal to experience some discomfort, frequent urination, and blood in your urine for a few days after the procedure.
  • Ureteral Stent: A temporary stent may have been placed to keep the ureter open and facilitate healing. You will be given specific instructions for its removal.
  • Recovery Focus: Adherence to discharge instructions is crucial for a smooth recovery and to minimize complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Expected Discomfort: Mild to moderate flank pain, bladder spasms, and a sensation of needing to urinate frequently are common.
  • Hematuria: Pink or red-tinged urine is expected, especially during the first few days, and may increase with activity.
  • Stent Symptoms: If a stent is present, you may feel bladder irritation, urgency, and discomfort in the kidney area, particularly during urination.
  • When to Contact Your Doctor: Report severe, unmanageable pain, fever (over 101°F or 38.3°C), chills, inability to urinate, heavy bright red bleeding with clots, or worsening symptoms immediately.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medication as directed. Do not wait for pain to become severe before taking medication.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting (over 10-15 lbs), and vigorous exercise for at least 1-2 weeks, or as advised by your surgeon.
  • Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water is best) throughout the day to help flush your system and prevent infection. Aim for 8-10 glasses daily.
  • Stent Care: If a stent was placed, ensure you understand the scheduled removal date and follow all instructions regarding its presence. Do not attempt to remove it yourself.
  • Infection Prevention: Complete the full course of any antibiotics prescribed, even if you feel better.
  • Driving: Do not drive while taking narcotic pain medication or if you are still experiencing significant discomfort.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fluid Intake: Maintain a high fluid intake (primarily water) to help dilute urine, reduce irritation, and flush any small stone fragments.
  • Initial Diet: Start with a light, easily digestible diet after discharge. Gradually return to your normal diet as tolerated.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit intake of caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods, which can irritate the bladder, especially if a stent is present.
  • Stone Prevention (Long-term): Discuss specific dietary modifications for stone prevention with your physician at your follow-up appointment, based on stone analysis.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Situations: Seek immediate medical attention at the nearest emergency department for uncontrolled bleeding, severe pain unresponsive to medication, high fever with chills, or complete inability to urinate.
  • Medication Adherence: Ensure the patient takes all prescribed medications, especially antibiotics and pain relievers, exactly as instructed.
  • Monitoring Symptoms: Closely monitor the patient for any signs of worsening pain, fever, or changes in urinary output and color.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Confirm and ensure the patient attends all scheduled follow-up appointments, including stent removal if applicable.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Expected Recovery: Most patients recover well from ureteroscopy within a few days to a week, though stent-related symptoms can persist until removal.
  • Stent Importance: The ureteral stent is a critical component of healing, preventing obstruction and allowing the ureter to recover. Its timely removal is essential.
  • Hydration is Key: Emphasize the importance of consistent high fluid intake to prevent complications and aid in the passage of any residual fragments.
  • Long-Term Stone Prevention: For patients with kidney stones, a comprehensive metabolic evaluation and tailored prevention strategies will be discussed at the follow-up visit.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Nurses should reinforce all discharge instructions, particularly regarding pain management, hydration, activity restrictions, and stent care.
  • Symptom Assessment: Educate patients on differentiating between expected post-operative symptoms and those requiring immediate medical attention.
  • Pain Assessment: Utilize a pain scale to assess and manage post-operative pain effectively, ensuring appropriate analgesic administration and patient comfort.
  • Urinary Output Monitoring: Instruct patients to monitor urinary output and report any significant decrease or inability to void.
  • Stent Removal Protocol: Ensure patients understand the process and importance of stent removal, including scheduling and potential discomfort.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Procedure Code: CPT codes typically range from 52351-52356 depending on the complexity and interventions performed (e.g., stone removal, biopsy).
  • Post-Op Complications: Potential complications include urinary tract infection (UTI), ureteral stricture, ureteral perforation, persistent hematuria, and stent-related discomfort.
  • Stent Types: Various types of ureteral stents exist (e.g., double-J, pigtail) designed for temporary internal drainage, typically made of biocompatible polymers.
  • Follow-up Imaging: Post-operative imaging (e.g., KUB X-ray, renal ultrasound) may be performed to confirm stone clearance and stent position, or to evaluate for complications.
  • Anticoagulation Management: Specific instructions regarding the resumption of anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents post-procedure must be provided based on individual patient risk.
