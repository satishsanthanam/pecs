Ureteral stent placement – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Kidneys and urinary system / Kidney stones

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of Stent: A ureteral stent is a thin, flexible tube placed in your ureter to help urine flow from the kidney to the bladder, especially after kidney stone treatment or to bypass an obstruction.
  • Temporary Device: This stent is a temporary measure. Its removal will be scheduled by your urologist. Do not attempt to remove it yourself.
  • Common Sensations: It is normal to experience some discomfort, bladder irritation, increased urinary frequency, urgency, and mild flank pain, especially during urination.
  • Blood in Urine: You may notice blood in your urine (hematuria), which can vary in color from pink to dark red. This is common and often increases with activity.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Expected Discomfort: Mild to moderate flank pain, bladder spasms, and a feeling of needing to urinate frequently are common.
  • Urinary Changes: Increased urgency and frequency of urination are typical due to the stent irritating the bladder.
  • Hematuria: Pink, red, or brownish urine is expected, particularly after physical activity.
  • Warning Signs (Seek Medical Attention): Report severe, unmanageable pain, fever (oral temperature over 100.4°F or 38°C), chills, foul-smelling urine, pus in urine, inability to urinate, or worsening, persistent bright red blood in urine.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous physical activity, heavy lifting (over 10-15 pounds), and vigorous exercise until the stent is removed or as advised by your physician.
  • Hydration: Maintain excellent hydration by drinking plenty of fluids (water is best) throughout the day to help flush your system and prevent infection.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including pain relievers and antibiotics, exactly as directed. Do not stop antibiotics prematurely.
  • Stent Removal Appointment: It is critical to attend your scheduled stent removal appointment. Leaving a stent in for too long can lead to complications like encrustation and infection.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit caffeine, alcohol, and spicy foods if they exacerbate bladder irritation or discomfort.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fluid Intake: Emphasize high fluid intake, aiming for 2-3 liters of water daily, unless otherwise specified by your doctor due to other medical conditions.
  • Bladder Irritants: Temporarily reduce or avoid known bladder irritants such as caffeine (coffee, tea, soda), alcohol, and highly acidic or spicy foods if they worsen your urinary symptoms.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Continue with a balanced diet to support healing and overall well-being.
  • Fiber Intake: Ensure adequate fiber intake to prevent constipation, which can increase abdominal pressure and discomfort.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: In case of severe, uncontrolled pain, high fever, inability to urinate, or significant bleeding, contact your urologist immediately or go to the nearest emergency department.
  • Medication Management: Ensure the patient takes all prescribed medications as directed, especially pain medication and antibiotics.
  • Hydration Monitoring: Encourage and monitor the patient's fluid intake to ensure adequate hydration.
  • Symptom Observation: Be vigilant for any signs of worsening symptoms, such as increased pain, fever, chills, or changes in urine color/odor, and report them promptly to the medical team.
  • Stent Removal Reminder: Help the patient remember and prepare for their scheduled stent removal appointment.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Stent is Therapeutic: Understand that the stent is a vital part of your recovery, facilitating urine flow and healing.
  • Manage Expectations: It's common to experience discomfort; manage it with prescribed medications and by following discharge instructions diligently.
  • Hydration is Key: Adequate fluid intake is paramount for stent function and preventing complications.
  • Do Not Delay Removal: Adherence to the stent removal schedule is non-negotiable to prevent serious complications.
  • Report Concerns: Do not hesitate to contact our office for any concerning symptoms or questions; early intervention is always best.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Systematically review all discharge instructions with the patient, ensuring comprehension regarding expected symptoms, warning signs, medication regimen, and follow-up.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Educate on pain scale use and the importance of regular pain medication administration, especially for bladder spasms.
  • Infection Surveillance: Instruct patients on signs of urinary tract infection (UTI) and sepsis, emphasizing when to seek immediate medical attention.
  • Hydration Promotion: Stress the critical role of fluid intake in preventing stent encrustation and UTIs.
  • Follow-up Importance: Highlight the absolute necessity of the stent removal appointment and potential complications of delayed removal.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Stent Composition: Ureteral stents are typically made of biocompatible polymers (e.g., silicone, polyurethane) designed for temporary indwelling.
  • Stent Migration: Potential complication where the stent moves from its intended position, potentially causing increased pain or obstruction.
  • Stent Encrustation: Mineral deposition on the stent surface, increasing with indwelling time, making removal difficult and increasing infection risk.
  • Biofilm Formation: Stents are susceptible to bacterial biofilm formation, contributing to recurrent UTIs.
  • Imaging Follow-up: KUB X-ray or ultrasound may be used to confirm stent position and assess for encrustation prior to removal.
