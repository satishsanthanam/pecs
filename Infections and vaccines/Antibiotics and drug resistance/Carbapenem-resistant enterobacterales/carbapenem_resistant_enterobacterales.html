Carbapenem-resistant enterobacterales

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Antibiotics and drug resistance

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding CRE: Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE) are a family of bacteria that are difficult to treat because they have become resistant to carbapenem antibiotics, a class of last-resort antibiotics.
  • Clinical Significance: CRE infections are associated with high mortality rates, especially in critically ill patients, due to limited treatment options and the ability of these bacteria to cause severe infections.
  • Common Types: Key resistance mechanisms often involve carbapenemase enzymes, such as Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase (KPC) and New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase (NDM).
  • Risk Factors: Patients at highest risk include those with prolonged hospital stays, indwelling medical devices (catheters, ventilators), previous exposure to broad-spectrum antibiotics (especially carbapenems), and immunocompromised states.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs): Dysuria, frequency, urgency, fever, flank pain, or altered mental status in elderly patients, indicating a severe infection.
  • Pneumonia: Persistent cough, high fever, shortness of breath, chest pain, and production of purulent sputum, often in ventilated patients.
  • Bloodstream Infections (BSI): High fever, chills, hypotension, tachycardia, and other systemic signs of sepsis or septic shock, requiring immediate intervention.
  • Wound Infections: Redness, swelling, increased pain, warmth, purulent discharge, and delayed wound healing, particularly in surgical or traumatic wounds.
  • Intra-abdominal Infections: Severe abdominal pain, tenderness, fever, nausea, vomiting, and signs of peritonitis or abscess formation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Hand Hygiene: Healthcare workers must perform meticulous hand hygiene (soap and water or alcohol-based hand rub) before and after patient contact, especially when caring for CRE-colonized or infected patients.
  • Contact Precautions: Implement contact precautions for all patients identified with CRE, including single-room placement or cohorting, and consistent use of gowns and gloves upon entry to the patient's room.
  • Environmental Cleaning: Ensure thorough and frequent cleaning and disinfection of patient care areas and shared medical equipment using EPA-approved disinfectants effective against CRE.
  • Antibiotic Stewardship: Promote judicious use of antibiotics, including appropriate selection, dosing, duration, and de-escalation, to minimize the development and spread of resistance.
  • Device Management: Minimize the use and duration of indwelling medical devices (e.g., urinary catheters, central venous catheters) as they are common sources of infection and colonization.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Support: Patients with severe CRE infections, especially those in critical care, may require aggressive nutritional support (enteral or parenteral) to maintain immune function and aid recovery.
  • Hydration Status: Ensure adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration, particularly in patients with fever, vomiting, or diarrhea. Intravenous fluids may be necessary to maintain electrolyte balance.
  • GI Symptom Management: If the CRE infection or its treatment causes gastrointestinal symptoms (e.g., severe diarrhea), dietary modifications such as a bland, low-fiber diet or temporary restriction of certain foods may be advised by a dietitian.
  • Medication-Related Considerations: Be aware of potential dietary interactions or requirements for specific antibiotics used to treat CRE, such as taking certain medications with food to reduce gastrointestinal upset.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Isolation Protocols: Strictly adhere to established isolation protocols for CRE patients to prevent cross-transmission to other patients and healthcare personnel.
  • Specimen Collection: Ensure proper collection and labeling of clinical specimens for culture and susceptibility testing to guide appropriate antibiotic therapy and track resistance patterns.
  • Reporting: Promptly report all confirmed CRE cases to hospital infection control and public health authorities as required by local and national guidelines.
  • Medical Device Care: Meticulously manage all indwelling medical devices, performing regular site care, assessing for necessity of removal, and ensuring proper insertion and maintenance.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and their families about CRE, the importance of infection control measures, and personal hand hygiene practices to prevent spread within the healthcare setting and at home.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Identification: Rapid identification of CRE through surveillance cultures or clinical specimens is crucial for timely implementation of infection control measures and initiation of appropriate, often complex, treatment regimens.
  • Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing: Always rely on comprehensive and up-to-date susceptibility testing to guide antibiotic selection, as resistance patterns can vary significantly and evolve.
  • Combination Therapy: For severe CRE infections, combination antibiotic therapy is often required, utilizing agents like polymyxins, tigecycline, aminoglycosides, or novel beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations (e.g., ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem-vaborbactam).
  • Infectious Disease Consultation: Consultation with an infectious disease specialist is highly recommended for optimal management of CRE infections, given their complexity and limited treatment options.
  • Preventing Spread: Emphasize the critical role of robust antibiotic stewardship programs and stringent infection prevention strategies in curbing the emergence and spread of CRE within healthcare facilities and the community.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Infection Control Champion: Nurses are frontline champions in implementing and reinforcing infection control practices, including meticulous hand hygiene, consistent contact precautions, and thorough environmental cleaning.
  • Patient Monitoring: Vigilantly monitor patients with CRE for signs of worsening infection, adverse effects of complex antibiotic regimens, and changes in clinical status, reporting concerns promptly.
  • Specimen Collection Proficiency: Ensure accurate and aseptic collection of specimens (e.g., urine, blood, wound swabs, respiratory secretions) for microbiological diagnosis and susceptibility testing.
  • Medication Administration: Administer complex antibiotic regimens, often requiring careful calculation, management of intravenous access, and monitoring for infusion reactions and drug toxicities.
  • Patient and Family Education: Educate patients and their families on the nature of CRE, the importance of isolation, personal hygiene practices, and adherence to treatment plans to prevent further spread and promote recovery.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Resistance Mechanisms: Primarily mediated by carbapenemase enzymes (e.g., KPC, NDM, OXA-48, VIM, IMP) that hydrolyze carbapenems, or by outer membrane porin loss combined with efflux pumps and extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs).
  • Diagnostic Methods: Culture-based methods with phenotypic susceptibility testing (e.g., modified Hodge test, carbapenem inactivation method), molecular tests (e.g., PCR for carbapenemase genes), and rapid diagnostic assays are used for identification.
  • Treatment Modalities: Often involves combination therapy with agents such as polymyxins (colistin), tigecycline, aminoglycosides, fosfomycin, and newer beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations (e.g., ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem-vaborbactam, imipenem-cilastatin-relebactam, cefiderocol).
  • Epidemiology: CRE are a global public health threat, with varying prevalence rates by region and healthcare setting. Active surveillance and genomic epidemiology are critical for tracking spread and identifying outbreaks.
  • Infection Prevention: Core strategies include active surveillance, contact precautions, environmental disinfection, robust antibiotic stewardship programs, and rapid diagnostic testing to limit transmission.
