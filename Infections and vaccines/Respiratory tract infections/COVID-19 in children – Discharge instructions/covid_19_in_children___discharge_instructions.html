COVID-19 in children – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Respiratory tract infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Isolation Period: Your child must complete the recommended isolation period as advised by the healthcare provider, typically 5-10 days from symptom onset or positive test, depending on symptom resolution.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Continue to closely monitor your child for any new or worsening symptoms, even after discharge.
  • Contagion Risk: COVID-19 remains highly contagious. Strict adherence to isolation guidelines is crucial to prevent further spread within the household and community.
  • Return to Activities: Your child may return to school or other activities only after meeting all criteria for ending isolation and being fever-free for 24 hours without fever-reducing medication, with improving symptoms.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Worsening Respiratory Distress: Watch for increased breathing rate, nasal flaring, retractions (sinking in of skin around ribs/neck with breathing), or grunting sounds.
  • Persistent High Fever: Fever (>100.4°F or 38°C) that does not respond to fever-reducing medication or recurs frequently.
  • Signs of Dehydration: Decreased urine output, dry mouth, absence of tears, sunken eyes, or lethargy.
  • Altered Mental Status: Unusual drowsiness, extreme irritability, confusion, or difficulty waking up.
  • New or Severe Pain: Unexplained severe headache, chest pain, or abdominal pain.
  • Skin Changes: New rash, pale, gray, or blue-colored skin, lips, or nail beds.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Home Isolation Protocol: Ensure your child remains isolated from other household members as much as possible, ideally in a separate room with a dedicated bathroom if available.
  • Hand Hygiene: All household members must practice frequent and thorough handwashing with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer (at least 60% alcohol).
  • Masking: If your child is old enough and able to tolerate it, they should wear a well-fitting mask when around others. Caregivers should also wear masks when in close contact with the child.
  • Surface Cleaning: Regularly clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces in the child's isolation area and common household areas.
  • Ventilation: Improve ventilation by opening windows or using air purifiers when safe and feasible.
  • Medication Adherence: Administer all prescribed medications exactly as directed by the healthcare provider.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Encourage frequent intake of clear fluids such as water, oral rehydration solutions, diluted juices, or clear broths to prevent dehydration.
  • Nutrient-Rich Foods: Offer small, frequent, easily digestible meals. Focus on soft, bland foods like toast, rice, bananas, applesauce, and lean proteins.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit foods that may worsen symptoms, such as spicy, greasy, or highly acidic foods, which can irritate the throat or stomach.
  • Monitor Appetite: Do not force your child to eat if they have a poor appetite, but prioritize fluid intake. Appetite should gradually improve with recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Know when to seek immediate medical attention. Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department if your child experiences severe difficulty breathing, persistent pain or pressure in the chest, new confusion, inability to wake or stay awake, or pale, gray, or blue-colored skin, lips, or nail beds.
  • Medication Management: Keep all medications out of reach of children. Follow dosage instructions precisely and do not give adult medications to children without specific medical advice.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Ensure all scheduled follow-up appointments with your pediatrician or specialist are attended to monitor recovery and address any lingering concerns.
  • Caregiver Well-being: Caregiving for a sick child can be stressful. Seek support from family, friends, or mental health resources if needed.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Rest and Recovery: Emphasize the importance of adequate rest for your child's full recovery. Gradual return to normal activities is key to prevent relapse or prolonged fatigue.
  • Vaccination: Ensure all eligible household members, including the child, are up-to-date on COVID-19 vaccinations and boosters to provide optimal protection against future infections and severe disease.
  • Post-Acute Sequelae: Be aware that some children may experience lingering symptoms or develop post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (Long COVID). Discuss any persistent concerns with your pediatrician.
  • Trust Your Instincts: If you are concerned about your child's condition at any point, do not hesitate to contact your healthcare provider for advice or re-evaluation.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Focus: Prioritize clear, concise education for parents/guardians on symptom recognition, isolation protocols, medication administration, and when to seek emergency care. Utilize teach-back methods.
  • Home Environment Assessment: Consider the family's living situation when providing discharge instructions, adapting advice for multi-generational households or limited space.
  • Resource Provision: Provide families with reliable resources for COVID-19 information (e.g., CDC, local health department) and contact numbers for follow-up questions.
  • Infection Control Reinforcement: Educate on proper donning/doffing of masks, waste disposal, and laundry handling to minimize household transmission.
  • Psychosocial Support: Acknowledge the emotional toll on families and offer resources for psychosocial support if indicated.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CDC Isolation Guidelines: Refer to the latest Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for isolation and precautions for individuals with COVID-19, specifically adapted for pediatric populations.
  • Viral Shedding Duration: Understand that viral shedding can persist for varying durations, influencing isolation recommendations. PCR tests can remain positive long after infectivity has passed.
  • Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome in Children (MIS-C): Educate parents on the rare but serious post-COVID complication, MIS-C, and its warning signs (e.g., persistent fever, rash, abdominal pain, red eyes).
  • Immunocompromised Children: For children who are immunocompromised, isolation periods and precautions may need to be extended and intensified based on specific clinical guidance.
  • Antiviral Eligibility: Be aware of current guidelines for antiviral treatment eligibility in pediatric patients with COVID-19 who are at high risk for severe disease.
