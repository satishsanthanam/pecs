Dengue fever

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Insect borne disease

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Viral Transmission: Dengue fever is a viral infection transmitted to humans through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, primarily Aedes aegypti.
  • Global Prevalence: It is a widespread disease in tropical and subtropical regions, posing a significant public health challenge.
  • Four Serotypes: There are four distinct serotypes of the dengue virus (DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, and DENV-4); infection with one serotype provides lifelong immunity to that serotype but only temporary immunity to others.
  • Risk of Severe Dengue: A second infection with a different dengue serotype significantly increases the risk of developing severe dengue.
  • Clinical Phases: The disease typically progresses through three phases: febrile, critical, and convalescent.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • High Fever: Sudden onset of high fever (40°C/104°F) lasting 2-7 days.
  • Severe Headache: Intense headache, often accompanied by retro-orbital (behind the eyes) pain.
  • Muscle and Joint Pain: Severe muscle, bone, and joint pain, giving rise to the term 'breakbone fever'.
  • Rash: Maculopapular rash, often appearing 3-4 days after fever onset, sometimes with petechiae.
  • Nausea and Vomiting: Common gastrointestinal symptoms.
  • Warning Signs of Severe Dengue: Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, rapid breathing, bleeding gums or nose, fatigue, restlessness, blood in vomit or stool, and cold, clammy extremities.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Mosquito Bite Prevention: Use insect repellents containing DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus on exposed skin.
  • Protective Clothing: Wear long-sleeved shirts and long pants, especially during dawn and dusk when Aedes mosquitoes are most active.
  • Eliminate Breeding Sites: Regularly empty and clean containers that can hold water (e.g., flower pots, tires, buckets) to prevent mosquito breeding.
  • Window and Door Screens: Ensure windows and doors have intact screens to keep mosquitoes out of living spaces.
  • Travel Awareness: Be informed about dengue prevalence in travel destinations and take appropriate preventive measures.
  • Avoid NSAIDs: Do not use aspirin, ibuprofen, or other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) if dengue is suspected, due to increased risk of bleeding; use acetaminophen for fever and pain.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Crucial to consume plenty of fluids such as water, oral rehydration solutions (ORS), coconut water, and clear broths to prevent dehydration, especially during the febrile phase.
  • Soft and Bland Foods: Opt for easily digestible foods like porridge, rice gruel, boiled vegetables, and fruit purees to minimize gastrointestinal discomfort.
  • Avoid Spicy and Oily Foods: These can exacerbate nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain.
  • Electrolyte-Rich Fluids: Consider electrolyte-rich drinks if there is significant fluid loss from vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eating smaller portions more frequently can help manage nausea and maintain energy levels.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Vigilant Monitoring for Warning Signs: Caregivers must be educated to recognize and immediately report any severe dengue warning signs, such as severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, lethargy, or any bleeding.
  • Strict Fluid Management: Ensure the patient maintains adequate oral fluid intake; monitor for signs of dehydration like reduced urination or dry mucous membranes.
  • Fever Control: Administer acetaminophen as prescribed for fever and pain, strictly avoiding NSAIDs.
  • Rest and Comfort: Provide a quiet and comfortable environment to promote rest and recovery.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know the location of the nearest medical facility and be prepared for immediate transport if warning signs develop.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Diagnosis: Dengue is often diagnosed clinically based on symptoms, travel history, and epidemiological context, confirmed by serological tests (NS1 antigen, IgM/IgG antibodies) or PCR.
  • Supportive Care: Management is primarily supportive, focusing on fluid balance, fever control with acetaminophen, and close monitoring for progression to severe dengue.
  • Hospitalization Criteria: Patients with warning signs, severe dengue, comorbidities, or social factors indicating poor home care require hospitalization for intravenous fluid therapy and intensive monitoring.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Consider other febrile illnesses such as malaria, chikungunya, Zika, and leptospirosis.
  • Vaccination: Dengvaxia (CYD-TDV) is approved in some endemic areas for individuals with confirmed prior dengue infection to reduce the risk of severe disease.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Fluid Balance Assessment: Meticulous monitoring of intake and output, daily weight, and assessment of hydration status (skin turgor, mucous membranes, capillary refill time).
  • Vital Sign Surveillance: Frequent monitoring of temperature, pulse, blood pressure, and respiratory rate, with particular attention during the critical phase for signs of shock.
  • Hematological Monitoring: Regular complete blood count (CBC) to track platelet count and hematocrit, which are crucial indicators of disease progression and plasma leakage.
  • Patient and Family Education: Educate on the disease course, warning signs, importance of hydration, and mosquito control measures.
  • Environmental Control: Participate in community health initiatives for vector control and public awareness campaigns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Dengue virus infection leads to endothelial dysfunction, increased vascular permeability, and plasma leakage, which can result in hypovolemic shock.
  • WHO Classification: Dengue is classified into Dengue without warning signs, Dengue with warning signs, and Severe Dengue.
  • Laboratory Findings: Common findings include leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, elevated hematocrit (due to plasma leakage), and elevated liver transaminases (AST/ALT).
  • Dengue Shock Syndrome (DSS): A severe manifestation characterized by rapid and weak pulse, narrow pulse pressure (≤20 mmHg), hypotension, and cold, clammy skin.
  • Hemorrhagic Manifestations: Petechiae, ecchymoses, epistaxis, gum bleeding, and gastrointestinal bleeding can occur due to thrombocytopenia and capillary fragility.
