Mumps

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Viral infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Viral Infection: Mumps is caused by the mumps virus, a highly contagious RNA virus belonging to the Paramyxoviridae family.
  • Transmission: Spreads through respiratory droplets from an infected person's coughs, sneezes, or talking.
  • Contagious Period: Individuals are most contagious 1-2 days before and up to 5 days after the onset of parotid gland swelling.
  • Vaccine Preventable: The Measles, Mumps, and Rubella (MMR) vaccine is highly effective in preventing mumps and its complications.
  • Incubation Period: Symptoms typically appear 16-18 days after exposure, with a range of 12-25 days.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Parotitis: Swelling and tenderness of one or both parotid salivary glands, often the most characteristic symptom.
  • Fever: Low-grade fever, usually preceding the onset of parotid swelling.
  • Headache: Common symptom, often accompanying the fever and general malaise.
  • Muscle Aches: Generalized body aches and fatigue are frequently reported.
  • Fatigue: Profound tiredness and a general feeling of being unwell.
  • Loss of Appetite: Reduced desire to eat, potentially due to discomfort from chewing and swallowing.
  • Orchitis: Inflammation of one or both testicles in post-pubertal males, a significant potential complication.
  • Oophoritis: Inflammation of the ovaries in post-pubertal females, though less common than orchitis.
  • Meningitis: Aseptic meningitis can occur, presenting with headache, stiff neck, and sensitivity to light (photophobia).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Vaccination: Ensure all eligible individuals receive two doses of the MMR vaccine according to recommended schedules.
  • Isolation: Isolate infected individuals for at least 5 days after the onset of parotid swelling to prevent further transmission.
  • Hand Hygiene: Practice frequent and thorough handwashing with soap and water or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer.
  • Respiratory Etiquette: Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or the elbow, and dispose of tissues properly.
  • Avoid Sharing: Do not share eating utensils, cups, or personal items with an infected person.
  • Monitor Contacts: Observe close contacts, especially unvaccinated individuals, for the development of symptoms.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Soft Foods: Offer soft, easy-to-chew and swallow foods due to potential pain from parotid gland swelling.
  • Hydration: Encourage ample fluid intake (water, clear broths, diluted juices) to prevent dehydration, especially with fever.
  • Avoid Acidic Foods: Restrict acidic foods and beverages (e.g., citrus fruits, vinegar-based items) as they can stimulate salivary glands and exacerbate pain.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Provide smaller, more frequent meals if appetite is diminished or chewing is painful.
  • Nutrient-Rich Options: Focus on bland, nutrient-dense options like mashed potatoes, yogurt, cooked cereals, and pureed soups.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Report to Public Health: Mumps is a nationally notifiable disease; report suspected or confirmed cases to local public health authorities promptly.
  • Monitor for Complications: Closely observe for signs of complications such as severe headache, stiff neck, testicular pain/swelling, or abdominal pain.
  • Pain Management: Administer analgesics (e.g., acetaminophen, ibuprofen) as prescribed to manage fever and pain.
  • Scrotal Support: For orchitis, recommend scrotal support and cold packs to help reduce swelling and discomfort.
  • School/Work Exclusion: Advise infected individuals to remain home from school or work for the recommended isolation period.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Diagnosis: Diagnosis is primarily clinical based on characteristic parotitis, but can be confirmed with laboratory tests (e.g., serology for IgM antibodies, RT-PCR).
  • Supportive Care: Treatment for mumps is supportive, focusing on symptom relief and preventing complications, as there is no specific antiviral therapy.
  • Vaccine Efficacy: Emphasize the critical role of the MMR vaccine in preventing mumps and its associated severe complications.
  • Complication Awareness: Counsel patients and families on potential complications including orchitis, oophoritis, aseptic meningitis, pancreatitis, and rare cases of permanent hearing loss.
  • Community Outbreaks: Maintain vigilance for mumps cases, especially in settings with low vaccination coverage or during known outbreaks.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Assessment: Conduct a thorough assessment for characteristic parotid gland swelling, fever, and other systemic symptoms. Gently palpate affected glands.
  • Isolation Protocols: Implement droplet precautions for hospitalized patients with suspected or confirmed mumps to prevent nosocomial spread.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and caregivers on disease transmission, symptom management, the importance of hydration, and the benefits of vaccination.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics and apply warm or cold compresses to swollen glands as tolerated for comfort.
  • Fluid Balance: Monitor hydration status, particularly in pediatric patients, and encourage consistent fluid intake.
  • Complication Monitoring: Closely monitor for signs of severe complications such such as nuchal rigidity, severe testicular pain, or altered mental status.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: Mumps virus, a single-stranded RNA virus of the genus Rubulavirus within the Paramyxoviridae family.
  • Pathophysiology: Virus replicates in the upper respiratory tract, then disseminates via viremia to target organs including salivary glands (parotid), testes, ovaries, pancreas, and central nervous system.
  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) from buccal swab or urine, or detection of mumps-specific IgM antibodies in serum.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Includes bacterial parotitis, parainfluenza virus infection, influenza virus infection, cytomegalovirus, HIV, Sjogren's syndrome, and salivary gland obstruction.
  • Vaccine Schedule: Two doses of MMR vaccine are recommended: the first dose at 12-15 months of age, and the second dose at 4-6 years of age.
  • Complications: Common complications include orchitis (up to 30% in post-pubertal males), aseptic meningitis (1-10%), and less commonly oophoritis, pancreatitis, and permanent sensorineural hearing loss.
