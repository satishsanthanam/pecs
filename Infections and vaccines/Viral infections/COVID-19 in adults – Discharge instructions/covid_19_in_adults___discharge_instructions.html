COVID-19 in adults – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Viral infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Continued Isolation: You must continue to isolate yourself at home to prevent spreading COVID-19 to others. Follow local public health guidelines for the duration of isolation, typically at least 10 days since symptom onset and 24 hours fever-free without fever-reducing medication, with improving symptoms.
  • Recovery Period: Recovery from COVID-19 can vary. Some symptoms may persist for weeks or even months. It is important to rest and allow your body to heal.
  • Monitoring Your Health: Regularly monitor your symptoms, especially fever, shortness of breath, and oxygen saturation if you have an oximeter. Keep a log of your readings.
  • Household Contact: Inform household members about the need for strict hygiene and social distancing within the home to protect them from infection.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Fever: A fever that returns or worsens after discharge, especially if accompanied by chills or body aches.
  • Worsening Respiratory Symptoms: Increased shortness of breath, difficulty breathing, new or worsening cough, or chest pain/pressure.
  • Decreased Oxygen Saturation: If you have an oximeter, a sustained reading below 92-94% (or as advised by your physician) requires immediate medical attention.
  • New or Severe Symptoms: Any new neurological symptoms (e.g., confusion, difficulty waking), severe weakness, dizziness, or inability to stay hydrated.
  • Gastrointestinal Distress: Persistent nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea that leads to dehydration.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Home Isolation: Remain in a separate room and use a separate bathroom if possible. Avoid contact with household members and pets.
  • Mask Usage: Wear a well-fitting mask if you must be around others in your home or if you need to leave for essential medical care.
  • Hand Hygiene: Wash hands frequently with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol.
  • Disinfection: Regularly clean and disinfect high-touch surfaces in your isolation area.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications as directed, including any antivirals or symptom-relief medications. Do not stop medications without consulting your doctor.
  • Avoid Public Transport: Do not use public transportation, ride-sharing, or taxis for non-emergency medical appointments.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths, electrolyte solutions) to prevent dehydration, especially if experiencing fever, vomiting, or diarrhea.
  • Nutrient-Rich Foods: Consume easily digestible, nutrient-dense foods to support recovery. Focus on fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: If appetite is poor or you experience nausea, try eating smaller, more frequent meals throughout the day.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit intake of highly processed foods, excessive sugar, caffeine, and alcohol, which can hinder recovery or worsen gastrointestinal symptoms.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Caregiver Protection: Caregivers should wear a mask, practice meticulous hand hygiene, and maintain physical distance from the patient as much as possible.
  • Dedicated Supplies: Use separate dishes, utensils, towels, and bedding for the patient. Wash items thoroughly with soap and hot water.
  • Laundry Handling: Wear gloves when handling the patient's laundry. Wash with regular detergent at the warmest appropriate water setting.
  • Symptom Monitoring for Caregivers: Caregivers should monitor themselves for symptoms of COVID-19 and get tested if symptoms develop.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know when to call emergency services (e.g., 911) if the patient's condition rapidly deteriorates (e.g., severe difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, new confusion, inability to wake).

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Follow-Up Care: Schedule and attend all recommended follow-up appointments with your primary care physician or specialist.
  • When to Seek Emergency Care: Immediately seek emergency medical attention for severe shortness of breath, persistent pain or pressure in the chest, new confusion, inability to wake or stay awake, pale/gray/blue-colored skin, lips, or nail beds.
  • Gradual Return to Activity: Do not rush back to strenuous activities. Gradually increase your activity level as tolerated, listening to your body's signals.
  • Mental Health Support: Be aware that anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress can occur after severe illness. Seek support if needed.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Ensure the patient and caregivers understand all discharge instructions, including isolation protocols, medication schedules, and red-flag symptoms.
  • Symptom Assessment: Educate on how to accurately monitor vital signs (temperature, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation if applicable) and recognize signs of clinical deterioration.
  • Infection Control Principles: Review proper hand hygiene, mask use, environmental cleaning, and waste disposal techniques for the home setting.
  • Resource Provision: Provide contact information for follow-up care, local public health departments, and mental health resources.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient and family concerns regarding isolation, fear of transmission, and the emotional impact of illness and recovery.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CDC Guidelines: Refer to the latest Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for isolation and precautions for individuals with COVID-19.
  • WHO Recommendations: Consult World Health Organization (WHO) guidance on clinical management of COVID-19 and post-COVID conditions.
  • Oxygen Saturation Targets: Clinical guidelines typically recommend maintaining SpO2 ≥ 92-94% for COVID-19 patients, with specific targets individualized by physician.
  • Antiviral Therapy: If prescribed, reference specific drug information for antivirals (e.g., Paxlovid, Remdesivir) regarding dosage, administration, and potential drug interactions.
  • Post-Acute Sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC): Acknowledge the potential for "Long COVID" and the need for ongoing monitoring and multidisciplinary care for persistent symptoms.
