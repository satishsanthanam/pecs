Exposure to HIV or hepatitis through blood or body fluids

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Hepatitis

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition of Exposure: Contact with blood, semen, vaginal secretions, cerebrospinal fluid, synovial fluid, pleural fluid, peritoneal fluid, pericardial fluid, or amniotic fluid from an infected individual via percutaneous injury, mucous membrane contact, or non-intact skin.
  • Urgency of Action: Immediate assessment and intervention are crucial, especially for HIV post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP), which is most effective when started within hours of exposure.
  • Risk Assessment: The risk of transmission depends on the type of fluid, the route of exposure, and the viral load of the source individual.
  • Hepatitis B/C Risk: Hepatitis B and C can also be transmitted through similar exposures, requiring distinct post-exposure management strategies including vaccination and immunoglobulin for Hepatitis B.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Acute Retroviral Syndrome (HIV): If PEP is not initiated or fails, symptoms like fever, fatigue, rash, sore throat, swollen lymph nodes, and muscle aches may appear 2-4 weeks post-exposure.
  • Acute Hepatitis (B or C): Symptoms such as fatigue, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, dark urine, clay-colored stools, and jaundice can develop weeks to months after exposure, indicating potential seroconversion.
  • Anxiety and Stress: Psychological symptoms like severe anxiety, fear, and stress are common following potential exposure, necessitating empathetic support.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Wound Care: For skin exposures, wash the area thoroughly with soap and water. For mucous membrane exposures, flush with copious amounts of water or saline.
  • Prompt Medical Evaluation: Seek urgent medical attention from an occupational health service or emergency department for risk assessment and potential initiation of PEP.
  • Standard Precautions: Healthcare workers must consistently use personal protective equipment (PPE) including gloves, gowns, masks, and eye protection when anticipating contact with blood or body fluids.
  • Safe Injection Practices: Always use sterile needles and syringes, and dispose of sharps immediately in puncture-resistant containers. Never recap, bend, or break used needles.
  • Avoid Sharing: Do not share needles, syringes, razors, toothbrushes, or other personal items that may come into contact with blood.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration and Nutrition during PEP: Maintain adequate hydration and a balanced diet to support overall health and manage potential gastrointestinal side effects of antiretroviral medications.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Avoid alcohol consumption, especially if liver function is being monitored or if there is a risk of hepatitis, as alcohol can further stress the liver.
  • Medication Adherence: Follow specific dietary instructions for PEP medications (e.g., take with food to reduce nausea or on an empty stomach for optimal absorption).

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Time-Sensitive Intervention: HIV PEP must be initiated as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours and no later than 72 hours post-exposure, for maximum effectiveness.
  • Complete the Full Course: Adhere strictly to the prescribed 28-day course of PEP, even if side effects occur, to ensure optimal viral suppression.
  • Follow-up Testing: Regular follow-up testing for HIV and hepatitis is essential at specified intervals (e.g., 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months) to monitor for seroconversion.
  • Risk Reduction during Follow-up: During the follow-up period, avoid blood donation, unprotected sexual contact, and sharing needles to prevent potential secondary transmission.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Immediate Assessment is Paramount: "Any potential exposure to blood or body fluids warrants immediate medical evaluation to assess risk and determine the need for post-exposure prophylaxis."
  • Adherence to PEP: "Strict adherence to the prescribed PEP regimen is critical for its efficacy in preventing HIV infection. Do not discontinue without medical advice."
  • Comprehensive Counseling: "Patients require thorough counseling on the risks, benefits, side effects of PEP, and the importance of follow-up testing and safe practices."
  • Hepatitis Management: "For hepatitis exposures, ensure appropriate vaccination status, consider Hepatitis B immunoglobulin, and monitor for Hepatitis C seroconversion."

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Rapid Assessment & Triage: Nurses are often the first point of contact; quickly assess the nature of exposure, source status, and time elapsed to facilitate timely medical intervention.
  • Patient Education & Support: Provide clear, concise education on PEP, potential side effects, the importance of adherence, and follow-up schedules. Offer emotional support to alleviate anxiety.
  • Specimen Collection: Ensure timely and accurate collection of baseline blood samples from the exposed individual and, if possible, the source patient for HIV, Hepatitis B, and Hepatitis C testing.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document the exposure incident, interventions, medications administered, patient education provided, and all follow-up plans.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): A short-term antiretroviral treatment given to reduce the likelihood of HIV infection after potential exposure.
  • Seroconversion: The development of antibodies to a pathogen in the blood serum as a result of infection or immunization.
  • Window Period: The time between infection and when a diagnostic test can reliably detect the infection or antibodies.
  • Source Patient Testing: Diagnostic testing of the individual whose blood or body fluids were the source of exposure to determine their infection status.
  • Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG): An antibody preparation given post-exposure to provide passive immunity against Hepatitis B.
  • Standard Precautions: Infection control practices designed to prevent transmission of infectious agents from recognized and unrecognized sources.
