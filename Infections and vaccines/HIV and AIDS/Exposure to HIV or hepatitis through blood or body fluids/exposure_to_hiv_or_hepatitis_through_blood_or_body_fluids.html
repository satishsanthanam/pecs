Exposure to HIV or hepatitis through blood or body fluids

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / HIV and AIDS

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Immediate Action Required: Exposure to blood or body fluids potentially contaminated with HIV or hepatitis viruses necessitates urgent medical evaluation for post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).
  • Types of Exposure: This includes percutaneous injury (e.g., needle stick), contact with non-intact skin, or mucous membrane exposure (eyes, nose, mouth) to blood, semen, vaginal secretions, CSF, synovial, pleural, peritoneal, pericardial, or amniotic fluids.
  • Time-Sensitive Intervention: PEP for HIV is most effective when initiated within hours of exposure, ideally within 72 hours. Delay significantly reduces efficacy.
  • Risk Assessment: A healthcare professional will assess the risk based on the type of exposure, the volume of fluid, and the known or suspected HIV/hepatitis status of the source.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Acute Retroviral Syndrome (HIV): If seroconversion occurs, initial symptoms may include fever, fatigue, rash, sore throat, lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes), and muscle/joint aches, typically appearing 2-4 weeks post-exposure.
  • Acute Hepatitis (HBV/HCV): Symptoms can range from asymptomatic to severe and may include fatigue, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, dark urine, jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes), and light-colored stools.
  • Local Site Reaction: Minor symptoms at the exposure site, such as redness or tenderness, are common but not indicative of viral transmission.
  • Psychological Distress: Significant anxiety and stress are common following potential exposure, regardless of actual infection status.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • First Aid Immediately: For skin exposure, wash the area thoroughly with soap and water. For mucous membrane exposure, flush with copious amounts of water or saline. Do not squeeze or scrub the wound.
  • Report the Incident: Promptly report the exposure to your supervisor or designated occupational health service according to institutional protocols.
  • Seek Medical Evaluation: Obtain immediate medical assessment from a healthcare provider experienced in managing bloodborne pathogen exposures.
  • Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): If indicated, begin PEP as soon as possible. Adhere strictly to the prescribed regimen and follow-up schedule.
  • Universal Precautions: Always practice universal precautions, including appropriate use of personal protective equipment (PPE) such as gloves, gowns, masks, and eye protection, when handling blood or body fluids.
  • Safe Sharps Handling: Implement and adhere to safe injection practices and proper disposal of sharps to prevent percutaneous injuries.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially if experiencing side effects from PEP medications, such as nausea or diarrhea.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Consume a well-balanced diet to support overall immune function and help manage potential medication side effects.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Avoid alcohol consumption, particularly during PEP, as it can exacerbate potential liver-related side effects of some antiretroviral drugs and may complicate monitoring of liver function.
  • Avoid Grapefruit Products: Some antiretroviral medications can interact with grapefruit or grapefruit juice, potentially altering drug levels. Consult your pharmacist or physician.
  • Medication Adherence: Take PEP medications with or without food as directed by the prescribing physician to optimize absorption and minimize gastrointestinal upset.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Confidentiality: Ensure strict confidentiality regarding the exposed individual and the source patient's information.
  • Emotional Support: Provide empathetic support and reassurance to the exposed individual, acknowledging the stress and anxiety associated with the incident.
  • Facilitate Prompt Care: Expedite access to medical evaluation and PEP initiation within the critical time window.
  • Education on Adherence: Emphasize the importance of strict adherence to the PEP regimen and all follow-up appointments and testing.
  • Risk Reduction Counseling: Counsel the exposed individual on temporary measures to prevent potential secondary transmission during the follow-up period (e.g., safe sex practices, avoiding blood donation).

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Rapid Risk Assessment: A thorough and rapid assessment of the exposure type, source patient's status, and exposed individual's baseline serostatus is paramount.
  • Timely PEP Initiation: Prescribe appropriate PEP regimens promptly, considering local resistance patterns and patient comorbidities.
  • Comprehensive Counseling: Provide clear counseling on PEP efficacy, potential side effects, the importance of adherence, and risk reduction strategies.
  • Serial Serological Testing: Implement a robust schedule for follow-up HIV and hepatitis testing (e.g., at baseline, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months) to monitor for seroconversion.
  • Management of Side Effects: Proactively manage PEP-related side effects to improve adherence and patient comfort.
  • Psychological Support: Recognize and address the psychological impact of exposure, offering referrals for mental health support if needed.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Universal Precautions Mastery: Reinforce the critical importance of consistent application of universal precautions in all clinical settings.
  • Exposure Protocol Knowledge: Understand and be able to articulate the institutional protocol for managing bloodborne pathogen exposures.
  • PPE Competency: Demonstrate correct donning and doffing of personal protective equipment (PPE) to prevent self-contamination.
  • Patient Education Role: Educate patients and colleagues on safe sharps disposal and the risks associated with blood and body fluid exposure.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Ensure meticulous and accurate documentation of exposure incidents, interventions, and follow-up plans.
  • Emotional First Aid: Provide initial emotional support and guidance to exposed individuals, directing them to appropriate resources.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): A short-term antiretroviral treatment to reduce the likelihood of HIV infection after potential exposure.
  • Acute Retroviral Syndrome (ARS): The initial symptomatic phase of HIV infection, often resembling a flu-like illness.
  • Seroconversion: The period during which antibodies to a pathogen become detectable in the blood.
  • Window Period: The time between infection and when a diagnostic test can reliably detect the infection.
  • Occupational Exposure: Exposure occurring in the workplace, typically in healthcare settings.
  • Non-Occupational Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (nPEP): PEP administered after non-occupational exposures, such as sexual contact or injection drug use.
  • Viral Load Testing: Measures the amount of HIV RNA in the blood, used for diagnosis and monitoring.
  • Hepatitis B Surface Antigen (HBsAg): A marker indicating current Hepatitis B infection.
  • Hepatitis C RNA (HCV RNA): Detects the presence of the Hepatitis C virus itself, indicating active infection.
