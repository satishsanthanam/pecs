Sepsis in adults – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Sepsis

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Sepsis Recovery is a Process: Understand that recovery from sepsis can be prolonged and may involve physical, cognitive, and emotional challenges.
  • Post-Sepsis Syndrome (PSS): Be aware of potential long-term effects such as fatigue, muscle weakness, memory problems, and anxiety/depression.
  • Importance of Follow-Up: Adhere strictly to all scheduled follow-up appointments with your primary care physician and specialists to monitor recovery and manage any lingering issues.
  • Preventing Recurrence: Identify and manage chronic conditions that increase infection risk, and practice good hygiene to prevent new infections.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Signs of Worsening Infection: Watch for new or worsening fever, chills, increased pain, redness, swelling, or pus at any infection site.
  • Respiratory Distress: Seek immediate attention for shortness of breath, rapid breathing, or difficulty breathing.
  • Altered Mental Status: Report any new confusion, disorientation, extreme sleepiness, or difficulty waking up.
  • Circulatory Issues: Be vigilant for lightheadedness, dizziness, significantly decreased urine output, or cold/clammy skin.
  • New Weakness or Malaise: Report profound fatigue, generalized weakness, or a feeling of being "unwell" that is worse than expected during recovery.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, especially antibiotics, exactly as directed and complete the full course, even if you feel better.
  • Wound Care: If applicable, follow specific instructions for wound care to prevent secondary infections and promote healing.
  • Hygiene Practices: Maintain meticulous hand hygiene, especially before eating and after using the restroom, and avoid contact with sick individuals.
  • Activity Restrictions: Gradually increase activity levels as tolerated; avoid heavy lifting or strenuous exercise until cleared by your physician.
  • Vaccination Status: Discuss with your doctor about recommended vaccinations, such as influenza and pneumococcal vaccines, to prevent future infections.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths) unless otherwise restricted by your physician, to support kidney function and overall recovery.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein, fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to support tissue repair and immune function.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: If appetite is poor, try eating smaller, more frequent meals throughout the day to ensure adequate caloric and nutrient intake.
  • Alcohol Avoidance: Avoid alcohol consumption, especially while taking antibiotics or other medications that may interact or stress the liver.
  • Monitor Bowel Habits: Report any significant changes in bowel habits, such as severe diarrhea or constipation, which can be common post-antibiotic treatment.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Recognize Warning Signs: Caregivers should be educated on the specific signs and symptoms that warrant immediate medical attention for the patient.
  • Medication Management: Assist the patient in organizing and administering medications correctly, ensuring adherence to the prescribed schedule.
  • Support Follow-Up: Help schedule and ensure attendance at all follow-up appointments, providing transportation if needed.
  • Emotional Support: Provide emotional support and encourage open communication about any physical or psychological challenges the patient is experiencing.
  • Emergency Contact: Know the patient's healthcare team contacts and be prepared to call emergency services (e.g., 911 or local equivalent) if severe symptoms arise.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prioritize Follow-Up Care: Emphasize the critical importance of attending all scheduled post-discharge appointments to monitor recovery and address any complications.
  • Gradual Return to Activity: Advise patients on a phased approach to resuming daily activities and work, stressing that full recovery can take weeks to months.
  • Manage Expectations: Counsel patients and families that post-sepsis syndrome symptoms are common and may require ongoing management and support.
  • Open Communication: Encourage patients to report any new or worsening symptoms promptly to their healthcare provider without hesitation.
  • Preventative Measures: Reinforce the importance of infection prevention strategies, including appropriate vaccinations and chronic disease management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Discharge Education: Nurses must provide thorough, individualized discharge teaching, ensuring patient and caregiver comprehension of instructions.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Perform meticulous medication reconciliation, explaining each drug's purpose, dosage, frequency, and potential side effects.
  • Wound Care Demonstration: If applicable, provide hands-on demonstration and return demonstration for wound care techniques.
  • Symptom Management & Red Flags: Educate on specific post-sepsis symptoms to monitor and clear instructions on when to seek urgent medical care.
  • Resource Provision: Provide contact information for follow-up appointments, home health services, and support groups for sepsis survivors.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Post-Sepsis Syndrome (PSS) Management: Recognize and educate on the common manifestations of PSS, including physical, cognitive, and psychological sequelae, requiring multidisciplinary follow-up.
  • Long-Term Organ Dysfunction: Monitor for potential long-term organ dysfunction (e.g., renal, cardiac, pulmonary) that may persist or develop post-sepsis.
  • Readmission Risk Factors: Identify and mitigate common risk factors for readmission post-sepsis, such as incomplete antibiotic courses, inadequate follow-up, and new infections.
  • Rehabilitation Needs: Assess for and refer to appropriate rehabilitation services (physical, occupational, speech therapy) to address functional deficits.
  • Psychological Support: Screen for and address psychological impacts like PTSD, anxiety, and depression, referring to mental health professionals as needed.
