Mastoiditis

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Head and neck infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Mastoiditis is an infection and inflammation of the mastoid air cells, located in the mastoid bone behind the ear.
  • Origin: It typically develops as a complication of an untreated or inadequately treated acute middle ear infection (acute otitis media).
  • Severity: While rare, it is a serious condition that can lead to significant complications if not promptly diagnosed and managed.
  • Treatment: Requires urgent medical attention, often involving intravenous antibiotics and sometimes surgical intervention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Postauricular Pain: Severe, throbbing pain behind the ear, often worsening at night.
  • Swelling and Redness: Noticeable swelling, tenderness, and redness over the mastoid process.
  • Ear Protrusion: The affected ear may appear to be pushed forward and downward due to swelling.
  • Fever: Elevated body temperature, indicating a systemic infection.
  • Ear Discharge: Persistent or increased purulent discharge from the affected ear.
  • Hearing Loss: Conductive hearing loss in the affected ear.
  • Headache: May indicate spread of infection or increased intracranial pressure.
  • Lethargy/Irritability: Especially in children, indicating general malaise or worsening condition.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Prompt AOM Treatment: Ensure acute otitis media is diagnosed and treated effectively and completely to prevent progression.
  • Complete Antibiotic Courses: Always finish the full course of prescribed antibiotics for ear infections, even if symptoms improve.
  • Monitor Ear Symptoms: Be vigilant for persistent or worsening ear pain, discharge, or new swelling behind the ear.
  • Avoid Self-Medication: Do not attempt to treat ear infections or mastoiditis with home remedies; seek professional medical advice.
  • Watch for Complications: Be alert for signs of facial weakness, severe dizziness, stiff neck, or changes in consciousness, which require immediate medical evaluation.
  • Post-Surgical Care: If mastoidectomy is performed, meticulously follow all wound care instructions and activity restrictions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain excellent fluid intake, especially if fever is present, to prevent dehydration.
  • Soft Foods: Opt for soft, easy-to-chew foods if jaw movement causes pain or if there is post-surgical discomfort.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Consume a balanced diet rich in vitamins and minerals to support immune function during recovery.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit very hot, cold, or acidic foods if oral intake is sensitive due to systemic illness or post-operative discomfort.
  • Post-Anesthesia Guidelines: If surgery is performed, adhere strictly to the medical team's instructions for reintroducing fluids and light foods.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Care: Seek immediate medical attention for sudden worsening pain, high fever, stiff neck, altered mental status, or facial weakness.
  • Medication Adherence: Administer all prescribed antibiotics and pain medications exactly as directed by the physician.
  • Pain Management: Monitor the patient's pain levels and ensure appropriate analgesia is provided and effective.
  • Post-Operative Monitoring: After mastoidectomy, closely observe for signs of infection, excessive bleeding, cerebrospinal fluid leakage, or facial nerve dysfunction.
  • Activity Restrictions: Ensure the patient adheres to any activity restrictions, especially after surgery, to promote healing and prevent complications.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prompt Diagnosis: Clinical suspicion based on symptoms, confirmed by high-resolution CT scan of the temporal bone, is crucial.
  • Aggressive Treatment: Initial management typically involves intravenous broad-spectrum antibiotics, often requiring hospitalization.
  • Surgical Intervention: Mastoidectomy (cortical, modified radical, or radical) may be necessary for complicated cases, abscess formation, or failure of antibiotic therapy.
  • Complication Awareness: Counsel patients and families on potential intracranial complications such as meningitis, brain abscess, or sigmoid sinus thrombosis, and extracranial complications like facial nerve palsy.
  • Follow-up: Emphasize the importance of regular follow-up appointments to ensure complete resolution of infection and monitor for any long-term sequelae.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Perform thorough ear examination, assess for postauricular tenderness, swelling, erythema, and ear protrusion. Monitor vital signs, especially temperature.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics, assess pain intensity using appropriate scales, and evaluate the effectiveness of interventions.
  • Antibiotic Administration: Ensure correct dosage, route, and schedule for intravenous and oral antibiotics, monitoring for adverse reactions.
  • Wound Care: For post-mastoidectomy patients, perform meticulous dressing changes, assess the wound for signs of infection, and maintain sterility.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and caregivers on medication adherence, symptom monitoring, signs of complications, and post-operative care instructions.
  • Fluid Balance: Monitor intake and output, especially in febrile patients, to prevent dehydration and ensure adequate hydration.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Infection spreads from the middle ear to the mastoid air cells, leading to inflammation, osteitis, and potential bone destruction.
  • Common Pathogens: Predominantly bacterial, including *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (especially in chronic cases).
  • Diagnostic Imaging: High-resolution Computed Tomography (CT) scan of the temporal bone is the gold standard for confirming diagnosis and assessing disease extent.
  • Surgical Procedures: Mastoidectomy (cortical, modified radical, radical), myringotomy with tympanostomy tube insertion, and drainage of subperiosteal abscess.
  • Potential Complications: Subperiosteal abscess, Bezold's abscess, facial nerve palsy, labyrinthitis, meningitis, brain abscess, sigmoid sinus thrombosis, and epidural abscess.
  • Antibiotic Therapy: Empiric broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics (e.g., ceftriaxone, vancomycin, piperacillin-tazobactam) are typically initiated, with adjustments based on culture and sensitivity results.
