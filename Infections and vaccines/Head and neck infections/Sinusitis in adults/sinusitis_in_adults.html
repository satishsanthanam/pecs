Sinusitis in adults

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Head and neck infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Sinusitis is the inflammation of the paranasal sinuses, air-filled cavities located in the bones around the nose.
  • Types: Classified by duration as acute (less than 4 weeks), subacute (4-12 weeks), or chronic (more than 12 weeks).
  • Common Causes: Most frequently viral infections (e.g., common cold), but can also be bacterial, fungal, or triggered by allergies and structural issues.
  • Risk Factors: Include allergic rhinitis, asthma, nasal polyps, deviated nasal septum, smoking, and compromised immune systems.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Facial Pain and Pressure: Often felt around the eyes, cheeks, forehead, or between the eyes.
  • Nasal Congestion: Difficulty breathing through the nose due to blocked nasal passages.
  • Discolored Nasal Discharge: Thick, yellow, green, or cloudy mucus.
  • Postnasal Drip: Mucus dripping down the back of the throat, often leading to a cough.
  • Headache: Can be a dull ache or severe pressure, particularly in the front of the head.
  • Reduced Sense of Smell or Taste: Impaired olfaction (anosmia) or gustation.
  • Cough: Frequently worse at night or upon waking due to postnasal drip.
  • Fever: More common in acute bacterial sinusitis.
  • Fatigue: General feeling of tiredness and malaise.
  • Dental Pain: Pain in the upper jaw and teeth, often mistaken for a dental issue.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids to help thin mucus and promote drainage.
  • Use Humidifiers: Keep nasal passages moist, especially in dry environments, to soothe irritation.
  • Perform Nasal Irrigation: Regular saline rinses can help clear mucus and irritants from sinus passages.
  • Avoid Irritants: Steer clear of cigarette smoke, strong chemical fumes, and known allergens.
  • Practice Hand Hygiene: Frequent hand washing helps prevent the spread of viral infections that can lead to sinusitis.
  • Manage Allergies: Effectively control underlying allergies to reduce inflammation and susceptibility.
  • Avoid Pressure Changes: Refrain from diving or flying during acute sinusitis episodes to prevent increased pain and complications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Increase Fluid Intake: Focus on water, clear broths, and herbal teas to aid in mucus thinning and hydration.
  • Limit Dehydrating Beverages: Reduce consumption of excessive caffeine and alcohol, which can contribute to dehydration.
  • Consume Warm Fluids: Warm drinks can provide soothing relief for a sore throat and help loosen mucus.
  • Monitor Dairy Intake: Some individuals report dairy products can thicken mucus; observe personal response and adjust as needed.
  • Prioritize Nutrient-Rich Foods: Incorporate fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins to support overall immune function.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Seek Medical Attention: Consult a physician if symptoms persist beyond 10 days, worsen, or include high fever, severe headache, or vision changes.
  • Antibiotic Stewardship: Use antibiotics only when prescribed by a healthcare provider for bacterial sinusitis; self-medication is strongly discouraged.
  • Decongestant Spray Use: Limit topical decongestant nasal sprays to 3-5 days to avoid rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa).
  • Recognize Complications: Be aware that untreated bacterial sinusitis can rarely lead to serious complications such as orbital cellulitis, meningitis, or brain abscess.
  • Chronic Sinusitis Management: Persistent or recurrent symptoms warrant referral to an Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT) specialist for comprehensive evaluation and management.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: Differentiating between viral and bacterial sinusitis is crucial for appropriate treatment selection.
  • Symptomatic Relief: Recommend a combination of saline nasal rinses, analgesics, and potentially oral or topical decongestants.
  • Judicious Antibiotic Use: Emphasize the importance of prescribing antibiotics only when a bacterial etiology is strongly suspected, to combat antimicrobial resistance.
  • Referral Considerations: Evaluate patients for ENT referral in cases of chronic, recurrent, or complicated sinusitis, or when structural abnormalities are suspected.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear instructions on self-care measures, medication adherence, and warning signs that necessitate further medical evaluation.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct thorough history taking, including symptom onset, duration, character of discharge, and pain localization, alongside a physical examination.
  • Patient Education: Instruct patients on the correct technique for nasal irrigation, proper use of prescribed nasal sprays, and the importance of completing antibiotic courses.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Vigilantly observe for signs of worsening infection, such as periorbital edema, severe localized pain, or neurological changes.
  • Comfort Measures: Advise on non-pharmacological interventions like humidification, adequate rest, and pain management strategies.
  • Interprofessional Collaboration: Facilitate communication with physicians, pharmacists, and specialists to ensure coordinated and effective patient care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Involves inflammation of the sinus mucosa, leading to ostial obstruction and impaired mucociliary clearance, creating an environment for microbial growth.
  • Common Pathogens: Viral (Rhinovirus, Influenza virus) are most common. Bacterial include *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*. Fungal sinusitis is less common.
  • Diagnostic Tools: Primarily clinical diagnosis. Endoscopy and CT scans of the paranasal sinuses are reserved for chronic, recurrent, or complicated cases.
  • Treatment Modalities: May include analgesics, decongestants, intranasal corticosteroids, antibiotics (for bacterial), and in some chronic cases, functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS).
  • ICD-10 Codes: J01.x for Acute sinusitis (e.g., J01.00 Acute maxillary sinusitis, unspecified), J32.x for Chronic sinusitis (e.g., J32.0 Chronic maxillary sinusitis).
