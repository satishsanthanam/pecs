Molluscum contagiosum

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Skin infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Contagious Viral Skin Infection: Caused by the molluscum contagiosum virus (MCV), a DNA poxvirus belonging to the Poxviridae family.
  • Characteristic Lesions: Presents as small, firm, dome-shaped papules with a distinctive central umbilication.
  • Transmission Routes: Primarily spreads through direct skin-to-skin contact, shared fomites (e.g., towels, clothing), and autoinoculation (self-spread by scratching).
  • Affected Populations: Most common in children, sexually active adults (often in the genital area), and individuals with compromised immune systems.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Pearly Papules: Small, flesh-colored, pink, or white bumps, typically 2-5 mm in diameter, often appearing in clusters.
  • Central Dimple: A characteristic umbilicated (indented) center, which may contain a white, waxy core.
  • Pruritus and Inflammation: Itching may occur, leading to scratching, which can cause inflammation, secondary bacterial infection, or further spread.
  • Common Locations: Lesions frequently appear on the trunk, face, extremities, and genital area, generally sparing the palms and soles.
  • Spread and Persistence: Lesions can spread to other body parts through scratching, shaving, or close contact, and may persist for months to years.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Scratching or Picking: Prevents autoinoculation (spreading to other body parts) and reduces the risk of secondary bacterial infections.
  • Strict Hand Hygiene: Frequent and thorough hand washing, especially after touching any lesions, is essential to prevent transmission.
  • Do Not Share Personal Items: Avoid sharing towels, clothing, razors, bath sponges, or other personal items that may come into contact with lesions.
  • Cover Visible Lesions: Cover lesions with clothing or waterproof bandages, especially during activities involving skin-to-skin contact (e.g., swimming, contact sports) to minimize spread.
  • Sexual Contact Considerations: In adults, avoid sexual contact if lesions are present in the genital or perianal areas to prevent sexual transmission.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Support Immune System: Encourage a balanced diet rich in vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants (found in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains) to bolster the body's natural immune response against viral infections.
  • Adequate Hydration: Ensure sufficient daily fluid intake to maintain overall systemic health and support skin barrier function.
  • Nutrient-Dense Foods: Prioritize lean proteins and healthy fats to provide essential building blocks for tissue repair and immune cell function.
  • Avoid Excessive Sugars/Processed Foods: While not a direct treatment, minimizing intake of highly processed foods and excessive sugars can generally support a healthier inflammatory response and overall well-being.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Consult Healthcare Provider: Seek medical evaluation for accurate diagnosis and to discuss appropriate treatment options, especially if lesions are widespread, persistent, or located in sensitive areas.
  • Monitor for Secondary Infection: Watch for signs of bacterial superinfection, such as increased redness, warmth, swelling, pain, or purulent discharge from the lesions.
  • Treatment Expectations: Be aware that various treatment modalities exist (e.g., cryotherapy, curettage, topical agents), and discuss the most suitable approach with a clinician, considering patient age and lesion characteristics.
  • Self-Limiting Nature: Inform patients that molluscum contagiosum is typically self-limiting and often resolves spontaneously within 6 to 18 months, though it can sometimes take longer.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Clinical Diagnosis: Diagnosis is usually clinical based on the characteristic appearance of the lesions; dermoscopy or biopsy can confirm atypical presentations.
  • Individualized Treatment Plan: Treatment decisions should be individualized, considering the patient's age, immune status, lesion location and number, potential for spread, and cosmetic concerns.
  • Patient and Caregiver Education: Emphasize the benign nature of the condition and provide comprehensive education on transmission prevention and hygiene practices.
  • Immunocompromised Patient Management: Be vigilant for more extensive, persistent, or atypical presentations in immunocompromised individuals, which may require more aggressive or prolonged management strategies.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Infection Control Protocols: Implement strict infection control measures in clinical settings, including proper hand hygiene and disinfection of shared equipment, to prevent nosocomial transmission.
  • Patient Counseling on Prevention: Educate patients and caregivers on effective strategies to prevent spread, such as covering lesions, avoiding scratching, and not sharing personal items.
  • Topical Medication Application: Provide clear instructions on the correct application techniques for any prescribed topical medications, ensuring patient adherence and safety.
  • Post-Procedure Care: Offer guidance on wound care following lesion removal procedures (e.g., cryotherapy, curettage) to promote healing and prevent complications.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: Caused by the Molluscum Contagiosum Virus (MCV), a large, double-stranded DNA virus of the Poxviridae family.
  • Histopathology: Characterized by epidermal hyperplasia with pathognomonic molluscum bodies (Henderson-Paterson bodies), which are large eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusions within keratinocytes.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Key considerations include warts (verruca vulgaris), herpes simplex virus infection, varicella, basal cell carcinoma, and cryptococcosis (especially in immunocompromised patients).
  • ICD-10 Code: B08.1 (Molluscum contagiosum).
