Paronychia

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Skin infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Paronychia is an inflammation of the nail fold, the skin and soft tissue surrounding the fingernail or toenail.
  • Types: It can be acute, typically caused by bacterial infection (often *Staphylococcus aureus*), or chronic, often due to fungal infection (*Candida*) or irritant exposure.
  • Common Causes: Trauma to the nail fold from nail biting, aggressive manicures, frequent water exposure, ingrown nails, or thumb sucking.
  • Risk Factors: Individuals with diabetes, compromised immune systems, or those whose hands are frequently wet are at higher risk.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Acute Paronychia: Characterized by rapid onset of redness, swelling, and throbbing pain around the nail. Pus formation may be visible beneath the nail fold.
  • Chronic Paronychia: Presents with less severe inflammation, tenderness, and often involves retraction of the cuticle, leading to a gap where moisture and irritants can enter. Nail dystrophy, such as discoloration or ridging, may also occur.
  • Systemic Signs: In rare cases of severe infection, fever, chills, or red streaking up the limb (lymphangitis) may indicate spreading infection.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Maintain Hand Hygiene: Keep hands and feet clean and dry, especially after washing or exposure to water.
  • Avoid Nail Trauma: Refrain from nail biting, picking at cuticles, or pushing cuticles back too aggressively during manicures.
  • Protective Measures: Wear waterproof gloves when performing tasks that involve prolonged water exposure or contact with harsh chemicals.
  • Early Intervention: Seek medical attention promptly for any persistent redness, swelling, or pain around the nails to prevent progression.
  • Diabetic Foot/Hand Care: Patients with diabetes must exercise extreme vigilance with nail care and inspect digits daily for any signs of infection.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Immune System Support: Ensure adequate intake of vitamins and minerals, particularly Vitamin C and Zinc, which are crucial for immune function and wound healing.
  • Hydration: Maintain good overall hydration by drinking sufficient water throughout the day to support general health and tissue repair.
  • Blood Sugar Management: For individuals with diabetes, strict control of blood glucose levels is paramount to enhance immune response and facilitate healing of any skin infections.
  • Anti-inflammatory Foods: Incorporate foods rich in omega-3 fatty acids (e.g., fatty fish, flaxseeds) and antioxidants (e.g., berries, leafy greens) to support the body's natural anti-inflammatory processes.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • When to Seek Medical Care: Consult a healthcare provider if symptoms worsen, pus develops, pain becomes severe, or if there are signs of spreading infection like fever or red streaks.
  • Avoid Self-Draining: Do not attempt to drain pus or cut the affected area at home, as this can worsen the infection or introduce new pathogens.
  • Complete Treatment Course: If antibiotics or antifungals are prescribed, complete the entire course as directed, even if symptoms improve, to prevent recurrence or resistance.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for signs of cellulitis (spreading skin infection) or osteomyelitis (bone infection), especially in immunocompromised individuals.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnosis: Paronychia is typically diagnosed clinically based on the characteristic appearance of inflammation around the nail. Cultures may be taken if pus is present or if the infection is severe/recurrent.
  • Treatment Strategy: Acute bacterial paronychia often responds to warm soaks, topical antibiotics, or oral antibiotics (e.g., dicloxacillin, cephalexin). Incision and drainage may be necessary for abscess formation. Chronic paronychia usually requires topical or oral antifungals (e.g., fluconazole) and avoidance of predisposing factors.
  • Patient Education: Emphasize proper nail care techniques, the importance of keeping hands dry, and the avoidance of irritants to prevent recurrence.
  • Addressing Underlying Conditions: Evaluate and manage any contributing factors such as diabetes, eczema, or occupational exposures that may predispose patients to paronychia.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Assessment: Thoroughly assess the affected digit for redness, swelling, warmth, pain, and presence of pus. Document the extent of inflammation and patient's pain level.
  • Interventions: Educate patients on warm soaks (e.g., 15 minutes, 3-4 times daily) to promote drainage. Administer prescribed topical or oral medications and provide wound care as needed.
  • Patient Teaching: Instruct patients on proper hand hygiene, avoiding nail trauma, and the importance of wearing protective gloves for wet work. Teach them to recognize signs of worsening infection.
  • Complication Monitoring: Monitor for signs of spreading infection (cellulitis, lymphangitis) or systemic involvement, especially in vulnerable populations.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: L03.011 (Cellulitis of finger), L03.012 (Cellulitis of toe), L03.019 (Cellulitis of unspecified digit). For chronic forms, B35.1 (Tinea unguium) or B37.2 (Candidiasis of skin and nail) may be relevant if fungal.
  • Common Pathogens: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus pyogenes* (acute bacterial); *Candida albicans* (chronic fungal).
  • Pharmacological Agents: Topical: Mupirocin, Fusidic acid (antibacterial); Clotrimazole, Nystatin (antifungal). Oral: Dicloxacillin, Cephalexin (antibacterial); Fluconazole, Itraconazole (antifungal).
  • Procedures: Incision and Drainage (I&D) for abscesses, partial or complete nail avulsion in severe cases.
