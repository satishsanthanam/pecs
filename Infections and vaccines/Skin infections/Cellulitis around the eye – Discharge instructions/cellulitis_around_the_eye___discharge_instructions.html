Cellulitis around the eye – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Skin infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Periorbital Cellulitis: This is a bacterial infection of the eyelid and skin around the eye, anterior to the orbital septum. It requires prompt and complete treatment to prevent serious complications.
  • Importance of Adherence: Strictly follow all prescribed medication regimens and follow-up instructions to ensure full recovery and prevent the infection from spreading deeper into the eye socket.
  • Potential for Progression: Untreated or inadequately treated periorbital cellulitis can progress to orbital cellulitis, a more severe infection behind the orbital septum, which can lead to vision loss or other life-threatening complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Worsening Redness and Swelling: Increased erythema or edema around the eye, especially if it extends beyond the initial area.
  • Increased Pain: Persistent or worsening pain around the eye, particularly with eye movement.
  • Fever or Chills: Development of a new fever (temperature >100.4°F or 38°C) or chills, indicating systemic infection.
  • Vision Changes: Any blurred vision, double vision, decreased visual acuity, or pain with eye movement.
  • Proptosis (Bulging Eye): If the affected eye starts to bulge forward, this is a critical sign of orbital involvement.
  • Inability to Move Eye: Difficulty or inability to move the eye in any direction (ophthalmoplegia).
  • New Headache or Confusion: Signs that the infection may be spreading to the central nervous system.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Complete Antibiotic Course: Take the full course of prescribed oral antibiotics, even if symptoms improve, to eradicate the infection completely. Do not stop early.
  • Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before and after applying any eye drops or ointments, and avoid touching or rubbing the affected eye.
  • Avoid Eye Makeup/Contact Lenses: Do not use eye makeup or wear contact lenses until the infection has fully resolved and your doctor approves.
  • Warm Compresses: Apply warm, moist compresses to the affected area for 10-15 minutes, several times a day, as directed by your physician, to help reduce swelling and discomfort.
  • Elevate Head: Sleeping with the head slightly elevated can help reduce swelling around the eye.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor treatment effectiveness and ensure complete resolution of the infection.
  • Emergency Care: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience any of the worsening symptoms listed above.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good hydration by drinking plenty of fluids (water, clear broths) to support overall recovery and medication absorption.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol, as it can interact with certain antibiotics and potentially hinder the body's immune response.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins to support immune function and healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Symptom Monitoring: Closely observe the patient for any signs of worsening infection, such as increased redness, swelling, pain, fever, or changes in vision.
  • Medication Adherence Support: Ensure the patient takes all prescribed medications exactly as directed, including the full course of antibiotics.
  • Hygiene Assistance: Assist with maintaining strict hand hygiene and remind the patient not to touch or rub the affected eye.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know when to seek immediate medical attention and have transportation ready for urgent care if needed.
  • Follow-up Coordination: Help schedule and ensure attendance at all necessary follow-up appointments.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Serious Condition: Periorbital cellulitis, while often manageable with oral antibiotics, is a serious infection that warrants close monitoring due to its proximity to the brain and eye.
  • Complete Treatment is Key: Emphasize the critical importance of completing the entire course of antibiotics to prevent recurrence or progression to more severe orbital cellulitis, which can lead to permanent vision impairment or intracranial complications.
  • Early Intervention: Reiterate that early recognition of worsening symptoms and prompt return for re-evaluation is crucial for preventing adverse outcomes.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Consider other causes of periorbital swelling, such as allergic reactions or insect bites, but treat suspected cellulitis aggressively.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients and caregivers thoroughly on medication administration, potential side effects, signs of worsening infection, and the importance of follow-up.
  • Assessment & Documentation: Regularly assess the affected eye for changes in redness, swelling, tenderness, visual acuity, extraocular movements, and pupillary response. Document findings meticulously.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics and educate on non-pharmacological pain relief methods (e.g., warm compresses).
  • Infection Control: Reinforce strict hand hygiene and appropriate wound care if any skin breakdown is present.
  • Discharge Planning: Ensure clear discharge instructions are provided verbally and in writing, including contact information for concerns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Definition: Preseptal (periorbital) cellulitis is an infection of the eyelids and periorbital tissues anterior to the orbital septum.
  • Common Pathogens: Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pyogenes, Haemophilus influenzae (less common now due to Hib vaccine).
  • Differential Diagnoses: Orbital cellulitis, allergic reaction, insect bite, dacryocystitis, chalazion, hordeolum, herpes zoster ophthalmicus.
  • Complications: Orbital cellulitis, subperiosteal abscess, orbital abscess, cavernous sinus thrombosis, meningitis, vision loss.
  • Treatment Principles: Oral antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin-clavulanate, cephalexin, clindamycin) for mild cases; IV antibiotics for severe cases, systemic symptoms, or progression.
  • Diagnostic Tools: Clinical examination, complete blood count (CBC), blood cultures, CT scan of orbits (if orbital cellulitis suspected).
