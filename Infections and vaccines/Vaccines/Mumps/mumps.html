Mumps

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Vaccines

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Mumps Virus: Highly contagious viral infection caused by the mumps rubulavirus.
  • Transmission: Spreads through respiratory droplets from an infected person's coughs, sneezes, or talking.
  • Incubation Period: Typically 16-18 days, ranging from 12-25 days post-exposure.
  • Contagious Period: Most contagious 1-2 days before and up to 5 days after parotid gland swelling onset.
  • Prevention: Primarily prevented by the Measles, Mumps, and Rubella (MMR) vaccine.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Parotitis: Swelling and tenderness of one or both parotid salivary glands, often the most characteristic symptom.
  • Fever: Low-grade fever, typically preceding parotid swelling.
  • Headache: Common complaint, often accompanying the fever.
  • Muscle Aches: Generalized body aches and malaise.
  • Fatigue: Feeling of tiredness and lack of energy.
  • Loss of Appetite: Reduced desire to eat.
  • Orchitis: Testicular inflammation in post-pubertal males, a potential complication.
  • Oophoritis: Ovarian inflammation in post-pubertal females, less common than orchitis.
  • Meningitis: Aseptic meningitis can occur, presenting with headache, stiff neck, and photophobia.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Vaccination: Ensure up-to-date MMR vaccination for individuals and close contacts, especially in outbreak settings.
  • Isolation: Isolate infected individuals for at least 5 days after the onset of parotid swelling to prevent transmission.
  • Hand Hygiene: Frequent and thorough handwashing with soap and water or alcohol-based hand sanitizer.
  • Respiratory Etiquette: Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or elbow.
  • Avoid Sharing: Do not share eating utensils, cups, or personal items with an infected person.
  • Contact Tracing: Identify and monitor close contacts of confirmed cases for symptom development.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Soft Foods: Offer soft, easy-to-chew foods due to painful chewing and swallowing from parotid swelling.
  • Hydration: Encourage ample fluid intake (water, clear broths, diluted juices) to prevent dehydration, especially with fever.
  • Avoid Acidic Foods: Restrict acidic foods and beverages (e.g., citrus fruits, vinegar-based dressings) that can stimulate salivary glands and exacerbate pain.
  • Avoid Chewy/Crunchy Foods: Foods requiring extensive chewing can increase discomfort.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: May be better tolerated than large meals.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor Complications: Watch for signs of severe complications like orchitis (severe testicular pain/swelling), meningitis (severe headache, stiff neck, altered mental status), or pancreatitis (severe abdominal pain).
  • Pain Management: Administer analgesics (e.g., acetaminophen, ibuprofen) as prescribed for fever and pain.
  • Supportive Care: Provide supportive care, including rest and hydration.
  • Isolation Adherence: Strictly enforce isolation protocols in healthcare settings and at home.
  • Reporting: Report suspected or confirmed cases to public health authorities as per local guidelines.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Diagnosis: Mumps is primarily diagnosed clinically based on parotitis, but laboratory confirmation (RT-PCR from buccal swab, serology) is crucial for public health surveillance.
  • No Specific Antiviral: Treatment is supportive, focusing on symptom relief and preventing complications.
  • Vaccine Efficacy: Emphasize the high efficacy of the MMR vaccine in preventing mumps and its complications.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Consider other causes of parotitis (e.g., bacterial parotitis, parainfluenza virus, HIV, Sjogren's syndrome).
  • Complication Management: Promptly address complications like orchitis (scrotal support, ice packs) and meningitis (supportive care).

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on transmission, symptoms, isolation, and the importance of vaccination.
  • Vaccine Administration: Understand proper MMR vaccine administration, contraindications (e.g., severe allergic reaction to previous dose, pregnancy, severe immunocompromise), and storage.
  • Symptom Assessment: Accurately assess and document parotid swelling, fever, and other associated symptoms.
  • Infection Control: Implement strict infection control measures in clinical settings to prevent nosocomial spread.
  • Community Health Role: Recognize the nurse's role in community health, including outbreak investigation and vaccination campaigns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: Mumps rubulavirus, an RNA virus of the Paramyxoviridae family.
  • Pathogenesis: Virus replicates in the upper respiratory tract, then spreads via viremia to salivary glands (parotid), testes, ovaries, pancreas, and central nervous system.
  • Diagnostic Codes: ICD-10 codes for mumps (B26.-).
  • Laboratory Confirmation: RT-PCR from buccal swab or urine, IgM antibodies in serum, or a fourfold rise in IgG antibody titers.
  • Vaccine Schedule: MMR vaccine typically given at 12-15 months and a second dose at 4-6 years of age.
  • Public Health Surveillance: Notifiable disease in many regions, requiring prompt reporting to public health authorities.
