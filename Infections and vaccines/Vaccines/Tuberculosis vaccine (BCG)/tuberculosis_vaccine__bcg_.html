Tuberculosis vaccine (BCG)

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Vaccines

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of BCG: The Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine primarily protects infants and young children against severe forms of tuberculosis (TB), such as TB meningitis and disseminated TB.
  • Efficacy Limitations: While effective in preventing severe childhood TB, BCG offers variable and often limited protection against pulmonary TB in adolescents and adults.
  • Global Use: It is widely used in countries with a high burden of TB, typically administered as a single dose at birth or early infancy.
  • Live Attenuated Vaccine: BCG contains a live, attenuated (weakened) strain of Mycobacterium bovis, which stimulates an immune response without causing disease.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Local Injection Site Reaction: A normal reaction includes the development of a papule at the injection site, which typically progresses to an ulcer and then heals with a characteristic scar over several weeks to months.
  • Regional Lymphadenitis: Swelling of lymph nodes in the armpit (axillary lymphadenitis) on the same side as the injection is a common, usually self-limiting, side effect.
  • Keloid Formation: In some individuals, particularly those with a genetic predisposition, the BCG scar may develop into a raised, thickened keloid.
  • Indications for Vaccination: The vaccine is indicated for infants and young children living in areas with high TB prevalence or those at high risk of exposure to TB.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immunocompromised Individuals: BCG is contraindicated in individuals with compromised immune systems, including those with HIV, undergoing chemotherapy, or receiving high-dose systemic corticosteroids.
  • Pregnancy: Vaccination is generally not recommended during pregnancy due to its live attenuated nature, though risks to the fetus are theoretical.
  • Severe Illness: Administration should be deferred in individuals with acute severe febrile illness until recovery.
  • Generalized Skin Conditions: Avoid vaccination in individuals with extensive skin infections or generalized dermatological conditions.
  • Tuberculin Skin Test (TST) Interference: BCG vaccination can cause a false-positive reaction to the TST, complicating future TB screening.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • No Specific Dietary Restrictions: There are no specific dietary directions or restrictions directly associated with the BCG vaccine administration.
  • Maintain Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially for infants, to support overall well-being and manage any mild fever that might occur post-vaccination.
  • Nutritional Support: Encourage age-appropriate, balanced nutrition to support a robust immune response and general health, particularly in infants and young children.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Injection Site Care: Keep the injection site clean and dry. Do not apply plasters, bandages, or antiseptic creams, as this can interfere with the normal healing process.
  • Expected Healing Process: Inform caregivers that the development of a papule, ulcer, and subsequent scar is a normal and expected reaction to the vaccine.
  • Avoid Manipulation: Advise against squeezing, rubbing, or attempting to drain the injection site lesion.
  • When to Seek Medical Advice: Caregivers should seek medical attention if the injection site becomes excessively swollen, painful, or if there is persistent pus discharge, or if the child develops a high fever or other concerning symptoms.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Risk-Benefit Assessment: Physicians must weigh the benefits of preventing severe childhood TB against the potential risks and contraindications, especially in specific patient populations.
  • Patient Education: Thoroughly educate parents/caregivers about the expected local reaction, the importance of the vaccine, and when to seek medical advice for adverse events.
  • Screening Considerations: In older children or adults, consider prior TB exposure or infection status (e.g., TST or IGRA) before administering BCG, as it is generally not recommended for those already infected.
  • Monitoring for Adverse Events: Clinicians should be vigilant for rare but serious adverse events such as disseminated BCG infection (BCGitis) or osteitis, particularly in immunocompromised infants.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Correct Administration Technique: BCG must be administered strictly intradermally, typically into the deltoid region of the upper arm, using a specific technique to ensure proper immune response and minimize adverse reactions.
  • Cold Chain Management: As a live attenuated vaccine, BCG requires strict adherence to cold chain protocols for storage and transport to maintain its potency.
  • Parental Counseling: Nurses play a crucial role in counseling parents about the normal course of the BCG reaction, proper site care, and signs requiring medical attention.
  • Documentation: Meticulous documentation of vaccine lot number, administration date, site, and any observed reactions is essential for patient safety and public health surveillance.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Vaccine Type: Live attenuated vaccine derived from a strain of Mycobacterium bovis.
  • Immunological Mechanism: Induces cell-mediated immunity, primarily T-cell responses, which are crucial for protection against mycobacterial infections.
  • WHO Recommendations: The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends BCG vaccination for all infants in countries with a high incidence of TB or high risk of exposure.
  • Adverse Event Reporting: Healthcare professionals are encouraged to report any significant or unusual adverse events following BCG vaccination to national pharmacovigilance systems.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Differentiate normal BCG reactions from atypical mycobacterial infections or other skin lesions that may occur at the injection site.
