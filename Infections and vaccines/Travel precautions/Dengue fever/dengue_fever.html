Dengue fever

🏠 Home / Infections and vaccines / Travel precautions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Mosquito-Borne Viral Infection: Dengue fever is caused by a virus transmitted primarily by the *Aedes aegypti* mosquito, and to a lesser extent, *Aedes albopictus*.
  • Global Prevalence: Endemic in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, posing a significant risk to travelers.
  • Four Serotypes: There are four distinct serotypes (DENV-1, -2, -3, -4); infection with one provides lifelong immunity to that serotype but only temporary protection against others. Subsequent infections with different serotypes increase the risk of severe dengue.
  • No Specific Treatment: Management is supportive, focusing on symptom relief and fluid balance.
  • Risk of Severe Dengue: A small percentage of cases can progress to severe dengue (dengue hemorrhagic fever or dengue shock syndrome), which can be life-threatening.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • High Fever: Sudden onset of high fever (40°C/104°F) lasting 2-7 days.
  • Severe Headache: Often accompanied by pain behind the eyes (retro-orbital pain).
  • Muscle and Joint Pain: Intense body aches, leading to its nickname 'breakbone fever'.
  • Rash: A characteristic skin rash may appear 2-5 days after fever onset.
  • Nausea and Vomiting: Common gastrointestinal symptoms.
  • Warning Signs of Severe Dengue: Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, rapid breathing, bleeding gums or nose, fatigue, restlessness, blood in vomit or stool, and cold, clammy extremities. These require immediate medical attention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Mosquito Bite Prevention: Use insect repellents containing DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus on exposed skin.
  • Protective Clothing: Wear long-sleeved shirts, long pants, and socks, especially during peak mosquito biting hours (dawn and dusk, but *Aedes* mosquitoes bite throughout the day).
  • Secure Accommodations: Stay in screened or air-conditioned rooms, or use mosquito nets if sleeping in unscreened areas.
  • Eliminate Breeding Sites: Regularly empty and clean containers that can hold standing water (e.g., flower pots, tires, buckets) around homes and accommodations to prevent mosquito breeding.
  • Travel Advisories: Consult travel health advisories (e.g., CDC, WHO) before traveling to dengue-endemic areas.
  • Dengue Vaccine (Dengvaxia): Currently recommended only for individuals aged 9-45 years with laboratory-confirmed prior dengue infection living in endemic areas. It is not a universal travel vaccine.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Crucial for patients with dengue fever to prevent dehydration. Encourage frequent intake of oral rehydration solutions, water, clear broths, and fruit juices.
  • Avoid Dehydrating Beverages: Limit or avoid caffeinated drinks and sugary sodas, which can contribute to dehydration.
  • Easily Digestible Foods: During the febrile phase, offer bland, easily digestible foods like porridge, rice, and soft fruits to manage nausea and vomiting.
  • Electrolyte Balance: Monitor and maintain electrolyte balance, especially if experiencing persistent vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Avoid NSAIDs: Do not consume alcohol or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like aspirin or ibuprofen, as they can increase the risk of bleeding.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Warning Signs: Vigilantly observe for any signs of severe dengue (e.g., severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, lethargy) and seek immediate medical care.
  • Fever Management: Administer acetaminophen (paracetamol) for fever and pain relief. Strictly avoid aspirin, ibuprofen, and other NSAIDs due to increased bleeding risk.
  • Ensure Adequate Rest: Encourage complete bed rest to aid recovery.
  • Fluid Intake: Actively encourage and monitor fluid intake to prevent dehydration.
  • Avoid Self-Medication: Do not use antibiotics or other medications without a physician's prescription, as dengue is a viral infection.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis and Monitoring: Emphasize the importance of early diagnosis and close monitoring of patients, especially during the critical phase (typically 3-7 days after fever onset), for signs of plasma leakage and hemorrhage.
  • Fluid Management: Aggressive but judicious fluid management is the cornerstone of severe dengue treatment, balancing hydration with avoiding fluid overload.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Consider dengue in patients presenting with acute febrile illness in endemic areas, differentiating it from other viral or bacterial infections.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and their families on warning signs, appropriate fever management, and mosquito bite prevention strategies.
  • Vector Control: Advocate for community-wide vector control measures to reduce mosquito populations and disease transmission.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct thorough assessments including vital signs, fluid balance (intake/output), skin assessment for rash or petechiae, and neurological status.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on the importance of hydration, recognizing warning signs, and adhering to medication schedules (e.g., acetaminophen).
  • Fluid Management Protocol: Implement and monitor intravenous fluid therapy as prescribed, carefully observing for signs of fluid overload or continued dehydration.
  • Bleeding Precautions: Monitor for any signs of bleeding (e.g., epistaxis, gum bleeding, petechiae, melena) and report promptly.
  • Psychological Support: Provide emotional support to patients and families, especially during the critical phase, due to the anxiety associated with the illness.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: Flavivirus genus, family Flaviviridae, with four distinct serotypes (DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, DENV-4).
  • Transmission: Primarily through the bite of infected female *Aedes aegypti* mosquitoes; *Aedes albopictus* is a secondary vector.
  • Diagnosis: Confirmed by laboratory tests including RT-PCR (early infection), NS1 antigen detection (early infection), and IgM/IgG antibody detection (later infection).
  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by increased vascular permeability leading to plasma leakage, thrombocytopenia, and in severe cases, shock and hemorrhage.
  • WHO Classification: Dengue is classified into Dengue without warning signs, Dengue with warning signs, and Severe Dengue, guiding clinical management.
