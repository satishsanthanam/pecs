Hidradenitis suppurativa

🏠 Home / Skin, hair, and nails / Hidradenitis suppurativa

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Chronic Inflammatory Condition: Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a long-term skin disease characterized by painful lumps, abscesses, and tunnels (sinus tracts) under the skin.
  • Follicular Occlusion: It primarily affects areas with apocrine sweat glands and hair follicles, leading to their blockage and subsequent inflammation.
  • Progressive Nature: HS can worsen over time, causing significant scarring, pain, and reduced quality of life if not managed effectively.
  • Common Locations: Lesions typically appear in the armpits, groin, buttocks, inner thighs, and under the breasts.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Painful Nodules: Deep, tender, pea-sized to marble-sized lumps that develop under the skin.
  • Abscesses: These nodules can rupture, draining pus and blood, often with a foul odor.
  • Sinus Tracts: Tunnels connecting lesions under the skin, which can persist and lead to chronic drainage and scarring.
  • Scarring: Repeated inflammation and healing can lead to thick, rope-like scars and contractures.
  • Itching and Burning: Patients often report sensations of itching, burning, or stinging in affected areas.
  • Recurrence: Lesions frequently reappear in the same or nearby areas, indicating the chronic nature of the condition.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Tight Clothing: Wear loose-fitting, breathable fabrics to reduce friction and irritation in affected areas.
  • Maintain Skin Hygiene: Gently cleanse affected areas daily with mild, antiseptic washes as advised by a clinician, avoiding harsh scrubbing.
  • Smoking Cessation: Smoking is a significant risk factor and can exacerbate HS; cessation is strongly recommended.
  • Weight Management: For individuals with obesity, weight loss can reduce skin folds and friction, potentially improving symptoms.
  • Avoid Shaving Affected Areas: Shaving can irritate hair follicles and potentially trigger new lesions or worsen existing ones.
  • Early Intervention: Seek medical attention promptly for new or worsening lesions to prevent progression and complications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Anti-inflammatory Diet: Focus on whole, unprocessed foods, rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and healthy fats to support overall health and potentially reduce systemic inflammation.
  • Identify Potential Triggers: Some individuals report specific food triggers like dairy products, brewer's yeast, or processed sugars; a food diary can help identify personal sensitivities.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, primarily water, to support skin health and overall bodily functions.
  • Avoid Processed Foods: Limit intake of highly processed foods, sugary drinks, and unhealthy fats, which can contribute to inflammation.
  • Consult a Dietitian: For personalized guidance, especially if considering significant dietary changes, consult a registered dietitian.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Adherence to Treatment: Strictly follow prescribed medication regimens, including topical treatments, oral antibiotics, or biologics, to manage symptoms and prevent flares.
  • Recognize Infection Signs: Be vigilant for increased redness, warmth, swelling, severe pain, or fever, which may indicate a secondary bacterial infection requiring immediate medical attention.
  • Pain Management: Utilize prescribed pain relief strategies and discuss persistent pain with your healthcare provider.
  • Avoid Self-Incision: Do not attempt to pop or drain lesions yourself, as this can worsen inflammation, introduce infection, and lead to more severe scarring.
  • Psychological Support: HS can significantly impact mental health; seek support from mental health professionals or patient support groups if experiencing distress.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: Prompt recognition and diagnosis of HS are crucial to initiate effective treatment and prevent disease progression.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Management is tailored to disease severity (Hurley stage), patient factors, and response to therapy, often involving a combination of medical and surgical approaches.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Collaboration with dermatologists, surgeons, pain specialists, and mental health professionals is often beneficial for comprehensive care.
  • Biologic Therapies: For moderate to severe HS, biologic medications targeting inflammatory pathways have shown significant efficacy.
  • Surgical Interventions: Incision and drainage, deroofing, or wide surgical excision may be necessary for persistent lesions, sinus tracts, or extensive disease.
  • Patient Education: Empowering patients with knowledge about their condition, triggers, and treatment options is vital for long-term management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology Understanding: Grasp the role of follicular occlusion, apocrine gland involvement, and immune dysregulation in HS development.
  • Wound Care Principles: Learn appropriate wound care techniques for draining lesions, including dressing selection, exudate management, and infection prevention.
  • Pain Assessment: Accurately assess and document pain levels using appropriate scales, advocating for effective pain management strategies.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on lifestyle modifications (e.g., smoking cessation, weight management), proper hygiene, and medication adherence.
  • Psychosocial Support: Recognize the significant psychological impact of HS and provide empathetic support, referring to mental health services as needed.
  • Pre- and Post-Operative Care: Understand the nursing care required for patients undergoing surgical interventions for HS, including wound monitoring and pain control.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Hurley Staging System: A clinical classification system for HS severity (Stage I: single or multiple isolated abscesses; Stage II: recurrent abscesses with sinus tracts and scarring; Stage III: diffuse or near-diffuse involvement with interconnected tracts and abscesses).
  • Differential Diagnoses: Includes folliculitis, furuncles, carbuncles, Crohn's disease (especially perianal involvement), and ruptured epidermal cysts.
  • Common Comorbidities: Metabolic syndrome, obesity, inflammatory bowel disease, spondyloarthropathy, polycystic ovary syndrome, and depression.
  • Treatment Modalities: Topical antibiotics (e.g., clindamycin), oral antibiotics (e.g., tetracyclines, rifampin/clindamycin combination), oral retinoids, hormonal therapies, biologics (e.g., adalimumab), CO2 laser therapy, and various surgical techniques.
  • Pathology: Histopathological findings include follicular occlusion, neutrophilic and lymphocytic infiltration, sinus tract formation, and fibrosis.
  • Genetic Predisposition: A familial component is observed in a subset of patients, suggesting genetic influences.
