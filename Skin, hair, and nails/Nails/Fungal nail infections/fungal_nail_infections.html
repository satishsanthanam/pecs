Fungal nail infections

🏠 Home / Skin, hair, and nails / Nails

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Onychomycosis Defined: A common fungal infection affecting fingernails or, more frequently, toenails.
  • Causative Agents: Primarily dermatophytes (e.g., Trichophyton rubrum), but also yeasts (e.g., Candida species) and non-dermatophytic molds.
  • Transmission Pathways: Spreads in warm, moist environments such as public showers, swimming pools, and through shared nail care tools.
  • Predisposing Factors: Advanced age, diabetes mellitus, peripheral vascular disease, immunosuppression, excessive sweating (hyperhidrosis), tight-fitting footwear, and nail trauma.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Nail Discoloration: Affected nails may turn yellow, brown, white, or black.
  • Thickening and Brittleness: Nails become unusually thick, brittle, crumbly, or ragged at the edges.
  • Distorted Shape: The nail plate may become distorted, misshapen, or lift from the nail bed (onycholysis).
  • Pain or Discomfort: Can occur with severe infection, especially when pressure is applied (e.g., wearing shoes or walking).
  • Foul Odor: Accumulation of debris under the nail can lead to an unpleasant smell.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Maintain Hygiene: Keep feet and hands clean and thoroughly dry, particularly after bathing or swimming.
  • Appropriate Footwear: Wear breathable shoes and change socks regularly, especially if prone to sweating; opt for cotton or moisture-wicking materials.
  • Public Area Protection: Always wear sandals or flip-flops in public showers, locker rooms, and around swimming pools.
  • Sterile Nail Care: Avoid sharing nail clippers or files; ensure any salon tools used are properly sterilized.
  • Prevent Nail Trauma: Protect nails from injury, as trauma can create entry points for fungal pathogens.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Immune System Support: Incorporate nutrient-dense foods rich in vitamins (e.g., Vitamin C, Vitamin D), minerals (e.g., Zinc, Selenium), and probiotics to bolster overall immune function.
  • Glycemic Control: For individuals with diabetes, strict management of blood glucose levels is paramount to support immune response and reduce susceptibility to infections.
  • Adequate Hydration: Ensure sufficient fluid intake to support overall skin and nail health, contributing to the body's natural barriers.
  • Anti-inflammatory Foods: Consider foods with anti-inflammatory properties (e.g., omega-3 fatty acids) to support general health and potentially aid in recovery.
  • Avoid Excessive Sugars: Limit intake of refined sugars and processed foods, which can potentially impact immune function.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Medical Consultation: Seek medical advice early to confirm diagnosis and initiate appropriate treatment, preventing spread and worsening of the infection.
  • Adherence to Treatment: Strictly follow the prescribed antifungal medication regimen, as treatment can be prolonged and requires consistency for efficacy.
  • Awareness of Contagion: Understand that fungal nail infections are contagious; avoid direct contact with infected nails and shared personal items.
  • Manage Underlying Conditions: Effectively manage any predisposing health conditions such as diabetes or peripheral vascular disease.
  • Regular Nail Inspection: Periodically inspect nails for any changes, especially if you are at a higher risk for fungal infections.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: Clinical examination combined with laboratory confirmation (e.g., KOH microscopy, fungal culture, PCR) is crucial to differentiate onychomycosis from other nail pathologies.
  • Tailored Treatment Plans: Treatment options range from topical antifungals for mild cases to oral antifungals (e.g., terbinafine, itraconazole) for more severe or resistant infections. Laser therapy or surgical debridement may also be considered.
  • Monitoring for Adverse Effects: Patients on oral antifungal medications, particularly those with pre-existing liver conditions, require monitoring of liver function tests.
  • Recurrence Prevention: Counsel patients on the high potential for recurrence and emphasize the importance of long-term preventative measures and adherence to post-treatment care.
  • Diabetic Patient Considerations: For diabetic patients, meticulous foot care and prompt, aggressive treatment are essential to prevent secondary bacterial infections and severe complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Focus: Educate patients comprehensively on proper nail hygiene, appropriate footwear choices, and the critical importance of completing the full course of prescribed treatment.
  • Medication Management: Understand the pharmacokinetics, potential side effects, and drug interactions of both oral and topical antifungal agents.
  • Assessment and Documentation: Accurately assess the severity and extent of nail involvement, documenting changes and patient response to treatment.
  • Risk Factor Identification: Identify patients at higher risk (e.g., elderly, immunocompromised, diabetics) to provide targeted education and screening.
  • Referral Pathways: Recognize when to refer patients to specialists such as a podiatrist or dermatologist for advanced diagnostic evaluation or specialized treatment.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: B35.1 (Onychomycosis), B35.3 (Tinea pedis with onychomycosis).
  • Key Diagnostic Tests: Potassium Hydroxide (KOH) microscopy, Fungal culture, Histopathology with PAS stain, PCR.
  • Common Pharmacological Agents: Terbinafine (Lamisil), Itraconazole (Sporanox), Fluconazole (Diflucan), Ciclopirox (Penlac), Efinaconazole (Jublia), Tavaborole (Kerydin).
  • Differential Diagnoses: Psoriasis of the nails, lichen planus, bacterial paronychia, nail trauma, subungual hematoma, yellow nail syndrome.
  • Primary Pathogens: Trichophyton rubrum, Trichophyton mentagrophytes, Candida albicans.
