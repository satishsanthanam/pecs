Lichen planus

🏠 Home / Skin, hair, and nails / Nails

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Inflammatory Condition: Lichen planus is a chronic inflammatory condition that can affect the skin, hair, mucous membranes, and nails.
  • Nail Involvement: When it affects the nails, it can lead to significant and often irreversible damage to the nail plate and matrix.
  • Autoimmune Basis: It is believed to be an autoimmune disorder where the body's immune system mistakenly attacks cells in the skin and nail matrix.
  • Prevalence: Nail involvement occurs in approximately 1-10% of all lichen planus cases, and can sometimes be the sole manifestation.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Longitudinal Ridging: Development of fine or coarse lines running from the cuticle to the tip of the nail.
  • Thinning and Brittleness: Nails may become progressively thinner, fragile, and prone to splitting.
  • Onycholysis: Separation of the nail plate from the nail bed, often starting at the free edge.
  • Pterygium Formation: A characteristic V-shaped scar where the proximal nail fold grows forward and fuses with the nail bed, leading to nail plate loss.
  • Anonychia: Complete loss of the nail plate, which can be permanent in severe cases.
  • Trachyonychia: Rough, sandpaper-like texture of the nail plate.
  • Other Body Areas: Patients may also present with pruritic, purple, polygonal papules on the skin, white lacy patterns (Wickham's striae) in the mouth, or scarring alopecia on the scalp.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Nail Trauma: Protect nails from injury, as trauma can trigger or worsen lesions (Koebner phenomenon).
  • Regular Dermatological Review: Maintain consistent follow-up appointments with a dermatologist to monitor disease progression and treatment efficacy.
  • Infection Prevention: Keep nails and surrounding skin clean and dry to prevent secondary bacterial or fungal infections, especially with onycholysis.
  • Gentle Nail Care: Avoid aggressive manicures, pedicures, or nail enhancements that could further damage fragile nails.
  • Sun Protection: While not directly related to nail LP, general sun protection is advised for skin LP patients.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Encourage a balanced diet rich in vitamins (especially biotin, Vitamin A, C, E) and minerals (zinc, iron) to support overall nail health and integrity.
  • Adequate Hydration: Ensure sufficient fluid intake to maintain skin and nail hydration.
  • Anti-inflammatory Foods: While not a direct treatment, incorporating foods with anti-inflammatory properties (e.g., omega-3 fatty acids, fruits, vegetables) may support overall health.
  • Avoid Irritants (Oral LP): If oral lichen planus is also present, advise avoiding spicy, acidic, or abrasive foods that can irritate oral lesions.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: Prompt identification of nail lichen planus is crucial to initiate treatment and potentially prevent irreversible nail destruction.
  • Adherence to Treatment: Emphasize the importance of consistent application of topical medications or adherence to systemic therapies as prescribed.
  • Psychological Support: Acknowledge the significant cosmetic impact of nail changes and offer resources for psychological support if needed.
  • Biopsy Consideration: A nail matrix biopsy may be necessary for definitive diagnosis, especially when clinical presentation is atypical.
  • Monitor for Malignancy: While rare in nail LP, chronic inflammatory conditions, particularly oral LP, carry a small risk of malignant transformation, warranting vigilance.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Treatment Challenges: Nail lichen planus can be challenging to treat, and complete resolution is often difficult, with a high potential for permanent nail dystrophy.
  • Therapeutic Options: Treatment typically involves potent topical corticosteroids, intralesional corticosteroid injections into the nail matrix, or systemic agents like oral corticosteroids, methotrexate, cyclosporine, or retinoids for severe, widespread, or progressive cases.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Always consider other conditions such as nail psoriasis, onychomycosis, trauma, eczema, or tumors when evaluating nail changes.
  • Prognosis: While some cases may spontaneously remit, many progress to permanent nail damage, including anonychia or pterygium formation.
  • Patient Education: Thoroughly educate patients on the chronic nature of the disease, realistic treatment expectations, and the importance of long-term management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education on Nail Care: Instruct patients on gentle nail care practices, avoiding harsh chemicals, and protecting nails from physical trauma.
  • Medication Administration: Educate on proper application techniques for topical treatments (e.g., occlusive dressings) and monitor for side effects of systemic medications.
  • Assessment of Nail Changes: Regularly assess and document the extent and type of nail changes, noting any signs of infection or treatment response.
  • Emotional Support: Provide empathetic support, recognizing the potential distress caused by visible nail disfigurement.
  • Referral Pathways: Understand when to refer patients to a dermatologist for specialized care or to mental health professionals for psychological support.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: T-cell mediated autoimmune response targeting basal keratinocytes of the nail matrix.
  • Histopathology: Characteristic findings in nail matrix biopsy include a lichenoid inflammatory infiltrate at the dermo-epidermal junction, vacuolar degeneration of basal cells, and colloid bodies.
  • Koebner Phenomenon: The development of new lesions at sites of trauma, common in lichen planus.
  • Pterygium Unguis: A specific and often irreversible manifestation where the dorsal nail fold adheres to the nail bed, leading to nail plate atrophy.
  • Anonychia: Complete absence of the nail plate, a severe outcome of extensive nail matrix damage.
  • Immunohistochemistry: May show increased expression of MHC class I and II antigens and adhesion molecules.
