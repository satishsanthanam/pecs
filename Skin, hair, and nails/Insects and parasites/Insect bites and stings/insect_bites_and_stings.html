Insect bites and stings

🏠 Home / Skin, hair, and nails / Insects and parasites

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Common Reactions: Most insect bites and stings result in localized reactions such as redness, swelling, itching, and mild pain at the site.
  • Allergic Reactions: Some individuals may experience more severe allergic reactions, ranging from widespread hives and swelling to life-threatening anaphylaxis.
  • Venom vs. Irritants: Stings from Hymenoptera (bees, wasps, hornets, fire ants) inject venom, while bites from mosquitoes, fleas, and bed bugs often cause reactions due to anticoagulants or irritants in their saliva.
  • Risk of Infection: Scratching bites can break the skin barrier, increasing the risk of secondary bacterial infections like cellulitis.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Symptoms:
    • Redness and Swelling: Erythema and edema around the bite or sting site.
    • Itching (Pruritus): Intense itching, especially with mosquito, flea, or bed bug bites.
    • Pain or Burning: Sharp pain or a burning sensation, common with bee or wasp stings.
    • Warmth: The affected area may feel warm to the touch.
  • Systemic Allergic Symptoms (Anaphylaxis):
    • Skin Reactions: Generalized hives (urticaria), flushing, or angioedema (swelling of lips, face, throat).
    • Respiratory Distress: Wheezing, shortness of breath, difficulty swallowing, throat tightness, or stridor.
    • Cardiovascular Symptoms: Dizziness, lightheadedness, rapid pulse, or a sudden drop in blood pressure (hypotension).
    • Gastrointestinal Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, or diarrhea.
    • Neurological Symptoms: Confusion, anxiety, or loss of consciousness.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Insect Repellents: Use EPA-registered insect repellents containing DEET, picaridin, IR3535, oil of lemon eucalyptus, or para-menthane-diol when outdoors.
  • Protective Clothing: Wear long sleeves, long pants, and hats, especially during peak insect activity times or in wooded areas.
  • Avoid Attractants: Minimize use of scented products (perfumes, lotions) and avoid bright-colored clothing, which can attract insects.
  • Environmental Control: Eliminate standing water to reduce mosquito breeding sites; seal cracks in homes to prevent insect entry; avoid disturbing insect nests or hives.
  • Stinger Removal: If stung by a bee, promptly scrape out the stinger horizontally with a credit card or fingernail, rather than squeezing it, to prevent further venom release.
  • Wound Care: Clean the bite/sting site immediately with soap and water to reduce infection risk.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially if experiencing systemic symptoms like fever, malaise, or vomiting, to prevent dehydration and support the body's recovery process.
  • Post-Anaphylaxis Diet: Following a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) that may involve throat swelling or difficulty swallowing, a temporary diet of clear liquids or soft, easily digestible foods may be necessary until symptoms fully resolve and normal swallowing function returns.
  • Medication-Related Guidelines: If oral antihistamines or other medications are prescribed, follow specific instructions regarding food intake (e.g., taking with food to reduce stomach upset) to optimize absorption and minimize side effects.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Care for Anaphylaxis: Seek immediate emergency medical attention (call 911 or local emergency services) if signs of a severe allergic reaction develop, including difficulty breathing, widespread hives, swelling of the face/throat, dizziness, or rapid pulse.
  • Epinephrine Auto-Injector Use: If prescribed an epinephrine auto-injector (e.g., EpiPen), administer it without delay at the first sign of a severe allergic reaction and then call for emergency medical help.
  • Monitor for Infection: Watch for signs of secondary bacterial infection at the bite site, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus drainage, or persistent pain beyond 24-48 hours.
  • Avoid Scratching: Advise patients to avoid scratching bites, as this can worsen itching, damage the skin, and introduce bacteria, leading to infection.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: Differentiate between common reactions, large local reactions, and systemic allergic reactions to guide appropriate treatment.
  • Pharmacological Management: Recommend topical corticosteroids or oral antihistamines for localized reactions. Prescribe oral corticosteroids for large local reactions. For severe allergic reactions, epinephrine is the first-line treatment.
  • Allergy Testing: Consider referral for allergy testing (skin prick tests or specific IgE blood tests) for patients with a history of severe reactions to insect stings to identify specific venom allergies and discuss venom immunotherapy.
  • Epinephrine Prescription: Ensure patients at risk for anaphylaxis are prescribed and educated on the proper use of an epinephrine auto-injector.
  • Tetanus Prophylaxis: Assess tetanus vaccination status, especially for bites that break the skin.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Assessment: Conduct a thorough assessment of the bite/sting site (location, size, appearance), patient's symptoms, and history of previous reactions or allergies.
  • First Aid Education: Educate patients and caregivers on immediate first aid measures, including stinger removal, wound cleaning, and application of cold compresses.
  • Medication Administration: Administer prescribed antihistamines, corticosteroids, or epinephrine as ordered, monitoring for effectiveness and adverse effects.
  • Anaphylaxis Monitoring: Closely monitor patients for signs of anaphylaxis, including vital signs, respiratory status, and skin changes, and be prepared for rapid intervention.
  • Infection Prevention: Provide instructions on preventing secondary infections, emphasizing good hand hygiene and avoiding scratching.
  • Patient Education on Prevention: Counsel patients on strategies to minimize exposure to insects, such as using repellents and wearing protective clothing.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Hymenoptera Venom Allergy: Hypersensitivity reaction to venom from bees, wasps, hornets, yellow jackets, and fire ants, potentially leading to anaphylaxis.
  • Urticaria and Angioedema: Skin manifestations of allergic reactions, characterized by itchy wheals (hives) and deeper tissue swelling, respectively.
  • Anaphylaxis: A severe, life-threatening systemic allergic reaction involving multiple organ systems, requiring immediate medical intervention.
  • Cellulitis: A common bacterial skin infection that can occur as a secondary complication of insect bites due to scratching and skin breakdown.
  • Venom Immunotherapy (VIT): A desensitization treatment for individuals with severe Hymenoptera venom allergy, involving gradually increasing doses of venom extract.
  • Pruritus: The medical term for itching, a common symptom associated with most insect bites.
