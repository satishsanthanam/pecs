Erythema nodosum

🏠 Home / Skin, hair, and nails / Erythema nodosum

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Erythema nodosum (EN) is an inflammatory condition characterized by tender, red, subcutaneous nodules, most commonly found on the shins.
  • Nature of Lesions: These nodules are typically painful, warm to the touch, and can evolve to resemble bruises as they resolve.
  • Underlying Cause: EN is often a reactive process, meaning it's a manifestation of an underlying systemic disease, infection, or medication, rather than a primary disease itself.
  • Prognosis: It is generally a self-limiting condition, with individual lesions resolving spontaneously within weeks to months, though new lesions may appear during this period.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Local Lesions: Development of painful, erythematous, firm nodules, typically 1-5 cm in diameter, predominantly on the anterior aspects of the lower legs.
  • Systemic Manifestations: Patients may experience prodromal symptoms such as fever, malaise, fatigue, and arthralgia (joint pain), particularly in the ankles and knees.
  • Preceding Events: Often follows an upper respiratory tract infection, gastrointestinal symptoms, or exposure to certain medications.
  • Underlying Conditions: Clinical indications prompting investigation for EN include recent streptococcal infection, sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis), tuberculosis, or certain drug exposures.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Accurate Diagnosis: It is crucial to differentiate erythema nodosum from other nodular skin conditions such as cellulitis, nodular vasculitis, or deep vein thrombosis, which may require different management.
  • Identify Underlying Cause: The primary precaution is to diligently investigate and identify the underlying trigger or associated systemic disease, as treating the primary condition is key to resolving EN.
  • Avoid Trauma: Protect affected areas from direct trauma or pressure, which can exacerbate pain and inflammation.
  • Medication Review: Carefully review all current medications, as some drugs (e.g., oral contraceptives, sulfonamides) can induce EN.
  • Monitoring for Progression: Watch for signs of worsening systemic symptoms, new lesion development, or atypical lesion characteristics that might suggest an alternative diagnosis.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially if experiencing systemic symptoms like fever or malaise, to support overall physiological function.
  • Medication-Related Considerations: If non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are prescribed for pain relief, advise taking them with food to minimize gastrointestinal irritation.
  • Corticosteroid Management: If systemic corticosteroids are used in severe cases, be aware of potential dietary implications such as increased appetite or fluid retention, and manage accordingly.
  • Supportive Nutrition: Focus on a balanced diet to support the immune system and overall healing, particularly when managing an underlying inflammatory condition.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Medical Evaluation: Seek immediate medical attention for new, rapidly spreading, or unusually painful skin nodules, especially if accompanied by fever, joint pain, or other systemic symptoms.
  • Do Not Self-Treat: Avoid attempting to drain, squeeze, or manipulate the nodules, as this can lead to infection or scarring.
  • Adherence to Treatment Plan: Strictly follow the healthcare provider's recommendations for managing both the erythema nodosum and any identified underlying conditions.
  • Report Medication Changes: Inform your doctor about any new medications or supplements, as they could potentially trigger or worsen EN.
  • Regular Follow-up: Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor the resolution of lesions and the management of the underlying cause.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Approach: Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on the characteristic appearance and distribution of lesions. Investigations often include a thorough history, physical exam, laboratory tests (e.g., ESR, CRP, ASO titer, ACE level, TB skin test), and sometimes a chest X-ray to rule out underlying causes like sarcoidosis or tuberculosis. A skin biopsy may be considered for atypical presentations.
  • Treatment Strategy: Management focuses on supportive care, including rest, leg elevation, compression stockings, and pain relief with NSAIDs. The cornerstone of treatment is identifying and addressing the underlying etiology. Systemic corticosteroids are generally reserved for severe, persistent, or idiopathic cases after ruling out infectious causes.
  • Patient Education: It is vital to educate patients about the benign, self-limiting nature of EN while emphasizing the importance of investigating and treating any associated systemic conditions. Reassurance regarding the typical resolution without scarring is also important.
  • Referral Considerations: Depending on the suspected underlying cause, referral to specialists such as rheumatologists, infectious disease specialists, or gastroenterologists may be necessary for comprehensive management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct a detailed skin assessment, noting the location, size, color, tenderness, and evolution of nodules. Assess for systemic symptoms such as fever, malaise, and arthralgia. Obtain a thorough medical history, including recent infections, travel, medications, and family history of autoimmune conditions.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics (e.g., NSAIDs) and educate patients on non-pharmacological pain relief strategies such as rest, leg elevation, and cool compresses.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear instructions on the nature of EN, the importance of identifying and treating the underlying cause, and signs/symptoms that warrant immediate medical attention. Advise on activity modification to avoid prolonged standing or trauma.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Observe for signs of secondary infection, ulceration (rare), or progression of underlying systemic disease. Monitor medication side effects, especially with NSAIDs or corticosteroids.
  • Emotional Support: Acknowledge the discomfort and potential cosmetic concerns associated with EN, offering reassurance and support throughout the diagnostic and treatment process.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Erythema nodosum is a form of septal panniculitis, characterized by inflammation of the subcutaneous fat septa without primary vasculitis. It is considered a delayed-type hypersensitivity reaction.
  • Etiology: Common causes include streptococcal infections, sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis), tuberculosis, fungal infections (e.g., coccidioidomycosis, histoplasmosis), certain medications (e.g., oral contraceptives, sulfonamides, penicillin), and Behçet's disease. Approximately 30-50% of cases are idiopathic.
  • Histopathology: Biopsy typically reveals septal panniculitis with a mixed inflammatory infiltrate composed of lymphocytes, histiocytes, neutrophils, and eosinophils. Characteristic Miescher's radial granulomas (small, well-defined nodular aggregates of histiocytes around a central cleft) may be present.
  • Differential Diagnoses: Key differential diagnoses include cellulitis, nodular vasculitis, erythema induratum (Bazin's disease), lupus panniculitis, deep fungal infections, pancreatic panniculitis, and other forms of panniculitis.
