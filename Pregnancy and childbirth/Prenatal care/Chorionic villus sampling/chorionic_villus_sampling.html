Chorionic villus sampling

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Prenatal care

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of CVS: Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS) is a prenatal diagnostic test to detect chromosomal abnormalities and genetic conditions in a developing fetus.
  • Timing of Procedure: Typically performed between 10 and 13 weeks of gestation, offering earlier results than amniocentesis.
  • Methodology: Involves obtaining a small sample of chorionic villi (placental tissue) for genetic analysis.
  • Approaches: Can be performed either transcervically (through the cervix) or transabdominally (through the abdominal wall), guided by ultrasound.
  • Diagnostic Accuracy: Provides definitive diagnostic information, distinguishing it from screening tests that only estimate risk.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Advanced Maternal Age: Indicated for pregnant individuals aged 35 years or older at the time of delivery due to increased risk of aneuploidy.
  • Previous Affected Pregnancy: Recommended if there is a history of a prior child with a chromosomal abnormality or genetic disorder.
  • Family History of Genetic Disease: Considered when there is a known family history of specific genetic conditions (e.g., cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia, fragile X syndrome).
  • Abnormal Screening Results: Prompted by concerning findings from first-trimester screening tests, such as elevated nuchal translucency or abnormal maternal serum markers.
  • Parental Carrier Status: Offered when one or both parents are known carriers for a genetic condition that can be diagnosed prenatally.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Informed Consent: Comprehensive discussion of risks, benefits, and potential outcomes with the patient prior to the procedure is mandatory.
  • Ultrasound Guidance: Continuous real-time ultrasound imaging is essential throughout the entire procedure to ensure precise needle/catheter placement and minimize risks.
  • Rh Immunoglobulin: Rh-negative mothers must receive Rh immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) after the procedure to prevent Rh sensitization.
  • Infection Risk: The transcervical approach carries a slightly higher risk of infection; active vaginal infections are a contraindication.
  • Post-Procedure Activity: Advise against strenuous physical activity, heavy lifting, and sexual intercourse for at least 24-48 hours, or as directed by the physician.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: No specific fasting is typically required before a CVS procedure.
  • Bladder Status (Transabdominal): A full bladder may be requested for the transabdominal approach to help elevate the uterus and improve visualization.
  • Bladder Status (Transcervical): An empty bladder may be preferred for the transcervical approach to facilitate access to the cervix.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration before and after the procedure.
  • Post-Procedure Diet: Resume normal diet immediately after the procedure unless otherwise instructed by the healthcare provider.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Complications: Instruct patients to immediately report any signs of heavy vaginal bleeding, persistent severe cramping, fever, chills, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
  • Activity Restrictions: Emphasize the importance of resting for 24-48 hours post-procedure and avoiding activities that could increase uterine contractions or introduce infection.
  • Avoid Vaginal Insertion: Advise against douching, using tampons, or engaging in sexual intercourse for at least one to two weeks following the procedure.
  • Pain Management: Mild cramping is common; advise on appropriate over-the-counter pain relief (e.g., acetaminophen) and when to seek medical attention for severe pain.
  • Emotional Support: Provide reassurance and resources for emotional support, as the waiting period for results can be a source of significant anxiety.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Genetic Counseling: Pre-procedure genetic counseling is paramount to ensure patients understand the implications of potential results and available options.
  • Risk-Benefit Assessment: Carefully weigh the diagnostic benefits of early genetic information against the small but inherent risk of miscarriage associated with the procedure.
  • Alternative Options: Discuss amniocentesis as an alternative diagnostic test, noting its later gestational timing and different risk profile.
  • Management Planning: Be prepared to counsel patients on potential management strategies based on the diagnostic findings, including further testing, medical interventions, or reproductive choices.
  • Patient Selection: Meticulous patient selection based on indications, gestational age, and individual risk factors is crucial for optimal outcomes.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Role: Nurses are vital in educating patients about the CVS procedure, pre-procedure preparations, and post-procedure care instructions.
  • Emotional Support Provider: Offer empathetic support to patients and their families, acknowledging the stress and anxiety associated with prenatal diagnostic testing.
  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Monitor vital signs, assess for signs of bleeding or uterine contractions, and evaluate the patient's comfort level post-CVS.
  • Rh Immunoglobulin Protocol: Understand and correctly implement the protocol for administering Rh immunoglobulin to Rh-negative patients after the procedure.
  • Documentation: Ensure thorough and accurate documentation of the procedure, patient education, and any observed post-procedure events or complications.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Karyotyping: Standard cytogenetic analysis of the chorionic villi cells to identify numerical and structural chromosomal abnormalities.
  • FISH (Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization): A rapid technique providing preliminary results for common aneuploidies (e.g., Trisomy 13, 18, 21, sex chromosome aneuploidies) within 24-48 hours.
  • Chromosomal Microarray Analysis (CMA): Detects smaller chromosomal deletions or duplications (microdeletions/microduplications) that may not be visible on standard karyotyping.
  • Placental Mosaicism: A condition where different cell lines (normal and abnormal) are present in the placenta, which may or may not reflect the fetal karyotype.
  • Maternal Cell Contamination: The presence of maternal cells in the villus sample, which can complicate interpretation; careful dissection of the sample is crucial.
