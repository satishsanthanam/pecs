# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is defined as blood loss of 500 mL or more after a vaginal birth or 1000 mL or more after a cesarean section within 24 hours of delivery.
* **Primary vs. Secondary PPH:** Primary PPH occurs within the first 24 hours, while secondary PPH occurs between 24 hours and 12 weeks postpartum.
* **Leading Cause:** PPH remains a leading cause of maternal morbidity and mortality worldwide, necessitating prompt recognition and management.
* **Risk Factors:** Conditions like uterine atony, retained placental fragments, prolonged labor, multiple gestations, and pre-eclampsia increase the risk.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Excessive Vaginal Bleeding:** Soaking more than one sanitary pad per hour, or passing large blood clots.
* **Signs of Hypovolemic Shock:** Dizziness, lightheadedness, fainting, pallor, rapid heart rate (tachycardia), low blood pressure (hypotension).
* **Uterine Atony:** A soft, boggy uterus that does not contract firmly after delivery.
* **Hematoma Formation:** Swelling and pain in the perineal or vaginal area due to blood collection.
* **Decreased Urine Output:** A sign of reduced renal perfusion due to significant blood loss.
* **Shortness of Breath:** Due to decreased oxygen-carrying capacity from anemia.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Active Management of Third Stage:** Routine administration of uterotonic agents (e.g., oxytocin) immediately after delivery, controlled cord traction, and uterine massage.
* **Risk Factor Identification:** Thorough assessment for pre-existing conditions or labor complications that increase PPH risk.
* **Continuous Monitoring:** Close observation of uterine tone, lochia amount, and vital signs for at least the first few hours postpartum.
* **Rapid Response Protocols:** Ensuring availability of blood products, emergency medications, and a trained multidisciplinary team for immediate intervention.
* **Early Detection:** Quantitative blood loss measurement rather than visual estimation for accurate assessment.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Iron-Rich Diet:** Encourage consumption of iron-fortified foods (e.g., red meat, leafy greens, fortified cereals) to aid in recovery from anemia.
* **Adequate Hydration:** Maintain sufficient fluid intake to support blood volume restoration and overall recovery.
* **Nutrient-Dense Foods:** Focus on a balanced diet rich in vitamins and minerals to support healing and energy levels.
* **Avoidance of Stimulants:** Limit caffeine and alcohol, especially if breastfeeding, as they can interfere with recovery and hydration.
* **Small, Frequent Meals:** May be better tolerated initially, especially if experiencing nausea or weakness.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Immediate Call for Help:** Activate the obstetric emergency team or rapid response system.
* **Fundal Massage:** Perform continuous uterine massage to stimulate contractions and expel clots.
* **Uterotonic Administration:** Administer first-line uterotonic agents (e.g., oxytocin) as prescribed, followed by second-line agents if bleeding persists.
* **IV Access and Fluid Resuscitation:** Establish two large-bore IV lines and rapidly infuse crystalloids or colloids.
* **Monitor Vitals and Blood Loss:** Continuously assess vital signs, urine output, and quantitatively measure blood loss.
* **Prepare for Interventions:** Anticipate and prepare for potential surgical interventions (e.g., uterine artery embolization, B-Lynch suture, hysterectomy) or blood transfusions.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **The 4 T's:** Always consider the primary causes of PPH – Tone (uterine atony), Trauma (lacerations, hematomas), Tissue (retained placenta), and Thrombin (coagulopathy).
* **Multidisciplinary Approach:** Effective management requires a coordinated effort from obstetricians, anesthesiologists, nurses, and blood bank personnel.
* **Early Intervention is Key:** Aggressive and timely management is crucial to prevent progression to severe hemorrhage and its complications.
* **Consider Underlying Conditions:** Investigate for pre-existing coagulopathies, placental abnormalities, or other medical conditions that may contribute to PPH.
* **Debriefing:** Post-event debriefing is vital for team learning and improving future responses.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Nursing Role in Prevention:** Administering uterotonics, performing fundal massage, and educating patients on warning signs.
* **Accurate Blood Loss Estimation:** Training in quantitative blood loss measurement techniques (e.g., weighing pads, using calibrated collection bags).
* **Medication Management:** Understanding the indications, contraindications, and side effects of various uterotonic agents.
* **Emotional Support:** Providing reassurance and clear communication to the patient and family during a stressful event.
* **Team Communication:** Effective SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) communication during obstetric emergencies.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pharmacological Agents:** Oxytocin, Methylergonovine, Carboprost tromethamine (Hemabate), Misoprostol, Tranexamic acid.
* **Surgical Interventions:** Uterine artery embolization, B-Lynch suture, uterine packing, hysterectomy.
* **Diagnostic Criteria:** Quantitative blood loss (QBL) >500mL (vaginal) or >1000mL (C-section) within 24 hours.
* **Laboratory Tests:** Complete Blood Count (CBC), Coagulation Profile (PT/INR, aPTT, Fibrinogen), Type and Crossmatch.
* **Classification:** Primary PPH (within 24 hours), Secondary PPH (24 hours to 12 weeks postpartum).
