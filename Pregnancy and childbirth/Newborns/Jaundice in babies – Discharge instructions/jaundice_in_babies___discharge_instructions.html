Jaundice in babies – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Newborns

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Jaundice: Jaundice is a common condition in newborns, characterized by a yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes. It occurs when there is an excess of bilirubin, a yellow pigment produced during the breakdown of red blood cells, in the baby's blood.
  • Physiological Jaundice: Most cases are 'physiological jaundice,' which is typically mild, appears after 24 hours of life, peaks around 3-5 days, and resolves within two weeks as the baby's liver matures.
  • Importance of Monitoring: While often harmless, high levels of bilirubin can be dangerous, so careful monitoring and follow-up are crucial to ensure appropriate management.
  • Bilirubin Excretion: Bilirubin is primarily excreted through the baby's stool and urine, making frequent feeding essential.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Yellow Skin and Eyes: The most obvious sign is a yellow tint to the baby's skin, often starting on the face and spreading downwards to the chest, abdomen, and limbs. The whites of the eyes (sclera) may also appear yellow.
  • Poor Feeding: A jaundiced baby may be excessively sleepy and difficult to wake for feedings, leading to reduced intake.
  • Lethargy and Irritability: Increased sleepiness, decreased activity, or unusual irritability can be signs of worsening jaundice.
  • Dark Urine: While uncommon in physiological jaundice, dark yellow or brown urine can indicate conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, which requires immediate medical attention.
  • Pale Stools: Very pale or chalky stools can also be a sign of a more serious underlying liver issue.
  • High-Pitched Cry or Arching: In severe, untreated cases, signs of acute bilirubin encephalopathy may include a high-pitched cry, arching of the back, or seizures.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Frequent Feeding: Ensure your baby feeds frequently (8-12 times in 24 hours for breastfed babies, or as directed for formula-fed) to promote regular bowel movements and bilirubin excretion.
  • Monitor Skin Color: Check your baby's skin color daily in good, natural light. Gently press on your baby's forehead or nose; if the skin looks yellow when you lift your finger, jaundice is present.
  • Avoid Direct Sunlight: Do not expose your baby to direct sunlight as a treatment for jaundice without specific medical advice, as it can cause sunburn and dehydration.
  • Follow-Up Appointments: Adhere strictly to all scheduled follow-up appointments with your pediatrician or healthcare provider for bilirubin level checks.
  • Recognize Worsening Signs: Be vigilant for signs of worsening jaundice, such as increasing yellowness, lethargy, poor feeding, or fever.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate hydration through breast milk or formula; do not give water or sugar water.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Frequent Breastfeeding: For breastfed infants, encourage frequent and effective nursing, at least 8-12 times in 24 hours, to ensure adequate milk intake and promote bilirubin excretion through stools.
  • Adequate Formula Intake: For formula-fed infants, ensure they are receiving appropriate volumes of formula as recommended by your pediatrician to maintain hydration and bowel movements.
  • Avoid Water or Sugar Water: Do not supplement with water or sugar water, as this can dilute essential nutrients and does not effectively aid in bilirubin excretion.
  • Maternal Hydration (Breastfeeding): Breastfeeding mothers should maintain good hydration to support milk production.
  • No Specific Maternal Dietary Restrictions: Generally, there are no specific dietary restrictions for breastfeeding mothers related to infant jaundice, unless advised by a healthcare provider for other reasons.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Medical Attention: Seek immediate medical attention if your baby's jaundice worsens rapidly, they become extremely lethargic or difficult to wake, have a high-pitched cry, arch their back, or develop a fever.
  • Phototherapy Instructions: If home phototherapy is prescribed, follow all instructions meticulously regarding duration, skin exposure, eye protection, and temperature monitoring.
  • Avoid Unproven Remedies: Do not use unproven home remedies or traditional treatments for jaundice without consulting your healthcare provider.
  • Medication Adherence: If any specific medications are prescribed (rare for typical physiological jaundice), administer them exactly as directed.
  • Report Concerns: Always report any concerns about your baby's feeding, activity, or color changes to your pediatrician promptly.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Assessment: Every jaundiced infant requires an individualized assessment based on their age in hours, bilirubin levels, and specific risk factors.
  • Timely Follow-Up: Adherence to follow-up appointments for bilirubin level checks is paramount to prevent potential complications.
  • Early Intervention: Early recognition and intervention for significant jaundice can prevent severe neurological damage (kernicterus).
  • Parental Education: Empowering parents with clear discharge instructions and knowledge of warning signs is a cornerstone of safe jaundice management.
  • Reassurance and Support: While vigilance is key, most cases of newborn jaundice are benign and resolve without intervention beyond frequent feeding.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Bilirubin Metabolism: Understand the physiological process of unconjugated bilirubin production, hepatic conjugation, and excretion, and how immaturity of these pathways contributes to neonatal jaundice.
  • Risk Factor Identification: Be proficient in identifying risk factors for severe hyperbilirubinemia, including prematurity, ABO/Rh incompatibility, G6PD deficiency, cephalohematoma, and exclusive breastfeeding.
  • Nursing Assessment: Master the technique of visual assessment for jaundice (cephalocaudal progression, blanching skin), transcutaneous bilirubinometry (TcB) use, and proper collection of serum bilirubin samples.
  • Parental Teaching: Develop comprehensive teaching plans for parents on discharge, covering signs of jaundice, feeding frequency, warning signs, and follow-up care.
  • Phototherapy Management: Understand the principles of phototherapy (photoisomerization), proper setup, eye protection, skin exposure, fluid balance, and temperature regulation during treatment.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Hyperbilirubinemia: Elevated levels of bilirubin in the blood, classified as unconjugated (indirect) or conjugated (direct).
  • Kernicterus: A rare but severe complication of untreated severe hyperbilirubinemia, leading to permanent brain damage.
  • Transcutaneous Bilirubinometry (TcB): A non-invasive method for estimating total serum bilirubin levels, often used for screening.
  • Phototherapy: A common treatment for neonatal jaundice involving exposure to specific wavelengths of light that convert unconjugated bilirubin into water-soluble isomers that can be excreted.
  • Exchange Transfusion: A procedure used in severe cases of hyperbilirubinemia where a baby's blood is replaced with donor blood to rapidly reduce bilirubin levels.
  • Bilirubin Nomogram: A chart used to plot a baby's bilirubin level against their age in hours to assess the risk of developing severe hyperbilirubinemia and guide treatment decisions.
  • Conjugated Hyperbilirubinemia: Elevated direct bilirubin, always pathological, indicating liver dysfunction or biliary obstruction, requiring urgent investigation.
