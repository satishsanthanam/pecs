Bronchopulmonary dysplasia

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Newborns

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Chronic Lung Condition: Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) is a chronic lung disease affecting premature infants, particularly those born very early or with low birth weight.
  • Lung Development Impairment: It results from injury to the developing lungs, often due to prolonged mechanical ventilation, oxygen therapy, and inflammation, leading to abnormal lung structure and function.
  • Oxygen Dependence: Infants with BPD typically require supplemental oxygen for an extended period, often beyond 28 days of age or 36 weeks postmenstrual age.
  • Long-Term Respiratory Issues: BPD can lead to persistent respiratory symptoms, increased susceptibility to infections, and potential developmental delays.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Tachypnea: Rapid breathing rate even at rest, indicating increased respiratory effort.
  • Retractions and Nasal Flaring: Visible pulling in of the skin between ribs, above the collarbone, or flaring of nostrils during breathing.
  • Oxygen Saturation Instability: Fluctuations or persistent low levels of oxygen in the blood, requiring supplemental oxygen.
  • Wheezing and Crackles: Abnormal breath sounds heard upon auscultation, indicating airway obstruction or fluid in the lungs.
  • Poor Weight Gain: Increased caloric expenditure due to respiratory effort, leading to difficulty gaining weight despite adequate intake.
  • Recurrent Respiratory Infections: Increased susceptibility to viral and bacterial respiratory infections, often requiring hospitalization.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Antenatal Corticosteroids: Administration to mothers at risk of preterm birth to accelerate fetal lung maturation.
  • Gentle Ventilation Strategies: Utilizing non-invasive ventilation (NIV) or minimal mechanical ventilation settings to reduce lung injury.
  • Early Surfactant Administration: Providing exogenous surfactant to premature infants with respiratory distress syndrome to improve lung compliance.
  • Strict Infection Control: Implementing rigorous hand hygiene and infection prevention protocols in the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU).
  • Judicious Fluid Management: Careful monitoring and restriction of fluid intake to prevent pulmonary edema, which can worsen lung function.
  • Optimized Nutrition: Ensuring adequate caloric and nutrient intake to support lung repair and growth, often requiring fortified breast milk or specialized formulas.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • High-Calorie Intake: Infants with BPD require increased caloric density in their feeds (e.g., fortified breast milk or high-calorie formula) to meet metabolic demands and support growth.
  • Fluid Restriction (If Indicated): In cases of significant pulmonary edema or fluid overload, careful fluid restriction may be necessary, balanced with nutritional needs.
  • Vitamin A Supplementation: Often administered to premature infants to support lung development and repair, as Vitamin A deficiency is common.
  • Vitamin D and Calcium: Essential for bone health, especially in infants with prolonged immobility or diuretic use.
  • Close Growth Monitoring: Regular assessment of weight, length, and head circumference to ensure adequate growth and adjust nutritional plans as needed.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Home Oxygen Management: Meticulous adherence to prescribed oxygen flow rates and duration, ensuring proper equipment maintenance and safety.
  • Medication Adherence: Consistent administration of prescribed medications, such as diuretics, bronchodilators, or steroids, as directed by the physician.
  • Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) Prophylaxis: Ensuring eligible infants receive palivizumab injections during RSV season to prevent severe infection.
  • Environmental Smoke Avoidance: Strict prohibition of smoking in the home or around the infant to protect fragile lungs from irritants.
  • Early Recognition of Illness: Promptly seeking medical attention for signs of respiratory distress, fever, or changes in feeding patterns.
  • CPR Training: Caregivers should be trained in infant cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) for emergency situations.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Care Plans: Management of BPD requires a highly individualized approach, adapting to the infant's specific respiratory needs and comorbidities.
  • Multidisciplinary Team: Collaboration with neonatologists, pulmonologists, dietitians, physical therapists, and social workers is crucial for comprehensive care.
  • Gradual Oxygen Weaning: A careful and monitored process of reducing supplemental oxygen as the infant's lung function improves, balancing oxygenation with growth.
  • Long-Term Follow-up: Regular follow-up with pediatric specialists is essential to monitor lung function, growth, and neurodevelopmental outcomes.
  • Parental Education and Support: Empowering parents with knowledge and resources to manage their child's condition at home is vital for successful outcomes.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Respiratory Assessment: Skillful evaluation of respiratory rate, effort, breath sounds, and oxygen saturation to identify changes in status.
  • Oxygen Delivery System Management: Proficiency in setting up, monitoring, and troubleshooting various oxygen delivery devices (nasal cannula, CPAP).
  • Nutritional Support Administration: Accurate preparation and administration of fortified feeds, monitoring feeding tolerance and gastric residuals.
  • Medication Administration: Safe and accurate delivery of respiratory medications, diuretics, and other prescribed drugs, observing for side effects.
  • Parental Education: Providing clear, empathetic instruction to parents on home care, medication schedules, warning signs, and emergency protocols.
  • Developmental Care: Implementing strategies to minimize stress and promote neurodevelopment in the NICU environment.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Definition of BPD: Chronic lung disease in infants born prematurely, characterized by oxygen dependence at 28 days of age or 36 weeks postmenstrual age.
  • Pathophysiology: Involves impaired alveolarization and dysregulated vascular growth, often secondary to inflammation and oxidative stress in immature lungs.
  • Risk Factors: Extreme prematurity, low birth weight, mechanical ventilation, prolonged oxygen exposure, sepsis, patent ductus arteriosus (PDA), and chorioamnionitis.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Based on the need for supplemental oxygen for at least 28 days, with severity classified by oxygen requirement at 36 weeks postmenstrual age.
  • Severity Classification: Mild (breathing room air at 36 weeks PMA), Moderate (requiring <30% oxygen at 36 weeks PMA), Severe (requiring ≥30% oxygen or positive pressure at 36 weeks PMA).
  • Therapeutic Interventions: Antenatal steroids, surfactant, gentle ventilation, fluid restriction, diuretics, bronchodilators, systemic/inhaled corticosteroids, and nutritional support.
