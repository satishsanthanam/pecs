Premature baby – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Newborns

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Transitioning Home: Premature infants require continued specialized care and close monitoring even after hospital discharge.
  • Corrected Age: Understand that your baby's developmental milestones will be assessed based on their corrected age (gestational age at birth plus chronological age), not their chronological age.
  • Follow-Up Care: Adherence to all scheduled follow-up appointments with the pediatrician, neonatologist, and any specialists is crucial for ongoing health and development.
  • Home Environment: Prepare a safe, clean, and quiet home environment conducive to your baby's continued growth and development.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Fever or Hypothermia: Rectal temperature above 100.4°F (38°C) or below 97.0°F (36.1°C).
  • Breathing Difficulties: Rapid breathing, grunting sounds, nasal flaring, retractions (sucking in of skin between ribs or above collarbone), or pauses in breathing lasting more than 15-20 seconds.
  • Color Changes: Bluish tint around the mouth or fingertips (cyanosis), persistent paleness, or worsening jaundice (yellowing of skin/eyes).
  • Poor Feeding: Refusing to feed, decreased wet diapers (fewer than 6-8 in 24 hours), or significant decrease in alertness during feeds.
  • Lethargy or Irritability: Excessive sleepiness, difficulty waking, or inconsolable crying.
  • Vomiting or Diarrhea: Forceful vomiting, persistent vomiting, or watery/bloody stools.
  • Signs of Infection: Redness, swelling, warmth, or discharge from the umbilical stump, circumcision site, or any skin area.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Infection Prevention: Practice strict hand hygiene for anyone handling the baby; limit visitors, especially those who are ill, and avoid crowded public places.
  • Safe Sleep Practices: Always place the baby on their back to sleep in a separate, safe sleep environment (crib or bassinet) without loose bedding, bumpers, pillows, or toys.
  • Temperature Regulation: Dress the baby appropriately for the environment; avoid overheating or chilling. Monitor room temperature to maintain a comfortable range.
  • Medication Administration: Administer all prescribed medications (e.g., vitamins, iron supplements, RSV prophylaxis) exactly as directed by the healthcare provider.
  • Car Seat Safety: Ensure proper installation and use of an infant car seat, specifically one approved for premature infants if necessary, and complete a car seat challenge prior to discharge.
  • Avoid Smoke Exposure: Keep the baby in a completely smoke-free environment at all times, including avoiding third-hand smoke on clothing or furniture.
  • CPR Training: Ensure primary caregivers are trained in infant cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) before discharge.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Feeding Schedule: Establish a consistent feeding schedule, typically every 2-3 hours, or as advised by the care team, even if it means gently waking the baby.
  • Breast Milk or Fortified Formula: Continue with breast milk, often fortified with human milk fortifier, or specialized premature infant formula as prescribed by the neonatologist.
  • Feeding Techniques: Ensure proper latch for breastfeeding or appropriate bottle-feeding techniques to minimize air intake and support efficient feeding.
  • Monitoring Intake: Track feeding volumes and duration, and monitor for signs of adequate hydration (e.g., sufficient wet diapers) and consistent weight gain.
  • Burping: Burp the baby frequently during and after feeds to reduce discomfort from swallowed air and aid digestion.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Know when to call 911 or seek immediate emergency medical care for severe breathing distress, unresponsiveness, seizures, or persistent cyanosis.
  • Apnea/Bradycardia Episodes: If the baby stops breathing for more than 15-20 seconds (apnea) or their heart rate drops significantly (bradycardia), initiate learned resuscitation steps and call for emergency medical help immediately.
  • Sudden Worsening: Any sudden and unexplained change in the baby's overall condition, behavior, or alertness warrants immediate medical evaluation.
  • Choking Hazard: Ensure all small objects are out of reach to prevent accidental choking.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Continuity of Care: Emphasize the critical importance of attending all scheduled follow-up appointments with the pediatrician, neonatologist, and any other specialists to monitor growth, development, and address any emerging concerns.
  • Trust Your Instincts: Parents know their baby best; if something feels wrong or you have concerns, do not hesitate to contact your healthcare provider immediately.
  • Medication Adherence: Stress the vital role of consistent and accurate administration of all prescribed medications and supplements, as they are crucial for your baby's health and development.
  • Immunizations: Discuss the importance of timely immunizations, including RSV prophylaxis (e.g., Palivizumab), as recommended for premature infants to protect against severe infections.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Discharge Planning: Develop an individualized discharge plan that includes assessing the home environment, parental readiness, and providing extensive education on essential care skills.
  • Parent Education & Reinforcement: Provide clear, concise, and repeated education on feeding techniques, medication administration, safe sleep practices, temperature monitoring, and identification of warning signs. Utilize teach-back methods to ensure comprehension.
  • Resource Provision: Ensure parents have readily accessible contact information for healthcare providers, emergency services, and relevant community support groups or early intervention programs.
  • Skill Demonstration & Competency: Observe and document parents demonstrating proficiency in critical care skills such as feeding, diapering, medication administration, and basic infant CPR.
  • Car Seat Challenge: Facilitate a car seat challenge prior to discharge to ensure cardiorespiratory stability while positioned in the car seat.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • RSV Prophylaxis Guidelines: Clinical guidelines for administration of Palivizumab (Synagis) to high-risk premature infants to prevent severe respiratory syncytial virus infection.
  • Apnea of Prematurity (AOP): Understanding the pathophysiology, monitoring requirements, and resolution criteria for AOP prior to discharge.
  • Neonatal Follow-up Clinics: Specialized multidisciplinary clinics providing ongoing developmental, medical, and therapeutic assessment for premature infants post-discharge.
  • Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC): Benefits and recommendations for continued skin-to-skin contact at home to promote physiological stability, bonding, and breastfeeding.
  • Infant Car Seat Challenge: A test performed prior to discharge to assess cardiorespiratory stability (apnea, bradycardia, desaturation) in the car seat position.
