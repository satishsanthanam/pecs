Congenital syphilis

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Newborns

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Congenital syphilis is a severe, multi-systemic infection in a newborn caused by the bacterium Treponema pallidum, transmitted from an infected mother to her baby during pregnancy or at birth.
  • Cause: The spirochete bacterium Treponema pallidum, which crosses the placenta from the mother to the fetus.
  • Impact: Can lead to stillbirth, prematurity, hydrops fetalis, or a wide range of clinical manifestations affecting nearly every organ system in the infant.
  • Prevention: It is entirely preventable with early and adequate penicillin treatment of the mother during pregnancy.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Early Congenital Syphilis (0-2 years): May present with hepatosplenomegaly, jaundice, anemia, mucocutaneous lesions (e.g., rash on palms and soles, perioral fissures), rhinitis ('snuffles'), pseudoparalysis (due to osteochondritis/periostitis), and lymphadenopathy.
  • Late Congenital Syphilis (after 2 years): Can manifest as Hutchinson's triad (interstitial keratitis, sensorineural hearing loss, Hutchinson's teeth), saddle nose deformity, saber shins, frontal bossing, neurosyphilis, and gummatous lesions.
  • Asymptomatic at Birth: Many infected infants are asymptomatic at birth but will develop signs of the disease if untreated.
  • Fetal Manifestations: Hydrops fetalis, intrauterine growth restriction, and prematurity can be observed prenatally.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Universal Maternal Screening: All pregnant women must be screened for syphilis at their first prenatal visit, again in the third trimester (28-32 weeks), and at delivery.
  • Prompt Maternal Treatment: Any pregnant woman diagnosed with syphilis must receive immediate and appropriate penicillin treatment to prevent fetal transmission.
  • Partner Notification and Treatment: Ensure all sexual partners of an infected pregnant woman are tested and treated to prevent reinfection.
  • Newborn Evaluation: Thorough clinical and laboratory evaluation of all newborns born to mothers with untreated or inadequately treated syphilis.
  • Avoidance of Self-Medication: Emphasize that only prescribed medical treatment is effective against syphilis; home remedies are ineffective and dangerous.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Support for Affected Infants: Infants with congenital syphilis, especially those with systemic involvement (e.g., hepatosplenomegaly, anemia), may require close monitoring of feeding and specialized nutritional support to ensure adequate growth and development.
  • Hydration Management: Ensure adequate fluid intake, particularly if the infant experiences fever or severe rhinitis that interferes with feeding.
  • Monitoring Growth Parameters: Closely track weight gain, length, and head circumference, adjusting feeding strategies as needed to support recovery and development.
  • Breastfeeding Considerations: Breastfeeding is generally safe for mothers with treated syphilis, but active lesions on the breast should be avoided. Consult with a healthcare provider.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Reporting Requirement: Congenital syphilis is a nationally notifiable condition; report all confirmed cases to public health authorities.
  • Maternal History Verification: Meticulously verify the mother's syphilis serology results and treatment history during pregnancy.
  • Comprehensive Newborn Assessment: Conduct a thorough physical examination of the newborn, specifically looking for stigmata of congenital syphilis.
  • Diagnostic Workup: Perform appropriate diagnostic tests on the newborn, including non-treponemal and treponemal serology, CSF analysis, complete blood count, liver function tests, and long bone radiographs.
  • Standard Precautions: Adhere to standard infection control precautions when caring for infants with congenital syphilis.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prevention is Paramount: The most effective strategy against congenital syphilis is universal prenatal screening and prompt, adequate treatment of infected pregnant women with penicillin.
  • Penicillin is the Cornerstone: Penicillin remains the only proven effective antibiotic for treating syphilis during pregnancy and for congenital syphilis in infants.
  • Thorough Evaluation: A comprehensive diagnostic workup is crucial for all infants at risk, even if asymptomatic, to guide appropriate treatment.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Management often requires collaboration among neonatologists, infectious disease specialists, ophthalmologists, and neurologists due to the multi-systemic nature of the disease.
  • Long-term Follow-up: Emphasize the necessity of long-term serological, audiological, ophthalmological, and neurodevelopmental follow-up for all treated infants.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate pregnant women on the importance of consistent prenatal care and syphilis screening to protect their babies.
  • Medication Administration: Accurately administer penicillin to infants, ensuring correct dosage, route, and monitoring for potential adverse reactions, including the Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Closely observe infants for signs of disease progression, treatment failure, or complications such as anemia, jaundice, or feeding difficulties.
  • Family Support: Provide empathetic support and clear education to families regarding the diagnosis, treatment plan, and the importance of follow-up appointments.
  • Documentation: Maintain meticulous records of maternal history, newborn assessment findings, diagnostic results, treatment administered, and follow-up plans.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Causative Agent: Treponema pallidum, a spirochete bacterium.
  • Transmission Route: Primarily transplacental (vertical transmission) from mother to fetus; rarely, direct contact with infectious lesions during birth.
  • Diagnostic Criteria (Infant): Positive non-treponemal test (RPR/VDRL) with a titer fourfold higher than mother's, positive treponemal test (TP-PA/FTA-ABS), clinical signs, abnormal CSF, or positive darkfield microscopy/PCR from lesions/tissue.
  • Treatment Protocol: Aqueous crystalline penicillin G or procaine penicillin G, dosage and duration depend on clinical classification and CSF findings.
  • Key Complications: Stillbirth, hydrops fetalis, prematurity, neurosyphilis, bone deformities, developmental delays, hearing loss, vision impairment, and hematological abnormalities.
