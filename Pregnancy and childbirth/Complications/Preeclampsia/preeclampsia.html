Preeclampsia

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Complications

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Preeclampsia is a serious hypertensive disorder that can occur during pregnancy, typically after 20 weeks gestation, or postpartum.
  • Key Features: Characterized by new-onset hypertension (blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg) and proteinuria, or hypertension with signs of organ dysfunction (e.g., kidney, liver, brain, blood).
  • Progression: It can range from mild to severe and, if untreated, may progress to eclampsia (seizures) or HELLP syndrome (Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets).
  • Maternal and Fetal Risk: Preeclampsia poses significant risks to both the mother and the developing fetus, requiring close monitoring and timely intervention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Headache: A new or worsening headache that does not improve with over-the-counter pain relievers.
  • Visual Disturbances: Blurred vision, seeing spots or flashing lights, or temporary loss of vision.
  • Right Upper Quadrant Pain: Severe pain in the abdomen, often described as epigastric pain or pain under the ribs on the right side.
  • Sudden Swelling: New or sudden swelling in the face, hands, or feet that is not typical for pregnancy.
  • Nausea or Vomiting: New onset of nausea or vomiting in the second half of pregnancy.
  • Shortness of Breath: Due to fluid in the lungs (pulmonary edema).
  • Hyperreflexia: Overactive reflexes, often assessed by a healthcare provider.
  • Decreased Urine Output: A significant reduction in the amount of urine produced.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Regular Prenatal Care: Adhere strictly to all scheduled prenatal appointments for blood pressure monitoring and urine tests.
  • Symptom Awareness: Be vigilant for any new or worsening symptoms and report them immediately to your healthcare provider.
  • Blood Pressure Monitoring: If advised, monitor your blood pressure at home and keep a detailed log for review.
  • Activity Modification: Follow specific recommendations regarding rest or activity restrictions as prescribed by your physician.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed antihypertensive medications exactly as directed, without skipping doses.
  • Avoidance of NSAIDs: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) should generally be avoided during pregnancy, especially with preeclampsia, as they can affect kidney function and blood pressure.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Intake: Moderate sodium restriction may be advised, focusing on avoiding highly processed foods and excessive salt. Severe sodium restriction is generally not recommended.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake as advised by your healthcare provider, typically water.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a diet rich in whole foods, lean proteins, fruits, and vegetables to support overall health.
  • Avoidance of Unhealthy Fats: Limit intake of saturated and trans fats found in fried foods and many processed snacks.
  • Calcium and Magnesium: Discuss with your physician if supplementation with calcium or magnesium is appropriate, as some studies suggest potential benefits.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Signs: Seek immediate medical attention for severe headache, persistent visual changes, severe right upper quadrant pain, or sudden shortness of breath.
  • Home Monitoring: If instructed, ensure accurate home blood pressure readings and maintain a symptom diary.
  • Support System: Educate family members or caregivers about preeclampsia symptoms and emergency protocols.
  • Postpartum Vigilance: Be aware that preeclampsia can develop or worsen in the postpartum period, requiring continued monitoring and symptom awareness.
  • Medication Management: Understand the purpose and side effects of all prescribed medications, especially magnesium sulfate if administered.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnosis: Preeclampsia is diagnosed based on specific criteria including blood pressure readings, proteinuria, and signs of organ dysfunction.
  • Management Goal: The primary goal is to prevent progression to severe complications like eclampsia and to ensure maternal and fetal well-being.
  • Definitive Treatment: Delivery of the baby and placenta is the only definitive cure for preeclampsia.
  • Medication Use: Antihypertensive medications (e.g., labetalol, nifedipine) are used to manage blood pressure, and magnesium sulfate is administered for seizure prophylaxis in severe cases.
  • Timing of Delivery: The timing of delivery is carefully considered based on gestational age, disease severity, and maternal/fetal status.
  • Long-term Health: Women with a history of preeclampsia have an increased lifetime risk of cardiovascular disease and should be monitored accordingly.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Perform frequent vital sign monitoring, including blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation.
  • Neurological Assessment: Monitor for changes in neurological status, including deep tendon reflexes, clonus, and headache severity.
  • Fluid Balance: Maintain strict intake and output records to assess renal function and fluid status.
  • Fetal Surveillance: Conduct regular fetal monitoring, including non-stress tests (NSTs) and biophysical profiles (BPPs), as ordered.
  • Magnesium Sulfate Administration: Understand the protocol for magnesium sulfate administration, including loading and maintenance doses, and monitor for signs of toxicity (e.g., respiratory depression, absent reflexes).
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and their families on symptom recognition, medication adherence, and the importance of follow-up care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by abnormal placental development leading to widespread maternal endothelial dysfunction, vasoconstriction, and systemic inflammation.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: ACOG guidelines define preeclampsia as new-onset hypertension (≥140/90 mmHg) after 20 weeks gestation with proteinuria or signs of end-organ damage.
  • Risk Factors: Nulliparity, history of preeclampsia, chronic hypertension, pre-gestational diabetes, multiple gestation, obesity, advanced maternal age, and certain autoimmune conditions.
  • Complications: Eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, placental abruption, acute kidney injury, pulmonary edema, stroke, fetal growth restriction, and preterm birth.
  • Pharmacology: Common medications include Labetalol, Nifedipine, Hydralazine (for acute severe hypertension), and Magnesium Sulfate (for seizure prophylaxis).
  • Postpartum Preeclampsia: Can occur up to six weeks postpartum, requiring continued vigilance and management.
