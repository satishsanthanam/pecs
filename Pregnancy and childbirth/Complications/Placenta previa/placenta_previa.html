Placenta previa

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Complications

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Placenta previa is a condition where the placenta partially or completely covers the cervix, the opening of the uterus.
  • Types: Can be complete (covers the entire os), partial (covers part of the os), marginal (reaches the edge of the os), or low-lying (implanted in the lower uterine segment but not covering the os).
  • Primary Concern: The main risk is severe bleeding (hemorrhage) during pregnancy or labor, as the cervix dilates and effaces.
  • Prevalence: More common in women with previous C-sections, multiple pregnancies, or advanced maternal age.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Painless Vaginal Bleeding: The hallmark symptom, typically bright red, occurring suddenly in the second or third trimester without pain or contractions.
  • Intermittent Bleeding: Bleeding episodes may stop spontaneously and then recur, sometimes becoming heavier.
  • Uterine Contractions: While usually painless, some women may experience mild, irregular uterine contractions accompanying the bleeding.
  • Fetal Distress: In cases of severe maternal hemorrhage, signs of fetal distress such as changes in heart rate may occur.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Vaginal Exams: Digital vaginal examinations are strictly contraindicated due to the high risk of precipitating severe hemorrhage.
  • Pelvic Rest: Abstain from sexual intercourse, douching, and insertion of tampons to prevent cervical irritation and bleeding.
  • Monitor Bleeding: Report any vaginal bleeding immediately to a healthcare provider, noting the amount, color, and frequency.
  • Hospitalization: May be required for close monitoring, especially with recurrent or heavy bleeding, to ensure immediate access to medical care.
  • Corticosteroid Administration: If preterm delivery is a possibility, corticosteroids may be given to accelerate fetal lung maturity.
  • Blood Transfusion Preparedness: Ensure blood products are cross-matched and readily available in anticipation of potential hemorrhage.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Iron-Rich Diet: Focus on foods high in iron (e.g., lean meats, fortified cereals, leafy greens) to prevent or manage iron-deficiency anemia due to potential blood loss.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake, especially if on bed rest, to prevent dehydration and support overall health.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate plenty of fiber (e.g., fruits, vegetables, whole grains) to prevent constipation, which can be exacerbated by reduced activity and iron supplements.
  • Avoid Stimulants: Limit caffeine and strictly avoid alcohol, as these can impact maternal and fetal well-being in a high-risk pregnancy.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Protocol Activation: Be prepared to activate the obstetric hemorrhage protocol immediately upon significant bleeding.
  • Maternal Vital Signs Monitoring: Continuously monitor maternal blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation for signs of hypovolemia.
  • Fetal Surveillance: Implement continuous fetal heart rate monitoring to assess fetal well-being and detect any signs of distress.
  • IV Access Management: Ensure at least one, preferably two, large-bore intravenous lines are patent for fluid resuscitation and blood product administration.
  • Emotional Support: Provide calm reassurance and psychological support to the patient and family, addressing their anxieties and fears.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnosis Confirmation: Placenta previa is definitively diagnosed via transvaginal ultrasound, typically during the second trimester.
  • Expectant Management: For asymptomatic cases, expectant management with close monitoring is the standard, allowing the placenta to potentially migrate away from the cervix.
  • Delivery Planning: Cesarean section is the preferred and often mandatory mode of delivery for complete placenta previa to prevent catastrophic hemorrhage.
  • Risk of Accreta Spectrum: Counsel patients on the increased risk of placenta accreta spectrum disorders, especially with a history of prior C-sections.
  • Postpartum Hemorrhage Risk: Be prepared for an increased risk of postpartum hemorrhage due to the lower uterine segment's reduced contractility.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients thoroughly on the signs of bleeding, the importance of pelvic rest, and when to seek immediate medical attention.
  • Monitoring Parameters: Understand the critical importance of accurate vital sign assessment, fetal heart rate interpretation, and estimated blood loss quantification.
  • Medication Administration: Be proficient in administering corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity and understanding the role of tocolytics if contractions are present.
  • Psychological Care: Recognize and address the significant psychological impact of a high-risk pregnancy on the patient and her family.
  • Pre-operative Preparation: Assist in preparing the patient for potential preterm delivery and Cesarean section, including consent, lab work, and emotional support.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: O44.0 (Placenta previa without hemorrhage), O44.1 (Placenta previa with hemorrhage).
  • Risk Factors: Previous Cesarean section, multiparity, advanced maternal age (>35 years), smoking, cocaine use, multiple gestation, previous uterine surgery.
  • Potential Complications: Preterm birth, severe maternal hemorrhage, placenta accreta spectrum, fetal growth restriction, stillbirth, need for hysterectomy.
  • Diagnostic Modality: Transvaginal ultrasound is the gold standard for accurate localization of the placenta relative to the internal cervical os.
  • Management Guidelines: Refer to current guidelines from organizations such as ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists) or RCOG (Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists).
