Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Complications

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is a liver disorder unique to pregnancy, characterized by impaired bile flow.
  • Primary Symptom: The hallmark symptom is intense itching (pruritus), often worse at night and predominantly affecting the palms of the hands and soles of the feet, without a rash.
  • Fetal Risk: ICP poses significant risks to the fetus, including an increased risk of preterm birth, meconium staining, fetal distress, and, in severe cases, stillbirth.
  • Maternal Impact: While primarily a concern for fetal well-being, mothers may experience discomfort from itching and potential vitamin K malabsorption.
  • Management: Requires close medical supervision, including regular monitoring of liver function, bile acid levels, and fetal well-being.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Pruritus: Generalized itching, often without a visible rash, intensifying at night and commonly affecting the palms and soles.
  • Jaundice: Yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes, occurring in a minority of cases due to bilirubin accumulation.
  • Dark Urine: Urine may appear darker than usual due to increased bilirubin excretion.
  • Pale Stools: Stools may be lighter in color due to reduced bile pigment reaching the intestines.
  • Fatigue: General tiredness and lethargy, which can be exacerbated by sleep disturbance from itching.
  • Nausea and Vomiting: While less specific, some pregnant individuals with ICP may report increased nausea or occasional vomiting.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Reporting of Itching: Any new or worsening severe itching, especially without a rash, must be reported to your healthcare provider immediately.
  • Adherence to Medication: Take prescribed medications, such as ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA), exactly as directed to help improve bile flow and reduce bile acid levels.
  • Fetal Surveillance: Expect and comply with regular fetal monitoring, including non-stress tests (NSTs) and biophysical profiles (BPPs), to assess fetal well-being.
  • Avoidance of Hepatotoxic Substances: Strictly avoid alcohol and any medications or supplements not approved by your physician, as these can further stress the liver.
  • Monitoring for Bleeding: Be vigilant for any signs of unusual bleeding or bruising, as severe ICP can sometimes lead to vitamin K deficiency and impaired clotting.
  • Postpartum Follow-up: Ensure follow-up liver function tests are performed postpartum to confirm resolution of the condition, as ICP typically resolves after delivery.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Reduced Fat Intake: Consider a diet lower in saturated and unhealthy fats to potentially ease the burden on the liver and gallbladder.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good hydration by drinking plenty of water throughout the day.
  • Vitamin K Supplementation: Your physician may recommend vitamin K supplementation due to potential malabsorption of fat-soluble vitamins, which can affect clotting factors.
  • Avoidance of Alcohol: Absolutely no alcohol consumption, as it is a known hepatotoxin and can worsen liver function.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eating smaller, more frequent meals may help manage any associated nausea or digestive discomfort.
  • Nutrient-Dense Foods: Focus on a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins to support overall health and liver function.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Symptom Reporting: Educate patients to report any new or escalating itching, especially nocturnal and palm/sole involvement, without delay.
  • Medication Compliance: Emphasize the critical importance of adhering to ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) regimen for maternal symptom relief and fetal protection.
  • Understanding Fetal Risks: Counsel patients on the increased risks of preterm birth, meconium aspiration, and stillbirth associated with ICP, and the rationale for intensive fetal monitoring.
  • No Self-Treatment: Advise against using over-the-counter creams or antihistamines for itching without prior consultation, as they are often ineffective for ICP-related pruritus and may mask symptoms.
  • Postpartum Resolution: Inform patients that ICP typically resolves rapidly after delivery, but follow-up liver function tests are essential to confirm complete resolution.
  • Recurrence Risk: Discuss the high likelihood of ICP recurrence in subsequent pregnancies and the need for early screening.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: Prompt diagnosis through serum bile acid levels and liver function tests is crucial for timely intervention and fetal risk mitigation.
  • Risk Stratification: Management decisions, including timing of delivery, are guided by the severity of bile acid elevation and gestational age.
  • Ursodeoxycholic Acid (UDCA): UDCA remains the first-line pharmacological treatment, improving maternal symptoms and potentially reducing fetal complications.
  • Intensive Fetal Surveillance: Close monitoring of fetal well-being with non-stress tests and biophysical profiles is paramount, especially in severe cases.
  • Individualized Delivery Planning: The decision for induction of labor is individualized, balancing the risks of prematurity against the increasing risk of stillbirth with advancing gestation in uncontrolled ICP.
  • Postpartum Assessment: Ensure complete resolution of biochemical abnormalities postpartum, as persistent elevation may indicate an underlying liver disorder.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients about ICP symptoms, the importance of fetal monitoring, and medication adherence.
  • Pruritus Management: Provide comfort measures for itching, such as cool baths, loose cotton clothing, and avoiding irritants, while reinforcing that these are palliative, not curative.
  • Monitoring Parameters: Understand the significance of liver function tests (LFTs) and serum bile acid levels in assessing disease severity and treatment efficacy.
  • Fetal Monitoring Support: Assist with and interpret fetal monitoring results (NSTs, BPPs) and recognize signs of fetal distress.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient anxiety and sleep disturbances related to severe itching and concerns about fetal well-being.
  • Postpartum Care: Educate on the need for postpartum follow-up to confirm resolution and discuss future pregnancy planning regarding ICP recurrence.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by impaired hepatobiliary transport of bile acids, leading to their accumulation in the maternal and fetal circulation, influenced by genetic predisposition and pregnancy hormones.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Diagnosis relies on the presence of pruritus in pregnancy, elevated serum total bile acids (>10 µmol/L), and exclusion of other liver diseases.
  • First-Line Treatment: Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) is the cornerstone of treatment, improving bile flow, reducing bile acid levels, and alleviating maternal pruritus.
  • Fetal Complications: Increased risks include spontaneous preterm birth, iatrogenic preterm birth, meconium-stained amniotic fluid, fetal distress, and sudden intrauterine fetal demise (stillbirth).
  • Maternal Complications: Primarily pruritus, but can include steatorrhea and vitamin K malabsorption, potentially leading to coagulopathy in severe, untreated cases.
  • Recurrence Rate: ICP has a high recurrence rate, estimated at 45-90% in subsequent pregnancies.
