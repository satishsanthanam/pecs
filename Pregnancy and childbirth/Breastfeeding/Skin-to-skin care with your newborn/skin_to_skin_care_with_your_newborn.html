Skin-to-skin care with your newborn

🏠 Home / Pregnancy and childbirth / Breastfeeding

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Enhanced Bonding: Facilitates immediate emotional connection between parent and newborn, promoting attachment.
  • Thermoregulation: Helps stabilize the newborn's body temperature more effectively than an incubator.
  • Breastfeeding Initiation: Encourages early and successful latching by allowing the baby to self-attach to the breast.
  • Reduced Crying: Calms the newborn, decreasing stress and energy expenditure.
  • Stable Vital Signs: Promotes more stable heart rate, breathing, and blood sugar levels in the infant.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Newborn Distress: Indicated for fussy or crying newborns to provide comfort and reduce stress.
  • Temperature Instability: Recommended for newborns experiencing mild hypothermia or difficulty maintaining warmth.
  • Feeding Challenges: Beneficial for infants struggling with initial latch or feeding cues, promoting natural instincts.
  • Parental Anxiety: Helps reduce stress and increase confidence in new parents by fostering a sense of connection and competence.
  • Low Blood Sugar Risk: Supports glucose stability in newborns, especially those at risk for hypoglycemia.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Clear Airway: Always ensure the newborn's nose and mouth are unobstructed and visible.
  • Secure Positioning: Position the baby upright on the parent's chest, tummy-to-tummy, with head turned to the side.
  • Parental Alertness: The parent should be awake and alert during skin-to-skin care to monitor the baby safely.
  • Supportive Environment: Ensure a warm, draft-free room and cover the baby's back with a blanket for added warmth.
  • Risk of Falls: Perform skin-to-skin in a safe, reclined position in a bed or chair, never while standing or walking.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Promotes Early Feeding: Skin-to-skin contact stimulates the newborn's innate rooting and sucking reflexes, facilitating the first breastfeed.
  • Enhances Milk Production: The close physical contact and hormonal release (oxytocin) support the mother's milk supply.
  • Recognizing Feeding Cues: Helps parents learn to identify early hunger cues from their newborn more effectively.
  • Parental Hydration: Encourage the breastfeeding parent to maintain adequate fluid intake to support milk production.
  • Post-Sedation Reintroduction: For parents who received epidural or other medications, skin-to-skin can help normalize baby's feeding readiness.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Support Parent Comfort: Assist the parent in finding a comfortable, reclined position for skin-to-skin care.
  • Ensure Privacy: Provide a private and calm environment to encourage relaxation and bonding.
  • Monitor Newborn: Regularly observe the newborn's breathing, color, and alertness.
  • Educate on Safety: Instruct parents on safe positioning, airway clearance, and when to call for assistance.
  • Facilitate Initial Latch: Offer gentle guidance for breastfeeding if the parent desires, without interrupting the natural process.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Neurodevelopmental Benefits: Crucial for optimal brain development and sensory integration in newborns.
  • Physiological Stability: Recommended for stabilizing heart rate, respiratory rate, and blood glucose levels, especially in vulnerable infants.
  • Attachment Promotion: Strongly endorsed for fostering secure parent-infant attachment and reducing postpartum mood disorders.
  • Duration Recommendation: Advise at least 60 minutes of uninterrupted skin-to-skin immediately after birth, and frequently thereafter.
  • Premature Infant Support: Essential component of Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC) for improving outcomes in preterm babies.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC): Understand the principles and application of KMC for both term and preterm infants.
  • Assessment of Readiness: Evaluate maternal and neonatal stability before initiating and during skin-to-skin care.
  • Parental Education: Provide comprehensive teaching on the benefits, safety, and techniques of skin-to-skin contact.
  • Documentation: Accurately record the initiation, duration, and infant's response to skin-to-skin care in the patient chart.
  • Promoting Early Initiation: Advocate for immediate skin-to-skin contact in the delivery room, unless contraindicated by medical necessity.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Thermoregulation: The physiological process by which the body maintains its core internal temperature.
  • Oxytocin Release: A hormone released during skin-to-skin contact, promoting bonding, uterine contractions, and milk ejection.
  • Cortisol Reduction: Skin-to-skin helps lower stress hormones in both parent and infant.
  • Neonatal Reflexes: Innate reflexes like rooting, sucking, and grasping are stimulated and supported by skin-to-skin.
  • Breast Crawl: The newborn's instinctive ability to move towards the breast and self-attach during skin-to-skin.
