Uveitis

🏠 Home / Eyes and vision / Eye inflammation

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Uveitis is an inflammation of the uvea, the middle layer of the eye, which includes the iris, ciliary body, and choroid.
  • Types: It is classified by the part of the uvea affected: anterior (iritis), intermediate (pars planitis), posterior (choroiditis), or panuveitis (all parts).
  • Causes: Can be caused by autoimmune diseases (e.g., ankylosing spondylitis, sarcoidosis), infections (e.g., herpes, toxoplasmosis, syphilis), eye injury, or may be idiopathic (unknown cause).
  • Impact: If left untreated, uveitis can lead to severe complications, including permanent vision loss.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Eye Pain: Often a deep, aching pain, particularly in anterior uveitis.
  • Redness: Significant redness of the eye, especially around the iris (limbal injection).
  • Photophobia: Increased sensitivity and discomfort in bright light.
  • Blurred Vision: Vision may become hazy or blurry due to inflammation and cells in the vitreous.
  • Floaters: Appearance of dark spots, lines, or 'cobwebs' moving across the field of vision, more common in intermediate and posterior uveitis.
  • Miosis: The pupil may appear constricted or irregular in shape.
  • Hypopyon: In severe cases, a visible layer of white blood cells (pus) may accumulate at the bottom of the anterior chamber.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Prompt Ophthalmological Evaluation: Seek immediate medical attention from an ophthalmologist if you experience sudden eye pain, redness, or vision changes.
  • Adherence to Treatment Plan: Strictly follow all prescribed medication regimens, including corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, or mydriatics, to control inflammation and prevent recurrence.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor disease activity, intraocular pressure, and potential side effects of treatment.
  • Avoid Self-Medication: Do not use over-the-counter eye drops or discontinue prescribed medications without consulting your eye care specialist.
  • Systemic Workup: If uveitis is recurrent or severe, cooperate with your physician for a thorough systemic evaluation to identify and manage any underlying conditions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Anti-inflammatory Diet: While not a direct treatment, a diet rich in omega-3 fatty acids (found in fish, flaxseed) and antioxidants (from fruits and vegetables) may support overall health and potentially reduce systemic inflammation.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support general ocular health and systemic well-being.
  • Medication Interactions: Be aware of potential dietary interactions with prescribed medications (e.g., grapefruit with certain immunosuppressants) and discuss these with your physician or pharmacist.
  • Avoidance of Processed Foods: Limiting highly processed foods, excessive sugar, and unhealthy fats may contribute to a healthier inflammatory profile, though direct evidence for uveitis-specific dietary restrictions is limited.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Risk of Vision Loss: Untreated or inadequately managed uveitis can lead to serious complications such as glaucoma, cataracts, macular edema, and permanent vision impairment.
  • Recurrence Potential: Uveitis often recurs; patients must remain vigilant for symptom re-emergence and seek prompt care.
  • Corticosteroid Side Effects: Long-term use of corticosteroids, a common treatment, carries risks including elevated intraocular pressure, cataract formation, and systemic side effects.
  • Immunosuppressant Monitoring: Patients on immunosuppressive therapy require regular blood tests to monitor for adverse effects and ensure therapeutic drug levels.
  • Driving Restrictions: Vision impairment due to uveitis or its treatment (e.g., dilating drops) may temporarily affect driving ability.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early and Aggressive Treatment: Timely diagnosis and initiation of appropriate therapy are critical to control inflammation, prevent structural damage, and preserve visual function.
  • Identify Underlying Causes: A comprehensive diagnostic workup is essential to uncover any systemic diseases or infections contributing to uveitis, guiding targeted treatment.
  • Individualized Management: Treatment protocols are highly individualized, tailored to the specific type, severity, and underlying etiology of uveitis.
  • Patient Education is Key: Educating patients about their condition, medication adherence, potential complications, and the importance of regular follow-ups empowers them in their long-term care.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Collaboration with rheumatologists, infectious disease specialists, or other subspecialists is often necessary for optimal patient outcomes, especially in cases of systemic association.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Ocular Assessment: Perform detailed history taking focusing on symptom onset, duration, associated systemic conditions, and current medications. Assess visual acuity, pupil reaction, and observe for signs of inflammation.
  • Medication Administration & Education: Ensure correct administration of topical eye drops (e.g., steroids, mydriatics) and systemic medications. Educate patients on dosage, frequency, potential side effects, and proper technique.
  • Pain and Photophobia Management: Advise patients on strategies to alleviate discomfort, such as wearing sunglasses, using dim lighting, and administering prescribed pain relief.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Educate patients on the signs and symptoms of potential complications like glaucoma (severe eye pain, halos around lights) or cataract progression, and when to seek urgent care.
  • Emotional Support: Provide empathetic support and resources for patients coping with a chronic eye condition that can impact daily life and vision.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: H20.0 (Acute iridocyclitis), H20.1 (Chronic iridocyclitis), H30.0 (Choroiditis), H30.2 (Posterior cyclitis), H44.1 (Panuveitis).
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Slit-lamp examination, fundoscopy, optical coherence tomography (OCT), fluorescein angiography, anterior chamber paracentesis, vitreous biopsy, laboratory tests (e.g., HLA-B27, ANA, ACE, RPR, Lyme serology).
  • Pharmacological Agents: Topical/systemic corticosteroids (e.g., prednisolone, dexamethasone), mydriatics (e.g., atropine, cyclopentolate), NSAIDs, immunosuppressants (e.g., methotrexate, azathioprine, cyclosporine), biologics (e.g., adalimumab, infliximab).
  • Potential Complications: Glaucoma, cataracts, macular edema, synechiae (adhesions), hypotony, retinal detachment, optic neuropathy, cystoid macular edema.
  • Differential Diagnoses: Conjunctivitis, acute angle-closure glaucoma, scleritis, endophthalmitis, ocular foreign body.
