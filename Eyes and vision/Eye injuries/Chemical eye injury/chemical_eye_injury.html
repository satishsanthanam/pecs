Chemical eye injury

🏠 Home / Eyes and vision / Eye injuries

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Immediate Danger: Chemical eye injuries are medical emergencies requiring immediate action to prevent permanent vision loss or severe ocular damage.
  • Alkali vs. Acid Burns: Alkali burns (e.g., lye, ammonia, cement) are generally more severe due to liquefaction necrosis, allowing deeper penetration into ocular tissues. Acid burns (e.g., battery acid, vinegar) cause coagulation necrosis, often limiting penetration but still highly damaging.
  • Urgency of Irrigation: The most critical first step is copious and immediate irrigation of the affected eye with water or saline for at least 15-30 minutes.
  • Potential for Complications: Even after initial treatment, complications such as corneal opacification, glaucoma, cataracts, symblepharon, and limbal stem cell deficiency can develop.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Eye Pain: Intense, sharp, or burning pain in the affected eye.
  • Redness and Irritation: Profound conjunctival hyperemia and irritation, often with chemosis (swelling of the conjunctiva).
  • Blurred or Decreased Vision: Immediate or progressive loss of visual acuity.
  • Excessive Tearing: Reflex lacrimation in response to irritation and pain.
  • Photophobia: Extreme sensitivity to light.
  • Inability to Open Eye: Due to pain, swelling, or blepharospasm.
  • Foreign Body Sensation: Feeling as if something is in the eye.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Protective Eyewear: Always wear appropriate safety goggles or face shields when handling chemicals, working in laboratories, or engaging in activities with potential for eye exposure.
  • Eyewash Station Access: Know the location of emergency eyewash stations in any environment where chemicals are present and ensure they are functional and easily accessible.
  • Immediate Irrigation: In case of chemical exposure, begin irrigation immediately, even before seeking medical help. Do not delay.
  • Avoid Rubbing: Do not rub the affected eye, as this can spread the chemical and worsen damage.
  • Contact Lens Removal: Remove contact lenses immediately during irrigation, as they can trap chemicals against the eye surface.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fluid for Irrigation: Use only clean tap water or sterile saline solution for immediate and continuous eye irrigation; avoid any other liquids or neutralizing agents.
  • Avoid Topical Irritants: Refrain from applying any unprescribed creams, ointments, or drops to the eye, as these can introduce infection or react adversely with residual chemicals.
  • Systemic Hydration Support: Maintain adequate systemic hydration to support overall physiological healing processes, especially if oral medications are prescribed for pain or inflammation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Call Emergency Services: Immediately contact emergency medical services (e.g., 911 or local equivalent) or transport the patient to the nearest emergency department.
  • Initiate Irrigation: Guide the patient to an eyewash station or use a gentle stream of water to irrigate the affected eye(s) for a minimum of 15-30 minutes. Ensure eyelids are held open.
  • Remove Contact Lenses: Assist the patient in removing any contact lenses during irrigation, if present.
  • Do Not Neutralize: Do not attempt to neutralize the chemical with another substance, as this can generate heat and cause further damage.
  • Identify Chemical: If safe to do so, try to identify the chemical agent involved and bring the container or safety data sheet (SDS) to the medical facility.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prompt Ophthalmologic Evaluation: Immediate consultation with an ophthalmologist is crucial for comprehensive assessment and management.
  • pH Assessment: Measure the ocular surface pH after initial irrigation to confirm neutralization (target pH 7.0-7.4) and guide further irrigation duration.
  • Chemical Identification: Ascertain the specific chemical agent (acid, alkali, solvent) to anticipate potential complications and tailor treatment.
  • Topical Medications: Prescribe appropriate topical antibiotics to prevent infection, cycloplegics to reduce pain and ciliary spasm, and potentially topical corticosteroids for inflammation in selected cases.
  • Pain Management: Administer systemic and topical analgesics to manage severe pain.
  • Monitor for Complications: Closely monitor for corneal epithelial defects, stromal opacification, intraocular pressure changes, and limbal stem cell damage.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Prioritize Irrigation: Understand that immediate and continuous irrigation is the single most important intervention in chemical eye injuries.
  • Proper Irrigation Technique: Learn and practice correct techniques for eye irrigation, ensuring eyelids are everted and the entire ocular surface is flushed.
  • Pain Assessment and Management: Accurately assess pain levels and administer prescribed analgesics, recognizing that severe pain can hinder patient cooperation.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on the importance of follow-up appointments, proper medication instillation, and signs of worsening condition.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document the time of injury, type of chemical, duration and volume of irrigation, initial and post-irrigation pH, visual acuity, and all interventions.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Liquefaction Necrosis: Characteristic tissue damage caused by alkali burns, leading to deep penetration and widespread destruction.
  • Coagulation Necrosis: Tissue damage caused by acid burns, forming a protective barrier that can limit deeper penetration but still causes significant surface damage.
  • Limbal Stem Cell Deficiency (LSCD): Damage to the stem cells at the corneal limbus, leading to chronic epithelial defects, vascularization, and vision loss.
  • Symblepharon: Adhesion between the conjunctiva of the eyelid and the eyeball, a common complication of severe chemical burns.
  • Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Monitoring: Essential due to the risk of secondary glaucoma following chemical injury, especially with severe inflammation.
  • Ocular Surface Reconstruction: Surgical interventions, such as limbal stem cell transplantation or amniotic membrane transplantation, for severe cases with chronic sequelae.
