# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Chronic hives, or chronic urticaria, are recurrent itchy welts (wheals) that appear daily or almost daily for six weeks or more.
* **Types:** Can be chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) with no identifiable cause, or chronic inducible urticaria (CIndU) triggered by specific physical stimuli like cold, pressure, or heat.
* **Prevalence:** Affects approximately 0.5-1% of the population, significantly impacting quality of life due to persistent itching and discomfort.
* **Nature:** While often mistaken for allergies, most cases of chronic urticaria are not allergic in nature; many are autoimmune or idiopathic.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Pruritic Welts:** Red or skin-colored raised welts (wheals) that are intensely itchy, vary in size, and can appear anywhere on the body, often fading within 24 hours without leaving a trace.
* **Angioedema:** Swelling in deeper layers of the skin, often around the eyes, lips, tongue, throat, or genitals, which can be painful or burning rather than itchy.
* **Pain/Burning Sensation:** Some individuals experience a burning or stinging sensation accompanying the welts, particularly with angioedema.
* **Systemic Symptoms:** In some cases, especially with underlying autoimmune conditions, fatigue, joint pain, headache, or fever may be present.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Trigger Avoidance:** If specific triggers for chronic inducible urticaria are identified (e.g., cold, pressure, heat), strict avoidance is crucial to prevent flare-ups.
* **Scratching Prevention:** Avoid excessive scratching to prevent skin damage, secondary infections, and further irritation of the hives.
* **Emergency Preparedness:** Patients with a history of severe angioedema, especially involving the throat or airways, should carry an epinephrine auto-injector and know how to use it.
* **Medication Adherence:** Strictly follow prescribed medication regimens, even during periods of remission, as chronic hives often require ongoing management.
* **Irritant Minimization:** Wear loose-fitting clothing and avoid harsh soaps or lotions that can irritate the skin and potentially exacerbate symptoms.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Histamine-Rich Foods:** Some individuals with chronic urticaria may benefit from avoiding histamine-rich foods (e.g., aged cheeses, fermented products, processed meats, certain fish) or histamine-releasing foods (e.g., strawberries, tomatoes, chocolate, alcohol), though this is not universally effective.
* **Pseudoallergen-Free Diet:** A trial of a pseudoallergen-free diet under medical supervision may be considered for some patients, eliminating food additives, preservatives, and natural salicylates.
* **Hydration:** Maintain adequate hydration to support overall skin health and potentially reduce skin dryness that can worsen itching.
* **Alcohol and Caffeine:** Limit or avoid alcohol and caffeine, as they can sometimes act as vasodilators and exacerbate urticaria symptoms in susceptible individuals.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Seek Urgent Care:** Immediately seek emergency medical attention if experiencing difficulty breathing, swallowing, dizziness, or a sudden drop in blood pressure, as these can indicate severe angioedema or anaphylaxis.
* **Avoid Self-Medication:** Do not self-prescribe or overuse over-the-counter medications, especially corticosteroids, without physician guidance, due to potential side effects.
* **Comprehensive Disclosure:** Inform all healthcare providers about your chronic urticaria diagnosis and all current medications, supplements, and herbal remedies to prevent adverse drug interactions.
* **Symptom Diary:** Maintain a detailed symptom diary, noting the timing, appearance, duration, and potential triggers of hives, as well as the effectiveness of medications.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Accurate Diagnosis:** A thorough history and physical examination are crucial to differentiate chronic urticaria from other skin conditions and to identify potential underlying causes or triggers.
* **First-Line Treatment:** High-dose, non-sedating H1 antihistamines are the cornerstone of treatment, often requiring doses up to four times the standard to achieve symptom control.
* **Advanced Therapies:** For refractory cases, advanced therapies such as omalizumab (anti-IgE monoclonal antibody), cyclosporine, or other immunosuppressants may be considered.
* **Referral:** Patients with persistent, severe, or atypical chronic urticaria should be referred to an allergist or dermatologist for specialized management and workup.
* **Psychological Support:** Acknowledge the significant psychological impact of chronic itching and discomfort; consider counseling or support groups as part of a holistic management plan.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pathophysiology:** Chronic urticaria involves mast cell degranulation and release of histamine and other mediators, leading to vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, and nerve stimulation.
* **Nursing Assessment:** Conduct a comprehensive skin assessment, document the characteristics of welts and angioedema, and obtain a detailed history regarding symptom onset, duration, triggers, and impact on daily life.
* **Patient Education:** Educate patients on medication adherence, proper use of emergency medications, trigger identification and avoidance, and skin care practices to minimize irritation.
* **Differential Diagnoses:** Be aware of conditions that can mimic chronic urticaria, such as drug reactions, autoimmune diseases (e.g., lupus, thyroid disease), mastocytosis, and urticarial vasculitis.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **ICD-10 Codes:** L50.1 (Idiopathic urticaria), L50.2 (Urticaria due to cold and heat), L50.3 (Dermatographic urticaria), L50.4 (Vibratory urticaria), L50.5 (Cholinergic urticaria), L50.6 (Contact urticaria), L50.8 (Other urticaria), L50.9 (Urticaria, unspecified).
* **Key Diagnostic Tools:** Detailed patient history, physical examination, Urticaria Activity Score (UAS7), blood tests (CBC, ESR, CRP, thyroid function tests, ANA, IgE levels) to rule out underlying conditions.
* **Pharmacological Classes:** H1-antihistamines (e.g., cetirizine, fexofenadine, loratadine, hydroxyzine), H2-antihistamines (e.g., ranitidine, cimetidine), Leukotriene receptor antagonists (e.g., montelukast), Omalizumab, Cyclosporine, Dapsone.
* **Urticaria Activity Score (UAS7):** A validated tool used to assess disease activity and treatment response, based on daily scores for wheals and pruritus over seven consecutive days.
