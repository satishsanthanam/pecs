How to use your child's metered dose inhaler

🏠 Home / Allergies and asthma / Asthma

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of MDIs: Metered Dose Inhalers deliver medication directly to the lungs, crucial for managing asthma symptoms and preventing exacerbations.
  • Importance of Spacer: A spacer device is highly recommended for children as it helps deliver more medication to the lungs and reduces medication deposition in the mouth and throat.
  • Regular Use: Controller medications in MDIs must be used regularly as prescribed, even when the child feels well, to maintain asthma control.
  • Rescue Inhalers: Rescue inhalers (e.g., albuterol) are used for quick relief of sudden asthma symptoms like wheezing or shortness of breath.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Wheezing: A high-pitched whistling sound, especially when exhaling, indicating narrowed airways.
  • Shortness of Breath: Difficulty breathing, rapid breathing, or gasping for air.
  • Persistent Cough: A cough that is dry, hacking, or worsens at night or with activity.
  • Chest Tightness: A feeling of pressure or squeezing in the chest.
  • Exercise-Induced Symptoms: Coughing, wheezing, or shortness of breath during or after physical activity.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Shake Inhaler Well: Always shake the MDI vigorously for 5 seconds before each puff to ensure proper mixing of the medication.
  • Prime New Inhalers: If the inhaler is new or hasn't been used for several days, prime it by spraying a few test puffs into the air away from the face until a fine mist appears.
  • Check Expiration Date: Ensure the medication has not expired. Expired medication may be less effective.
  • Clean Spacer Regularly: Wash the spacer device at least once a week with warm water and mild detergent, air dry completely without wiping to prevent static.
  • Monitor for Side Effects: Observe for potential side effects such as jitters, increased heart rate (with rescue inhalers), or oral thrush (with steroid inhalers).

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Post-Inhalation Oral Care: After using a corticosteroid inhaler, have the child rinse their mouth thoroughly with water and spit it out to prevent oral thrush. Brushing teeth afterward is also beneficial.
  • Hydration: Encourage adequate fluid intake, especially during asthma exacerbations, to help thin mucus and ease breathing.
  • Avoid Irritants: While not a dietary restriction, avoid foods or beverages that may trigger reflux, which can sometimes exacerbate asthma symptoms in sensitive individuals.
  • No Specific Food Restrictions: There are no general dietary restrictions directly associated with the use of metered dose inhalers themselves, beyond managing potential side effects like oral thrush.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Follow Asthma Action Plan: Adhere strictly to the child's individualized asthma action plan provided by their healthcare provider.
  • Emergency Situations: Seek immediate medical attention if the child's breathing difficulty worsens rapidly, lips or fingernails turn blue, or they have trouble speaking.
  • Correct Dosage: Never exceed the prescribed number of puffs or frequency of use without consulting a healthcare professional.
  • Do Not Share: Inhalers are prescribed for individual use and should never be shared with others.
  • Proper Storage: Store inhalers at room temperature, away from extreme heat or cold, and out of direct sunlight.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Demonstrate Technique: Parents/guardians should be able to confidently demonstrate the correct inhaler technique with a spacer to their physician or nurse.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Schedule regular appointments to review the child's asthma control, adjust medication as needed, and re-evaluate inhaler technique.
  • Personalized Action Plan: Ensure the family has a clear, written asthma action plan detailing daily management and steps for worsening symptoms.
  • Adherence is Key: Emphasize that consistent and correct use of controller medications is vital for preventing asthma attacks and improving quality of life.
  • Environmental Triggers: Advise on identifying and minimizing exposure to environmental asthma triggers in the home and school.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate parents/guardians and older children on the purpose, correct technique, and maintenance of MDIs and spacers.
  • Technique Assessment: Observe and assess the child's and caregiver's inhaler technique, providing constructive feedback and re-demonstration as needed.
  • Spacer Selection: Understand the different types of spacers available (e.g., valved holding chambers) and their appropriate use for various age groups.
  • Documentation: Document patient education provided, demonstrated technique, and any observed challenges or side effects.
  • Recognizing Poor Technique: Identify common errors such as not shaking the inhaler, incorrect breathing coordination, or inadequate spacer seal.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • MDI Components: Comprises a pressurized canister containing medication, a metering valve, and an actuator.
  • Mechanism of Action: Delivers a precise dose of medication as a fine mist, which is inhaled into the lungs.
  • Spacer Functionality: Reduces the velocity of the aerosolized medication, allowing for better coordination of inhalation and minimizing oropharyngeal deposition.
  • Common Medications: Includes bronchodilators (e.g., albuterol, levalbuterol) and inhaled corticosteroids (e.g., fluticasone, budesonide).
  • Flow Rate & Coordination: Optimal MDI use requires slow, deep inhalation coordinated with actuation, which spacers facilitate, especially in children.
