Cough in adults

🏠 Home / Allergies and asthma / Allergies

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Allergic Cough Characteristics: Often a dry, persistent cough, frequently worse at night or upon waking, and typically non-productive.
  • Common Triggers: Caused by exposure to airborne allergens such as pollen, dust mites, pet dander, mold spores, and certain irritants like smoke or strong perfumes.
  • Distinguishing Feature: Unlike coughs from infections, allergic coughs are not usually accompanied by fever or body aches, but often by other allergy symptoms.
  • Underlying Conditions: Can be a primary symptom of allergic rhinitis (hay fever) or a manifestation of allergic asthma.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Postnasal Drip: Sensation of mucus dripping down the back of the throat, leading to throat clearing and cough.
  • Nasal Symptoms: Concurrent sneezing, runny nose (rhinorrhea), or nasal congestion (stuffy nose).
  • Ocular & Pharyngeal Itching: Itchy, watery eyes, and an itchy sensation in the nose, throat, or roof of the mouth.
  • Throat Irritation: A scratchy or tickling sensation in the throat that prompts coughing.
  • Wheezing & Dyspnea: In cases where allergic asthma is present, wheezing, chest tightness, or shortness of breath may accompany the cough.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Allergen Avoidance: Identify and minimize exposure to specific allergens through environmental control measures (e.g., using HEPA filters, dust mite covers).
  • Medication Adherence: Consistently take prescribed antihistamines, nasal corticosteroids, or leukotriene modifiers as directed to manage allergic response.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially warm liquids, to help soothe the throat and thin mucus, making it easier to clear.
  • Avoid Irritants: Steer clear of tobacco smoke, strong chemical fumes, and highly fragranced products that can exacerbate airway irritation.
  • Environmental Control: Regularly clean living spaces, vacuum with HEPA filters, and ensure proper ventilation to reduce indoor allergen load.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Warm Fluid Intake: Encourage warm teas, broths, and water to help soothe an irritated throat and keep mucus thin.
  • Avoid Irritating Foods: Limit very cold beverages, highly acidic foods (e.g., citrus, tomatoes), or excessively spicy foods if they trigger throat irritation or cough.
  • Consider Oral Allergy Syndrome (OAS): Be aware that certain raw fruits, vegetables, or nuts can cause oral itching or throat irritation in individuals with pollen allergies due to cross-reactivity.
  • Anti-inflammatory Diet: Focus on a diet rich in anti-inflammatory foods, such as those high in omega-3 fatty acids and antioxidants, which may support overall respiratory health.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Persistent Cough: Seek medical evaluation if the cough lasts longer than 3-8 weeks, despite allergen avoidance and over-the-counter treatments.
  • Worsening Symptoms: Consult a physician if the cough becomes more severe, is accompanied by wheezing, shortness of breath, or chest tightness.
  • New or Concerning Symptoms: Promptly report any new symptoms such as fever, unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or coughing up blood.
  • Ineffective Treatment: If prescribed allergy medications are not providing adequate relief or if symptoms significantly impact daily activities or sleep.
  • Asthma Exacerbation: Be vigilant for signs of an asthma attack, such as severe shortness of breath, rapid breathing, or blue lips, and seek immediate medical attention.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: Emphasize the importance of identifying specific allergens through skin prick tests or specific IgE blood tests to guide targeted treatment.
  • Personalized Treatment Plan: Recommend a comprehensive approach including allergen avoidance, pharmacotherapy (antihistamines, nasal corticosteroids, leukotriene modifiers), and potentially immunotherapy.
  • Asthma Screening: Evaluate for co-existing asthma, as allergic cough can be a primary symptom, and ensure appropriate management if diagnosed.
  • Patient Education: Counsel patients on proper medication administration techniques (e.g., inhaler use, nasal spray technique) and the importance of adherence.
  • Regular Follow-up: Schedule follow-up appointments to monitor symptom control, adjust treatment as needed, and address any new concerns.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Assessment: Conduct a thorough history focusing on cough characteristics (onset, duration, triggers, relieving factors), associated symptoms, and environmental exposures.
  • Education on Allergen Avoidance: Instruct patients on practical strategies for reducing allergen exposure in their home and work environments.
  • Medication Teaching: Provide clear instructions on the purpose, dosage, administration, and potential side effects of prescribed allergy medications.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Educate patients on how to track their symptoms and identify signs of worsening or potential complications, such as asthma exacerbation.
  • Referral for Allergy Testing: Prepare patients for allergy testing procedures and explain the rationale behind identifying specific triggers.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Allergic Rhinitis: Inflammation of the nasal passages caused by an allergic reaction to airborne allergens, often leading to postnasal drip and cough.
  • Postnasal Drip Syndrome (PNDS) / Upper Airway Cough Syndrome (UACS): A common cause of chronic cough where mucus from the nose and sinuses drips down the back of the throat.
  • Asthma: A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways, often triggered by allergens, leading to bronchoconstriction, wheezing, and cough.
  • Allergy Testing: Diagnostic methods including skin prick tests (SPT) and specific IgE blood tests to identify causative allergens.
  • Pharmacotherapy: Includes H1-antihistamines, intranasal corticosteroids (INCS), leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRAs), and mast cell stabilizers.
  • Immunotherapy: A long-term treatment option (allergy shots or sublingual tablets) that desensitizes the immune system to specific allergens.
