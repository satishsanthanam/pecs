Nerve blocks

🏠 Home / Anesthesia / Anesthesia for surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of Nerve Blocks: Nerve blocks are injections of local anesthetic near specific nerves or nerve bundles to temporarily block pain signals, providing regional anesthesia or analgesia for surgery, post-operative pain management, or chronic pain conditions.
  • Mechanism of Action: Local anesthetics reversibly bind to sodium channels in nerve membranes, preventing the generation and conduction of nerve impulses, thereby numbing the targeted area.
  • Types of Blocks: Can range from single-shot injections for acute pain to continuous catheter infusions for prolonged analgesia, targeting peripheral nerves (e.g., brachial plexus, femoral nerve) or central neuraxial structures (e.g., epidural, spinal).
  • Surgical Application: Often used as the primary anesthetic for extremity surgeries, or as an adjunct to general anesthesia to reduce opioid requirements and improve post-operative pain control.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Surgical Pain: Indicated for patients undergoing surgery on an extremity, torso, or head/neck region where targeted pain relief is beneficial.
  • Chronic Pain Conditions: Used diagnostically or therapeutically for conditions like neuropathic pain, complex regional pain syndrome, or cancer-related pain.
  • Post-Operative Pain Management: Employed to reduce severe pain following surgical procedures, facilitating earlier mobilization and rehabilitation.
  • Contraindications to General Anesthesia: Considered for patients with significant comorbidities where general anesthesia poses higher risks.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Allergy to Local Anesthetics: Thoroughly assess for any known allergies to local anesthetic agents or other components of the block solution.
  • Anticoagulation Status: Evaluate patient's coagulation profile and current anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy to minimize the risk of hematoma formation, especially for deep blocks.
  • Infection at Injection Site: Avoid performing a nerve block through an area of active infection to prevent the spread of pathogens.
  • Pre-Existing Neuropathy: Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing neurological deficits, as nerve block complications may exacerbate or be difficult to differentiate from baseline.
  • Patient Cooperation: Ensure the patient can remain still during the procedure, especially if nerve stimulation or ultrasound guidance is used.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: If the nerve block is performed with sedation or as part of a surgical procedure requiring general anesthesia, standard NPO (nil per os) guidelines for solids and clear liquids must be followed.
  • Post-Procedure Hydration: Encourage adequate hydration post-procedure, especially if sedation was administered, to aid recovery.
  • Reintroduction of Diet: Unless otherwise specified by the surgical team or due to other concurrent medical conditions, patients can typically resume their regular diet once fully awake and alert after the block and any associated sedation.
  • Swallowing Assessment: For blocks affecting the neck or upper airway (e.g., deep cervical plexus block), assess swallowing function before reintroducing oral intake.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Numbness/Weakness: Expect the blocked area to be numb and potentially weak. Protect the limb from injury (e.g., falls, pressure sores) until sensation and motor function return.
  • Signs of Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST): Watch for early signs such as perioral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, dizziness, or later signs like seizures and cardiac arrhythmias. Report immediately.
  • Signs of Nerve Injury: Persistent numbness, tingling, or weakness beyond the expected duration of the block, or new onset of sharp, shooting pain, should be reported to the medical team.
  • Infection at Site: Observe the injection site for redness, swelling, warmth, or purulent discharge, which could indicate an infection.
  • Pain Management: Understand the expected duration of the block's effect and when to start oral pain medications as the block wears off to maintain comfort.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Approach: Each nerve block plan is tailored to the patient's anatomy, surgical requirements, and co-morbidities to maximize efficacy and minimize risks.
  • Informed Consent: Thoroughly discuss the benefits, risks (e.g., nerve injury, infection, LAST), alternatives, and expected duration of the block with the patient prior to the procedure.
  • Ultrasound Guidance: Utilize ultrasound guidance routinely to enhance precision, reduce the risk of complications, and confirm local anesthetic spread.
  • Continuous Monitoring: Maintain vigilant monitoring of vital signs and patient comfort throughout the block placement and during its active duration.
  • Post-Block Assessment: Regularly assess the effectiveness of the block and manage any breakthrough pain or complications promptly.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Anatomical Landmarks: Review relevant anatomy of nerves and surrounding structures for common block sites (e.g., brachial plexus, femoral nerve, sciatic nerve).
  • Sterile Technique: Adhere strictly to aseptic technique during block preparation and administration to prevent infection.
  • Patient Positioning: Assist with appropriate patient positioning to optimize access for the anesthetist and ensure patient comfort and safety.
  • Monitoring and Documentation: Continuously monitor vital signs, assess sensory and motor function, and document block onset, duration, and any adverse events.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and their families on what to expect during and after the block, including signs of complications and limb protection.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Local Anesthetics: Bupivacaine, Ropivacaine, Lidocaine – understanding their potency, duration, and toxicity profiles.
  • Adjuvants: Dexamethasone, Epinephrine, Clonidine – agents added to local anesthetics to prolong block duration or enhance effect.
  • Nerve Stimulator: Device used to identify nerves by eliciting a motor response with a low-current electrical impulse.
  • Ultrasound Guidance: Real-time imaging technique used to visualize nerves, surrounding structures, and local anesthetic spread during injection.
  • Common Blocks: Interscalene block (shoulder/upper arm), Supraclavicular block (forearm/hand), Femoral nerve block (thigh/knee), Popliteal sciatic block (lower leg/foot).
  • LAST Management: Intralipid 20% emulsion as a rescue therapy for local anesthetic systemic toxicity.
