Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Irregular heartbeat

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) is a rapid heart rate originating from above the ventricles, typically 150-250 beats per minute.
  • Mechanism: It involves abnormal electrical activity or re-entry circuits within the atria or atrioventricular (AV) node.
  • Common Types: Includes AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT), AV re-entrant tachycardia (AVRT, often associated with Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome), and atrial tachycardia.
  • Onset: Episodes often begin and end abruptly, lasting from a few seconds to several hours.
  • Severity: While generally not life-threatening in individuals with healthy hearts, SVT can cause significant discomfort and anxiety.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Palpitations: A sudden sensation of a racing, pounding, or fluttering heart.
  • Dizziness or Lightheadedness: Due to reduced blood flow to the brain during rapid heart rates.
  • Shortness of Breath: Feeling breathless, especially during an episode.
  • Chest Discomfort: A feeling of pressure, tightness, or mild pain in the chest.
  • Anxiety: A common emotional response to the sudden onset of rapid heartbeats.
  • Fatigue: Feeling unusually tired, even after an episode resolves.
  • Syncope (Fainting): Occurs in severe cases, particularly if the heart rate is extremely fast or prolonged, leading to significant drops in blood pressure.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Identify and Avoid Triggers: Common triggers include excessive caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, stress, fatigue, and certain over-the-counter medications (e.g., decongestants).
  • Learn Vagal Maneuvers: Techniques like the Valsalva maneuver (bearing down as if having a bowel movement), coughing forcefully, or immersing the face in cold water can sometimes terminate an episode. Always learn these under medical guidance.
  • Medication Adherence: If prescribed antiarrhythmic medications, take them exactly as directed to prevent episodes or reduce their severity.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know when to seek immediate medical attention, such as for prolonged episodes, severe chest pain, significant dizziness, or fainting.
  • Hydration and Electrolyte Balance: Maintain adequate fluid intake and ensure a balanced diet to support electrolyte levels, as imbalances can sometimes contribute to arrhythmias.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Caffeine Restriction: Limit or completely avoid coffee, tea, energy drinks, and chocolate, as caffeine is a known SVT trigger for many individuals.
  • Alcohol Moderation: Reduce or eliminate alcohol consumption, as it can disrupt heart rhythm and trigger SVT episodes.
  • Adequate Hydration: Drink sufficient water throughout the day to maintain hydration, which supports overall cardiovascular function and electrolyte balance.
  • Electrolyte-Rich Foods: Incorporate foods rich in potassium and magnesium (e.g., bananas, leafy greens, nuts, seeds) to support healthy heart rhythm.
  • Avoid Large, Heavy Meals: Some individuals find that very large meals can trigger SVT due to vagal nerve stimulation; opt for smaller, more frequent meals.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Patient Education: Educate patients on recognizing SVT symptoms, identifying personal triggers, and the proper technique for vagal maneuvers.
  • Medication Management: Provide clear instructions on prescribed antiarrhythmic medications, including dosage, timing, potential side effects, and drug interactions.
  • Emergency Protocol: Instruct patients on when to contact emergency services (e.g., persistent symptoms, severe chest pain, syncope, or if vagal maneuvers are ineffective).
  • Lifestyle Modification Support: Guide patients on stress reduction techniques, adequate sleep hygiene, and the importance of avoiding identified triggers.
  • Follow-up Care: Emphasize the necessity of regular cardiology follow-ups to monitor the condition and adjust treatment as needed.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: A 12-lead ECG during an episode is crucial for differentiating SVT from other tachyarrhythmias and guiding appropriate treatment.
  • Acute Management: Initial treatment often involves vagal maneuvers, followed by intravenous adenosine if vagal maneuvers are unsuccessful. Beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers may also be used.
  • Long-Term Management: Options include lifestyle modifications, antiarrhythmic medications, or catheter ablation for definitive cure, especially for recurrent or symptomatic SVT.
  • Identify Underlying Causes: Investigate and address any contributing factors such as thyroid dysfunction, electrolyte imbalances, or structural heart disease.
  • Risk Stratification: Assess the patient's overall cardiovascular health and risk factors to tailor a personalized treatment plan and prevent complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • ECG Interpretation: Develop proficiency in recognizing the characteristic narrow QRS complex tachycardia, rapid rate, and often obscured or retrograde P waves of SVT.
  • Vagal Maneuver Assistance: Understand and be able to coach patients through appropriate vagal maneuvers, such as the Valsalva technique.
  • Adenosine Administration: Be prepared for rapid IV push administration of adenosine, followed by a saline flush, and monitor for transient asystole or bradycardia.
  • Patient Monitoring: Implement continuous cardiac monitoring, frequent vital sign assessment, and symptom evaluation during and after an SVT episode.
  • Post-Ablation Care: Understand the post-procedure care requirements for patients undergoing catheter ablation, including monitoring for complications like hematoma or pericardial effusion.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Electrophysiology Study (EPS): An invasive diagnostic procedure used to precisely map the heart's electrical pathways and identify the mechanism of SVT.
  • Adenosine: A short-acting antiarrhythmic drug that temporarily blocks the AV node, often used to terminate SVT or unmask underlying atrial activity.
  • Catheter Ablation: A minimally invasive procedure that uses radiofrequency energy or cryotherapy to destroy the small area of heart tissue causing the abnormal electrical signals.
  • AV Nodal Re-entrant Tachycardia (AVNRT): The most common type of SVT, involving a re-entry circuit within the AV node.
  • Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) Syndrome: A condition involving an accessory pathway (Bundle of Kent) that can lead to AVRT and predispose to rapid conduction during atrial fibrillation.
