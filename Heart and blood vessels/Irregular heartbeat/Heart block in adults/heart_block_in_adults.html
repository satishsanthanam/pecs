Heart block in adults

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Irregular heartbeat

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Heart Block: Heart block, or atrioventricular (AV) block, is a disruption in the electrical signal transmission from the atria to the ventricles, leading to a slower or irregular heartbeat.
  • Types of Heart Block: It's categorized into first-degree (delayed conduction), second-degree (some impulses blocked, Mobitz Type I and II), and third-degree (complete block, no impulses reach ventricles).
  • Impact on Cardiac Function: The severity depends on the degree of block; third-degree heart block is a medical emergency requiring immediate intervention due to significantly reduced cardiac output.
  • Common Causes: Can result from age-related degeneration of the conduction system, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, certain medications (e.g., beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers), electrolyte imbalances, or congenital heart defects.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Dizziness and Lightheadedness: Often due to reduced blood flow to the brain from a slow heart rate.
  • Syncope (Fainting): A sudden, temporary loss of consciousness, especially with higher degrees of block.
  • Fatigue and Weakness: Persistent tiredness, even with minimal exertion, as the heart struggles to pump enough blood.
  • Shortness of Breath (Dyspnea): Particularly during physical activity, indicating reduced cardiac output.
  • Chest Pain or Discomfort: May occur if the heart is not receiving enough oxygen due to a very slow rate.
  • Palpitations: A sensation of a racing, pounding, or fluttering heart, sometimes felt as skipped beats.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Adherence: Strictly follow prescribed medication regimens, especially for underlying conditions like hypertension or heart failure, and report any new symptoms or side effects.
  • Avoidance of Triggers: Identify and avoid factors that may worsen bradycardia or arrhythmias, such as excessive caffeine, alcohol, or certain over-the-counter medications.
  • Regular Medical Follow-ups: Attend all scheduled appointments for monitoring heart rhythm, pacemaker function (if applicable), and overall cardiac health.
  • Pacemaker Care (Post-Implant): Protect the incision site, avoid heavy lifting or arm movements on the affected side initially, and be aware of electromagnetic interference sources.
  • Electrolyte Balance: Maintain adequate hydration and monitor electrolyte levels, especially potassium and magnesium, as imbalances can affect cardiac conduction.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Restriction: Limit high-sodium foods to help manage blood pressure and reduce fluid retention, which can strain the heart.
  • Potassium and Magnesium Balance: Consume a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to support healthy electrolyte levels, crucial for cardiac electrical stability.
  • Caffeine and Stimulant Moderation: Reduce or avoid excessive intake of caffeine and other stimulants that can potentially exacerbate arrhythmias or increase heart rate variability.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake unless otherwise restricted by a physician for conditions like heart failure, as dehydration can impact electrolyte balance.
  • Alcohol Limitation: Moderate or avoid alcohol consumption, as it can affect heart rhythm and interact with certain cardiac medications.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Seek Immediate Medical Attention: Any sudden onset of severe dizziness, fainting, prolonged chest pain, or significant shortness of breath warrants emergency medical evaluation.
  • Medication Interactions: Always inform healthcare providers about all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies being taken to prevent adverse drug interactions that could affect heart rhythm.
  • Pacemaker Malfunction Signs: Be vigilant for symptoms like persistent dizziness, fainting, muscle twitching, or prolonged hiccups, which could indicate a pacemaker issue.
  • Driving Restrictions: Individuals with certain degrees of heart block, especially before pacemaker implantation, may have temporary driving restrictions due to syncope risk.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Carry a medical alert card or wear a medical ID bracelet indicating heart block and any implanted devices.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Evaluation: Diagnosis involves a thorough medical history, physical examination, ECG, Holter monitoring, event recorders, and sometimes electrophysiology studies to pinpoint the type and severity of heart block.
  • Treatment Modalities: First-degree heart block often requires no treatment but close monitoring. Second-degree Mobitz Type I may also be monitored. Second-degree Mobitz Type II and third-degree heart block typically necessitate permanent pacemaker implantation.
  • Medication Review: A critical step is to review and adjust any medications that might be contributing to the heart block, such as beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: Advise patients on managing underlying conditions like hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia through diet, exercise, and medication to support overall cardiovascular health.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on recognizing symptoms of worsening heart block or pacemaker malfunction and the importance of regular follow-up care.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • ECG Interpretation: Nurses must be proficient in identifying PR interval prolongation, dropped QRS complexes (Mobitz I vs. II), and AV dissociation (third-degree block) on ECGs.
  • Patient Assessment: Focus on assessing for signs of decreased cardiac output (e.g., altered mental status, cool clammy skin, hypotension, decreased urine output) and monitoring vital signs closely.
  • Pre- and Post-Pacemaker Care: Educate patients on pre-procedure fasting, post-procedure activity restrictions, incision site care, and signs of infection or lead displacement.
  • Medication Management: Understand the impact of antiarrhythmics, beta-blockers, and calcium channel blockers on AV conduction and monitor for adverse effects.
  • Patient Education on Self-Care: Instruct patients on symptom recognition, when to seek emergency care, pacemaker care, and the importance of adherence to follow-up appointments.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • First-Degree AV Block: Characterized by a prolonged PR interval (>0.20 seconds) with every P wave followed by a QRS complex.
  • Second-Degree AV Block, Mobitz Type I (Wenckebach): Progressive lengthening of the PR interval until a QRS complex is dropped; the cycle then repeats.
  • Second-Degree AV Block, Mobitz Type II: Consistent PR interval with intermittent, unexpected dropped QRS complexes; often progresses to third-degree block.
  • Third-Degree (Complete) AV Block: No electrical impulses conduct from the atria to the ventricles; P waves and QRS complexes are independent (AV dissociation), with a slow, escape ventricular rhythm.
  • Etiology: Ischemic heart disease, inferior myocardial infarction, degenerative fibrosis of the conduction system, myocarditis, hyperkalemia, digitalis toxicity, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers.
  • Treatment Guidelines: ACC/AHA/HRS guidelines recommend permanent pacemaker implantation for symptomatic second-degree Mobitz Type II and all third-degree AV blocks, regardless of symptoms.
