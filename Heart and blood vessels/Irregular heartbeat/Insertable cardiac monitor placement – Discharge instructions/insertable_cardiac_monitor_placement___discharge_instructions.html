Insertable cardiac monitor placement – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Irregular heartbeat

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Device Purpose: Your insertable cardiac monitor (ICM) is a small device placed under the skin to continuously record your heart's electrical activity, helping to detect irregular heart rhythms that may be infrequent or difficult to capture with other tests.
  • Post-Procedure Expectation: You may experience mild discomfort, bruising, or swelling at the insertion site for a few days. This is normal and should gradually improve.
  • Monitoring Function: The ICM automatically detects and records abnormal heart rhythms. You will also be provided with a patient activator to manually record symptoms you experience, such as palpitations, dizziness, or fainting.
  • Follow-up Importance: Regular follow-up appointments are crucial to review the data collected by your ICM and ensure its proper functioning.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Symptoms Prompting ICM: The ICM was placed to investigate symptoms such as unexplained fainting (syncope), recurrent dizziness, palpitations, or stroke of unknown cause.
  • Report Worsening Original Symptoms: If you experience a recurrence or worsening of the symptoms that led to the ICM placement, use your patient activator to record the event and contact your clinic.
  • Infection Signs: Immediately report any signs of infection at the insertion site, including increased redness, warmth, swelling, pus-like drainage, or fever.
  • Unusual Pain or Discomfort: While mild discomfort is expected, report any severe or persistent pain at the insertion site that is not relieved by over-the-counter pain medication.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Incision Site Care: Keep the insertion site clean and dry for at least 7 days, or as instructed by your healthcare provider. Avoid submerging the site in baths, hot tubs, or swimming pools until cleared.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, or repetitive arm movements on the side of the implant for 1-2 weeks to allow the incision to heal properly.
  • Electromagnetic Interference: While ICMs are generally safe, avoid direct contact with strong magnetic fields (e.g., MRI machines unless cleared, large industrial magnets, certain security detectors) as they may temporarily interfere with device function. Always inform medical personnel about your ICM.
  • Device ID Card: Always carry your ICM identification card, which contains important information about your device, especially when traveling or seeking medical care.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Post-Sedation Hydration: If you received sedation, start with clear liquids and light foods, gradually returning to your normal diet as tolerated. Avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours post-sedation.
  • General Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration to support overall healing and well-being.
  • Caffeine and Alcohol: If caffeine or alcohol are known triggers for your arrhythmias, continue to limit or avoid them as advised by your physician, as they can influence heart rhythm.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced, nutrient-rich diet to support your body's recovery and overall cardiovascular health.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Services (Call 911): Seek immediate emergency medical attention if you experience severe chest pain, sudden shortness of breath, prolonged fainting spells, or severe dizziness.
  • Contact Clinic/Doctor: Call your cardiology clinic or physician's office if you notice signs of infection at the insertion site (fever, increased redness, swelling, drainage), persistent or worsening pain, or if you have difficulty using your patient activator or remote monitor.
  • Device Malfunction: If you receive an alert from your remote monitor indicating a device malfunction or low battery, contact your clinic promptly.
  • Driving Post-Sedation: Do not drive or operate heavy machinery for at least 24 hours after receiving sedation. Ensure a responsible adult can transport you home.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Monitoring: It is paramount to consistently use your patient activator to mark symptomatic events and ensure your remote monitor is properly transmitting data. This provides critical information for diagnosis and treatment.
  • Medication Review: Continue all prescribed medications as directed. Do not stop or change any medications without consulting your physician, especially those related to your heart condition.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: Discuss any necessary lifestyle modifications, such as managing stress, maintaining a healthy weight, and avoiding arrhythmia triggers, to optimize your heart health.
  • Long-Term Management: The ICM is a diagnostic tool. Your physician will use the collected data to formulate a long-term management plan, which may include medication adjustments, further interventions, or continued monitoring.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Nurses should reinforce all discharge instructions, particularly regarding incision care, activity restrictions, and the proper use of the patient activator and remote monitor.
  • Insertion Site Assessment: Educate patients on how to monitor their insertion site for signs of infection (REEDA: Redness, Edema, Ecchymosis, Drainage, Approximation) and when to report concerns.
  • Remote Monitoring Setup: Assist patients in understanding the setup and function of their remote monitoring system, ensuring they know how to place it and confirm data transmission.
  • Symptom Correlation: Emphasize the importance of correlating symptoms with manual event recordings to provide comprehensive data for the physician.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Device Model & Manufacturer: Note the specific model and manufacturer of the implanted ICM for future reference and compatibility with diagnostic equipment.
  • Remote Monitoring System: Document the type of remote monitoring system provided (e.g., bedside monitor, smartphone app) and confirm successful initial setup and data transmission.
  • Battery Life Expectancy: Provide the patient with an estimated battery life for their specific ICM model, typically 3-5 years, and explain that the device will alert when battery replacement is needed.
  • Event Recorder Usage: Detail the specific instructions for using the patient event recorder, including how to activate it during symptomatic episodes and how to confirm successful recording.
