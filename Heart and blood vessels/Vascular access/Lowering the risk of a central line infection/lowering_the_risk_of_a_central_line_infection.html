Lowering the risk of a central line infection

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Vascular access

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Central Line Purpose: Central venous catheters (CVCs) provide essential access for medications, fluids, nutrition, and blood sampling, but carry a significant risk of infection.
  • CLABSI Risk: Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infections (CLABSIs) are serious, preventable complications that can lead to prolonged hospital stays, increased healthcare costs, and higher mortality rates.
  • Prevention is Key: Strict adherence to infection prevention bundles and protocols is paramount to minimize the risk of CLABSI.
  • Patient and Caregiver Role: Active participation from patients and their caregivers in understanding and reporting concerns is vital for early detection and prevention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Local Site Infection: Redness, swelling, tenderness, warmth, or pain at the central line insertion site.
  • Purulent Drainage: Presence of pus or cloudy fluid around the catheter exit site.
  • Systemic Infection Signs: Unexplained fever, chills, rigors, or malaise, especially without another clear source of infection.
  • Elevated Inflammatory Markers: Laboratory findings such as an elevated white blood cell count (leukocytosis) or increased C-reactive protein (CRP).
  • Hemodynamic Instability: In severe cases, signs of sepsis including hypotension, tachycardia, or altered mental status.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Hand Hygiene: Perform meticulous hand hygiene (soap and water or alcohol-based hand rub) before and after any contact with the central line or dressing.
  • Maximal Sterile Barrier: During central line insertion, ensure use of sterile gloves, gown, mask, cap, and a large sterile drape to cover the patient.
  • Skin Antisepsis: Prepare the insertion site with an appropriate antiseptic solution (e.g., chlorhexidine gluconate) and allow it to air dry completely before insertion.
  • Daily Assessment: Routinely assess the necessity of the central line and remove it promptly when no longer clinically indicated.
  • Dressing Care: Maintain a sterile, intact, and dry dressing over the insertion site. Change dressings according to protocol (e.g., every 5-7 days for transparent dressings, or immediately if soiled/loose).
  • Hub Disinfection: Scrub the hub vigorously with an appropriate antiseptic (e.g., alcohol or chlorhexidine) for at least 15 seconds before accessing the line.
  • Avoid Non-Essential Access: Minimize the frequency of accessing the central line to reduce contamination risk.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Support: Ensure adequate nutritional intake, whether oral, enteral, or parenteral, to support the patient's immune system and overall healing capacity.
  • Hydration Status: Maintain appropriate hydration levels as guided by clinical assessment, especially if fluids are being administered via the central line.
  • TPN Management: For patients receiving Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN) via a central line, strictly adhere to infusion protocols, monitor for metabolic complications, and ensure timely bag and tubing changes to prevent microbial growth.
  • Oral Hygiene: Promote good oral hygiene, as oral bacteria can sometimes be a source of bloodstream infections in immunocompromised patients.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Do Not Touch: Instruct family and visitors not to touch the central line, tubing, or dressing.
  • Report Changes: Educate attendants to immediately report any signs of infection (redness, swelling, pain, fever, chills) to the nursing staff.
  • Hand Hygiene for Visitors: Emphasize the importance of hand hygiene for all visitors before and after entering the patient's room.
  • Maintain Cleanliness: Reinforce the need to keep the patient's environment clean and free from clutter.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Line Necessity Review: Conduct daily rounds to evaluate the ongoing need for all central lines and order removal as soon as clinically appropriate.
  • Bundle Adherence: Champion and enforce adherence to central line insertion and maintenance bundles across all clinical settings.
  • Antimicrobial Stewardship: Prescribe antibiotics judiciously, reserving them for confirmed or highly suspected infections, and ensure appropriate empiric and definitive therapy.
  • Staff Education: Ensure all staff involved in central line care receive regular training and competency assessments on aseptic technique and infection prevention.
  • Surveillance and Feedback: Participate in CLABSI surveillance efforts and utilize data to provide feedback and drive continuous quality improvement initiatives.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Aseptic Technique Mastery: Practice and maintain strict aseptic technique during all central line insertions, dressing changes, and access procedures.
  • Site Assessment: Regularly assess the central line insertion site for signs of infection, integrity of the dressing, and proper catheter securement.
  • Flushing Protocols: Adhere to established flushing protocols to maintain line patency and prevent biofilm formation.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and their families about central line care, signs of infection, and the importance of hand hygiene.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document all central line insertions, dressing changes, site assessments, and any signs of complications.
