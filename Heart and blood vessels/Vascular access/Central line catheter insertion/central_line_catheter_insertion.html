Central line catheter insertion

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Vascular access

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose: Central line catheters provide direct, long-term access to large central veins for various medical treatments, including medication administration, nutrition, and blood sampling.
  • Indications: Used for prolonged intravenous therapy, chemotherapy, total parenteral nutrition (TPN), hemodialysis, frequent blood draws, administration of vesicant medications, and hemodynamic monitoring.
  • Location: Commonly inserted into the internal jugular, subclavian, or femoral veins, with the catheter tip typically resting in the superior vena cava.
  • Types: Includes Peripherally Inserted Central Catheters (PICCs), Non-tunneled Central Venous Catheters (CVCs), Tunneled CVCs, and Implanted Ports, each suited for different durations and clinical needs.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Need for Long-Term Access: Patients requiring extended intravenous medication, fluids, or nutrition beyond the capacity of peripheral veins.
  • Inadequate Peripheral Access: Clinical situations where peripheral veins are difficult to access or maintain for therapy.
  • Administration of Vesicants: Requirement to deliver medications that are irritating or damaging to peripheral veins.
  • Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN): Inability to meet nutritional needs through the gastrointestinal tract.
  • Hemodynamic Instability: Conditions necessitating continuous central venous pressure monitoring for fluid management and vasopressor titration.
  • Renal Failure: For patients requiring regular hemodialysis or other renal replacement therapies.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Sterile Technique: Strict adherence to maximal sterile barrier precautions during insertion and all subsequent dressing changes to prevent infection.
  • Site Selection: Careful consideration of the insertion site to minimize risks such as pneumothorax, arterial puncture, or nerve damage.
  • Coagulation Status: Assessment of the patient's coagulation profile (e.g., INR, platelet count) prior to insertion to mitigate bleeding risk.
  • Patient Positioning: Proper patient positioning (e.g., Trendelenburg for jugular/subclavian access) to engorge veins and reduce the risk of air embolism.
  • Post-Insertion Confirmation: Mandatory chest X-ray or other imaging post-insertion to confirm catheter tip placement and rule out complications like pneumothorax.
  • Daily Assessment: Regular, daily assessment of the insertion site for any signs of infection (redness, swelling, pain, warmth, discharge).
  • Catheter Patency: Consistent flushing protocols to maintain catheter patency and prevent occlusion or thrombus formation.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Patients typically require NPO (nothing by mouth) for 6-8 hours prior to insertion if moderate sedation or general anesthesia is planned.
  • Hydration Status: Ensure adequate hydration before the procedure, unless medically contraindicated, to facilitate venous access.
  • Post-Procedure Reintroduction: If sedation was used, resume normal diet and fluids as tolerated once the patient is fully awake and alert.
  • TPN Considerations: If the central line is specifically for Total Parenteral Nutrition, oral dietary intake may be restricted or modified as per the nutritional plan.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Infection Monitoring: Attendants must immediately report any signs of infection at the insertion site, such as redness, swelling, warmth, pain, or pus, to the healthcare team.
  • Catheter Integrity: Advise against pulling, tugging, or kinking the catheter tubing, and ensure it is securely taped or dressed.
  • Dressing Care: Emphasize keeping the dressing clean, dry, and intact. Report any loose, soiled, or wet dressings promptly.
  • Activity Restrictions: Caution against activities that could dislodge the catheter or cause trauma to the insertion site, as advised by the medical staff.
  • Emergency Contact: Provide clear instructions on who to contact and what steps to take in case of emergency or concerns regarding the central line.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Risk-Benefit Assessment: Always perform a thorough risk-benefit analysis, weighing the necessity of central venous access against potential complications like infection, pneumothorax, bleeding, and thrombosis.
  • Appropriate Catheter Selection: Select the most suitable type and size of central line based on the patient's specific clinical needs, anticipated duration of therapy, and medication requirements.
  • Ultrasound Guidance: Strongly recommend and utilize ultrasound guidance for central line insertion to enhance success rates and significantly reduce procedural complications.
  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education to patients and their families regarding the purpose, care, potential complications, and signs to watch for with the central line.
  • Timely Removal: Emphasize the importance of removing the central line as soon as it is no longer clinically indicated to minimize the risk of infection and other complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Aseptic Technique Mastery: Reinforce the critical importance of strict aseptic technique during central line insertion, dressing changes, and all access procedures to prevent CLABSI.
  • Complication Recognition: Educate on the early recognition and management of central line complications, including infection, occlusion, air embolism, pneumothorax, and catheter migration.
  • Dressing Change Protocol: Master and consistently apply proper sterile dressing change procedures using chlorhexidine-based antiseptics, ensuring a secure and occlusive dressing.
  • Flushing Protocols: Understand and correctly implement appropriate flushing techniques (e.g., SASH method: Saline, Administer, Saline, Heparin/Saline) to maintain catheter patency and prevent medication incompatibility.
  • Patient Assessment: Develop skills for comprehensive assessment of patients with central lines, including vital signs, insertion site integrity, pain levels, and overall well-being.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CDC Guidelines: Refer to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for the prevention of intravascular catheter-related infections.
  • CLABSI Prevention Bundles: Implement evidence-based Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection (CLABSI) prevention bundles, including hand hygiene, maximal sterile barrier precautions, chlorhexidine skin antisepsis, optimal site selection, and daily review of line necessity.
  • Catheter Material: Note common catheter materials such as polyurethane and silicone, understanding their properties and suitability for different applications.
  • Lumens: Differentiate between single, double, and triple lumen catheters, and their respective clinical uses for simultaneous infusions or monitoring.
  • Antimicrobial Catheters: Consider the use of antimicrobial-impregnated or coated catheters in specific high-risk patient populations as per institutional guidelines.
