# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Procedure Overview:** Angiography is an X-ray imaging technique used to visualize blood vessels throughout the body, including arteries and veins.
* **Purpose:** It helps diagnose blockages, narrowing, aneurysms, or other abnormalities in blood vessels that can affect blood flow.
* **Method:** A special contrast dye is injected into the blood vessels, making them visible on X-ray images (fluoroscopy).
* **Common Types:** Includes coronary angiography (heart), cerebral angiography (brain), and peripheral angiography (limbs).


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Chest Pain (Angina):** Often performed to investigate unexplained or worsening chest pain indicative of coronary artery disease.
* **Leg Pain or Numbness (Claudication):** Used to diagnose peripheral artery disease causing pain, cramping, or weakness in the legs during activity.
* **Stroke or TIA Symptoms:** Investigates blockages or abnormalities in brain arteries following transient ischemic attacks or stroke-like symptoms.
* **Unexplained Bleeding:** Can identify the source of internal bleeding in various organs.
* **Suspected Aneurysm:** Evaluates pulsating masses or symptoms suggestive of an arterial aneurysm.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Allergy to Contrast Dye:** Inform your healthcare provider about any known allergies to iodine, shellfish, or previous reactions to contrast agents.
* **Kidney Function:** Patients with pre-existing kidney impairment are at higher risk for contrast-induced nephropathy; kidney function tests are essential.
* **Anticoagulant Medications:** Discuss all blood-thinning medications (e.g., warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) with your doctor, as they may need to be adjusted or temporarily stopped to reduce bleeding risk.
* **Diabetes Medications:** Metformin may need to be held for 48 hours post-procedure due to interaction with contrast dye and kidney function.
* **Pregnancy:** Inform your doctor if you are pregnant or suspect you might be, as radiation exposure can be harmful to the fetus.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Fasting Prior to Procedure:** Typically, you will be instructed not to eat or drink anything (NPO) for 6-8 hours before the angiography.
* **Hydration:** Adequate hydration before and especially after the procedure is crucial to help flush the contrast dye from your kidneys.
* **Medication Review:** Discuss all current medications, including over-the-counter drugs and supplements, with your physician for specific instructions regarding the fasting period.
* **Post-Procedure Fluids:** Encourage increased fluid intake post-procedure unless medically contraindicated.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Access Site Care:** Maintain pressure on the access site (usually groin or wrist) as instructed and keep the limb straight for several hours to prevent bleeding.
* **Monitor for Bleeding:** Watch for any signs of bleeding, swelling, or a growing hematoma at the puncture site. Report immediately if observed.
* **Allergic Reactions:** Be vigilant for delayed allergic reactions to the contrast dye, such as rash, itching, or difficulty breathing.
* **Hydration:** Ensure the patient drinks plenty of fluids post-procedure to aid in the excretion of the contrast material.
* **Activity Restrictions:** Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, and bending for at least 24-48 hours post-procedure.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Risk-Benefit Assessment:** Carefully weigh the diagnostic benefits against potential risks, especially in patients with comorbidities like renal insufficiency or severe allergies.
* **Informed Consent:** Thoroughly explain the procedure, potential complications (e.g., bleeding, infection, contrast nephropathy, arterial injury), and alternative diagnostic options to the patient.
* **Pre-Procedure Workup:** Ensure comprehensive pre-procedure evaluation, including renal function tests, coagulation status, and allergy history.
* **Post-Procedure Management:** Provide clear instructions for post-procedure care, including activity restrictions, hydration, and medication adjustments.
* **Interventional Potential:** Be prepared for potential immediate intervention (e.g., angioplasty, stenting) if significant blockages are identified during the diagnostic angiography.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pre-Procedure Assessment:** Conduct a thorough nursing assessment, including vital signs, allergy history, current medications, and baseline neurological status.
* **Patient Education:** Educate the patient on fasting requirements, what to expect during the procedure, and post-procedure care, including access site management.
* **Monitoring Post-Procedure:** Closely monitor vital signs, access site for bleeding/hematoma, peripheral pulses, and neurological status (especially for cerebral angiography).
* **Contrast Nephropathy Prevention:** Administer IV fluids as ordered to ensure adequate hydration and prevent contrast-induced acute kidney injury.
* **Emergency Preparedness:** Be prepared to manage potential complications such as anaphylaxis, retroperitoneal bleeding, or vasovagal reactions.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Imaging Modality:** Fluoroscopy (real-time X-ray imaging).
* **Contrast Agent:** Iodinated contrast media (e.g., iohexol, iodixanol).
* **Access Sites:** Femoral artery (most common), radial artery, brachial artery.
* **Complications:** Contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN), arterial dissection, pseudoaneurysm, hematoma, infection, allergic reaction, stroke (cerebral).
* **Indications:** Coronary artery disease (CAD), peripheral artery disease (PAD), cerebrovascular disease, arteriovenous malformations (AVMs), aneurysms.
* **Equipment:** C-arm fluoroscope, catheter, guidewire, pressure injector.
