Transesophageal echocardiogram

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: A Transesophageal Echocardiogram (TEE) is a specialized ultrasound test that provides detailed images of your heart.
  • Methodology: A thin, flexible tube with an ultrasound transducer is gently guided down your esophagus, positioning it directly behind your heart.
  • Enhanced Clarity: This proximity allows for clearer, more precise images of heart valves, chambers, and blood flow compared to a standard transthoracic echocardiogram, as it bypasses ribs and lung tissue.
  • Primary Purpose: Often used to detect blood clots, infections (endocarditis), structural abnormalities, or evaluate valve function with greater accuracy.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Unexplained Fever or New Murmur: Clinical suspicion of infective endocarditis, requiring detailed visualization of heart valves.
  • Cryptogenic Stroke or TIA: To identify potential cardiac sources of emboli, such as patent foramen ovale (PFO) or left atrial appendage thrombus.
  • Valvular Heart Disease: Assessment of severity, prosthetic valve dysfunction, or pre-surgical planning for valve repair/replacement.
  • Atrial Fibrillation: Prior to cardioversion, to rule out the presence of thrombi in the left atrial appendage that could lead to stroke.
  • Aortic Pathology: Evaluation of aortic dissection, aneurysm, or other structural abnormalities of the aorta.
  • Congenital Heart Defects: Detailed assessment of complex congenital heart disease, septal defects, or shunts.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fasting Requirements: Strictly adhere to NPO (nothing by mouth) guidelines for 6-8 hours prior to the procedure to prevent aspiration during sedation.
  • Sedation Safety: You will receive medication to help you relax and minimize discomfort; vital signs will be continuously monitored throughout.
  • Esophageal Contraindications: Inform your physician of any history of esophageal strictures, varices, recent esophageal surgery, or active gastrointestinal bleeding.
  • Dental Prosthetics: Remove all dentures, partials, or other removable dental appliances before the procedure to prevent dislodgement or damage.
  • Allergy Disclosure: Clearly communicate any known allergies to medications, especially sedatives or local anesthetics.
  • Anticoagulant Use: Discuss current anticoagulant therapy with your doctor, as adjustments may be necessary depending on the clinical context, though often not for diagnostic TEE.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Do not eat any solid food for at least 6-8 hours before your scheduled TEE.
  • Clear Liquid Restriction: Avoid clear liquids for at least 2-4 hours prior to the procedure.
  • Medication with Water: Small sips of water may be permitted for essential medications, but confirm with your healthcare provider.
  • Post-Procedure Reintroduction: Remain NPO until your gag reflex has fully returned, typically 1-2 hours after the procedure.
  • Gradual Intake: Begin with small sips of water, then progress to clear liquids, and finally light, soft foods as tolerated once the gag reflex is confirmed.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Transportation Arrangement: Due to sedation, you will not be permitted to drive yourself home. Arrange for a responsible adult to transport you.
  • Post-Procedure Observation: The patient should be monitored for signs of discomfort, difficulty swallowing, or any unusual symptoms for several hours post-procedure.
  • Activity Restrictions: For 24 hours following the procedure, avoid operating heavy machinery, making important decisions, or engaging in activities requiring full alertness.
  • Sore Throat Expectation: A mild sore throat or hoarseness is common after a TEE and usually resolves within 24-48 hours.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • High Diagnostic Yield: TEE is an invaluable tool for precise diagnosis of complex cardiac conditions, offering superior resolution for posterior structures and valvular pathology.
  • Risk-Benefit Assessment: While invasive, the diagnostic benefits often outweigh the minimal risks, especially when critical information is needed for patient management.
  • Informed Consent Process: Thoroughly discuss the procedure, potential risks (e.g., esophageal injury, aspiration), benefits, and alternative diagnostic options with the patient.
  • Sedation Protocol: Administer appropriate conscious sedation to ensure patient comfort and cooperation while maintaining airway patency and hemodynamic stability.
  • Interdisciplinary Collaboration: Often performed in conjunction with cardiology, anesthesiology, and nursing teams to ensure optimal patient safety and procedural success.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Provide clear, concise explanations of the TEE procedure, including fasting requirements, sedation effects, and post-procedure expectations.
  • Pre-Procedure Checklist: Verify informed consent, NPO status, IV access, baseline vital signs, and removal of dental prosthetics.
  • Intra-Procedure Monitoring: Continuously monitor oxygen saturation, heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and level of consciousness during sedation.
  • Airway Management Preparedness: Ensure suction equipment is readily available and functional in case of aspiration or excessive secretions.
  • Post-Procedure Care: Assess for return of gag reflex before allowing oral intake, monitor for complications like dysphagia, sore throat, or signs of bleeding.
  • Discharge Instructions: Educate patient and accompanying adult on post-sedation precautions, expected side effects, and when to seek medical attention.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Key Indications: Evaluation of infective endocarditis, intracardiac thrombi (especially left atrial appendage), PFO/ASD, aortic dissection, prosthetic valve dysfunction, and complex congenital heart disease.
  • Contraindications: Absolute contraindications include active upper GI bleeding, esophageal stricture/obstruction, recent esophageal surgery, and unstable cervical spine. Relative contraindications include large esophageal varices, diverticula, or severe dysphagia.
  • Potential Complications: Esophageal perforation (rare but serious), aspiration pneumonitis, transient arrhythmias, dental injury, pharyngeal trauma, and adverse reactions to sedation.
  • Probe Disinfection: TEE probes require high-level disinfection after each use according to institutional protocols to prevent cross-contamination.
  • Standard Views: Includes midesophageal, transgastric, and deep transgastric views to visualize all cardiac chambers, valves, and great vessels.
