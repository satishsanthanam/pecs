Cardiac catheterization – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Recovery Period: Expect to feel tired for a few days. Rest is crucial for proper healing.
  • Access Site Care: Keep the bandage clean and dry for 24-48 hours as instructed. Avoid rubbing or scratching the site.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting (over 10 lbs), strenuous exercise, and pushing/pulling for at least 1 week.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications exactly as directed, especially antiplatelet agents or anticoagulants.
  • Follow-up Appointment: Ensure you have a scheduled follow-up with your cardiologist to review results and ongoing care.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Bleeding or Swelling: Any new or increased bleeding, bruising, or a rapidly growing lump at the catheter insertion site.
  • Severe Pain: Worsening pain, numbness, tingling, or coolness in the leg or arm used for the procedure.
  • Chest Pain: New or worsening chest pain, pressure, or discomfort.
  • Fever or Chills: A temperature above 101°F (38.3°C) or signs of infection at the access site (redness, warmth, pus).
  • Shortness of Breath: Sudden difficulty breathing or increased shortness of breath.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Baths and Swimming: Do not submerge the access site in water (e.g., baths, hot tubs, swimming pools) for at least 1 week to prevent infection. Showering is generally permitted after 24-48 hours, keeping the site clean.
  • No Driving: Do not drive for at least 24-48 hours after the procedure due to the effects of sedation. Arrange for someone to drive you home.
  • Avoid Straining: Prevent constipation and straining during bowel movements, as this can put pressure on the access site.
  • Monitor for Allergic Reactions: Be vigilant for signs of allergic reaction to new medications, such as rash, itching, or difficulty breathing.
  • Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (unless restricted by a medical condition) to help flush the contrast dye from your system.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fluid Intake: Increase your fluid intake (water, clear juices) for the next 24-48 hours to aid in kidney clearance of contrast dye.
  • Light Meals: Start with light, easily digestible meals after discharge, gradually returning to your regular diet as tolerated.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Avoid alcohol consumption for at least 24-48 hours, especially if you received sedation or are on new medications.
  • Caffeine Moderation: Limit caffeine intake, as it can sometimes exacerbate post-procedure anxiety or heart palpitations.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Instruct the patient and caregiver on when to call 911 immediately (e.g., uncontrolled bleeding, severe chest pain, sudden weakness).
  • Transportation Home: Ensure the patient has a responsible adult to drive them home and stay with them for at least 24 hours post-procedure.
  • Medication Management: Assist the patient in understanding and adhering to their new medication regimen, especially antiplatelets.
  • Observation: Closely monitor the patient for any signs of complications, particularly at the access site and for changes in mental status.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Instructions: Following these discharge instructions meticulously is paramount for a smooth recovery and to minimize complications.
  • Medication Importance: Your prescribed medications, particularly blood thinners, are critical for preventing future cardiac events. Do not stop them without consulting me.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: This procedure is a step towards better heart health. Continue to discuss and implement long-term lifestyle changes, such as diet and exercise, with your care team.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact my office or seek emergency care if you experience any concerning symptoms or have questions.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Discharge Teaching: Ensure all aspects of post-catheterization care are thoroughly explained, including access site care, activity restrictions, and medication reconciliation.
  • Warning Signs Education: Emphasize and verify patient understanding of critical warning signs requiring immediate medical attention.
  • Teach-Back Method: Utilize the teach-back method to confirm patient and caregiver comprehension of all instructions, especially medication schedules and emergency protocols.
  • Documentation: Document all discharge teaching provided, patient/caregiver understanding, and any resources given (e.g., contact numbers, written instructions).

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Sheath Removal Site Management: Review specific protocols for femoral vs. radial access site hemostasis (e.g., manual compression, vascular closure devices, TR Band application).
  • Anticoagulation/Antiplatelet Therapy: Understand the rationale for dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) post-stenting and the importance of strict adherence to prevent stent thrombosis.
  • Contrast-Induced Nephropathy (CIN) Prevention: Reinforce the importance of post-procedure hydration, especially in patients with pre-existing renal impairment, to mitigate CIN risk.
  • Vascular Complications: Be aware of potential post-catheterization vascular complications such as hematoma, pseudoaneurysm, arteriovenous fistula, and retroperitoneal bleed, and their clinical presentations.
