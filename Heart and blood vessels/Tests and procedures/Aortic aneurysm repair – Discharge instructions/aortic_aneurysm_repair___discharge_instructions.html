Aortic aneurysm repair – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Recovery Expectations: Expect a gradual recovery period, typically several weeks to months, with increasing energy and strength over time.
  • Incision Management: Keep your surgical incision clean and dry; observe for any signs of infection or delayed healing.
  • Pain Control: Take prescribed pain medications as directed to manage discomfort and facilitate mobility and deep breathing exercises.
  • Activity Progression: Gradually increase your activity level as tolerated, adhering strictly to lifting and movement restrictions.
  • Follow-Up Care: Attending all scheduled post-operative appointments is crucial for monitoring your healing and the long-term success of the repair.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Infection Signs: Report any new or worsening redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or increasing pain at the incision site, or a fever above 101°F (38.3°C).
  • Bleeding or Hematoma: Seek immediate attention for new or excessive bleeding, significant bruising, or a rapidly growing lump near the surgical area.
  • Leg Ischemia (Post-EVAR): Watch for new numbness, tingling, coolness, pallor, or severe pain in the legs or feet, which may indicate a stent-graft complication.
  • Abdominal/Back Pain: Report any sudden, severe, or persistent abdominal or back pain, as this could signal a serious complication.
  • Respiratory Distress: Notify your care team if you experience new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, or a persistent cough.
  • Neurological Changes: Be alert for sudden weakness, numbness on one side of the body, difficulty speaking, or vision changes, which could indicate a stroke.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Wound Care Protocol: Wash hands thoroughly before and after touching your incision; avoid soaking in baths, hot tubs, or swimming until cleared by your surgeon.
  • Lifting Restrictions: Do not lift anything heavier than 5-10 pounds (e.g., a gallon of milk) for at least 6-8 weeks, or as specifically instructed by your surgeon.
  • Driving Limitations: Refrain from driving until you are off strong pain medications, can react quickly, and are cleared by your surgeon, typically several weeks post-op.
  • Avoid Straining: Prevent constipation by maintaining adequate hydration and fiber intake; use prescribed stool softeners if necessary to avoid straining during bowel movements.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including blood pressure medications, antiplatelets, or anticoagulants, exactly as instructed without skipping doses.
  • Smoking Cessation: Absolutely avoid all tobacco products, as smoking significantly impairs healing and increases the risk of future vascular complications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Heart-Healthy Eating: Focus on a diet rich in lean proteins, whole grains, fresh fruits, and vegetables; limit saturated and trans fats, cholesterol, and processed foods.
  • Sodium Restriction: Reduce intake of high-sodium foods to help manage blood pressure and prevent fluid retention, which can strain the cardiovascular system.
  • Adequate Hydration: Drink plenty of water and other clear fluids throughout the day to prevent dehydration and support overall recovery and bowel regularity.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate high-fiber foods (e.g., whole grains, fruits, vegetables) to prevent constipation, especially if taking opioid pain medications.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Preparedness: Ensure immediate access to emergency contact numbers for the surgical team and local emergency services.
  • Medication Assistance: Help the patient organize and administer all prescribed medications accurately and on schedule.
  • Vigilant Monitoring: Closely observe the patient for any of the warning signs of complications listed under 'Associated Symptoms' and seek immediate medical attention if they occur.
  • Activity Support: Assist the patient in adhering to activity restrictions, such as lifting limits and avoiding strenuous movements, and provide support for daily tasks as needed.
  • Emotional Support: Offer consistent emotional support and encouragement, recognizing that recovery can be a challenging period for the patient.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Commitment to Recovery: Your active participation and strict adherence to all discharge instructions are paramount for a successful recovery and to minimize the risk of complications.
  • Scheduled Follow-Ups: Do not underestimate the importance of your post-operative appointments; these visits are critical for monitoring your healing, graft integrity, and overall cardiovascular health.
  • Long-Term Health: Embrace a heart-healthy lifestyle, including dietary modifications, regular physical activity (as cleared), and complete smoking cessation, to protect your vascular system for years to come.
  • Prompt Reporting: Any new or concerning symptoms, no matter how minor they seem, should be reported to our office immediately to allow for timely assessment and intervention.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Patient Education: Reinforce patient understanding of incision care, pain management strategies, activity limitations, and the importance of medication adherence.
  • Vital Sign Surveillance: Educate patients and caregivers on target ranges for blood pressure, heart rate, and temperature, and specific parameters for reporting deviations.
  • Neurovascular Assessment: Instruct on performing regular checks for pulses, sensation, and motor function in the extremities, especially crucial after endovascular repairs.
  • Bowel Management: Provide education on preventing constipation, including dietary fiber, hydration, and appropriate use of prescribed stool softeners or laxatives.
  • Psychosocial Support: Assess for signs of anxiety, depression, or coping difficulties related to surgery and recovery, providing appropriate resources and referrals.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Aortic Aneurysm: A localized, permanent dilation of the aorta, typically exceeding 1.5 times its normal diameter, requiring repair to prevent rupture.
  • Open Surgical Repair: Involves a laparotomy or thoracotomy, clamping the aorta, and replacing the aneurysmal segment with a synthetic graft (e.g., Dacron).
  • Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR/TEVAR): A minimally invasive procedure where a stent-graft is deployed percutaneously within the aneurysm to exclude it from blood flow.
  • Post-operative Complications: Potential risks include graft infection, endoleak (EVAR), limb ischemia, renal dysfunction, spinal cord ischemia, myocardial infarction, and stroke.
  • Surveillance Imaging: Regular follow-up imaging (e.g., CT angiography, duplex ultrasound) is essential, particularly after EVAR, to monitor for endoleaks, graft migration, or aneurysm expansion.
  • Anticoagulation/Antiplatelet Therapy: Often prescribed post-operatively based on the type of repair and individual patient risk factors to prevent thrombotic events.
