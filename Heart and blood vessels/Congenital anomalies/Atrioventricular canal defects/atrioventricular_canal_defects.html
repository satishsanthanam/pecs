Atrioventricular canal defects

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Congenital anomalies

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Atrioventricular canal defects (AVCD), also known as endocardial cushion defects, are congenital heart conditions where there is a hole between the heart's chambers and problems with the mitral and tricuspid valves.
  • Types: Can be partial (affecting only the atria and one valve) or complete (affecting both atria and ventricles, with a common AV valve).
  • Impact: Leads to increased blood flow to the lungs, causing pulmonary hypertension, and can result in heart enlargement and heart failure if uncorrected.
  • Associated Conditions: AVCD is frequently observed in infants with Down syndrome (Trisomy 21).

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Infant Presentation: Poor feeding, failure to gain weight (failure to thrive), rapid breathing (tachypnea), shortness of breath, and frequent respiratory infections.
  • Cyanosis: Bluish discoloration of the skin or lips, especially during crying or feeding, indicating low oxygen levels.
  • Heart Murmur: A distinct sound heard during a physical examination, often one of the first signs detected.
  • Fatigue: Easy tiring, particularly during feeding or physical activity in older infants or children.
  • Swelling: Edema in the legs, ankles, or abdomen, indicating fluid retention due to heart failure.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Stabilization: Meticulous management of heart failure symptoms with medications and nutritional support to optimize the infant's condition before surgery.
  • Infection Prevention: Strict hand hygiene and avoidance of sick contacts to prevent respiratory infections, which can severely complicate the infant's condition.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Close observation for arrhythmias, heart block, residual shunts, and signs of pulmonary hypertension in the immediate post-surgical period.
  • Endocarditis Prophylaxis: Adherence to antibiotic prophylaxis guidelines for certain dental or medical procedures to prevent infective endocarditis in high-risk patients.
  • Activity Restrictions: Following specific guidelines regarding physical activity and exertion, especially during recovery and for individuals with residual heart issues.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • High-Calorie Needs: Infants with AVCD often require increased caloric intake due to higher metabolic demands from heart failure; breast milk or formula may need fortification.
  • Frequent, Smaller Feeds: To conserve energy and prevent fatigue, infants may benefit from more frequent, smaller volume feedings.
  • Feeding Assistance: Use of specialized nipples or feeding techniques to reduce effort during feeding and ensure adequate intake.
  • Fluid Management: Careful monitoring of fluid intake and output, as fluid restriction may be necessary in cases of severe heart failure.
  • Post-Surgical Reintroduction: Gradual reintroduction of oral feeds post-surgery, starting with clear fluids and progressing as tolerated, while monitoring for feeding intolerance.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Recognize Worsening Symptoms: Caregivers must be vigilant for signs of worsening heart failure, such as increased breathing effort, poor feeding, lethargy, or increased cyanosis.
  • Medication Adherence: Strict adherence to prescribed medications (e.g., diuretics, digoxin) is vital for managing symptoms and stabilizing the child's condition.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know when to seek immediate medical attention for acute changes in condition, such as severe respiratory distress or sudden collapse.
  • Growth Monitoring: Regular monitoring of weight and growth parameters is crucial to assess nutritional status and overall health progression.
  • Immunizations: Ensure the child receives all recommended immunizations on schedule, with specific considerations for those with chronic heart conditions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis: Prompt diagnosis, often via fetal echocardiography or postnatal echocardiography, is critical for timely intervention and improved outcomes.
  • Surgical Repair: Surgical correction is the definitive treatment for AVCD, typically performed in infancy to prevent irreversible pulmonary vascular disease.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: A collaborative approach involving pediatric cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, intensivists, and nurses is essential for comprehensive management.
  • Long-term Follow-up: Lifelong cardiology follow-up is necessary to monitor for potential complications such as residual valve regurgitation, arrhythmias, or pulmonary hypertension.
  • Genetic Counseling: For families with a child diagnosed with AVCD, especially those with associated syndromes like Down syndrome, genetic counseling is recommended.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Nursing Care: Focus on optimizing respiratory status, managing fluid balance, administering cardiac medications, and providing nutritional support.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Intensive care nursing involves continuous hemodynamic monitoring, chest tube management, pain control, and vigilant assessment for complications.
  • Parent Education: Educating parents on medication administration, recognizing signs of distress, feeding techniques, and the importance of follow-up appointments.
  • Developmental Support: Addressing the developmental needs of infants and children with chronic heart conditions, including early intervention services if indicated.
  • Psychosocial Support: Providing emotional support to families coping with the diagnosis and treatment of a complex congenital heart defect.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by a common atrioventricular valve and a defect in the atrial and/or ventricular septum, leading to left-to-right shunting and pulmonary overcirculation.
  • Surgical Techniques: Primary repair involves patching the atrial and ventricular septal defects and reconstructing the common AV valve into two separate valves.
  • Complications: Potential post-operative complications include complete heart block, residual AV valve regurgitation, subaortic stenosis, and persistent pulmonary hypertension.
  • Echocardiography: The primary diagnostic tool, providing detailed anatomical and functional assessment of the defect and associated cardiac structures.
  • Genetic Associations: Strong association with Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), accounting for a significant percentage of AVCD cases.
