Ventricular fibrillation

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Cardiac arrest

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Life-Threatening Arrhythmia: Ventricular fibrillation (VFib) is a severe cardiac arrhythmia where the heart's lower chambers (ventricles) quiver uselessly instead of pumping blood effectively.
  • Immediate Cardiac Arrest: This disorganized electrical activity leads to an immediate cessation of effective blood circulation, resulting in sudden cardiac arrest.
  • Loss of Consciousness: Patients experiencing VFib will rapidly lose consciousness and collapse due to the lack of blood flow to the brain.
  • Medical Emergency: VFib is a critical medical emergency requiring immediate intervention, primarily defibrillation, to restore a normal heart rhythm.
  • Common Causes: Often triggered by acute myocardial infarction (heart attack), severe electrolyte imbalances, drug toxicity, or underlying structural heart disease.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sudden Collapse: The most prominent sign is an abrupt loss of consciousness and collapse.
  • Absence of Pulse: No palpable pulse can be detected in major arteries (e.g., carotid, femoral).
  • Absence of Breathing: The patient will stop breathing or exhibit agonal gasps (infrequent, ineffective gasps).
  • Unresponsiveness: The patient will not respond to verbal commands or painful stimuli.
  • Prior Symptoms (if any): In some cases, preceding symptoms like chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, or shortness of breath may occur if VFib is preceded by other arrhythmias like ventricular tachycardia.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Early Recognition of Cardiac Arrest: Prompt identification of unresponsiveness, absence of normal breathing, and lack of pulse is paramount.
  • Immediate Activation of EMS: Call emergency medical services (e.g., 911) without delay.
  • Initiate High-Quality CPR: Begin chest compressions immediately and continue until a defibrillator is available or EMS arrives.
  • Rapid Defibrillation: Access to and prompt use of an automated external defibrillator (AED) or manual defibrillator is the most critical intervention.
  • Safety During Defibrillation: Ensure the patient and all bystanders are clear of contact with the patient before delivering a shock.
  • Management of Underlying Causes: Address predisposing factors such as electrolyte imbalances, myocardial ischemia, or drug toxicities to prevent recurrence.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Electrolyte Management: For patients at risk or post-VFib, careful monitoring and dietary management of potassium and magnesium levels are crucial, especially with diuretic use.
  • Heart-Healthy Diet for Prevention: Adherence to a diet low in saturated and trans fats, cholesterol, and sodium can help mitigate risk factors for coronary artery disease, a common precursor to VFib.
  • Fluid Balance Post-Resuscitation: In the post-cardiac arrest phase, fluid intake may be carefully managed, particularly in patients with heart failure or cerebral edema.
  • Avoidance of Stimulants: Limiting excessive caffeine or other stimulants may be advised for individuals prone to arrhythmias, though not a direct treatment for VFib itself.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Stay Calm and Act Quickly: Your immediate actions can significantly impact the patient's survival.
  • Call for Help: Immediately dial emergency services and clearly state the situation and location.
  • Start Chest Compressions: If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin chest compressions at a rate of 100-120 per minute, pressing at least 2 inches deep.
  • Locate and Use an AED: If an AED is available, retrieve it and follow its voice prompts precisely. Apply pads to the bare chest and allow the device to analyze the rhythm.
  • Clear Before Shock: Ensure no one is touching the patient when the AED advises a shock.
  • Continue CPR: Resume CPR immediately after any shock or if no shock is advised, until EMS takes over or the patient shows signs of life.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Time-Sensitive Intervention: Every minute without defibrillation significantly reduces survival chances; rapid intervention is paramount.
  • Adherence to ACLS Protocols: Management follows Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) guidelines, including high-quality CPR, early defibrillation, and appropriate pharmacotherapy (e.g., epinephrine, antiarrhythmics).
  • Post-Resuscitation Care: Comprehensive post-cardiac arrest care focuses on optimizing hemodynamics, targeted temperature management, and neurological prognostication.
  • Etiology Investigation: A thorough workup is essential to identify and treat the underlying cause of VFib to prevent recurrence (e.g., coronary angiography, electrolyte correction, toxicology screening).
  • Secondary Prevention: For survivors, consideration of an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) is often indicated, especially if no reversible cause is found.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • ECG Recognition: Learn to identify the characteristic chaotic, irregular waveform of VFib on an electrocardiogram, lacking discernible P waves, QRS complexes, or T waves.
  • CPR Proficiency: Master the techniques for high-quality chest compressions and effective ventilations according to current guidelines.
  • Defibrillator Operation: Understand the safe and effective operation of both manual and automated external defibrillators, including pad placement and energy selection.
  • Medication Administration: Be familiar with ACLS medications such as epinephrine, amiodarone, and lidocaine, including their indications, dosages, and routes of administration during cardiac arrest.
  • Post-Arrest Monitoring: Understand the critical importance of continuous cardiac monitoring, neurological assessment, and vital sign surveillance in the post-resuscitation phase.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by multiple re-entrant wavelets or focal discharges within the ventricular myocardium, leading to disorganized electrical activity and mechanical standstill.
  • ECG Findings: Irregular, chaotic deflections of varying amplitude and morphology, with no identifiable P waves, QRS complexes, or T waves. The baseline is undulating and coarse.
  • Treatment Modalities: Primary treatment is immediate unsynchronized direct current (DC) defibrillation. Adjunctive therapies include high-quality chest compressions, airway management, and intravenous medications (e.g., epinephrine, amiodarone, lidocaine).
  • Prognosis Factors: Survival rates are directly correlated with the time to defibrillation, quality of CPR, and the underlying etiology of the VFib event.
  • ICD Indications: Class I indication for secondary prevention in patients who have survived an episode of VFib not due to a transient or reversible cause.
