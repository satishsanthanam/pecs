Heart failure

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Heart failure

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Heart failure (HF) is a chronic, progressive condition where the heart muscle is unable to pump enough blood to meet the body's needs for blood and oxygen.
  • Not a 'Heart Attack': It does not mean the heart has stopped working, but rather that it is not working as efficiently as it should.
  • Types: Can be classified as left-sided (systolic or diastolic) or right-sided, affecting different parts of the body's circulation.
  • Progression: HF often worsens over time, requiring ongoing management and lifestyle adjustments.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Shortness of Breath (Dyspnea): Especially during exertion, lying flat (orthopnea), or waking up breathless at night (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea).
  • Fatigue and Weakness: Feeling tired and weak, even with minimal activity, due to reduced blood flow to muscles.
  • Swelling (Edema): Accumulation of fluid in the legs, ankles, feet, and sometimes the abdomen (ascites) or lungs.
  • Persistent Cough or Wheezing: Often accompanied by white or pink blood-tinged phlegm, indicating fluid buildup in the lungs.
  • Rapid or Irregular Heartbeat: Palpitations or a feeling that the heart is racing or skipping beats.
  • Increased Urination at Night (Nocturia): Fluid shifts from the legs back into circulation when lying down, increasing kidney activity.
  • Sudden Weight Gain: Rapid increase in weight over a few days, indicating fluid retention.
  • Lack of Appetite or Nausea: Due to fluid buildup around the digestive system.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications exactly as directed, without skipping doses or stopping abruptly.
  • Daily Weight Monitoring: Weigh yourself daily at the same time, using the same scale, and report any sudden weight gain (e.g., 2-3 lbs in a day or 5 lbs in a week) to your healthcare provider.
  • Fluid Restriction: Adhere strictly to fluid intake limits as advised by your physician, typically 1.5-2 liters per day.
  • Sodium Restriction: Follow a low-sodium diet to prevent fluid retention; avoid processed foods, canned goods, and excessive salt.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Attend all scheduled appointments with your cardiologist and other healthcare providers to monitor your condition.
  • Vaccinations: Stay up-to-date with influenza and pneumococcal vaccinations to prevent respiratory infections that can exacerbate HF.
  • Avoid NSAIDs: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) can worsen fluid retention and kidney function in HF patients.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Be vigilant for worsening symptoms such as increased shortness of breath, swelling, or fatigue, and report them promptly.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Restriction: Limit sodium intake to typically less than 2000 mg per day. Avoid high-sodium foods like processed meats, canned soups, frozen dinners, and salty snacks.
  • Fluid Management: Restrict total fluid intake as per physician's orders, often between 1.5 to 2 liters daily, including all beverages, soups, and foods with high water content.
  • Potassium Monitoring: Depending on medications (e.g., diuretics, ACE inhibitors), potassium levels may need careful monitoring. Discuss potassium-rich foods with your doctor or dietitian.
  • Healthy Fats: Choose healthy fats from sources like olive oil, avocados, and nuts, and limit saturated and trans fats.
  • Lean Proteins: Opt for lean protein sources such as fish, poultry without skin, beans, and lentils.
  • Limit Alcohol: Alcohol can weaken the heart muscle and interact with medications; limit or avoid as advised by your doctor.
  • Avoid Caffeine: Excessive caffeine intake can sometimes trigger arrhythmias; discuss appropriate limits with your healthcare provider.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Caregiver Education: Family members and caregivers should be educated on recognizing signs of worsening heart failure and how to respond in an emergency.
  • Medication Management Support: Assist the patient with medication adherence, especially if they have cognitive impairments or complex regimens.
  • Emergency Plan: Have a clear emergency plan, including contact numbers for healthcare providers and local emergency services, readily accessible.
  • Emotional Support: Provide emotional support and encourage participation in activities within the patient's physical limits.
  • Home Environment Safety: Ensure a safe home environment to prevent falls, especially if the patient experiences dizziness or weakness.
  • Fluid and Sodium Tracking: Help the patient track daily fluid intake and monitor food labels for sodium content.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: Prompt identification and management of heart failure can significantly improve patient outcomes and quality of life.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Optimal care involves a team including cardiologists, nurses, dietitians, and physical therapists to address all aspects of the condition.
  • Personalized Treatment Plans: Therapy is tailored to the individual, considering the type of HF, comorbidities, and patient preferences.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: Adherence to dietary restrictions (low sodium, fluid limits) and regular, appropriate physical activity are fundamental to management.
  • Medication Optimization: Titration of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) is crucial for symptom control, preventing hospitalizations, and improving survival.
  • Advanced Therapies: For select patients with advanced HF, options such as cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT), implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs), left ventricular assist devices (LVADs), or heart transplantation may be considered.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Perform thorough cardiac, respiratory, and fluid status assessments, including daily weights, intake/output, lung sounds, peripheral edema, and jugular venous distension (JVD).
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on self-management strategies, medication purpose and side effects, dietary restrictions, symptom monitoring, and when to seek medical attention.
  • Medication Administration: Understand the mechanisms of action, dosages, and potential adverse effects of common HF medications (e.g., diuretics, ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, ARNIs, SGLT2 inhibitors).
  • Fluid and Electrolyte Balance: Monitor for signs of electrolyte imbalances (e.g., hypokalemia with loop diuretics) and fluid overload or dehydration.
  • Discharge Planning: Facilitate smooth transitions of care by coordinating follow-up appointments, medication reconciliation, and home health services.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address the psychological impact of chronic illness, providing resources for coping and support groups.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Ejection Fraction (EF): A key measure of heart function, indicating the percentage of blood pumped out of the left ventricle with each beat. Used to classify HF as HFrEF (reduced EF) or HFpEF (preserved EF).
  • BNP/NT-proBNP: Biomarkers used in the diagnosis, prognosis, and management of heart failure, elevated in response to ventricular stretch and wall stress.
  • NYHA Functional Classification: New York Heart Association classification system (Classes I-IV) used to categorize the severity of heart failure symptoms and their impact on physical activity.
  • Diagnostic Tools: Echocardiography (gold standard), Electrocardiogram (ECG), Chest X-ray, Cardiac MRI, and Blood tests (e.g., kidney function, electrolytes).
  • Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT): Includes ACE inhibitors/ARBs/ARNIs, beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs), and SGLT2 inhibitors as foundational therapies for HFrEF.
  • Non-Pharmacological Interventions: Cardiac rehabilitation, implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) for primary/secondary prevention of sudden cardiac death, and cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) for specific conduction abnormalities.
  • Comorbidities: Management of co-existing conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, coronary artery disease, and renal dysfunction is critical in HF.
