Angiography

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Blood clots in the legs or lungs

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Diagnostic Imaging: Angiography is a medical imaging technique used to visualize the inside, or lumen, of blood vessels and organs of the body, with a particular focus on arteries, veins, and the heart chambers.
  • Contrast Dye Application: The procedure involves injecting a radiopaque contrast agent into the bloodstream, which allows blood vessels to be seen clearly on X-ray images.
  • Clot Detection: It is a critical tool for identifying blockages, narrowing, or other abnormalities in blood vessels, often caused by blood clots (e.g., deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism).
  • Procedure Types: Depending on the area of interest, it can be referred to as pulmonary angiography (for lungs), peripheral angiography (for limbs), or coronary angiography (for heart).

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) Indications: Unilateral leg swelling, pain or tenderness in the calf or thigh, warmth, and redness of the affected limb.
  • Pulmonary Embolism (PE) Indications: Sudden onset of shortness of breath, chest pain that worsens with deep breaths, cough (sometimes with bloody sputum), rapid heart rate, and unexplained anxiety or dizziness.
  • Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) Indications: Leg pain during exertion (claudication) that resolves with rest, numbness or weakness in the leg, coldness in the lower leg or foot, and non-healing sores on the legs or feet.
  • Vascular Occlusion Suspicions: Any signs or symptoms suggesting impaired blood flow to an organ or limb, such as sudden limb discoloration or loss of pulse.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Allergy to Contrast Material: Inform the medical team about any history of allergic reactions to iodine, shellfish, or previous contrast dyes. Pre-medication may be necessary.
  • Kidney Function Assessment: Pre-procedure blood tests (e.g., creatinine, GFR) are essential to assess renal function, as contrast dye can be nephrotoxic, especially in patients with pre-existing kidney impairment.
  • Anticoagulant Management: Discuss all blood-thinning medications (e.g., warfarin, DOACs, aspirin) with your physician, as they may need to be temporarily stopped or adjusted to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Diabetes Medication: Patients on metformin for diabetes may need to discontinue it for a period before and after the procedure to prevent lactic acidosis, especially if kidney function is compromised.
  • Pregnancy Status: Angiography is generally avoided during pregnancy due to radiation exposure risks to the fetus; alternative imaging methods should be considered.
  • Hydration Status: Adequate hydration before and after the procedure is crucial to help flush the contrast dye from the kidneys.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Typically, patients are required to fast (NPO – nothing by mouth) for 4-8 hours prior to the angiography, especially if sedation is anticipated.
  • Fluid Intake Post-Procedure: Encourage increased oral fluid intake post-procedure, unless medically contraindicated, to aid in the excretion of the contrast dye through the kidneys.
  • Medication with Sips of Water: Discuss with your physician which essential medications can be taken with small sips of water during the fasting period.
  • Avoidance of Alcohol/Caffeine: Advise against alcohol and excessive caffeine consumption for at least 24 hours prior to the procedure, as they can affect hydration and heart rate.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Closely observe the patient for signs of bleeding, swelling, or hematoma at the catheter insertion site (e.g., groin, wrist).
  • Activity Restrictions: Ensure the patient adheres to prescribed bed rest and limb immobilization (e.g., keeping the leg straight) for several hours post-procedure to prevent bleeding and promote healing.
  • Report New Symptoms: Immediately report any new or worsening pain, numbness, tingling, coldness, or discoloration in the limb distal to the access site.
  • Hydration Assistance: Assist the patient with adequate fluid intake post-procedure to facilitate contrast dye excretion.
  • Allergic Reaction Watch: Be vigilant for delayed allergic reactions to the contrast dye, such as rash, itching, or difficulty breathing.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Indications for Procedure: Angiography is indicated when non-invasive imaging is inconclusive or when a more precise anatomical assessment of vascular pathology is required for diagnosis or intervention planning.
  • Risk-Benefit Analysis: A thorough evaluation of the patient's overall health, comorbidities, and the potential risks (e.g., bleeding, infection, contrast nephropathy, allergic reaction) versus the diagnostic and therapeutic benefits is paramount.
  • Alternative Imaging Modalities: Consider and discuss alternative imaging options like CT angiography (CTA) or Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA) and justify the choice of conventional angiography based on clinical need.
  • Informed Consent Process: Ensure the patient and/or their legal guardian fully understand the procedure, its purpose, potential risks, benefits, and alternatives before obtaining informed consent.
  • Personalized Management: Tailor pre- and post-procedure care, including medication adjustments and hydration protocols, to the individual patient's specific medical history and renal status.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-Procedure Checklist: Verify patient identity, informed consent, NPO status, allergy history, and recent lab results (creatinine, INR/PTT).
  • Patient Education: Provide clear explanations of the procedure, including sensations (e.g., warm flush from dye), expected duration, and post-procedure care requirements.
  • Access Site Care: Monitor the puncture site frequently for bleeding, hematoma formation, and assess distal pulses, color, temperature, and sensation of the affected limb.
  • Contrast Reaction Management: Be prepared to recognize and manage acute allergic reactions or contrast-induced nephropathy, including administering prescribed medications and monitoring vital signs.
  • Fluid Balance Monitoring: Accurately monitor intake and output, and administer intravenous fluids as ordered to support renal function and aid contrast excretion.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Contrast Media: Typically uses iodine-based, non-ionic, low-osmolality contrast agents to minimize adverse reactions.
  • Access Sites: Common arterial access sites include the femoral artery, radial artery, or brachial artery; venous access for venography.
  • Imaging Technique: Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) is often employed to enhance visualization of blood vessels by subtracting bone and soft tissue images.
  • Indications: Diagnosis of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), peripheral arterial disease (PAD), arterial occlusions, aneurysms, and vascular malformations.
  • Potential Complications: Bleeding or hematoma at the puncture site, pseudoaneurysm, arterial dissection, contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN), allergic reactions, infection, and rarely, stroke or myocardial infarction.
