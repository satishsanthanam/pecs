Heart attack

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Coronary heart disease

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A heart attack, or myocardial infarction (MI), occurs when blood flow to a part of the heart is blocked, usually by a blood clot, causing heart muscle damage.
  • Urgency: It is a medical emergency requiring immediate attention to minimize heart damage and save lives.
  • Primary Cause: Most heart attacks are caused by coronary artery disease, where plaque buildup (atherosclerosis) narrows arteries, making them prone to clot formation.
  • Consequences: Untreated, it can lead to permanent heart damage, heart failure, arrhythmias, or sudden cardiac death.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Chest Discomfort: Pressure, tightness, squeezing, or aching in the center of the chest that may last more than a few minutes, or goes away and comes back.
  • Pain Radiation: Discomfort spreading to one or both arms (often the left), the back, neck, jaw, or stomach.
  • Shortness of Breath: Occurring with or without chest discomfort.
  • Cold Sweat: Sudden onset of sweating, often described as a cold sweat.
  • Nausea or Vomiting: Feeling sick to the stomach or actually vomiting.
  • Lightheadedness or Dizziness: Feeling faint or dizzy.
  • Unusual Fatigue: Especially in women, unexplained tiredness can be a symptom.
  • Atypical Presentation: Women, older adults, and individuals with diabetes may experience less typical symptoms like indigestion, back pain, or extreme fatigue without classic chest pain.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Emergency Call: If you suspect a heart attack, call emergency services (e.g., 911 or your local emergency number) immediately. Do not drive yourself to the hospital.
  • Aspirin Use: If advised by emergency dispatch and not allergic or contraindicated, chew a regular-strength aspirin (325 mg) to help prevent further clotting.
  • Nitroglycerin Administration: If prescribed nitroglycerin, take it as directed while awaiting emergency medical help.
  • Risk Factor Management: Proactively manage conditions like high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and diabetes through medication and lifestyle changes.
  • Smoking Cessation: Quitting smoking is one of the most impactful steps to reduce heart attack risk.
  • Regular Check-ups: Adhere to routine medical examinations to monitor cardiovascular health and address emerging risks.
  • Medication Adherence: Consistently take all prescribed medications for heart conditions or risk factors as directed by your physician.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Acute Phase: Initially, patients are often NPO (nil per os) or on clear liquids, progressing to a soft, low-sodium, low-fat diet as tolerated.
  • Sodium Restriction: Limit intake of processed foods, canned goods, cured meats, and restaurant meals high in sodium to manage blood pressure and fluid retention.
  • Saturated and Trans Fats: Avoid red and processed meats, fried foods, full-fat dairy, and commercially baked goods to reduce cholesterol levels.
  • Cholesterol Intake: Minimize foods high in dietary cholesterol such as organ meats and egg yolks, especially if hypercholesterolemia is a concern.
  • Increase Fiber: Incorporate plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes to support heart health and cholesterol management.
  • Omega-3 Fatty Acids: Include sources like fatty fish (salmon, mackerel), flaxseeds, and walnuts, which have anti-inflammatory properties.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, typically water, unless specific fluid restrictions are in place due to heart failure.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Remain Calm: Reassure the patient and help them stay as calm as possible.
  • Call Emergency Services: Immediately dial emergency services (e.g., 911/112) and clearly state the patient's symptoms.
  • Loosen Clothing: Help the patient loosen any tight clothing around their neck or chest to ease breathing.
  • Positioning: Assist the patient into a comfortable position, often sitting up with knees bent, to help with breathing.
  • Medication Assistance: If the patient has prescribed nitroglycerin, help them take it as directed, provided they are conscious and able to swallow.
  • Do Not Delay: Emphasize to the patient that time is critical and immediate medical attention is essential; do not wait for symptoms to subside.
  • Monitor Vitals: Observe the patient for changes in consciousness, breathing, or skin color while awaiting paramedics.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Recognition is Key: Educate patients on recognizing heart attack symptoms and the critical importance of calling emergency services immediately.
  • Rapid Diagnosis: Emphasize the need for prompt ECG and cardiac biomarker (e.g., troponin) assessment to confirm diagnosis and guide treatment.
  • Reperfusion Therapy: Highlight the urgency of restoring blood flow, either through percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or thrombolytic therapy, within the therapeutic window.
  • Comprehensive Management: Stress the importance of long-term medical therapy (antiplatelets, statins, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors) and aggressive risk factor modification.
  • Cardiac Rehabilitation: Recommend participation in structured cardiac rehabilitation programs to optimize recovery, improve functional capacity, and reduce future events.
  • Patient Education: Provide thorough education on medication adherence, lifestyle changes, and symptom recurrence to empower patients in their self-management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology Review: Understand the mechanisms of coronary artery occlusion, myocardial ischemia, and subsequent infarction, including cellular changes.
  • ECG Interpretation: Master the identification of ST-segment elevation (STEMI), non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), and other ischemic changes.
  • Pharmacology: Familiarize with acute and long-term medications, including antiplatelets, anticoagulants, nitrates, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and statins, including their indications, dosages, and side effects.
  • Nursing Interventions: Prioritize pain management, oxygen therapy, continuous cardiac monitoring, vital sign assessment, and emotional support for the patient and family.
  • Post-MI Complications: Monitor for and manage potential complications such as arrhythmias, heart failure, cardiogenic shock, and pericarditis.
  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive teaching on lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, smoking cessation), medication regimen, and recognizing recurrent symptoms.
  • Cardiac Rehabilitation Referral: Facilitate patient enrollment in cardiac rehabilitation programs and explain their benefits.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: I21.0-I21.9 (Acute myocardial infarction, various types and unspecified).
  • Key Biomarkers: Cardiac Troponin I (cTnI) and Troponin T (cTnT), Creatine Kinase-MB (CK-MB).
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Electrocardiogram (ECG), Echocardiography, Coronary Angiography, Cardiac MRI.
  • Pharmacological Interventions (Acute): Aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors (Clopidogrel, Ticagrelor, Prasugrel), Heparin (UFH/LMWH), Nitroglycerin, Morphine, Beta-blockers.
  • Pharmacological Interventions (Chronic): Statins, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, Beta-blockers, Aldosterone antagonists.
  • Interventional Procedures: Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) with stent placement, Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG) surgery.
  • Complications: Arrhythmias (Ventricular Fibrillation, Ventricular Tachycardia), Heart Failure, Cardiogenic Shock, Pericarditis, Ventricular Septal Rupture, Papillary Muscle Rupture.
