# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a type of high blood pressure that affects the arteries in your lungs and the right side of your heart.
* **Progression:** It is a serious, progressive condition that can lead to right heart failure if left untreated.
* **Types:** Classified into five groups based on cause, including pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), PH due to left heart disease, PH due to lung diseases, chronic thromboembolic PH (CTEPH), and PH with unclear multifactorial mechanisms.
* **Impact:** The increased pressure makes it harder for your heart to pump blood through your lungs, leading to reduced oxygen delivery to the body.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Dyspnea:** Shortness of breath, especially during exertion, which may worsen over time.
* **Fatigue:** Persistent tiredness and lack of energy, even with minimal activity.
* **Chest Pain:** Angina-like chest discomfort, often located in the front of the chest.
* **Dizziness/Syncope:** Lightheadedness or fainting spells, particularly during physical activity, due to insufficient blood flow to the brain.
* **Edema:** Swelling in the ankles, legs, and abdomen due to fluid retention.
* **Cyanosis:** Bluish discoloration of the lips or skin, indicating low oxygen levels.
* **Palpitations:** Sensation of a rapid or irregular heartbeat.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Medication Adherence:** Strictly follow prescribed medication regimens, including vasodilators, diuretics, and anticoagulants, without skipping doses.
* **Avoid Strenuous Activity:** Limit activities that cause significant shortness of breath or chest pain; discuss appropriate exercise levels with your physician.
* **Manage Underlying Conditions:** Diligently treat any co-existing conditions such as heart failure, lung disease, or sleep apnea, as they can worsen PH.
* **Avoid High Altitudes:** High altitudes can decrease oxygen levels and exacerbate symptoms; consult your doctor before traveling to elevated areas.
* **Vaccinations:** Stay up-to-date with influenza and pneumococcal vaccinations to prevent respiratory infections that can complicate PH.
* **NSAID Use:** Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) as they can cause fluid retention and worsen heart failure symptoms.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Low Sodium Diet:** Restrict sodium intake to help manage fluid retention and reduce strain on the heart.
* **Fluid Management:** Your physician may recommend fluid restriction if you have significant fluid overload or heart failure symptoms.
* **Avoid Excessive Caffeine/Alcohol:** Limit intake of stimulants and alcohol, as they can affect heart rate and blood pressure.
* **Maintain Healthy Weight:** Work towards maintaining a healthy body weight to reduce the workload on your heart and lungs.
* **Balanced Nutrition:** Focus on a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins to support overall health and energy levels.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Regular Follow-ups:** Attend all scheduled appointments with your cardiologist and pulmonologist for ongoing monitoring and treatment adjustments.
* **Oxygen Therapy:** If prescribed, use supplemental oxygen as directed to maintain adequate oxygen saturation, especially during activity or sleep.
* **Recognize Worsening Symptoms:** Be vigilant for signs of decompensation, such as increased shortness of breath, swelling, or fainting, and report them immediately.
* **Support System:** Engage with support groups or family members to cope with the emotional and physical challenges of living with PH.
* **Travel Planning:** Discuss travel plans, especially air travel, with your healthcare team to ensure necessary precautions and oxygen arrangements are made.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Early Diagnosis:** Prompt and accurate diagnosis, often involving right heart catheterization, is crucial for initiating timely and effective treatment.
* **Multidisciplinary Care:** Management of PH requires a collaborative approach involving cardiologists, pulmonologists, and other specialists.
* **Tailored Treatment:** Treatment plans are highly individualized, focusing on specific PH types and patient response to therapies, including advanced vasodilators.
* **Patient Education:** Empowering patients with knowledge about their condition and treatment is vital for self-management and adherence.
* **Lifestyle Modifications:** Emphasize the importance of smoking cessation, appropriate exercise, and dietary adjustments as adjuncts to medical therapy.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pathophysiology:** Understand the mechanisms of PH, including endothelial dysfunction, vasoconstriction, and vascular remodeling in pulmonary arteries.
* **Nursing Assessments:** Conduct thorough respiratory and cardiovascular assessments, monitor vital signs, oxygen saturation, and fluid balance.
* **Medication Management:** Educate patients on the purpose, dosage, administration, and potential side effects of PH-specific medications (e.g., prostacyclin analogues, endothelin receptor antagonists).
* **Oxygen Safety:** Instruct patients on the safe use and maintenance of oxygen delivery systems at home.
* **Patient Education:** Provide comprehensive teaching on symptom recognition, activity limitations, dietary modifications, and the importance of adherence to the treatment plan.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Diagnostic Tools:** Key diagnostics include Echocardiogram, Right Heart Catheterization (gold standard), CT Pulmonary Angiogram, Ventilation-Perfusion (V/Q) Scan, and Pulmonary Function Tests.
* **WHO Classification:** The World Health Organization (WHO) categorizes PH into five groups based on etiology and pathophysiology.
* **Drug Classes:** Specific pharmacotherapies include Prostacyclin Analogues (e.g., epoprostenol, treprostinil), Endothelin Receptor Antagonists (e.g., bosentan, ambrisentan), Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) Inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil), and Soluble Guanylate Cyclase (sGC) Stimulators (e.g., riociguat).
* **NYHA Functional Classification:** Used to assess the severity of symptoms and functional limitations in patients with PH, similar to heart failure.
* **Hemodynamic Parameters:** Mean Pulmonary Artery Pressure (mPAP) > 20 mmHg at rest, Pulmonary Artery Wedge Pressure (PAWP) ≤ 15 mmHg, and Pulmonary Vascular Resistance (PVR) > 2 Wood units are diagnostic criteria for pre-capillary PH.
