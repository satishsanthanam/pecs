Aortic stenosis

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Heart valve disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Aortic stenosis is the narrowing of the aortic valve opening, which restricts blood flow from the left ventricle to the aorta.
  • Impact: This narrowing forces the heart to work harder to pump blood, leading to left ventricular hypertrophy and potential heart failure.
  • Causes: Most commonly caused by age-related calcification, but can also be due to a congenital bicuspid aortic valve or rheumatic fever.
  • Progression: The condition often progresses slowly over many years, with symptoms typically appearing in later stages.
  • Severity: Classified as mild, moderate, or severe based on the degree of valve narrowing and pressure gradient.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Angina: Chest pain or discomfort, often exacerbated by physical exertion.
  • Syncope: Fainting or lightheadedness, especially during exercise or sudden standing.
  • Dyspnea: Shortness of breath, initially with exertion, progressing to rest in advanced stages.
  • Fatigue: Persistent tiredness and reduced exercise tolerance due to inadequate blood supply.
  • Palpitations: Sensation of a rapid, fluttering, or pounding heart.
  • Heart Murmur: A characteristic harsh systolic ejection murmur heard on auscultation, often radiating to the carotid arteries.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Activity Restriction: Avoid strenuous physical activity, heavy lifting, and competitive sports, particularly if symptomatic or with severe stenosis.
  • Infection Prophylaxis: Maintain excellent oral hygiene and discuss antibiotic prophylaxis with your physician for certain dental or surgical procedures, especially if a prosthetic valve is present.
  • Medication Adherence: Strictly follow all prescribed medications for symptom management, blood pressure control, and heart rate regulation.
  • Regular Monitoring: Adhere to scheduled follow-up appointments, including echocardiograms, to monitor disease progression and ventricular function.
  • Avoid Dehydration: Maintain adequate hydration, but avoid excessive fluid intake that could strain the heart, especially if heart failure symptoms are present.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Restriction: Limit sodium intake to help manage blood pressure and reduce fluid retention, thereby decreasing cardiac workload.
  • Heart-Healthy Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins to support overall cardiovascular health.
  • Fluid Management: Monitor fluid intake closely, particularly if experiencing symptoms of heart failure, as advised by your cardiologist.
  • Avoid Stimulants: Limit caffeine and other stimulants that can increase heart rate and potentially exacerbate symptoms.
  • Weight Management: Maintain a healthy body weight to reduce the overall strain on the heart.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Sudden Worsening: Seek immediate medical attention for sudden onset or worsening of chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or recurrent fainting spells.
  • Infection Signs: Report any signs of infection (e.g., fever, chills, unexplained fatigue) promptly, especially if you have a prosthetic heart valve.
  • Medication Side Effects: Be aware of potential side effects of prescribed medications and report any concerning symptoms to your healthcare provider.
  • Dental Procedures: Always inform your dentist about your aortic stenosis and any prosthetic valves before undergoing any dental procedures.
  • Pregnancy Risk: Pregnancy can pose significant risks for women with severe aortic stenosis; discuss family planning thoroughly with your cardiologist.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis: Emphasize the critical importance of early diagnosis through physical examination and echocardiography to guide timely intervention.
  • Symptom Recognition: Educate patients on recognizing and promptly reporting symptoms to prevent complications and improve outcomes.
  • Treatment Options: Discuss medical management for symptoms, and surgical (SAVR) or transcatheter (TAVR) aortic valve replacement options for severe, symptomatic cases.
  • Risk Factor Modification: Advise on aggressive management of cardiovascular risk factors such as hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes.
  • Prognosis: Explain that severe symptomatic aortic stenosis carries a poor prognosis without intervention, highlighting the benefits of timely treatment.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Assessment Focus: Prioritize auscultation for a characteristic systolic ejection murmur, palpation for a thrill, and assessment for pulsus parvus et tardus (weak and delayed pulse).
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on symptom recognition, medication adherence, activity restrictions, and signs of worsening heart failure.
  • Pre-Op/Post-Op Care: Understand pre-operative preparation and post-operative care for SAVR/TAVR patients, including monitoring for complications like arrhythmias, bleeding, and infection.
  • Hemodynamic Monitoring: Be proficient in monitoring vital signs, cardiac rhythm, and hemodynamic parameters, especially in critical care settings.
  • Lifestyle Counseling: Provide counseling on sodium restriction, fluid management, and the importance of regular follow-up with cardiology.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Echocardiography: Gold standard for diagnosis, assessing valve morphology, leaflet motion, transvalvular gradients, and ventricular function.
  • Cardiac Catheterization: Used to confirm diagnosis, assess coronary artery disease, and measure gradients in complex or discordant cases.
  • Electrocardiogram (ECG): May show left ventricular hypertrophy, left atrial enlargement, or conduction abnormalities.
  • Chest X-ray: May reveal post-stenotic dilation of the ascending aorta, calcification of the aortic valve, or signs of pulmonary congestion.
  • BNP/NT-proBNP: Biomarkers that can indicate ventricular stretch and aid in assessing heart failure severity and prognosis.
  • Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement (SAVR): Open-heart surgery to replace the diseased aortic valve with a prosthetic valve.
  • Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR): Minimally invasive procedure for valve replacement, often preferred for high-risk or inoperable patients.
