Transcatheter aortic valve implantation – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Heart valve disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting (over 10 lbs) and strenuous activities for at least 4-6 weeks or as advised by your doctor. Gradually increase your activity level.
  • Incision Site Care: Keep the access site clean and dry. Avoid soaking in baths or swimming pools until cleared by your physician. Watch for signs of infection.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, especially antiplatelet agents, exactly as directed. Do not stop any medication without consulting your doctor.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Attend all scheduled follow-up visits and diagnostic tests (e.g., echocardiograms) to monitor your valve function and recovery.
  • Warning Signs: Know when to seek immediate medical attention for symptoms like new chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or signs of infection at the incision site.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • New or Worsening Chest Pain: Any discomfort, pressure, or pain in the chest that is new, severe, or not relieved by rest.
  • Sudden Shortness of Breath: Difficulty breathing at rest or with minimal exertion, or a sudden worsening of existing breathlessness.
  • Dizziness or Fainting Spells: Episodes of lightheadedness, vertigo, or loss of consciousness.
  • Incision Site Changes: Increased redness, swelling, warmth, pus-like discharge, or severe pain at the groin or chest incision.
  • Unexplained Fever: A temperature above 100.4°F (38°C) without a clear cause, which could indicate infection.
  • Significant Leg Swelling: New or worsening swelling in one or both legs, especially if accompanied by pain or tenderness.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Bleeding Risk Management: Be vigilant for signs of bleeding (e.g., unusual bruising, nosebleeds, blood in urine/stool) due to antiplatelet medications. Use a soft toothbrush and electric razor.
  • Infection Prevention: Practice meticulous hand hygiene. Avoid crowded places or contact with sick individuals during the initial recovery phase. Report any signs of infection promptly.
  • Driving Restrictions: Refrain from driving for at least 1-2 weeks post-procedure, or as advised by your cardiologist, especially if you experienced any neurological events.
  • Dental Procedures: Inform your dentist about your TAVI. Prophylactic antibiotics may be recommended for certain dental procedures to prevent infective endocarditis, based on current guidelines.
  • Avoid Straining: Prevent constipation by maintaining adequate hydration and fiber intake to avoid straining during bowel movements, which can increase pressure on the incision site.
  • Weight Management: Avoid significant weight gain or loss without consulting your healthcare provider, as this can impact cardiac function and medication dosages.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Restriction: Limit intake of high-sodium foods (processed foods, canned soups, cured meats) to help manage fluid retention and blood pressure.
  • Fluid Intake: Maintain adequate hydration as advised by your doctor. If on diuretics, monitor fluid intake closely to prevent dehydration or fluid overload.
  • Potassium Monitoring: If prescribed diuretics or ACE inhibitors, your doctor may monitor potassium levels. Discuss any dietary supplements containing potassium.
  • Consistent Vitamin K Intake: If you are on warfarin (less common post-TAVI but possible for other conditions), maintain a consistent intake of Vitamin K-rich foods (e.g., leafy greens) to avoid fluctuations in INR.
  • Alcohol Consumption: Limit or avoid alcohol as it can interact with medications and affect heart rhythm. Discuss safe limits with your physician.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a heart-healthy diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins to support overall recovery and cardiovascular health.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Recognize Emergency Signs: Be familiar with the patient's warning signs (e.g., severe chest pain, sudden breathlessness, fainting) and know when to call emergency services.
  • Medication Support: Assist the patient in organizing and taking medications as prescribed, ensuring no doses are missed, especially antiplatelets.
  • Activity Assistance: Help the patient with daily activities initially, ensuring they do not overexert themselves or lift heavy objects.
  • Emotional Support: Provide a calm and supportive environment. Listen to the patient's concerns and encourage open communication with the healthcare team.
  • Emergency Contacts: Keep a list of emergency contacts, including the cardiologist's office and local emergency services, readily accessible.
  • Incision Site Monitoring: Regularly check the patient's incision site for any signs of infection or bleeding and report concerns to the medical team.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Commitment to Follow-up: Adherence to all scheduled follow-up appointments and diagnostic imaging is paramount for long-term success and monitoring of your new valve.
  • Medication Compliance is Key: Your prescribed medications, particularly antiplatelets, are critical to prevent complications like blood clots. Do not alter or discontinue them without direct medical advice.
  • Gradual Recovery Process: Recovery is a journey, not a race. Listen to your body, gradually increase activity, and avoid pushing yourself too hard too soon.
  • Proactive Symptom Reporting: Any new or worsening symptoms, especially cardiac or related to the access site, must be reported to your healthcare team immediately. Early intervention is crucial.
  • Lifestyle Optimization: Embrace heart-healthy lifestyle changes, including diet and exercise, to maximize the benefits of your TAVI and improve overall cardiovascular well-being.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact your care team with any questions or concerns. We are here to support your recovery.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Discharge Education: Ensure the patient and caregiver fully understand all discharge instructions, including medication regimen, activity restrictions, and warning signs. Use teach-back methods.
  • Post-Procedure Assessment: Prioritize assessment of the access site for hematoma, pseudoaneurysm, or infection. Monitor vital signs, peripheral pulses, and neurological status.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Verify all discharge medications, especially antiplatelets and anticoagulants, ensuring correct dosages, frequencies, and patient understanding of their purpose.
  • Early Complication Recognition: Educate on and monitor for signs of post-TAVI complications such as stroke, acute kidney injury, new-onset atrial fibrillation, or valve-related issues.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient anxiety and provide emotional support. Connect patients with cardiac rehabilitation programs and support groups as appropriate.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Meticulously document all patient education, assessments, and communications with the healthcare team to ensure continuity of care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT): Typically prescribed for 3-6 months post-TAVI, followed by single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) indefinitely, based on individual risk assessment and current guidelines (e.g., ACC/AHA, ESC).
  • Anticoagulation Management: For patients with pre-existing indications for oral anticoagulation (e.g., atrial fibrillation), a careful balance with antiplatelet therapy is required, often involving a short period of triple therapy or adjusted DAPT.
  • Echocardiographic Surveillance: Baseline echocardiogram post-TAVI, followed by repeat studies at 1 month, 6-12 months, and annually thereafter, or as clinically indicated, to assess valve function and identify potential complications.
  • Access Site Complications: Common complications include hematoma, pseudoaneurysm, arteriovenous fistula, and retroperitoneal hemorrhage. Vigilant monitoring and early intervention are crucial.
  • Paravalvular Leak (PVL): Mild PVL is common and often benign, but moderate-to-severe PVL can lead to hemolysis or heart failure and may require intervention.
  • Infective Endocarditis Prophylaxis: Current guidelines generally recommend prophylaxis only for patients with prosthetic valves undergoing dental procedures involving manipulation of gingival tissue or periapical region of teeth, or perforation of the oral mucosa, if they have a history of infective endocarditis.
