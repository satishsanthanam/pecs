Pericarditis in children

🏠 Home / Heart and blood vessels / Myopericardial disease

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Pericarditis is an inflammation of the pericardium, the two-layered sac surrounding the heart, often causing chest pain.
  • Common Causes in Children: Most cases are idiopathic or viral (e.g., Coxsackievirus, adenovirus). Other causes include bacterial infections, autoimmune diseases, or post-cardiac injury syndrome.
  • Clinical Course: It can be acute, recurrent, or chronic. While often benign and self-limiting, it requires careful monitoring for complications like cardiac tamponade or constrictive pericarditis.
  • Age Group: Can affect children of any age, though it is less common than in adults and often presents with atypical symptoms in younger children.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Chest Pain: Typically sharp, stabbing, or pleuritic, often worse with deep breathing, coughing, or lying flat, and relieved by leaning forward. In younger children, it may manifest as abdominal pain or irritability.
  • Fever: Common, especially in infectious etiologies, and may be accompanied by chills.
  • Fatigue and Malaise: General feeling of tiredness, weakness, and discomfort, often disproportionate to other symptoms.
  • Dyspnea: Shortness of breath, which may be due to pain or, in severe cases, pericardial effusion leading to cardiac tamponade.
  • Pericardial Friction Rub: A classic auscultatory finding, a scratchy sound heard best at the left sternal border, indicating inflamed pericardial layers rubbing together.
  • Muffled Heart Sounds: A sign of significant pericardial effusion, where fluid accumulation dampens the heart sounds.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Monitor for Cardiac Tamponade: Closely observe for signs of hemodynamic compromise such as muffled heart sounds, hypotension, jugular venous distension, and pulsus paradoxus, which require urgent intervention.
  • Restrict Physical Activity: Children with pericarditis should strictly avoid strenuous physical activity and competitive sports until symptoms resolve and inflammatory markers normalize to prevent recurrence or complications like myopericarditis.
  • Medication Adherence: Ensure strict adherence to prescribed anti-inflammatory medications (e.g., NSAIDs, colchicine) and complete the full course as directed by the physician.
  • Avoid NSAIDs in Specific Conditions: Exercise caution or avoid NSAIDs in children with certain contraindications like active peptic ulcer disease, significant renal impairment, or specific viral infections where NSAIDs might be less effective or contraindicated.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Encourage adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration, especially if the child has a fever or is experiencing malaise.
  • Soft, Bland Diet: If chest pain or general discomfort makes eating difficult, offer easily digestible, soft, and bland foods to ensure nutritional intake.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit foods and beverages that may exacerbate gastrointestinal upset, particularly if NSAIDs are prescribed (e.g., spicy foods, excessive caffeine, highly acidic juices).
  • Medication-Specific Advice: Administer certain medications (e.g., NSAIDs) with food or milk to minimize gastric irritation and improve tolerability.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Seek Immediate Medical Attention: If the child experiences worsening chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting spells, signs of shock (e.g., cold clammy skin, rapid heart rate), or decreased urine output.
  • Activity Restrictions: Strictly enforce activity restrictions as advised by the physician to prevent complications and promote healing; explain the rationale to the child and family.
  • Medication Management: Do not alter medication dosages or discontinue treatment without consulting a healthcare provider, even if symptoms improve.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Ensure all scheduled follow-up appointments with cardiology are attended to monitor recovery, assess for recurrence, and detect any long-term complications.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Approach: Diagnosis involves clinical evaluation, ECG (often showing diffuse ST elevation and/or PR depression), echocardiogram (to assess for effusion and cardiac function), and blood tests (inflammatory markers like CRP, ESR, and cardiac biomarkers like troponin).
  • Treatment Strategy: Management typically includes anti-inflammatory medications such as NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen) and often colchicine to reduce inflammation and prevent recurrence. Corticosteroids may be used in refractory cases or specific etiologies like autoimmune conditions.
  • Identifying Underlying Cause: It is crucial to investigate and treat any underlying cause, such as bacterial infection, autoimmune disease, or metabolic disorder, to ensure comprehensive management.
  • Prognosis: Most cases of acute pericarditis in children have a good prognosis, but careful monitoring for recurrence or progression to constrictive pericarditis is essential, especially in recurrent or severe cases.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pain Assessment and Management: Regularly assess the child's chest pain using age-appropriate scales (e.g., Wong-Baker FACES, numeric scale) and administer prescribed analgesics effectively, noting pain characteristics and alleviating factors.
  • Vital Sign Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, including heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation, watching for signs of cardiac tamponade (e.g., pulsus paradoxus, narrow pulse pressure) or worsening condition.
  • Patient and Parent Education: Educate parents on the nature of pericarditis, medication administration, activity restrictions, and signs/symptoms requiring urgent medical attention, ensuring they understand the importance of adherence.
  • Emotional Support: Provide emotional support to the child and family, addressing anxieties related to a heart condition and hospitalization, and facilitating coping strategies.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: While often viral, consider bacterial (e.g., Staphylococcus, Streptococcus), fungal, tuberculous, autoimmune (e.g., SLE, JIA), metabolic (uremia), or post-cardiac injury syndrome (Dressler's syndrome) causes.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Diagnosis typically requires at least two of the following: characteristic chest pain, pericardial friction rub, new widespread ST-segment elevation or PR depression on ECG, and pericardial effusion (new or worsening).
  • Complications: Potential complications include pericardial effusion, cardiac tamponade (a medical emergency requiring pericardiocentesis), and chronic constrictive pericarditis.
  • Myopericarditis: Concurrent inflammation of the myocardium (heart muscle) and pericardium, indicated by elevated cardiac biomarkers (e.g., troponin) and potential myocardial dysfunction on echocardiography.
