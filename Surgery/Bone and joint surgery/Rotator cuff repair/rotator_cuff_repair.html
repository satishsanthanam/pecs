Rotator cuff repair

🏠 Home / Surgery / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Purpose: Surgical repair of one or more torn tendons within the rotator cuff of the shoulder.
  • Primary Goal: To alleviate pain, restore strength, and improve the range of motion in the affected shoulder.
  • Surgical Approaches: Can be performed arthroscopically (minimally invasive), mini-open, or through an open incision, depending on the tear's size and complexity.
  • Recovery Timeline: Involves a significant post-operative rehabilitation period, often requiring several months for full recovery and return to normal activities.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Chronic Shoulder Pain: Often worsens with overhead activities, lifting, or sleeping on the affected side.
  • Arm Weakness: Difficulty lifting, rotating, or reaching for objects.
  • Limited Range of Motion: Inability to fully raise or move the arm without discomfort.
  • Clicking or Catching: Sensations within the shoulder joint during movement.
  • Night Pain: Persistent discomfort that disrupts sleep, especially when lying on the injured shoulder.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Medical Clearance: Ensure all necessary medical evaluations are completed to confirm fitness for surgery.
  • Medication Disclosure: Inform your surgeon about all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies, especially blood thinners, which may need to be stopped before surgery.
  • Smoking Cessation: Strongly advised to stop smoking several weeks prior to surgery to optimize healing and reduce complication risks.
  • Post-operative Immobilization: Strict adherence to wearing the prescribed sling or immobilizer as directed by the surgeon.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid lifting, pushing, pulling, or sudden movements with the affected arm during the initial recovery phase.
  • Fall Prevention: Secure your home environment to prevent falls that could re-injure the shoulder.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nothing by mouth) guidelines for food and liquids for the specified duration before surgery.
  • Post-anesthesia Reintroduction: Begin with clear liquids, then progress to light, easily digestible foods as tolerated after the procedure.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on consuming adequate protein, vitamins (especially C and D), and minerals (calcium, zinc) to support tissue repair and bone health.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration with water and clear fluids to aid recovery and medication metabolism.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Arranged Transportation: Ensure a responsible adult is available to drive you home after surgery due to the effects of anesthesia.
  • Post-operative Support: Arrange for assistance with daily tasks, such as dressing, bathing, and meal preparation, for the initial recovery period.
  • Pain Management Adherence: Follow the prescribed pain medication regimen precisely to manage discomfort effectively.
  • Wound Care Instructions: Adhere to all instructions for surgical site care, including dressing changes and keeping the incision clean and dry.
  • Monitor for Complications: Watch for signs of infection (fever, increased redness, swelling, warmth, pus from the incision) or deep vein thrombosis (calf pain, swelling) and report immediately.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Commitment to Rehabilitation: Successful outcomes heavily rely on consistent and dedicated participation in the prescribed physical therapy program.
  • Patience with Recovery: Understand that regaining full strength and range of motion is a gradual process that requires time and adherence to restrictions.
  • Avoid Premature Activity: Resist the urge to rush back to strenuous activities or sports to prevent re-injury of the repaired tendons.
  • Regular Follow-up: Attend all scheduled post-operative appointments to monitor healing progress and adjust the rehabilitation plan as needed.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Provide comprehensive teaching on post-operative expectations, pain management, sling application, and activity limitations.
  • Neurovascular Assessment: Perform regular checks of distal pulses, capillary refill, sensation, and motor function of the affected extremity.
  • Pain Assessment and Management: Utilize appropriate pain scales, administer analgesics as prescribed, and evaluate their effectiveness.
  • Mobility and Positioning: Assist with safe transfers and ensure proper positioning and sling use to protect the surgical repair.
  • Patient and Family Education: Reinforce discharge instructions, signs of complications, and the importance of adherence to physical therapy.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: S46.0 (Tear of muscle(s) and tendon(s) of rotator cuff of shoulder).
  • CPT Codes: 29827 (Arthroscopy, shoulder, surgical; with rotator cuff repair).
  • Anesthesia Type: Typically general anesthesia, often supplemented with a regional nerve block for post-operative pain control.
  • Post-operative Protocol: Involves phases of immobilization, passive range of motion, active range of motion, and progressive strengthening exercises.
  • Potential Complications: Infection, stiffness, re-tear, nerve injury, adhesive capsulitis, and persistent pain.
