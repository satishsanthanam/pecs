# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Procedure Overview:** Vertebroplasty and kyphoplasty are minimally invasive procedures used to treat vertebral compression fractures (VCFs), often caused by osteoporosis, trauma, or tumors.
* **Vertebroplasty:** Involves injecting bone cement (polymethylmethacrylate or PMMA) directly into the fractured vertebra to stabilize it and reduce pain.
* **Kyphoplasty:** Similar to vertebroplasty, but a balloon is first inserted into the fractured vertebra and inflated to create a cavity and partially restore vertebral height before the cement is injected.
* **Primary Goal:** Both procedures aim to alleviate severe back pain caused by VCFs and improve spinal stability. Kyphoplasty also seeks to correct spinal deformity (kyphosis) and restore lost vertebral height.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Acute or Chronic Back Pain:** Severe, localized pain in the back, often worsening with movement, standing, or walking, and sometimes radiating.
* **Localized Tenderness:** Pain upon palpation over the affected vertebral segment.
* **Loss of Height:** Gradual or sudden decrease in stature due to multiple compression fractures.
* **Stooped Posture (Kyphosis):** Development of an exaggerated forward curvature of the spine, particularly in the thoracic region.
* **Limited Mobility:** Difficulty bending, twisting, or performing daily activities due to pain and spinal instability.
* **Neurological Symptoms (Rare):** In some cases, severe compression can lead to nerve root irritation or spinal cord compression, causing numbness, tingling, or weakness in the extremities.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Anticoagulant Management:** Patients on blood thinners (e.g., warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) must discontinue them for a specified period before the procedure to minimize bleeding risk, as directed by the physician.
* **Allergy Assessment:** Inform the medical team of any allergies, especially to contrast dye, local anesthetics, or bone cement components.
* **Infection Screening:** The procedure is contraindicated in the presence of active systemic or local spinal infection. Pre-operative screening may be necessary.
* **Spinal Canal Compromise:** Careful imaging review is essential to ensure no significant spinal canal narrowing or nerve root compression that could be exacerbated by cement injection.
* **Pregnancy Status:** Women of childbearing age should be screened for pregnancy due to radiation exposure during fluoroscopy.
* **Post-Procedure Activity:** Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous activities, and twisting motions for several weeks post-procedure to allow for proper healing and cement setting.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Pre-Procedure Fasting:** Patients typically need to fast (NPO - nothing by mouth) for 6-8 hours prior to the procedure, especially if sedation or general anesthesia is planned, to prevent aspiration.
* **Hydration Post-Sedation:** After the procedure and once fully awake, begin with clear liquids and gradually advance to a light diet as tolerated to prevent nausea and ensure adequate hydration.
* **Bone Health Support:** Long-term dietary focus should include adequate intake of calcium and Vitamin D to support bone health and help prevent future osteoporotic fractures, if applicable.
* **Avoid Alcohol:** Refrain from alcohol consumption for at least 24-48 hours post-procedure, especially if sedatives were administered, as it can interact with medications and impair judgment.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Post-Procedure Monitoring:** Closely observe the patient for any new or worsening back pain, numbness, tingling, weakness in the legs, or changes in bowel/bladder function. Report immediately.
* **Activity Restrictions:** Ensure the patient adheres strictly to post-operative activity limitations, including avoiding bending, twisting, and lifting heavy objects, as advised by the surgeon.
* **Pain Management:** Assist with prescribed pain medication administration and monitor its effectiveness and any side effects.
* **Incision Site Care:** Keep the small incision site clean and dry. Monitor for signs of infection such as redness, swelling, warmth, or discharge.
* **Mobilization Assistance:** Provide assistance with ambulation and transfers initially, as the patient may experience some residual pain or weakness.
* **Emergency Signs:** Educate the patient and family on signs requiring immediate medical attention, including fever, severe unremitting pain, new neurological deficits, or signs of infection.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Indications:** These procedures are primarily indicated for painful vertebral compression fractures that have not responded to conservative management (rest, pain medication, bracing) for several weeks.
* **Diagnostic Confirmation:** Accurate diagnosis requires imaging studies such as X-rays, MRI, or CT scans to confirm the fracture, assess its age, and rule out other causes of back pain or spinal instability.
* **Risk-Benefit Analysis:** A thorough discussion of potential benefits (pain relief, improved mobility) versus risks (cement leakage, infection, nerve damage, pulmonary embolism) is crucial for informed consent.
* **Underlying Cause Management:** It is imperative to address the underlying cause of the VCF, particularly osteoporosis, through medication, lifestyle modifications, and follow-up care to prevent future fractures.
* **Patient Selection:** Careful patient selection is key to successful outcomes, considering factors like fracture stability, neurological status, and overall health.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pre-operative Assessment:** Conduct a comprehensive neurological assessment (motor, sensory, reflexes), pain assessment, and review of medication history, especially anticoagulants.
* **Intra-operative Support:** Assist with patient positioning (prone), ensure sterile field maintenance, monitor vital signs, and provide support during fluoroscopy-guided needle placement.
* **Post-operative Neurovascular Checks:** Perform frequent neurovascular assessments of the lower extremities (sensation, motor strength, pulses, capillary refill) to detect any neurological compromise.
* **Pain Management & Education:** Administer analgesics as ordered and educate the patient on pain scale use, medication side effects, and non-pharmacological pain relief strategies.
* **Complication Monitoring:** Vigilantly monitor for potential complications such as cement extravasation (leakage), infection, hematoma formation, or pulmonary embolism.
* **Discharge Planning:** Provide clear instructions on activity restrictions, wound care, medication regimen, signs of complications, and the importance of follow-up appointments.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Key Terms:** Vertebral Compression Fracture (VCF), Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA), Fluoroscopy, Balloon Kyphoplasty, Osteoporosis, Spinal Canal, Radiculopathy.
* **Anatomical Structures:** Thoracic Vertebrae, Lumbar Vertebrae, Spinal Cord, Nerve Roots, Pedicle.
* **Imaging Modalities:** X-ray, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), Computed Tomography (CT) Scan, Bone Scan.
* **Cement Leakage:** Extravasation of PMMA cement outside the vertebral body, potentially into the spinal canal, neural foramen, or vascular structures.
* **Indications for Procedure:** Painful osteoporotic VCFs, traumatic VCFs, or VCFs secondary to metastatic disease or multiple myeloma.
* **Contraindications:** Asymptomatic VCFs, active osteomyelitis, coagulopathy not correctable, spinal canal compromise with neurological deficit, burst fractures.
