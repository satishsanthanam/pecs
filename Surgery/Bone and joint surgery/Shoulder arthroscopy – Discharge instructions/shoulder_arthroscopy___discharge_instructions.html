Shoulder arthroscopy – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Surgery / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Discharge Readiness: You are being discharged after shoulder arthroscopy, a minimally invasive procedure to diagnose and treat shoulder joint problems.
  • Recovery Timeline: Initial recovery involves pain management and protecting the surgical site; full recovery can take several weeks to months, depending on the specific repair performed.
  • Follow-up Care: A crucial follow-up appointment with your surgeon is scheduled for [Date/Time] to monitor healing progress and initiate rehabilitation.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Expected Discomfort: Mild to moderate pain, swelling, and bruising around the shoulder and arm are common and manageable with prescribed analgesics.
  • Numbness/Tingling: Temporary numbness or tingling in the arm or hand may occur due to nerve block effects or post-operative swelling, but should gradually resolve.
  • Warning Signs: Immediately report increasing pain not relieved by medication, fever above 101°F (38.3°C), excessive redness or warmth around the incision, pus-like drainage, or sudden severe swelling.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Sling Use: Wear your sling continuously as instructed by your surgeon, typically for [X] weeks, removing it only for prescribed exercises or hygiene as permitted.
  • Wound Care: Keep incision sites clean and dry. Do not submerge the shoulder in water (no baths, swimming, or hot tubs) until explicitly cleared by your surgeon. Change dressings as instructed.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid lifting, pushing, pulling, or reaching overhead with the operative arm. Do not drive while taking narcotic pain medication or until cleared by your surgeon.
  • Ice Application: Apply ice packs to the shoulder for 15-20 minutes every 1-2 hours to reduce swelling and pain, ensuring a barrier (e.g., towel) between the ice pack and your skin.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Post-Anesthesia Diet: Start with clear liquids and light, easily digestible foods (e.g., crackers, toast) and gradually advance to your regular diet as tolerated to prevent nausea.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration by drinking plenty of non-alcoholic fluids to aid recovery, support tissue healing, and prevent constipation, especially if taking pain medications.
  • Nutrient Support: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein, vitamins (especially C and D), and minerals (calcium) to support tissue repair and overall healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assistance with ADLs: Be prepared to assist the patient with activities of daily living (ADLs) such as dressing, bathing, and meal preparation, particularly during the initial post-operative period.
  • Medication Management: Help the patient adhere to their prescribed medication schedule, especially for pain relief, and monitor for any adverse side effects.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Be vigilant for signs of infection (fever, increased redness, drainage) or other complications and know when to contact the medical team.
  • Safe Environment: Ensure a safe home environment, free of tripping hazards, and assist with mobility as needed to prevent falls, especially when the patient is on pain medication.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to PT: Strict adherence to the prescribed physical therapy regimen is paramount for restoring range of motion, strength, and functional recovery of the shoulder.
  • Pain Management: Manage pain effectively to facilitate participation in physical therapy and improve overall comfort, utilizing prescribed medications as directed.
  • Gradual Return to Activity: Avoid rushing the recovery process; a gradual, supervised return to activities and sports is essential to prevent re-injury and ensure long-term success.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact your surgeon's office with any concerns or questions that arise during your recovery period.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Post-Op Assessment: Conduct regular neurovascular assessments (color, temperature, sensation, capillary refill, pulses) of the affected extremity to detect any compromise.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Utilize a standardized pain scale to assess pain levels and evaluate the effectiveness of analgesia; educate the patient on non-pharmacological pain relief methods.
  • Patient Education: Reinforce all discharge instructions regarding sling use, wound care, activity restrictions, and identification of signs and symptoms of complications.
  • Mobility & Safety: Assist with safe transfers and ambulation, emphasizing fall prevention strategies, especially given potential post-anesthesia effects and pain medication side effects.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Arthroscopy: A minimally invasive surgical procedure using an arthroscope to visualize, diagnose, and treat problems inside a joint, commonly performed on the shoulder.
  • Rotator Cuff Repair: A common arthroscopic procedure involving the repair of torn tendons within the rotator cuff, crucial for shoulder movement and stability.
  • Labral Repair: Surgical repair of the labrum, a rim of cartilage around the shoulder socket, often performed to address instability or tears.
  • Post-operative Complications: Potential complications include infection, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), nerve injury, stiffness (arthrofibrosis), and re-tear of the repaired structures.
