Endovascular surgery

🏠 Home / Surgery / General surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Minimally Invasive Approach: Endovascular surgery treats vascular conditions from inside blood vessels using catheters, guidewires, and real-time imaging.
  • Key Advantages: Typically involves smaller incisions, reduced pain, shorter hospital stays, and faster recovery compared to traditional open surgery.
  • Common Applications: Used for conditions like aortic aneurysms (abdominal and thoracic), peripheral artery disease (PAD), carotid artery disease, and deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
  • Imaging Guidance: Procedures rely heavily on advanced imaging techniques such as fluoroscopy and intravascular ultrasound for precise device placement.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Aortic Aneurysm: May be asymptomatic until rupture, but can present with deep, constant back or abdominal pain, or a pulsatile mass in the abdomen.
  • Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD): Characterized by claudication (leg pain with walking), non-healing wounds on the feet or legs, numbness, or coldness in the affected limb.
  • Carotid Artery Disease: Often indicated by transient ischemic attacks (TIAs) or stroke symptoms such as sudden weakness, numbness, vision changes, or difficulty speaking.
  • Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT): Symptoms include swelling, pain, tenderness, warmth, and redness in the affected leg or arm.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Allergy Disclosure: Inform the medical team about any allergies, especially to contrast dye, iodine, or shellfish, prior to the procedure.
  • Medication Review: Discuss all current medications, particularly anticoagulants (blood thinners), antiplatelets, and diabetes medications, as adjustments may be necessary.
  • Kidney Function Assessment: Renal function will be evaluated before the procedure due to the use of contrast dye, which can impact kidney health.
  • Infection Prevention: Prophylactic antibiotics may be administered to minimize the risk of infection at the access site or within the vascular system.
  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Closely observe the access site (e.g., groin, wrist) for signs of bleeding, hematoma, or infection.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Patients are typically required to be NPO (nothing by mouth) for 6-8 hours before the surgery to prevent aspiration during sedation or anesthesia.
  • Hydration Protocol: Adequate fluid intake is encouraged before and after the procedure to help flush the contrast dye from the kidneys.
  • Post-Procedure Diet: Diet will be gradually advanced, usually starting with clear liquids and progressing to a regular diet as tolerated, especially after general anesthesia.
  • Medication-Related Considerations: Specific dietary restrictions related to medications (e.g., Vitamin K for warfarin) should be discussed with the physician if applicable to long-term management.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Informed Consent: Ensure the patient and/or legal guardian fully understands the procedure, potential risks, benefits, and alternatives before signing consent.
  • Vital Sign Monitoring: Continuous and meticulous monitoring of vital signs, including heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, and respiratory rate, is essential.
  • Access Site Surveillance: Regularly assess the vascular access site for bleeding, hematoma expansion, pulse changes, and signs of infection.
  • Neurovascular Checks: Perform frequent neurovascular assessments of the limb distal to the access site and neurological status, especially after carotid or cerebral interventions.
  • Activity Restrictions: Adhere strictly to post-procedure bed rest orders and activity limitations to prevent complications at the access site.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Patient Selection: Careful consideration of patient comorbidities, anatomical suitability, and overall health status is paramount in determining candidacy for endovascular repair versus open surgery.
  • Precision Imaging: The success of endovascular procedures hinges on high-quality intraoperative imaging (e.g., fluoroscopy, IVUS) for accurate device deployment and complication avoidance.
  • Multidisciplinary Collaboration: A team approach involving vascular surgeons, interventional radiologists, anesthesiologists, and specialized nursing staff optimizes patient outcomes.
  • Long-Term Surveillance: Emphasize the critical importance of regular post-procedure follow-up imaging (e.g., CTA, ultrasound) to monitor device integrity and detect potential complications like endoleaks.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-Operative Assessment: Conduct thorough patient history, medication reconciliation, allergy verification, baseline vital signs, and comprehensive peripheral pulse assessment.
  • Intra-Operative Support: Assist with patient positioning, maintain sterile field integrity, monitor patient hemodynamics, and anticipate equipment needs for the interventional team.
  • Post-Operative Care: Implement strict access site care, perform frequent neurovascular checks, manage pain effectively, monitor fluid balance, and provide detailed discharge education.
  • Complication Recognition: Be vigilant for signs of bleeding, hematoma, pseudoaneurysm, limb ischemia, contrast-induced nephropathy, or neurological deficits.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Key Procedures: Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR), Thoracic Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (TEVAR), Angioplasty, Stenting, Embolization, Thrombectomy.
  • Devices: Stent-grafts, bare metal stents, drug-eluting stents, coils, balloons, catheters, guidewires, embolic agents.
  • Imaging Modalities: Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA), Fluoroscopy, Intravascular Ultrasound (IVUS), Computed Tomography Angiography (CTA), Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA).
  • Potential Complications: Endoleak, access site complications (hematoma, pseudoaneurysm, dissection), contrast-induced nephropathy, stroke, device migration or fracture, infection.
