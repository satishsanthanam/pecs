Surgical gastrostomy

🏠 Home / Surgery / General surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Surgical gastrostomy involves creating an opening (stoma) through the abdominal wall directly into the stomach to insert a feeding tube.
  • Primary Purpose: Provides a long-term route for nutritional support, hydration, and medication administration when oral intake is insufficient or unsafe.
  • Indications: Commonly performed for patients with severe dysphagia, neurological conditions affecting swallowing, head and neck cancers, or conditions causing chronic malnutrition.
  • Types of Gastrostomy: Can be performed via open surgery, laparoscopically, or percutaneously (PEG), depending on patient factors and surgeon preference.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Dysphagia: Chronic difficulty or inability to swallow safely, leading to inadequate oral intake.
  • Significant Weight Loss: Unintentional and substantial weight loss due to malnourishment from impaired oral feeding.
  • Recurrent Aspiration Pneumonia: Repeated lung infections resulting from food or liquids entering the airway during swallowing.
  • Neurological Impairment: Conditions such as stroke, ALS, Parkinson's disease, or severe brain injury affecting swallowing reflexes.
  • Esophageal Obstruction: Blockages or strictures in the esophagus that prevent food passage, often due to tumors or severe scarring.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Stoma Site Care: Meticulous daily cleaning of the skin around the gastrostomy site with mild soap and water, ensuring it remains dry to prevent skin breakdown and infection.
  • Tube Patency: Regularly flush the gastrostomy tube with water before and after each feed or medication administration to prevent blockages.
  • Aspiration Prevention: Always elevate the patient's head to at least a 30-45 degree angle during feeding and maintain this position for 30-60 minutes afterward.
  • Medication Administration: Ensure all medications are in liquid form or finely crushed and dissolved before administration, flushing the tube before and after.
  • Leakage Monitoring: Watch for any signs of gastric fluid leakage around the stoma, which can indicate tube displacement or skin irritation, and report promptly.
  • Tube Security: Secure the gastrostomy tube to the abdomen to prevent accidental dislodgement or tension on the stoma site.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Enteral Formula Selection: Administer only prescribed enteral feeding formulas, ensuring they are at room temperature to prevent gastrointestinal discomfort.
  • Feeding Schedule Adherence: Strictly follow the physician-ordered feeding schedule, whether it's bolus feeds or continuous infusion, and prescribed volumes.
  • Water Flushes: Administer prescribed amounts of water flushes throughout the day to maintain hydration and tube patency, especially before and after feeds and medications.
  • Oral Intake Restrictions: Adhere to any specific NPO (nil per os) orders or restricted oral intake guidelines provided by the medical team.
  • Monitoring for Intolerance: Observe for signs of feeding intolerance such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension, cramping, or diarrhea, and report to the healthcare provider.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Protocol: Understand the immediate steps to take and who to contact in case of tube dislodgement, severe pain, signs of peritonitis, or significant bleeding.
  • Caregiver Training: Ensure all primary caregivers receive comprehensive training on gastrostomy tube care, feeding techniques, and complication recognition.
  • Proper Positioning: Always maintain an upright or semi-Fowler's position during and for at least 30 minutes after feeding to minimize aspiration risk.
  • Infection Watch: Monitor the stoma site daily for signs of infection, including redness, swelling, warmth, pain, or purulent discharge.
  • Equipment Maintenance: Regularly check feeding pump settings, tubing connections, and expiration dates of formulas and supplies.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Patient Selection: Careful evaluation of patient's prognosis, nutritional needs, and ability to tolerate surgery is paramount for gastrostomy placement.
  • Risk-Benefit Analysis: Discuss potential surgical risks (e.g., infection, bleeding, tube complications) against the significant benefits of improved nutrition and quality of life.
  • Long-Term Management: Emphasize the importance of ongoing follow-up with a multidisciplinary team, including dietitians, nurses, and gastroenterologists, for optimal care.
  • Patient and Family Education: Thorough education on home care, complication recognition, and when to seek medical attention is crucial for successful outcomes.
  • Alternative Feeding Routes: Consider other feeding options (e.g., nasogastric tube, jejunostomy) if gastrostomy is contraindicated or not feasible.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Perform regular assessments of the gastrostomy site for skin integrity, signs of infection, and tube security. Monitor nutritional status, weight, and hydration.
  • Feeding Administration: Master techniques for bolus and continuous enteral feeding, including proper formula preparation, pump programming, and flushing protocols.
  • Medication Management: Understand safe medication administration via gastrostomy tube, including crushing, dissolving, and flushing techniques to prevent tube occlusion.
  • Patient Education Role: Educate patients and families on daily stoma care, feeding procedures, troubleshooting common issues (e.g., tube blockage), and recognizing complications.
  • Complication Management: Be prepared to identify and manage common gastrostomy complications such as tube dislodgement, leakage, infection, and aspiration.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Procedure Codes: CPT codes for gastrostomy placement (e.g., 43830 for gastrostomy, open; 43831 for gastrostomy, laparoscopic; 49440 for percutaneous gastrostomy).
  • Diagnosis Codes: ICD-10 codes related to indications (e.g., R13.10 for dysphagia, unspecified; E43 for severe protein-calorie malnutrition; I69.391 for dysphagia following cerebral infarction).
  • Tube Types: Common gastrostomy tube types include balloon-retained tubes, mushroom-tip tubes, and low-profile (button) devices.
  • Potential Complications: Peritonitis, stoma infection, gastric leakage, tube occlusion, tube dislodgement, buried bumper syndrome, gastrocolic fistula.
  • Nutritional Support Guidelines: Refer to ASPEN (American Society for Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition) guidelines for enteral nutrition in adults and pediatric patients.
