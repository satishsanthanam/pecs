Myomectomy

🏠 Home / Surgery / Gynecologic surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Myomectomy is a surgical procedure to remove uterine fibroids (myomas) while preserving the uterus.
  • Primary Goal: To alleviate symptoms such as heavy menstrual bleeding, pelvic pain, pressure, and to improve fertility outcomes.
  • Preservation of Fertility: Unlike hysterectomy, myomectomy allows women to retain their uterus and potentially carry future pregnancies.
  • Types of Myomectomy: Can be performed via open abdominal incision (laparotomy), minimally invasive laparoscopy, or hysteroscopy (for fibroids inside the uterine cavity).
  • Indications: Typically recommended for symptomatic fibroids, fibroids causing infertility, or those rapidly growing.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Heavy Menstrual Bleeding: Prolonged or excessively heavy periods (menorrhagia) often leading to anemia.
  • Pelvic Pain and Pressure: Chronic discomfort, a feeling of fullness, or pressure in the lower abdomen.
  • Urinary Frequency or Difficulty: Due to fibroids pressing on the bladder.
  • Constipation: Resulting from fibroids pressing on the rectum.
  • Infertility or Recurrent Miscarriage: When fibroids distort the uterine cavity or interfere with implantation.
  • Dyspareunia: Painful intercourse, particularly with certain fibroid locations.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Assessment: Thorough evaluation of anemia, coagulation status, and overall health to minimize surgical risks.
  • Blood Transfusion Risk: Patients should be aware of the potential need for blood transfusion, especially with large or multiple fibroids.
  • Uterine Scar Integrity: For future pregnancies, a C-section may be recommended after a transmural myomectomy to prevent uterine rupture.
  • Adhesion Formation: Risk of post-surgical adhesions, which can sometimes lead to chronic pain or infertility.
  • Fibroid Recurrence: Myomectomy removes existing fibroids but does not prevent new ones from developing over time.
  • Contraindications: Active pelvic infection, severe uncontrolled medical conditions, or suspected malignancy may contraindicate myomectomy.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Strict adherence to NPO (nothing by mouth) guidelines for solids and liquids for the specified hours before surgery to prevent aspiration.
  • Post-operative Hydration: Begin with clear liquids post-surgery, gradually advancing to a full liquid diet, then soft foods as tolerated.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate high-fiber foods and adequate fluid intake post-operatively to prevent constipation, a common side effect of anesthesia and pain medication.
  • Avoid Irritants: Initially avoid spicy, greasy, or gas-producing foods that may cause discomfort during recovery.
  • Nutrient-Dense Diet: Focus on protein-rich foods, fruits, and vegetables to support wound healing and overall recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Pain Management: Expect post-operative pain; adhere strictly to prescribed pain medication schedules.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and prolonged standing for several weeks as advised by the surgeon.
  • Vaginal Bleeding/Discharge: Light vaginal bleeding or discharge is common post-myomectomy; report heavy bleeding or foul odor immediately.
  • Signs of Infection: Monitor for fever, increased pain, redness or discharge at the incision site, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Attend all scheduled post-operative visits to monitor healing and discuss pathology results.
  • Sexual Activity: Abstain from sexual intercourse and tampon use for the period recommended by your surgeon, typically 4-6 weeks.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Treatment: The choice of myomectomy approach depends on fibroid size, number, location, and the patient's fertility goals.
  • Minimally Invasive Preference: Whenever clinically appropriate, laparoscopic or hysteroscopic approaches are preferred for reduced recovery time and scarring.
  • Comprehensive Counseling: Thorough discussion of potential risks, benefits, and alternatives to myomectomy is essential for informed patient consent.
  • Future Pregnancy Planning: Patients desiring future pregnancies must be counseled on the implications of uterine scarring and potential need for C-section.
  • Recurrence Potential: Patients should be informed that myomectomy treats existing fibroids but does not eliminate the possibility of new fibroid growth.
  • Pre-operative Management: Consider pre-operative GnRH agonists to reduce fibroid size and vascularity, potentially minimizing blood loss.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Provide clear instructions on fasting, bowel preparation (if ordered), and what to expect before, during, and after surgery.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, pain levels, incision site for signs of infection, urinary output, and bowel function.
  • Early Ambulation: Encourage early and progressive ambulation to prevent complications such as deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and promote bowel motility.
  • Wound Care: Educate the patient on proper incision care, signs of infection, and when to seek medical attention.
  • Pain Assessment: Regularly assess pain using a validated scale and administer analgesics as prescribed, evaluating their effectiveness.
  • Discharge Planning: Provide comprehensive discharge instructions including medication regimen, activity restrictions, warning signs, and follow-up appointments.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: D25 (Leiomyoma of uterus), N85.2 (Hypertrophy of uterus).
  • CPT Codes: 58140 (Myomectomy, abdominal approach), 58545 (Laparoscopic myomectomy), 58561 (Hysteroscopic myomectomy).
  • Pathology: Excised fibroid tissue is sent for histopathological examination to confirm benignity and rule out leiomyosarcoma.
  • Imaging Modalities: Pelvic ultrasound and MRI are crucial for accurate mapping of fibroid size, number, and location pre-operatively.
  • Anesthesia Type: Typically performed under general anesthesia.
  • Surgical Approaches: Laparotomy (open), laparoscopy (minimally invasive), hysteroscopy (transcervical), and robotic-assisted laparoscopy.
