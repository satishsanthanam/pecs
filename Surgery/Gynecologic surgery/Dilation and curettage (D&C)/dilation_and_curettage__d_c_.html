Dilation and curettage (D&C)

🏠 Home / Surgery / Gynecologic surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Dilation and curettage (D&C) is a surgical procedure to remove tissue from the inside of the uterus.
  • Primary Indications: Commonly performed to diagnose and treat abnormal uterine bleeding, remove tissue after a miscarriage or abortion, or to remove uterine polyps or fibroids.
  • Methodology: Involves dilating the cervix (opening of the uterus) and then using a surgical instrument called a curette or a suction device to gently scrape or suction tissue from the uterine lining.
  • Anesthesia: Typically performed under general anesthesia, regional anesthesia, or local anesthesia with sedation, depending on the patient's health and the procedure's complexity.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Abnormal Uterine Bleeding: Persistent heavy, prolonged, or irregular menstrual bleeding that warrants investigation.
  • Postmenopausal Bleeding: Any vaginal bleeding occurring after a woman has gone through menopause, which requires immediate evaluation.
  • Symptoms of Incomplete Miscarriage: Continued bleeding, cramping, or passage of tissue following a suspected miscarriage.
  • Unexplained Pelvic Pain: Chronic pelvic discomfort or pain that may be associated with uterine abnormalities.
  • Suspected Uterine Polyps or Fibroids: Symptoms such as heavy bleeding, pressure, or pain that suggest the presence of growths within the uterus.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Disclosure: Inform your healthcare provider about all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies you are taking, especially blood thinners (anticoagulants), as these may need to be adjusted or stopped prior to the procedure.
  • Allergy Awareness: Clearly communicate any known allergies to medications, latex, or anesthesia agents.
  • Pre-procedure Fasting: Adhere strictly to fasting instructions (typically NPO for food and clear liquids for a specified period) before the procedure to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
  • Post-procedure Restrictions: Avoid using tampons, douching, or engaging in sexual intercourse for at least 2-4 weeks post-procedure, or as advised by your physician, to prevent infection and allow healing.
  • Infection Monitoring: Watch for signs of infection such as fever, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, or increasing abdominal pain, and report them immediately.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-procedure Fasting: You will be instructed to refrain from eating solid foods for at least 6-8 hours before the procedure. Clear liquids may be permitted up to 2 hours prior, as per your anesthesiologist's guidelines.
  • Post-anesthesia Reintroduction: After the procedure, begin with clear liquids (water, broth, clear juice) once fully awake and nausea-free. Gradually advance to light, easily digestible foods as tolerated.
  • Avoid Irritants: For the first 24-48 hours post-procedure, avoid spicy, greasy, or heavy foods that might upset your stomach, especially if you experienced nausea from anesthesia.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration by drinking plenty of fluids post-procedure, unless otherwise instructed by your medical team.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Transportation Arrangement: Due to the effects of anesthesia, you must arrange for a responsible adult to drive you home after the procedure. Public transportation or driving yourself is not permitted.
  • Post-procedure Supervision: A responsible adult should remain with you for at least the first 24 hours following the D&C to monitor for any complications and assist with needs.
  • Emergency Contact: Ensure your attendant knows who to contact and what signs to look for that would necessitate immediate medical attention (e.g., heavy bleeding, severe pain, fever).
  • Activity Restriction: The attendant should help ensure you avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, and prolonged standing for the initial recovery period.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Informed Consent: A thorough discussion of the procedure, its risks, benefits, and alternative treatment options is crucial before obtaining informed consent.
  • Pathology Review: Emphasize the importance of sending all removed tissue for pathological examination to obtain an accurate diagnosis and guide subsequent management.
  • Post-procedure Follow-up: Schedule and stress the importance of a follow-up appointment to review pathology results, assess recovery, and discuss future care or family planning.
  • Emotional Support: Recognize and address the potential emotional impact of a D&C, especially in cases of miscarriage, and offer appropriate support resources.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Assessment: Conduct a comprehensive nursing assessment including vital signs, medical history, allergies, last menstrual period, and confirmation of NPO status. Verify informed consent is signed.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear, concise pre- and post-operative instructions, including pain management expectations, signs of complications, and activity restrictions.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, pain level, vaginal bleeding (amount, color, presence of clots), and recovery from anesthesia. Assess for bladder distension.
  • Emotional Support & Counseling: Offer empathetic support and resources, particularly for patients undergoing D&C due to miscarriage or termination, acknowledging the potential psychological impact.
  • Discharge Planning: Ensure the patient has a responsible adult for transport, understands all discharge instructions, and has contact information for post-procedure concerns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Medical Terminology: Dilation, curettage, endometrial biopsy, suction aspiration, uterine perforation, Asherman's syndrome.
  • Instrumentation: Speculum, tenaculum, Hegar dilators, Pratt dilators, uterine sound, sharp curette, suction curette (Karman cannula).
  • Anesthesia Types: General anesthesia, spinal anesthesia, paracervical block with IV sedation.
  • Potential Complications: Uterine perforation, hemorrhage, infection (endometritis), cervical laceration, intrauterine adhesions (Asherman's syndrome), incomplete evacuation.
  • Histopathology: Microscopic examination of removed tissue to diagnose conditions such as endometrial hyperplasia, carcinoma, retained products of conception, or polyps.
