How to protect the chest after heart surgery

🏠 Home / Surgery / After surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Sternal Healing: The breastbone (sternum) needs approximately 6-8 weeks to heal completely after a sternotomy. Protecting this incision is paramount to prevent complications.
  • Activity Restrictions: Adhering strictly to activity restrictions is crucial. Any undue strain on the chest can compromise healing and lead to serious issues.
  • Supportive Measures: Using supportive techniques, such as hugging a pillow, provides stability to the sternum during movements that might otherwise cause strain.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Increased Chest Pain: Worsening or new onset of sharp pain in the sternal area, especially with movement or deep breaths.
  • Clicking or Popping Sensation: Any audible or palpable clicking, popping, or grinding sensation from the sternum, indicating potential instability.
  • Signs of Infection: Redness, warmth, swelling, pus-like discharge from the incision site, or fever and chills.
  • Shortness of Breath: New or worsening difficulty breathing, which could indicate a complication requiring immediate medical attention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Pushing, Pulling, Lifting: Do not lift anything heavier than 5-10 pounds (e.g., a gallon of milk) for the first 6-8 weeks. Avoid pushing or pulling with your arms (e.g., opening heavy doors, pushing up from a chair without leg assistance).
  • Hug a Pillow: Always hug a pillow firmly against your chest when coughing, sneezing, laughing, or during bowel movements to provide sternal support.
  • Log Roll Technique: When getting out of bed, roll onto your side first, then use your legs to help push your body up while keeping your arms close to your body.
  • Avoid Arm Extremes: Do not raise your elbows above your shoulders or reach behind your back for the initial healing period. Keep arm movements within a comfortable range.
  • Protect Incision: Keep the incision clean and dry. Follow specific instructions for showering and dressing changes to prevent infection.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Prevent Constipation: Consume a high-fiber diet (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) and ensure adequate fluid intake to prevent straining during bowel movements, which can put pressure on the chest.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration by drinking plenty of water unless otherwise restricted by your physician, as this supports overall healing and bowel regularity.
  • Sodium Restriction: Follow any prescribed sodium restrictions to manage fluid retention, which can impact cardiac workload and overall recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assist with Transfers: Help the patient with getting in and out of bed or chairs, ensuring they use proper body mechanics to avoid sternal strain.
  • Remind of Precautions: Gently remind the patient about sternal precautions during daily activities, especially when reaching, lifting, or coughing.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for any signs of infection (redness, discharge, fever), increased pain, or clicking/popping sounds from the sternum and report them immediately to the medical team.
  • Medication Adherence: Ensure the patient takes prescribed pain medication as directed to manage discomfort and facilitate adherence to activity restrictions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence is Key: Strict adherence to sternal precautions is non-negotiable for optimal healing and to prevent serious complications like sternal dehiscence or infection.
  • Gradual Activity Increase: Physical activity should be gradually increased as advised by your cardiac rehabilitation team, ensuring not to overexert the sternum.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Regular follow-up appointments are essential to monitor healing, assess cardiac function, and adjust medications as needed.
  • Recognize Red Flags: Patients and caregivers must be educated on recognizing and promptly reporting any signs of sternal instability or infection.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education on sternal precautions, proper body mechanics, and the importance of compliance for successful recovery.
  • Pain Management: Assess and manage post-operative pain effectively to allow the patient to participate in deep breathing exercises and mobility without excessive discomfort.
  • Wound Care: Teach patients and caregivers proper incision care, including signs of infection and when to seek medical attention.
  • Cardiac Rehabilitation Referral: Facilitate timely referral and emphasize the importance of participation in cardiac rehabilitation programs for structured recovery and exercise progression.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Sternal Dehiscence: Separation of the sternal wound, a serious complication requiring medical intervention.
  • Mediastinitis: Infection of the mediastinum (chest cavity) following sternotomy, often a life-threatening condition.
  • Sternal Precautions: A set of guidelines designed to protect the sternum from undue stress during the healing period post-cardiac surgery.
  • Cardiac Rehabilitation: A medically supervised program designed to improve cardiovascular health after a cardiac event or surgery.
