Negative pressure wound therapy

🏠 Home / Surgery / After surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Therapy Overview: Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (NPWT), also known as Vacuum-Assisted Closure (VAC), is a medical device system that promotes wound healing by applying continuous or intermittent negative pressure to the wound bed.
  • Mechanism of Action: It works by drawing out fluid from the wound, increasing blood flow, reducing swelling, and promoting the formation of new tissue.
  • Components: The system typically includes a wound dressing (foam or gauze), a transparent adhesive film to create an airtight seal, tubing, a collection canister, and a portable vacuum pump.
  • Post-Surgical Application: Often used for complex surgical wounds, dehiscence, or wounds at high risk of infection to accelerate healing and manage exudate.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Non-Healing Wounds: Indicated for chronic wounds that fail to progress with conventional treatment, such as diabetic ulcers or pressure injuries.
  • Excessive Exudate: Applied to wounds producing large amounts of fluid, which can impede healing and increase infection risk.
  • Wound Dehiscence: Used for surgical incisions that have separated or opened, requiring support for granulation tissue formation.
  • Deep Tissue Injury: Beneficial for wounds extending into deeper tissues, including muscle or bone, to promote a healthy wound bed.
  • Signs of Infection: While not a primary treatment for active infection, NPWT can help manage wounds with localized infection by removing contaminated exudate, often alongside systemic antibiotics.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Contraindications: Avoid use on wounds with untreated osteomyelitis, necrotic tissue with eschar (unless debrided), exposed blood vessels, organs, or nerves, and wounds with malignancy.
  • Bleeding Risk: Exercise extreme caution in patients on anticoagulants or with known bleeding disorders due to the risk of hemorrhage, especially if blood vessels are near the wound bed.
  • Airtight Seal: Ensure the dressing maintains a complete airtight seal around the wound to effectively deliver negative pressure; leaks will compromise therapy efficacy.
  • Pain Management: Anticipate potential pain during dressing changes and ensure adequate analgesia is provided, particularly for sensitive wound beds.
  • Infection Monitoring: Continuously monitor the wound and surrounding skin for signs of infection, such as increased redness, warmth, swelling, purulent discharge, or fever.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Protein Intake: Emphasize high-quality protein sources (lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes) to support tissue repair and collagen synthesis critical for wound healing.
  • Vitamin C & Zinc: Encourage intake of foods rich in Vitamin C (citrus fruits, berries, bell peppers) and Zinc (nuts, seeds, whole grains, red meat) to aid immune function and wound repair.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain optimal fluid intake to support overall tissue perfusion, nutrient transport, and prevent dehydration, which can impair healing.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Promote a diet rich in whole foods, fruits, vegetables, and healthy fats to provide essential micronutrients and energy required for the metabolic demands of wound healing.
  • Avoid Malnutrition: Screen for and address any signs of malnutrition, as poor nutritional status significantly hinders wound closure and increases complication risk.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • System Alarms: Familiarize yourself with the NPWT device alarms (e.g., leak, canister full, low battery) and understand appropriate troubleshooting steps.
  • Dressing Integrity: Regularly inspect the dressing for leaks or dislodgement; a compromised seal will reduce the effectiveness of the therapy.
  • Canister Management: Monitor the fill level of the collection canister and change it according to manufacturer guidelines or when full, ensuring proper disposal of biohazardous waste.
  • Signs of Complication: Immediately report any excessive bleeding, sudden severe pain, fever, foul odor from the wound, or significant changes in wound appearance to the healthcare provider.
  • Patient Mobility: Assist the patient with safe movement and positioning to prevent dislodgement of the dressing or tubing, while encouraging mobility as tolerated.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Therapy: Stress the critical importance of continuous adherence to the prescribed NPWT regimen for optimal wound healing outcomes.
  • Regular Assessment: Conduct frequent wound assessments to monitor progress, identify complications, and determine the appropriate duration of NPWT.
  • Debridement: Ensure adequate debridement of non-viable tissue prior to and during NPWT to create a healthy wound bed conducive to healing.
  • Underlying Conditions: Address and manage any underlying medical conditions (e.g., diabetes, peripheral vascular disease) that may impede wound healing.
  • Transition Planning: Plan for the transition from NPWT to conventional wound care dressings once sufficient granulation tissue has formed or the wound is ready for closure.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Wound Assessment: Utilize standardized tools (e.g., PUSH tool, Bates-Jensen Wound Assessment Tool) to document wound characteristics, size, and exudate.
  • Aseptic Technique: Maintain strict aseptic technique during dressing changes to prevent contamination and infection of the wound.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and caregivers thoroughly on NPWT system operation, troubleshooting, signs of complications, and when to seek medical attention.
  • Skin Protection: Apply skin protectants or barrier films to periwound skin to prevent maceration and skin breakdown from exudate or adhesive.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document wound status, NPWT settings, dressing change details, patient tolerance, and any interventions or observations.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Indications: Acute and chronic wounds, traumatic wounds, surgical wounds (dehiscence, sternotomy), burns, pressure injuries, diabetic foot ulcers.
  • Pressure Settings: Typically ranges from -75 mmHg to -125 mmHg, with continuous or intermittent modes depending on wound type and patient tolerance.
  • Dressing Types: Hydrophobic foam (polyurethane) for deeper wounds, hydrophilic gauze for tunneling or irregular wounds.
  • Physiological Effects: Macro-deformation (wound contraction), micro-deformation (cellular stretch), increased blood flow, reduced edema, removal of infectious materials.
  • Evidence Base: Supported by numerous clinical studies demonstrating improved wound healing rates, reduced hospital stays, and decreased infection rates in appropriate wound types.
