Surgical site infection

🏠 Home / Surgery / After surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A surgical site infection (SSI) is an infection that occurs in the part of the body where surgery was performed.
  • Timing: SSIs can manifest within 30 days of surgery, or up to a year if an implant was placed.
  • Severity: Infections can range from superficial skin infections to more serious infections involving tissues under the skin, organs, or implanted material.
  • Risk Factors: Factors increasing risk include diabetes, obesity, smoking, prolonged surgery, poor nutritional status, and compromised immune systems.
  • Prevention Focus: Adherence to pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative protocols is crucial for prevention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Redness (Erythema): Increased redness or a spreading red area around the surgical incision.
  • Warmth: The skin around the incision feels unusually warm to the touch.
  • Swelling (Edema): Increased swelling or hardness at the surgical site.
  • Pain: New or worsening pain, tenderness, or throbbing at the incision that is not relieved by pain medication.
  • Drainage: Pus, cloudy fluid, or foul-smelling discharge from the wound.
  • Fever and Chills: Systemic signs of infection, such as an elevated body temperature or shivering.
  • Delayed Healing: The wound does not appear to be healing properly or the edges are separating.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Hand Hygiene: Always wash hands thoroughly with soap and water or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer before and after touching the surgical wound or dressing.
  • Wound Care: Follow all specific instructions for dressing changes, keeping the wound clean and dry, and protecting it from injury.
  • Avoid Touching: Refrain from touching the incision unnecessarily to prevent introducing bacteria.
  • Monitor for Signs: Regularly inspect the wound for any signs of infection and report concerns immediately to your healthcare provider.
  • Antibiotic Adherence: If prescribed prophylactic antibiotics, ensure they are taken exactly as directed, and complete the full course if prescribed for an active infection.
  • Hygiene Practices: Shower or bathe as instructed by your surgeon, avoiding direct water pressure on the incision until advised.
  • Smoking Cessation: Avoid smoking, as it significantly impairs wound healing and increases infection risk.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Protein Intake: Ensure adequate protein consumption (e.g., lean meats, fish, eggs, legumes) to support tissue repair and immune function essential for wound healing.
  • Vitamin C: Increase intake of Vitamin C-rich foods (e.g., citrus fruits, berries, bell peppers) to aid collagen synthesis and immune response.
  • Zinc: Consume zinc-rich foods (e.g., nuts, seeds, whole grains, meat) which are vital for immune function and wound repair.
  • Hydration: Maintain excellent hydration by drinking plenty of water to support overall physiological function and nutrient transport to the wound site.
  • Fiber: Include fiber-rich foods to prevent constipation, as straining can put pressure on the surgical incision.
  • Avoidance: Limit highly processed foods, excessive sugars, and unhealthy fats that can contribute to inflammation and hinder healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Contact: Contact your surgeon or healthcare provider immediately if you notice any signs of infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pain, or discharge from the wound, or if you develop a fever.
  • Do Not Self-Treat: Never attempt to treat a suspected SSI with home remedies or unprescribed antibiotics, as this can worsen the condition or mask symptoms.
  • Complete Antibiotics: If antibiotics are prescribed, complete the entire course, even if symptoms improve, to ensure the infection is fully eradicated and prevent antibiotic resistance.
  • Follow-Up Appointments: Attend all scheduled post-operative follow-up appointments to allow for professional wound assessment and care.
  • Avoid Submerging: Do not submerge the incision in baths, hot tubs, or swimming pools until cleared by your surgeon.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Recognition: Emphasize to patients the critical importance of early recognition and reporting of SSI symptoms to prevent complications and ensure timely intervention.
  • Aseptic Technique: Reinforce the paramount role of strict aseptic technique throughout the surgical process and during all post-operative wound care.
  • Risk Stratification: Utilize pre-operative risk assessment tools to identify high-risk patients and implement targeted preventative strategies.
  • Antibiotic Stewardship: Advocate for judicious use of prophylactic antibiotics, adhering to guidelines for appropriate agent, dose, and duration.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear, concise, and repeated education to patients and caregivers on proper wound care, hygiene, and warning signs of infection.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Collaborate with nursing, infectious disease specialists, and wound care teams for comprehensive management of established SSIs.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Wound Assessment: Develop proficiency in systematic wound assessment, including observation for erythema, edema, warmth, pain, drainage characteristics (COCA: Color, Odor, Consistency, Amount), and wound edge approximation.
  • Sterile Technique: Master sterile dressing change procedures, ensuring adherence to aseptic principles to prevent contamination.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on post-operative wound care, signs and symptoms of SSI, and when to seek medical attention.
  • Documentation: Accurately document all wound assessments, interventions, patient teaching, and communication with the healthcare team.
  • Antibiotic Administration: Administer prescribed antibiotics, monitor for therapeutic effects and adverse reactions, and educate patients on adherence.
  • Infection Control: Implement hospital infection control policies, including hand hygiene and environmental cleaning, to minimize SSI risk.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CDC SSI Criteria: Refer to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) definitions for classifying surgical site infections (superficial incisional, deep incisional, organ/space).
  • Common Pathogens: Recognize common causative organisms such as Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA), Coagulase-negative Staphylococci, Enterococcus species, and Gram-negative bacilli (e.g., E. coli, Pseudomonas aeruginosa).
  • Risk Factors: Understand intrinsic (e.g., diabetes, obesity, malnutrition, immunosuppression) and extrinsic (e.g., prolonged surgery, poor surgical technique, inadequate sterilization) risk factors.
  • SCIP Measures: Familiarize with Surgical Care Improvement Project (SCIP) measures related to SSI prevention, including appropriate antibiotic selection, timing, and discontinuation.
  • Wound Classification: Apply the Altemeier classification of surgical wounds (Class I: Clean, Class II: Clean-Contaminated, Class III: Contaminated, Class IV: Dirty/Infected) to assess infection risk.
  • Antimicrobial Stewardship: Adhere to principles of antimicrobial stewardship to optimize antibiotic use and minimize resistance.
