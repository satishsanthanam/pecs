Hiatal hernia

🏠 Home / Surgery / Hernia

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Hiatal Hernia: A condition where part of the stomach pushes up through the diaphragm into the chest cavity, often leading to gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) symptoms.
  • Surgical Intervention: Surgery is typically considered for severe symptoms unresponsive to medical management, complications like strangulation, or large paraesophageal hernias.
  • Types of Surgery: Most commonly, laparoscopic fundoplication (e.g., Nissen fundoplication) is performed to repair the hernia and reinforce the lower esophageal sphincter.
  • Goal of Surgery: To alleviate symptoms, prevent complications, and restore the normal anatomical position of the stomach.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Heartburn: A burning sensation in the chest, often worse after eating or when lying down.
  • Regurgitation: The backward flow of stomach acid or undigested food into the throat or mouth.
  • Dysphagia: Difficulty or pain when swallowing, feeling like food is stuck.
  • Chest Pain: Non-cardiac chest pain that can mimic heart attack symptoms, often related to acid reflux.
  • Shortness of Breath: Especially with large paraesophageal hernias, due to compression of the lungs.
  • Chronic Cough or Hoarseness: Resulting from irritation of the vocal cords by stomach acid.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Medication Review: Discuss all medications, especially anticoagulants (blood thinners) and anti-inflammatory drugs, with your surgeon well in advance.
  • Smoking Cessation: Stop smoking several weeks before surgery to improve healing and reduce respiratory complications.
  • Weight Management: Achieving a healthy weight pre-surgery can reduce surgical risks and improve outcomes.
  • Post-operative Lifting Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting (typically >10-15 lbs) for 6-8 weeks post-surgery to prevent strain on the repair.
  • Avoid Straining: Prevent constipation and straining during bowel movements to protect the surgical site.
  • Incision Care: Keep surgical incisions clean and dry; follow specific instructions for dressing changes.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines, typically no food or drink for 6-8 hours before surgery.
  • Post-operative Liquid Diet: Start with clear liquids, progressing to full liquids (e.g., broth, yogurt, protein shakes) as tolerated for the first few days.
  • Soft Food Progression: Gradually introduce soft, pureed foods (e.g., mashed potatoes, scrambled eggs, well-cooked vegetables) for several weeks.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eat smaller portions more frequently to avoid overfilling the stomach and reduce pressure on the repair.
  • Avoid Trigger Foods: Limit acidic, spicy, fatty, and gas-producing foods (e.g., citrus, tomatoes, caffeine, carbonated drinks) that can exacerbate symptoms or cause discomfort.
  • Adequate Hydration: Sip fluids throughout the day, but avoid drinking large volumes with meals to prevent bloating.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Pain Management: Follow prescribed pain medication regimen; report uncontrolled pain to the medical team.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activities, bending, twisting, and sudden movements as advised by the surgeon.
  • Wound Care: Monitor surgical incisions for signs of infection such as redness, swelling, warmth, or discharge.
  • Signs of Complications: Be vigilant for persistent nausea/vomiting, severe abdominal pain, difficulty swallowing, fever, or shortness of breath, and seek immediate medical attention.
  • Deep Breathing Exercises: Perform incentive spirometry or deep breathing exercises as instructed to prevent pulmonary complications.
  • Early Ambulation: Walk frequently for short distances to promote circulation and prevent blood clots.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Surgical Candidacy: Careful patient selection is crucial, considering symptom severity, hernia type, and overall health.
  • Laparoscopic Approach: Minimally invasive techniques are preferred, offering faster recovery and less post-operative pain.
  • Expected Outcomes: Most patients experience significant improvement in GERD symptoms and quality of life post-surgery.
  • Long-term Management: While surgery addresses the anatomical defect, lifestyle modifications remain important for sustained relief.
  • Follow-up Care: Regular post-operative visits are essential to monitor recovery, assess symptom resolution, and address any concerns.
  • Potential for Recurrence: Although rare, recurrence of the hernia or new onset of dysphagia or gas bloat syndrome can occur and requires further evaluation.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Educate patients on the surgical procedure, expected recovery, pain management, and dietary progression.
  • Post-operative Assessment: Monitor vital signs, pain levels, bowel sounds, urine output, and incision sites for complications.
  • Dysphagia Monitoring: Closely observe for difficulty swallowing, especially during initial reintroduction of oral intake, and report to the physician.
  • Gas Bloat Syndrome: Educate patients on the potential for temporary difficulty belching or vomiting post-fundoplication and strategies to manage it.
  • Activity Progression: Guide patients on safe ambulation, deep breathing exercises, and gradual increase in activity levels.
  • Discharge Planning: Provide comprehensive discharge instructions covering diet, activity restrictions, medication, wound care, and signs/symptoms to report.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Nissen Fundoplication: A surgical procedure where the gastric fundus is wrapped 360 degrees around the lower esophagus to reinforce the sphincter.
  • Paraesophageal Hernia Repair: Surgical correction of a hernia where the fundus of the stomach herniates alongside the esophagus into the chest.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Barium swallow, upper endoscopy, esophageal manometry, and 24-hour pH monitoring are key for diagnosis and surgical planning.
  • Mesh Reinforcement: May be used in some cases, particularly for large hiatal defects, to reduce recurrence rates.
  • Complications: Potential complications include dysphagia, gas bloat syndrome, recurrence of hernia, infection, and vagal nerve injury.
  • Post-operative Imaging: May include barium swallow to assess fundoplication integrity and rule out obstruction.
