Coronary artery bypass graft surgery – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Surgery / Coronary artery bypass graft surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Recovery Timeline: Expect a gradual recovery over several weeks to months. Full sternal healing typically takes 6-8 weeks, but overall recovery can extend for several months.
  • Activity Progression: Gradually increase your activity levels as tolerated. Avoid heavy lifting, pushing, or pulling with your arms during the initial sternal healing period.
  • Pain Management: Mild discomfort, soreness, or numbness around the incision sites is normal. Follow your prescribed pain medication schedule and report any uncontrolled or worsening pain.
  • Emotional Well-being: It is common to experience mood swings, anxiety, or depression after major surgery. Seek support from family, friends, or healthcare providers if these feelings persist or become overwhelming.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Signs of Infection: Immediately report fever (over 100.4°F or 38°C), chills, increased redness, swelling, warmth, or pus-like drainage from any incision site (chest or leg).
  • Cardiac Concerns: Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations, dizziness, fainting spells, or sudden swelling in your ankles or feet.
  • Leg Incision Issues: Watch for increased pain, swelling, redness, or discharge from leg incision sites (if a vein graft was taken from the leg).
  • Neurological Changes: Report sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, difficulty speaking, or vision changes, as these could indicate a stroke.
  • Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT): Be aware of sudden swelling, pain, or tenderness in the calf or thigh, which may indicate a blood clot.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Incision Care: Keep incision sites clean and dry. Avoid soaking in baths, hot tubs, or swimming pools until cleared by your surgeon. Showering is generally permitted after 48 hours, gently washing with mild soap.
  • Sternal Precautions: For 6-8 weeks, avoid lifting more than 5-10 pounds, pushing, or pulling with your arms. Use a pillow to brace your chest when coughing, sneezing, or laughing. Avoid reaching overhead or behind your back.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications exactly as directed. Do not stop any medication, especially blood thinners, without consulting your doctor.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive until cleared by your surgeon, typically 4-6 weeks post-surgery, and only when you are no longer taking opioid pain medication.
  • Smoking Cessation: Absolutely avoid smoking and exposure to secondhand smoke to promote healing, prevent graft failure, and reduce the risk of future heart problems.
  • Sexual Activity: Resume sexual activity gradually when you feel ready and can climb two flights of stairs or walk a mile without discomfort.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Heart-Healthy Diet: Focus on a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins (fish, poultry, legumes), and healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil). Limit saturated and trans fats, cholesterol, and processed foods.
  • Sodium Restriction: Reduce sodium intake to help manage blood pressure and fluid retention. Avoid high-sodium processed foods, canned soups, cured meats, and excessive table salt.
  • Fluid Intake: Maintain adequate hydration by drinking plenty of water, unless specific fluid restrictions have been prescribed by your physician due to other medical conditions.
  • Weight Management: Work towards achieving and maintaining a healthy body weight to reduce strain on your heart and improve overall cardiovascular health.
  • Alcohol Moderation: Limit or avoid alcohol consumption as advised by your doctor, as it can interact with medications and affect heart function.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for any signs of infection, worsening pain, shortness of breath, or neurological changes in the patient and report them immediately to the healthcare team.
  • Medication Assistance: Help the patient organize and remember to take their medications as prescribed, especially during the initial recovery phase when they may be fatigued or in discomfort.
  • Activity Support: Assist with light household tasks and encourage gradual, supervised ambulation. Ensure the patient adheres strictly to sternal precautions and activity restrictions.
  • Emotional Support: Provide reassurance, patience, and a calm environment. Be aware of potential mood changes and encourage the patient to express their feelings and seek professional help if needed.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know when and how to contact the medical team or emergency services (e.g., 911) if the patient experiences severe or life-threatening symptoms.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Follow-Up Appointments: Adhere strictly to all scheduled post-operative appointments with your surgeon and cardiologist to monitor your recovery, assess graft patency, and adjust your care plan as needed.
  • Cardiac Rehabilitation: Enroll in and actively participate in a supervised cardiac rehabilitation program. This is a critical component of recovery, improving physical fitness, managing risk factors, and providing education and support.
  • Medication Management: Understand that lifelong medication adherence, including antiplatelets, statins, beta-blockers, and blood pressure medications, is vital for maintaining graft patency and overall cardiovascular health.
  • Risk Factor Modification: Commit to aggressive lifestyle changes such as a heart-healthy diet, regular exercise, stress management, and smoking cessation to control risk factors like hypertension, diabetes, and high cholesterol.
  • When to Call: Do not hesitate to call your doctor or seek emergency care for any concerning symptoms, especially new or worsening chest pain, severe shortness of breath, signs of infection, or sudden neurological changes.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Key Discharge Teaching: Prioritize comprehensive patient and family education on wound care, medication regimen (purpose, dose, side effects, importance of adherence), activity restrictions (sternal precautions), signs and symptoms of complications, and follow-up care.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Regularly assess and manage post-operative pain using a validated scale, ensuring adequate analgesia while monitoring for side effects of pain medications.
  • Psychosocial Support: Screen for and address patient and family anxiety, depression, or fear related to recovery. Provide resources for emotional support and coping strategies.
  • Cardiac Rehab Referral: Ensure a timely and appropriate referral to cardiac rehabilitation, emphasizing its importance and benefits during discharge teaching.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Perform thorough medication reconciliation at discharge to prevent discrepancies and ensure the patient understands their new medication list, including over-the-counter medications and supplements.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Graft Patency: Long-term success of CABG is highly dependent on maintaining graft patency, which is influenced by antiplatelet therapy (e.g., aspirin, clopidogrel), statin therapy, and aggressive risk factor modification.
  • Medication Pharmacology: Review the mechanism of action for common post-CABG medications: antiplatelets (prevent thrombus formation), beta-blockers (reduce myocardial oxygen demand, control arrhythmias), ACE inhibitors/ARBs (blood pressure control, cardiac remodeling), and statins (lipid lowering, plaque stabilization).
  • Cardiac Rehabilitation Phases: Understand the structured phases of cardiac rehabilitation: Phase 1 (inpatient education and mobilization), Phase 2 (outpatient, supervised exercise, education, and counseling), and Phase 3 (maintenance, independent exercise and lifestyle management).
  • Sternal Healing Physiology: The sternum heals by secondary intention, requiring immobilization and protection for 6-8 weeks to prevent dehiscence, non-union, and promote robust bone fusion.
  • Risk Factor Management: Emphasize the importance of managing hypertension (target <130/80 mmHg), dyslipidemia (LDL <70 mg/dL), diabetes (HbA1c <7%), and obesity to prevent progression of atherosclerosis in native coronary arteries and bypass grafts.
