Questions to ask if you are having surgery or a procedure

🏠 Home / Surgery / Anesthesia for surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Procedure: Clarify the exact name of the surgery or procedure, its purpose, and why it is recommended for your specific condition.
  • Anesthesia Plan: Inquire about the type of anesthesia planned (e.g., general, regional, local with sedation), its risks, and how it will be administered.
  • Care Team Roles: Understand who will be part of your surgical and anesthesia team, including the surgeon, anesthesiologist, and nurses.
  • Expected Outcomes & Alternatives: Discuss the anticipated results of the procedure, potential complications, and if there are any alternative treatments available.
  • Recovery Timeline: Ask about the expected duration of your hospital stay, immediate post-operative care, and the general recovery period at home.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Pre-existing Medical Conditions: Discuss any chronic illnesses such as heart disease, lung conditions, diabetes, or kidney issues, as these can impact anesthesia choice and surgical risk.
  • Allergies & Sensitivities: Report all known allergies to medications, latex, or any previous adverse reactions to anesthesia.
  • Current Medications & Supplements: Provide a complete list of all prescription drugs, over-the-counter medications, herbal supplements, and recreational substances you are currently taking.
  • Previous Surgical History: Detail any prior surgeries, including the type of anesthesia received and any complications experienced.
  • Anxiety or Fear: Communicate any anxieties or fears regarding the surgery or anesthesia, as these can be addressed with appropriate support and information.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fasting Instructions: Adhere strictly to all pre-operative fasting guidelines for food and liquids to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
  • Medication Adjustments: Follow specific instructions regarding which medications to stop or continue before surgery, especially blood thinners, insulin, or certain cardiac drugs.
  • Smoking & Alcohol Cessation: Avoid smoking and alcohol for the recommended period before surgery to optimize lung function and reduce complication risks.
  • Arranging Transportation: Ensure you have a responsible adult to drive you home after the procedure, especially if you receive sedation or general anesthesia.
  • Personal Item Restrictions: Understand what personal items (e.g., jewelry, contact lenses, dentures) need to be removed before surgery.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Strictly follow the 'Nothing By Mouth' (NPO) orders, typically involving no solid food for 6-8 hours and no clear liquids for 2-4 hours prior to anesthesia.
  • Hydration Guidelines: Clarify specific instructions regarding sips of water for medication or if any special clear liquid protocols apply.
  • Post-Sedation Reintroduction: Understand the gradual reintroduction of fluids and light foods post-procedure, starting with clear liquids and progressing as tolerated.
  • Avoidance of Specific Foods: Discuss any foods or beverages to avoid in the days leading up to surgery, such as those that might cause indigestion or interact with medications.
  • Nutritional Support: Inquire about any specific nutritional recommendations for optimizing recovery, particularly for major surgeries.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contacts: Ensure the hospital has up-to-date emergency contact information for your designated support person.
  • Post-Discharge Care: Understand the specific care instructions for the immediate post-operative period at home, including wound care, activity restrictions, and medication schedules.
  • Signs of Complications: Be aware of warning signs that require immediate medical attention, such as fever, excessive pain, bleeding, or difficulty breathing.
  • Medication Management: Clarify all post-operative medication prescriptions, including dosage, frequency, and potential side effects.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Confirm the schedule for post-operative follow-up visits with your surgeon and other specialists.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Open Communication: Always encourage patients to ask any and all questions they have, no matter how minor they may seem.
  • Honest Medical History: Emphasize the critical importance of providing a complete and accurate medical history, including all medications and supplements.
  • Informed Consent: Ensure the patient fully understands the risks, benefits, and alternatives to the procedure before signing the informed consent.
  • Pain Management Plan: Discuss the post-operative pain management strategy to ensure patient comfort and facilitate recovery.
  • Realistic Expectations: Help patients develop realistic expectations regarding the surgical outcome and recovery process.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Teaching: Reinforce patient education regarding fasting, medication adjustments, and what to expect on the day of surgery.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Conduct thorough medication reconciliation to prevent drug interactions and ensure patient safety.
  • Anxiety Assessment: Assess and address patient anxiety levels, providing reassurance and clarifying any misunderstandings about the procedure or anesthesia.
  • Advocacy for Questions: Empower patients to voice their concerns and questions to the entire healthcare team, ensuring they feel heard and informed.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Understand key parameters for monitoring patients in the PACU (Post-Anesthesia Care Unit) and during initial recovery.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ASA Physical Status Classification: A system used by anesthesiologists to assess a patient's overall health status before surgery, influencing anesthetic choice and risk assessment.
  • Informed Consent: The legal and ethical process by which a patient gives permission for a medical procedure after understanding all relevant information.
  • NPO (Nil Per Os): Medical abbreviation meaning 'nothing by mouth,' referring to fasting instructions before surgery.
  • Regional Anesthesia: Anesthesia that numbs a specific region of the body (e.g., spinal, epidural, nerve block) while the patient may remain awake or lightly sedated.
  • General Anesthesia: Anesthesia that induces a state of unconsciousness, rendering the patient unaware of the surgery and unable to feel pain.
  • PACU (Post-Anesthesia Care Unit): The recovery room where patients are closely monitored immediately after surgery as they emerge from anesthesia.
