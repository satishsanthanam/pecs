Mastectomy

🏠 Home / Surgery / Breast surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Mastectomy is a surgical procedure to remove the entire breast, often performed to treat or prevent breast cancer.
  • Types of Mastectomy: Includes total (simple), modified radical (breast and some lymph nodes), skin-sparing, and nipple-sparing mastectomies, chosen based on cancer stage and patient factors.
  • Purpose: Primarily to remove cancerous tissue, but also used as a prophylactic measure for individuals at very high risk of developing breast cancer.
  • Reconstruction Options: Many patients consider breast reconstruction, which can be immediate or delayed, using implants or the patient's own tissue.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Palpable Breast Lump: A new or persistent lump or mass in the breast or armpit that may be painless or tender.
  • Skin Changes: Dimpling, puckering, redness, scaling, or thickening of the breast skin, often described as an "orange peel" texture (peau d'orange).
  • Nipple Abnormalities: Nipple inversion (turning inward), persistent nipple discharge (especially bloody or clear), or scaling/crusting around the nipple.
  • Persistent Pain: Unexplained and persistent breast or nipple pain that does not resolve with typical remedies.
  • Lymph Node Swelling: Swelling or lumps in the armpit or collarbone area, indicating potential spread to lymph nodes.
  • Abnormal Imaging Findings: Suspicious masses, calcifications, or architectural distortion identified on mammogram, ultrasound, or MRI, confirmed by biopsy.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Assessment: Complete all required cardiac, pulmonary, and blood work assessments to ensure surgical readiness.
  • Medication Review: Disclose all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies to your surgeon; specific medications like anticoagulants may need to be stopped prior to surgery.
  • Smoking Cessation: Cease smoking several weeks before surgery to improve healing and reduce complication risks.
  • Post-operative Drain Care: Understand how to manage surgical drains, including emptying, measuring output, and recognizing signs of infection.
  • Activity Restrictions: Strictly adhere to limitations on arm and shoulder movement on the affected side to prevent wound complications and promote healing.
  • Lymphedema Prevention: Protect the affected arm from injury, infection, and tight clothing; avoid blood pressure cuffs or venipuncture on the affected arm if lymph nodes were removed.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Follow strict fasting guidelines (typically NPO after midnight) before surgery to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
  • Post-operative Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support healing and prevent dehydration, especially if experiencing nausea.
  • Gradual Diet Progression: Start with clear liquids post-surgery, advancing to soft foods and then a regular diet as tolerated, avoiding heavy or greasy foods initially.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate fiber-rich foods and stool softeners as prescribed to prevent constipation, a common side effect of pain medication.
  • Nutrient-Dense Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein, vitamins, and minerals to support tissue repair and immune function during recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Signs of Infection: Monitor for fever (above 100.4°F or 38°C), increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or pus-like drainage from the incision site.
  • Excessive Bleeding/Drainage: Report any sudden increase in drainage from surgical drains or significant bleeding from the incision.
  • Severe Pain: Contact your healthcare provider if pain is unmanageable with prescribed medication or suddenly worsens.
  • Swelling in Arm: Be vigilant for new or worsening swelling in the arm on the side of the surgery, which could indicate lymphedema.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Attend all scheduled post-operative appointments for wound checks, drain removal, and ongoing care.
  • Emotional Support: Seek support from family, friends, support groups, or mental health professionals to cope with the emotional impact of surgery and diagnosis.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Shared Decision-Making: Engage in thorough discussions with your surgical oncologist to understand all treatment options, including lumpectomy with radiation, and make an informed decision.
  • Realistic Expectations: Understand the potential physical changes, recovery timeline, and possible need for further treatments like radiation or chemotherapy.
  • Reconstruction Consultation: If considering reconstruction, consult with a plastic surgeon pre-operatively to discuss options and timing.
  • Long-term Surveillance: Adhere to recommended follow-up schedules, including regular clinical exams and imaging, for long-term monitoring and early detection of recurrence.
  • Genetic Counseling: Consider genetic counseling and testing if there is a strong family history of breast or ovarian cancer to assess hereditary risk.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Provide comprehensive patient education on the surgical procedure, expected post-operative course, pain management, and drain care.
  • Post-operative Assessment: Regularly assess vital signs, pain level, incision site for signs of infection or hematoma, and surgical drain output and patency.
  • Mobility and Positioning: Assist with early ambulation and proper positioning to prevent complications like DVT and promote comfort, while respecting arm restrictions.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient anxieties, body image concerns, and provide resources for emotional and psychological support.
  • Lymphedema Risk Management: Educate patients on lymphedema prevention strategies, including skin care, avoiding constrictive clothing, and recognizing early signs.
  • Discharge Planning: Ensure patients and caregivers are fully prepared for home care, including medication administration, wound/drain care, activity limitations, and when to seek medical attention.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: Relevant codes for breast cancer (e.g., C50.x) and prophylactic mastectomy (e.g., Z40.01).
  • CPT Codes: Surgical procedure codes for various mastectomy types (e.g., 19303 for simple mastectomy, 19307 for modified radical mastectomy).
  • Pathology Report Interpretation: Understanding tumor characteristics (ER/PR/HER2 status), grade, stage, and margin status for treatment planning.
  • Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB): A procedure to identify and remove the first lymph nodes to which cancer cells are likely to spread, guiding further axillary management.
  • Oncoplastic Surgery: Techniques combining cancer removal with plastic surgery principles to optimize cosmetic outcomes, sometimes involving immediate reconstruction.
  • Adjuvant Therapies: Post-surgical treatments such as chemotherapy, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, or targeted therapy, based on pathology findings.
