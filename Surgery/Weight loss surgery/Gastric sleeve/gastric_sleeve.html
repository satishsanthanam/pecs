Gastric sleeve

🏠 Home / Surgery / Weight loss surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Gastric sleeve surgery, or sleeve gastrectomy, is a restrictive bariatric procedure that removes approximately 75-80% of the stomach.
  • Mechanism of Action: It creates a banana-shaped stomach pouch, significantly reducing food intake capacity and decreasing the production of ghrelin, the hunger-stimulating hormone.
  • Primary Goal: To achieve substantial and sustained weight loss, leading to improvement or resolution of obesity-related co-morbidities.
  • Eligibility Criteria: Typically considered for individuals with a Body Mass Index (BMI) of 40 or higher, or a BMI of 35-39.9 with at least one significant obesity-related health condition.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Pre-operative Indications: Morbid obesity, uncontrolled Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, severe obstructive sleep apnea, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and significant joint pain exacerbated by weight.
  • Early Post-operative Symptoms: Mild abdominal pain, nausea, fatigue, and potential dehydration if fluid intake is insufficient.
  • Potential Complications Symptoms: Persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, fever, rapid heart rate (suggesting a leak); chronic acid reflux; symptoms of vitamin deficiencies (e.g., fatigue, neurological changes).
  • Dumping Syndrome (Less Common): Rapid heart rate, sweating, dizziness, and diarrhea after consuming high-sugar or high-fat foods.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Diet Adherence: Strict compliance with the prescribed liquid diet before surgery is essential to shrink the liver and reduce surgical risks.
  • Medication Management: Discontinue blood thinners, NSAIDs, and certain diabetes medications as instructed by the surgical team well in advance of the procedure.
  • Hydration Protocol: Maintain rigorous hydration post-surgery, sipping fluids constantly throughout the day to prevent dehydration.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and abdominal straining for at least 4-6 weeks post-operatively to allow for proper healing.
  • Lifelong Follow-up: Commit to regular post-operative appointments with the surgeon, dietitian, and other specialists for monitoring and support.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Staged Diet Progression: Follow a strict post-operative diet plan, typically progressing from clear liquids to full liquids, pureed foods, soft foods, and then regular solid foods over several weeks.
  • Portion Control & Chewing: Consume very small portions, chew food thoroughly until it is a paste-like consistency, and eat slowly to prevent discomfort and vomiting.
  • Protein Priority: Focus on consuming protein-rich foods first at each meal to ensure adequate protein intake and preserve muscle mass.
  • Fluid Separation: Do not drink fluids with meals; wait at least 30 minutes before and after eating to prevent stomach distension and aid digestion.
  • Avoid Specific Foods: Strictly avoid carbonated beverages, high-sugar foods, high-fat foods, tough meats, and fibrous vegetables in the early stages.
  • No Straws: Avoid using straws as they can introduce air into the stomach, causing discomfort and gas.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Signs: Immediately report any signs of complications such as persistent fever, severe or worsening abdominal pain, rapid heart rate, shortness of breath, or pus/redness at the incision site.
  • Nutritional Supplementation: Lifelong daily supplementation with a bariatric-specific multivitamin, Vitamin B12, Vitamin D, and Calcium is mandatory to prevent deficiencies.
  • Pregnancy Post-Surgery: Women of childbearing age should avoid pregnancy for at least 12-18 months post-surgery to ensure stable weight loss and optimal nutritional status for both mother and baby.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Avoid alcohol consumption, especially in the initial post-operative period, as tolerance may be significantly reduced and it can contribute to dehydration.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Holistic Approach: Gastric sleeve surgery is a tool, not a cure. Long-term success hinges on a comprehensive commitment to dietary changes, regular physical activity, and psychological support.
  • Risk-Benefit Discussion: Thoroughly discuss the potential benefits of significant weight loss and co-morbidity resolution against the inherent surgical risks and potential long-term complications.
  • Patient Selection: Careful patient selection, including psychological evaluation, is crucial to identify individuals who are committed to the necessary lifestyle modifications.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Emphasize the importance of consistent follow-up to monitor nutritional status, weight loss trajectory, and manage any emerging complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Provide comprehensive patient education on the surgical process, pre-op diet, expected post-op course, and potential complications.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Implement effective pain assessment tools and administer appropriate analgesia to ensure patient comfort and facilitate early mobilization.
  • Fluid & Electrolyte Balance: Closely monitor intake and output, vital signs, and electrolyte levels to prevent and manage dehydration and imbalances.
  • Wound Care & Infection Prevention: Educate patients on incision care, monitor for signs of infection, and ensure sterile technique during dressing changes.
  • Nutritional Counseling Reinforcement: Reinforce dietary guidelines, portion control, and the importance of protein and vitamin supplementation.
  • Psychosocial Support: Assess for and address body image concerns, emotional eating patterns, and provide resources for support groups.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CPT Code: Commonly associated with CPT code 43775 (Laparoscopy, surgical, gastric restrictive procedure; longitudinal gastrectomy (i.e., sleeve gastrectomy)).
  • Physiological Impact: Resection of the gastric fundus significantly reduces ghrelin (hunger hormone) production, contributing to decreased appetite and increased satiety.
  • Anatomical Modification: Involves the removal of the greater curvature of the stomach, creating a narrow, tubular stomach pouch along the lesser curvature.
  • Key Complications: Gastric leak (most serious early complication), stricture, new-onset or worsening Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD), and long-term nutritional deficiencies.
  • Expected Outcomes: Average excess weight loss typically ranges from 50-70% within 1-2 years, with significant improvements in obesity-related co-morbidities.
