Cannabis hyperemesis syndrome

🏠 Home / Children's health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Cannabis hyperemesis syndrome (CHS) is a condition characterized by recurrent, severe nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain in chronic cannabis users.
  • Key Feature: Symptoms are often temporarily relieved by hot showers or baths, a distinctive diagnostic clue.
  • Prevalence: Increasingly recognized, particularly among adolescents and young adults who use cannabis frequently and in high doses.
  • Causation: Directly linked to chronic and heavy cannabis use, with symptoms resolving only upon complete cessation.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Prodromal Phase: Early morning nausea, abdominal discomfort, and fear of vomiting, often preceding full-blown episodes by weeks or months.
  • Hyperemetic Phase: Intense, cyclical bouts of nausea and profuse vomiting, often multiple times per hour, leading to significant distress.
  • Abdominal Pain: Diffuse, crampy abdominal pain that can be severe and debilitating.
  • Compulsive Hot Bathing: Patients often report an irresistible urge to take hot showers or baths, as the heat provides temporary relief from nausea and pain.
  • Dehydration: Due to persistent vomiting, patients are at high risk for severe dehydration and electrolyte imbalances.
  • Weight Loss: Chronic episodes can lead to significant unintentional weight loss.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Cannabis Cessation: The most critical precaution and the only definitive treatment is complete and sustained abstinence from all cannabis products.
  • Hydration Management: Vigilant monitoring and management of hydration status to prevent severe dehydration and electrolyte disturbances.
  • Avoidance of Triggers: While cannabis is the primary trigger, some individuals report certain foods (e.g., spicy, fatty) or stress can exacerbate symptoms.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Watch for signs of acute kidney injury, esophageal tears (Mallory-Weiss tears), dental erosion, or aspiration pneumonia due to prolonged vomiting.
  • Safe Environment: Ensure a safe environment during episodes of compulsive hot bathing to prevent burns or falls, especially if the patient is disoriented.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Acute Phase: During active vomiting, maintain NPO (nil per os) or allow only sips of clear fluids if tolerated to prevent further emesis.
  • Reintroduction of Diet: Once vomiting subsides, gradually reintroduce bland, easily digestible foods such as crackers, toast, rice, bananas, and applesauce.
  • Hydration: Encourage oral rehydration solutions (ORS) to replenish fluids and electrolytes; intravenous fluids may be necessary for severe dehydration.
  • Avoidance: Restrict spicy, fatty, acidic, and highly processed foods, as well as caffeine, which can irritate the gastrointestinal tract and potentially trigger symptoms.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Advise small, frequent meals rather than large ones to minimize gastric distension and nausea.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Care: Seek immediate medical attention for intractable vomiting, signs of severe dehydration (e.g., decreased urination, lethargy, dizziness), or altered mental status.
  • Supportive Measures: Administer prescribed antiemetics (though often ineffective for CHS), benzodiazepines, or topical capsaicin cream to the abdomen for symptomatic relief.
  • Parental/Guardian Education: Educate parents and guardians about the direct link between cannabis use and the child's symptoms, emphasizing the need for cessation.
  • Psychological Support: Provide resources for substance use counseling and mental health support to address underlying cannabis use disorder.
  • Environmental Control: Help create a calm, quiet environment during episodes to reduce sensory overload that might worsen nausea.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Approach: Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on a history of chronic cannabis use, cyclical vomiting, abdominal pain, and the characteristic relief with hot bathing, after ruling out other causes of cyclic vomiting.
  • Treatment Cornerstone: Emphasize that complete and sustained cannabis cessation is the only curative treatment; all other interventions are supportive.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Consider and exclude other conditions such as cyclic vomiting syndrome, gastroparesis, acute abdomen, and other causes of recurrent vomiting.
  • Pharmacological Management: While traditional antiemetics are often ineffective, haloperidol, benzodiazepines, and tricyclic antidepressants have shown some utility in managing acute symptoms.
  • Referral: Strongly recommend referral to substance abuse specialists, gastroenterologists, and mental health professionals for comprehensive care.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Obtain a detailed history of cannabis use (type, frequency, duration, method), symptom onset, duration, frequency of episodes, and alleviating factors (e.g., hot showers).
  • Fluid & Electrolyte Monitoring: Closely monitor intake and output, vital signs, and laboratory values (electrolytes, renal function) to detect and manage dehydration and imbalances.
  • Pain & Nausea Management: Administer prescribed medications for pain and nausea, and explore non-pharmacological interventions such as warm compresses, distraction, and a quiet environment.
  • Patient Education: Educate the patient and family about CHS, the importance of cannabis cessation, and the potential for relapse if use continues. Provide clear instructions on dietary management and when to seek emergency care.
  • Psychosocial Support: Offer empathetic support and connect patients with appropriate counseling services for substance use disorder.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Thought to involve dysregulation of the endocannabinoid system, affecting gastrointestinal motility, central thermoregulation, and the emetic center in the brainstem.
  • Cannabinoid Receptors: Activation of CB1 receptors in the gut and brain is implicated, potentially leading to delayed gastric emptying and altered visceral sensation.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: While no universally accepted criteria exist, diagnosis relies on chronic cannabis use, cyclical vomiting, abdominal pain, and resolution with cessation, often supported by the hot bathing behavior.
  • Pharmacological Interventions: Acute management may include intravenous fluids, antiemetics (e.g., ondansetron, metoclopramide, haloperidol), benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam), and topical capsaicin cream.
  • Prognosis: Excellent with complete cannabis cessation; high recurrence rate if cannabis use is resumed.
