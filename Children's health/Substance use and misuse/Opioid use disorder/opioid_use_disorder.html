Opioid use disorder

🏠 Home / Children's health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Opioid Use Disorder (OUD): OUD is a chronic, relapsing brain disease characterized by compulsive opioid seeking and use despite harmful consequences. In children and adolescents, it can significantly impact brain development and overall well-being.
  • Vulnerability in Youth: Adolescents are particularly vulnerable due to developing brains, peer pressure, and increased risk-taking behaviors. Early exposure to opioids, even prescribed, can heighten this risk.
  • Progression of the Disorder: OUD can develop rapidly in young individuals, often starting with misuse of prescription opioids before potentially transitioning to illicit substances like heroin or fentanyl.
  • Impact on Development: Sustained opioid use in youth can disrupt critical brain development, affecting cognitive function, emotional regulation, and social skills.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Physical Indicators: Pinpoint pupils, drowsiness, slurred speech, constipation, nausea, and vomiting are common. Unexplained weight loss or poor hygiene may also be present.
  • Behavioral Changes: Secretive behavior, sudden changes in friends or activities, declining academic performance, increased irritability, mood swings, and loss of interest in previously enjoyed hobbies.
  • Withdrawal Symptoms: If opioid use is stopped, symptoms can include severe muscle aches, diarrhea, abdominal cramping, dilated pupils, goosebumps, excessive yawning, and intense drug cravings.
  • Overdose Signs: Slowed or stopped breathing, blue lips or fingertips, unresponsiveness, gurgling sounds, and limp body are critical signs requiring immediate emergency intervention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Safe Medication Storage: All prescription opioids in the home must be stored securely, preferably in a locked cabinet, and out of reach of children and adolescents.
  • Proper Disposal of Unused Medications: Unused or expired opioids should be disposed of immediately through drug take-back programs or by following FDA guidelines for flushing or mixing with undesirable substances.
  • Educating Youth on Risks: Open and honest conversations with children and adolescents about the dangers of opioid misuse, including prescription medications, are vital for prevention.
  • Naloxone Availability: Families with a history of opioid use or those with prescribed opioids should consider having naloxone (an opioid overdose reversal medication) readily available and know how to use it.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration During Withdrawal: Maintaining adequate fluid intake is crucial during opioid withdrawal to prevent dehydration, especially with symptoms like vomiting and diarrhea. Electrolyte-rich fluids may be beneficial.
  • Nutritional Support for Recovery: Opioid use often leads to poor dietary habits and malnutrition. A balanced diet rich in vitamins, minerals, and fiber is essential to support physical and mental recovery.
  • Addressing Gastrointestinal Issues: Constipation is a common side effect of opioid use. Increasing fiber intake from fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, along with sufficient fluids, can help manage this.
  • Avoiding Stimulants/Depressants: During recovery, it is important to avoid substances like excessive caffeine, alcohol, or other non-prescribed drugs that can interfere with recovery or medication-assisted treatment.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Recognize Overdose and Act Immediately: If an overdose is suspected, call emergency services (e.g., 911) immediately. Administer naloxone if available and trained to do so. Stay with the individual until help arrives.
  • Seek Professional Help Promptly: If you suspect a child or adolescent is misusing opioids or has OUD, seek medical and behavioral health evaluation without delay. Early intervention significantly improves outcomes.
  • Maintain a Non-Judgmental Stance: Approach the situation with empathy and support. Stigma can be a major barrier to seeking and adhering to treatment.
  • Ensure a Safe Environment: Remove all access to opioids and other substances from the home. Create a supportive and structured environment conducive to recovery.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment is Key: A thorough medical, psychological, and social assessment is essential to diagnose OUD and identify co-occurring mental health conditions or other substance use disorders.
  • Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT): MAT, including buprenorphine or naltrexone, is an evidence-based treatment for OUD in adolescents and adults. It should be considered as part of a comprehensive treatment plan.
  • Behavioral Therapies: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Motivational Interviewing (MI), and family-based therapies are crucial components of OUD treatment, addressing underlying issues and developing coping skills.
  • Long-Term Management: OUD is a chronic condition requiring ongoing support and monitoring. Physicians should facilitate access to long-term recovery support services, including peer support and relapse prevention strategies.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Screening and Early Identification: Nurses play a vital role in screening adolescents for substance use, utilizing validated tools, and recognizing early signs of opioid misuse or OUD in various clinical settings.
  • Patient and Family Education: Educate patients and families on the risks of opioids, safe storage and disposal, overdose recognition, naloxone administration, and the importance of adherence to treatment plans.
  • Withdrawal Management: Monitor patients for opioid withdrawal symptoms and administer prescribed medications to manage discomfort, ensuring patient safety and comfort during detoxification.
  • Advocacy and Resource Navigation: Advocate for adolescents with OUD, connecting them and their families to appropriate treatment programs, mental health services, and community support resources.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria for OUD: Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, provides specific criteria for diagnosing Opioid Use Disorder based on a pattern of problematic opioid use.
  • ASAM Criteria: The American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) Criteria are widely used to assess the severity of substance use disorders and determine appropriate levels of care for treatment.
  • SAMHSA Guidelines: The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) offers evidence-based guidelines and resources for the prevention and treatment of OUD, including MAT.
  • Pharmacotherapy Options: Key medications include Buprenorphine (often combined with naloxone as Suboxone), Naltrexone (oral or extended-release injectable), and Methadone (typically in specialized clinics).
