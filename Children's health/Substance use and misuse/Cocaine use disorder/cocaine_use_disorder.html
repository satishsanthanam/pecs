Cocaine use disorder

🏠 Home / Children's health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Nature of Disorder: Cocaine use disorder is a highly addictive condition involving compulsive seeking and use of cocaine despite harmful consequences, severely impacting physical and mental health.
  • Impact on Children: Children living in households with a caregiver's substance use disorder are at significantly increased risk for neglect, abuse, developmental delays, and developing their own substance use issues.
  • Adolescent Vulnerability: Adolescents are particularly susceptible to the neurobiological effects of cocaine due to their still-developing brains, leading to quicker progression to addiction and more severe long-term consequences.
  • Forms of Cocaine: Cocaine can be snorted as powder, injected, or smoked as crack cocaine, each method carrying distinct risks and patterns of use.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Behavioral Changes (Adolescent): Sudden decline in academic performance, secretiveness, withdrawal from family activities, new peer groups, increased irritability, or unexplained mood swings.
  • Physical Signs (Adolescent): Dilated pupils, frequent nosebleeds or runny nose without cold symptoms, track marks (if injecting), significant weight loss, or poor hygiene.
  • Parental Use Indicators: Erratic behavior, unexplained financial difficulties, neglect of child's basic needs (food, hygiene, supervision), frequent absences, or legal problems.
  • Withdrawal Symptoms: Severe depression, intense cravings, fatigue, irritability, anxiety, and anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure) after cessation of use.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Early Intervention: Prompt identification and intervention for adolescents showing signs of substance use are critical to prevent progression to a full-blown disorder.
  • Safe Environment: Ensuring children are in a stable, nurturing, and safe environment, especially when a caregiver is struggling with substance use, is paramount.
  • Preventative Education: Age-appropriate education for children and adolescents about the risks of substance use, refusal skills, and healthy coping mechanisms.
  • Harm Reduction for Caregivers: Educating caregivers on preventing accidental exposure of children to cocaine or paraphernalia, and the dangers of second-hand smoke from crack cocaine.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Deficiencies: Chronic cocaine use often leads to poor appetite, irregular eating patterns, and significant malnutrition, requiring nutritional assessment and support during recovery.
  • Hydration Status: Dehydration is common due to the stimulant effects of cocaine and neglect of basic fluid intake; ensuring adequate hydration is crucial.
  • Structured Meals: Establishing regular, balanced, and nutritious meals is vital during recovery to restore physical health and stabilize mood, particularly for adolescents.
  • Avoidance of Stimulants: Limiting or avoiding other stimulants like caffeine during recovery can help prevent exacerbating anxiety, sleep disturbances, and cravings.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Overdose Recognition: Be aware of signs of cocaine overdose, including seizures, respiratory arrest, cardiac arrest, extreme agitation, hyperthermia, and psychosis. Call emergency services immediately.
  • Child Safety: If a child is in immediate danger due to a caregiver's substance use or neglect, contact child protective services or local emergency services without delay.
  • Never Leave Alone: Individuals experiencing acute cocaine intoxication or severe withdrawal symptoms should not be left unsupervised due to risks of medical complications or self-harm.
  • Avoid Confrontation: Do not confront an intoxicated individual aggressively; prioritize safety, maintain a calm demeanor, and seek professional help.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough medical, psychological, and social assessment is essential for diagnosing cocaine use disorder and identifying co-occurring mental health conditions or other substance use.
  • Integrated Treatment: Treatment plans should integrate behavioral therapies (e.g., Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, Motivational Interviewing) with family therapy, especially for adolescent patients.
  • Pharmacological Support: While no specific FDA-approved medication exists for cocaine use disorder, medications may be used to manage withdrawal symptoms or treat co-occurring psychiatric disorders.
  • Long-term Recovery: Emphasize that recovery is a long-term process requiring ongoing support, relapse prevention strategies, and addressing underlying psychosocial factors.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Withdrawal Management: Nurses play a critical role in monitoring vital signs, assessing withdrawal symptoms, providing supportive care, and administering prescribed medications to manage discomfort and prevent complications.
  • Patient and Family Education: Educate patients and families on the neurobiology of addiction, the importance of adherence to treatment plans, relapse warning signs, and available community resources.
  • Trauma-Informed Care: Recognize the high prevalence of trauma in individuals with substance use disorders and provide care that is sensitive, supportive, and avoids re-traumatization.
  • Referral Pathways: Facilitate referrals to appropriate levels of care, including inpatient rehabilitation, intensive outpatient programs, and support groups like Narcotics Anonymous.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Diagnosis of cocaine use disorder is based on specific criteria outlined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, including impaired control, social impairment, risky use, and pharmacological criteria.
  • Neurobiology of Addiction: Cocaine primarily acts by blocking the reuptake of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin, leading to an accumulation of these neurotransmitters in the synaptic cleft, particularly dopamine, which mediates its reinforcing effects.
  • Polysubstance Use: Cocaine use disorder frequently co-occurs with other substance use disorders, such as alcohol or opioid use, complicating treatment and increasing health risks.
  • Co-occurring Disorders: High comorbidity with mental health conditions like depression, anxiety disorders, ADHD, and PTSD, necessitating integrated treatment approaches.
