Crohn disease in children

🏠 Home / Children's health / Digestive system

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Chronic Inflammation: Crohn disease in children is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that can affect any part of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, from the mouth to the anus, characterized by transmural inflammation.
  • Periods of Activity: The disease typically presents with periods of active inflammation (flare-ups) and periods of remission, making long-term management crucial.
  • Growth & Development Impact: Pediatric Crohn's can significantly impair a child's growth, pubertal development, and overall nutritional status due to chronic inflammation, malabsorption, and reduced caloric intake.
  • Increasing Incidence: There is a rising incidence of Crohn disease in the pediatric population globally, emphasizing the need for early recognition and specialized care.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Abdominal Pain: Persistent or recurrent abdominal pain, often localized to the right lower quadrant, is a common presenting symptom.
  • Chronic Diarrhea: Prolonged episodes of diarrhea, sometimes with blood or mucus, are frequently observed.
  • Weight Loss & Poor Growth: Unexplained weight loss, failure to gain weight, or growth deceleration (failure to thrive) are critical indicators in children.
  • Fatigue & Anemia: Chronic inflammation and malabsorption can lead to significant fatigue and iron-deficiency anemia.
  • Extraintestinal Manifestations: Children may experience symptoms outside the GI tract, including joint pain (arthralgia/arthritis), skin lesions (e.g., erythema nodosum), eye inflammation (uveitis), and fever.
  • Mouth Sores: Aphthous ulcers (canker sores) in the mouth can be an early sign.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Growth Monitoring: Meticulous and regular monitoring of height, weight, and pubertal development is paramount to detect and address growth failure promptly.
  • Nutritional Assessment: Ongoing assessment for malnutrition, vitamin deficiencies (e.g., B12, D), and iron deficiency is essential, often requiring supplementation or specialized nutritional support.
  • Medication Adherence: Strict adherence to prescribed medications (e.g., immunomodulators, biologics, corticosteroids) is vital for inducing and maintaining remission and preventing complications.
  • Infection Risk: Children on immunosuppressive therapies are at increased risk of infections; ensure all age-appropriate non-live vaccinations are up-to-date and monitor for signs of infection.
  • Bone Health: Chronic inflammation and corticosteroid use can impact bone density; monitor bone health and ensure adequate calcium and vitamin D intake.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address the significant emotional and social impact of chronic illness on the child and family, providing access to support groups and mental health professionals.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Exclusive Enteral Nutrition (EEN): Often used as a primary therapy for inducing remission in pediatric Crohn's, involving a liquid formula as the sole source of nutrition for several weeks.
  • Trigger Food Identification: Work with a dietitian to identify and potentially eliminate individual trigger foods that exacerbate symptoms (e.g., high-fiber foods during flares, lactose if intolerant, fatty or spicy foods).
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially during periods of diarrhea, to prevent dehydration.
  • Vitamin & Mineral Supplementation: Many children require supplements for iron, vitamin B12, vitamin D, calcium, and other micronutrients due to malabsorption or dietary restrictions.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: During active disease or recovery, smaller, more frequent meals may be better tolerated and help meet caloric needs.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Symptoms: Educate parents/caregivers on signs requiring immediate medical attention, such as severe abdominal pain, high fever, persistent vomiting, significant rectal bleeding, or signs of dehydration.
  • Medication Side Effects: Provide clear instructions on potential side effects of all prescribed medications and when to report them to the healthcare team.
  • Disease Complications: Be aware of potential complications like strictures, fistulas, abscesses, and perianal disease, and monitor for their development.
  • Surgical Considerations: Understand that surgical intervention may be necessary for complications like strictures or fistulas, and be prepared for pre- and post-operative care.
  • Long-term Monitoring: Emphasize the chronic nature of Crohn's and the need for ongoing medical follow-up, even during remission, to monitor disease activity and prevent relapse.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early & Accurate Diagnosis: Prioritize prompt and precise diagnosis using endoscopy with biopsies and advanced imaging to initiate appropriate therapy and prevent irreversible complications like growth failure.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Advocate for a comprehensive, multidisciplinary team approach involving pediatric gastroenterologists, nutritionists, social workers, psychologists, and surgeons.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Tailor treatment strategies based on disease location, severity, patient age, growth status, and response to therapy, aiming for mucosal healing and sustained remission.
  • Growth & Development Focus: Consistently integrate growth and pubertal development assessment into every clinical encounter, as these are critical markers of disease control in children.
  • Patient & Family Education: Empower families with thorough education about the disease, treatment options, potential complications, and the importance of adherence and follow-up.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient & Family Education: Nurses are crucial in educating families about medication administration (including injectables), dietary modifications, symptom monitoring, and recognizing signs of flare-ups or complications.
  • Growth & Nutritional Assessment: Regularly assess and plot growth parameters, conduct nutritional screenings, and identify signs of malabsorption or deficiencies.
  • Medication Management: Understand the indications, mechanisms of action, potential side effects, and safe administration of various Crohn's medications, including biologics and immunomodulators.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide emotional support to children and families coping with a chronic illness, connecting them with appropriate resources and support networks.
  • Symptom Management: Implement nursing interventions to manage symptoms like pain, diarrhea, and fatigue, improving the child's comfort and quality of life.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Diagnostic Modalities: Endoscopy (colonoscopy, EGD) with biopsies, MRI enterography, CT enterography, stool studies (calprotectin), blood tests (ESR, CRP, albumin, CBC, serologic markers like ASCA).
  • Pharmacological Treatments: 5-aminosalicylates (5-ASAs), corticosteroids (prednisone, budesonide), immunomodulators (azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine, methotrexate), biologics (anti-TNF agents like infliximab, adalimumab; anti-integrins like vedolizumab; anti-IL-12/23 like ustekinumab).
  • Common Complications: Strictures, fistulas, abscesses, perianal disease, growth failure, osteopenia/osteoporosis, increased risk of colorectal cancer (long-term).
  • Monitoring Parameters: Clinical assessment, laboratory tests (inflammatory markers, drug levels, nutritional status), endoscopic evaluation, imaging studies, bone density scans.
  • Surgical Interventions: Strictureplasty, bowel resection, ostomy creation (in severe cases or for complications).
