Chronic granulomatous disease

🏠 Home / Children's health / Infections and vaccines

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Inherited Immunodeficiency: Chronic granulomatous disease (CGD) is a rare genetic disorder affecting the immune system's ability to fight certain bacteria and fungi.
  • Phagocyte Dysfunction: It results from defects in phagocytes (a type of white blood cell) that impair their capacity to produce reactive oxygen species necessary for killing ingested microbes.
  • Recurrent Infections: Individuals with CGD are highly susceptible to severe, recurrent bacterial and fungal infections, often leading to abscesses and granuloma formation in various organs.
  • Early Onset: Symptoms typically manifest in early childhood, often prompting diagnosis due to persistent or unusual infections.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Recurrent Abscesses: Frequent and deep-seated abscesses in the skin, lymph nodes, liver, lungs, and bones.
  • Pneumonia: Chronic or recurrent pneumonia, often caused by unusual pathogens.
  • Granuloma Formation: Development of granulomas that can obstruct the gastrointestinal or genitourinary tracts, leading to symptoms like chronic diarrhea or difficulty urinating.
  • Osteomyelitis: Bone infections that can be difficult to treat.
  • Failure to Thrive: Poor growth and weight gain, especially in infants and young children.
  • Fungal Infections: Increased susceptibility to invasive fungal infections, particularly aspergillosis.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Prophylactic Medications: Strict adherence to prescribed prophylactic antibiotics (e.g., trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) and antifungals (e.g., itraconazole) to prevent infections.
  • Infection Control: Meticulous hand hygiene, avoiding exposure to moldy environments (e.g., decaying leaves, construction sites), and minimizing contact with sick individuals.
  • Vaccination Guidelines: Avoidance of live bacterial or viral vaccines (e.g., BCG, oral polio, rotavirus) due to the risk of disseminated infection; consult an immunologist for specific recommendations.
  • Early Intervention: Prompt medical evaluation and treatment for any signs of infection, such as fever, localized pain, swelling, or changes in respiratory status.
  • Environmental Awareness: Caution with gardening, mulching, or activities that disturb soil, which can harbor *Aspergillus* and other environmental pathogens.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Support: Ensure adequate caloric and protein intake to support growth, development, and immune function, especially during periods of illness or inflammation.
  • Food Safety: Practice strict food hygiene, including thorough cooking of meats, poultry, and eggs, washing fruits and vegetables, and avoiding unpasteurized dairy products to prevent foodborne infections.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration, particularly when experiencing fever or gastrointestinal symptoms.
  • Vitamin D Supplementation: Discuss with a physician about vitamin D supplementation, as chronic inflammation or malabsorption can affect levels.
  • Avoidance of Contaminants: Be mindful of potential fungal contaminants in certain foods (e.g., moldy cheeses, dried fruits if not stored properly), though this is less common than environmental exposure.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Preparedness: Caregivers must be educated on recognizing signs of severe infection and when to seek immediate emergency medical attention.
  • Medication Management: Maintain a detailed record of all medications, dosages, and schedules, ensuring consistent administration of prophylactic treatments.
  • Caregiver Education: Provide comprehensive education to all caregivers (parents, guardians, school nurses) regarding CGD, its management, and specific infection prevention strategies.
  • Genetic Counseling: Families should be offered genetic counseling to understand the inheritance pattern and implications for future pregnancies.
  • Travel Precautions: Advise on specific precautions for travel, including carrying medical documentation and ensuring access to medical care.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Management of CGD requires a collaborative team including immunologists, infectious disease specialists, pulmonologists, and gastroenterologists.
  • Regular Monitoring: Routine clinical assessments, laboratory tests (e.g., inflammatory markers, complete blood count), and imaging studies are crucial to monitor disease activity and detect complications.
  • Interferon-gamma Therapy: Recombinant human interferon-gamma is often prescribed to reduce the frequency and severity of infections in CGD patients.
  • Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT): HSCT is a potentially curative option for eligible patients, especially those with severe disease, and should be discussed early in the disease course.
  • Anticipatory Guidance: Proactive counseling on potential complications, such as inflammatory bowel disease-like symptoms or obstructive granulomas, is essential for early detection and management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient and Family Education: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients and families on medication adherence, infection prevention techniques, and symptom recognition.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Vigilant assessment for signs of infection, inflammation, and organ dysfunction (e.g., respiratory distress, abdominal pain, skin lesions).
  • Vaccine Schedule Adaptation: Understand the modified immunization schedule for CGD patients, emphasizing inactivated vaccines and avoiding live vaccines.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide emotional support and resources to families coping with a chronic, life-threatening illness, addressing the impact on daily life and mental well-being.
  • Sterile Technique: Reinforce strict sterile technique for any invasive procedures, wound care, or central line management to prevent iatrogenic infections.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Genetic Basis: Caused by mutations in genes encoding components of the NADPH oxidase complex (e.g., *CYBB* for X-linked CGD, *NCF1*, *NCF2*, *NCF4*, *CYBA* for autosomal recessive forms).
  • Diagnostic Tests: Nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) test, dihydrorhodamine (DHR) flow cytometry assay (gold standard), and genetic sequencing for definitive diagnosis.
  • Pathogens of Concern: Predominantly catalase-positive organisms such as *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Aspergillus* species, *Nocardia*, *Serratia marcescens*, and *Burkholderia cepacia*.
  • Treatment Modalities: Prophylactic antibiotics (e.g., trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole), prophylactic antifungals (e.g., itraconazole), interferon-gamma, and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT).
  • Inflammatory Complications: Beyond infections, CGD can lead to sterile inflammatory conditions, including colitis, pulmonary inflammation, and chorioretinal lesions.
