Atrioventricular canal defects

🏠 Home / Children's health / Congenital disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Atrioventricular canal defect (AVCD), also known as endocardial cushion defect, is a congenital heart condition involving a hole between the heart's upper and lower chambers and a single, common atrioventricular valve instead of separate mitral and tricuspid valves.
  • Types: Can be complete (large defect, common valve) or partial (ostium primum atrial septal defect with a cleft mitral valve).
  • Associated Conditions: Strongly linked with Down syndrome (Trisomy 21), affecting approximately 40-50% of infants with this genetic condition.
  • Clinical Impact: Leads to increased blood flow to the lungs, causing pulmonary hypertension and eventually heart failure if not surgically corrected.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Feeding Difficulties: Poor feeding, fatigue during feeds, and inadequate weight gain (failure to thrive) due to increased cardiac workload.
  • Respiratory Distress: Rapid breathing (tachypnea), shortness of breath (dyspnea), and recurrent respiratory infections.
  • Cyanosis: Bluish discoloration of the skin, lips, or nail beds, especially during crying or feeding, indicating low oxygen levels.
  • Heart Murmur: A distinct heart murmur is often detected during a physical examination.
  • Sweating: Excessive sweating, particularly during feeding, is a common sign of increased metabolic demand.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Early Diagnosis: Prompt diagnosis via echocardiography is vital to plan timely surgical intervention and prevent irreversible pulmonary vascular disease.
  • Infection Prophylaxis: Patients may require antibiotic prophylaxis against infective endocarditis, particularly before dental procedures or other surgeries, as advised by a cardiologist.
  • Monitoring for Heart Failure: Close observation for signs of worsening heart failure, such as increased respiratory effort, poor feeding, or fluid retention.
  • Surgical Timing: Adherence to the recommended surgical window, typically between 3-6 months of age, to optimize outcomes and minimize complications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Feeding Strategies: Infants may benefit from smaller, more frequent feeds, specialized nipples, or gavage feeding to conserve energy and ensure adequate caloric intake.
  • Nutritional Support: High-calorie formulas or breast milk fortification may be necessary to support growth in infants with increased metabolic demands.
  • Fluid Management: Careful monitoring of fluid intake is essential, especially if diuretics are prescribed or if there are signs of fluid overload due from heart failure.
  • Post-Surgical Reintroduction: Gradual reintroduction of oral feeds post-surgery, starting with clear fluids and progressing as tolerated.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Parental Education: Educate parents on recognizing and reporting signs of respiratory distress, feeding intolerance, or changes in activity level.
  • Medication Adherence: Emphasize strict adherence to prescribed medications (e.g., diuretics, digoxin) and understanding their purpose and potential side effects.
  • Activity Restrictions: Discuss appropriate activity levels for the child, especially pre-surgery and during the post-operative recovery period, to prevent overexertion.
  • Follow-up Schedule: Stress the importance of consistent follow-up appointments with pediatric cardiology for ongoing monitoring and management.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Surgical Correction: Surgical repair is the definitive treatment for AVCD, involving patching the septal defects and reconstructing the common AV valve into two functional valves.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Optimal management requires a collaborative approach involving pediatric cardiologists, cardiac surgeons, intensivists, and specialized nurses.
  • Genetic Counseling: Recommend genetic counseling for families, especially given the strong association with Down syndrome, to understand recurrence risks and implications.
  • Long-term Prognosis: With successful surgical repair, most children with AVCD can lead active lives, though lifelong cardiac follow-up is necessary to monitor for potential late complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Focus on thorough cardiac and respiratory assessments, including heart sounds, respiratory rate and effort, oxygen saturation, and peripheral perfusion.
  • Medication Management: Understand the indications, dosages, and potential side effects of common cardiac medications used in infants with AVCD (e.g., furosemide, spironolactone, digoxin).
  • Post-operative Care: Key nursing interventions include pain management, fluid balance monitoring, arrhythmia detection, and respiratory support (e.g., ventilator management, chest physiotherapy).
  • Family Support: Provide emotional support and clear, concise education to families, empowering them to participate in their child's care and manage complex medical needs.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Results from incomplete fusion of the endocardial cushions during cardiac development, leading to a common AV valve and defects in the atrial and ventricular septa.
  • Hemodynamics: Characterized by left-to-right shunting at both atrial and ventricular levels, causing volume overload of the right ventricle and increased pulmonary blood flow.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Echocardiography is the primary diagnostic tool, supplemented by ECG (often showing left axis deviation and biventricular hypertrophy) and Chest X-ray (cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary vascular markings).
  • Surgical Techniques: Primary repair involves placing patches to close the atrial and ventricular septal defects and reconstructing the common AV valve into separate mitral and tricuspid valves.
