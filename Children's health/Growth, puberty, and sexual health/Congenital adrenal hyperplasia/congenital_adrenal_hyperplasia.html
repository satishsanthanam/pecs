Congenital adrenal hyperplasia

🏠 Home / Children's health / Growth, puberty, and sexual health

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH): A group of inherited disorders affecting the adrenal glands' ability to produce essential hormones like cortisol and aldosterone.
  • Enzyme Deficiency: Most commonly caused by a deficiency in the 21-hydroxylase enzyme, leading to impaired cortisol and aldosterone synthesis.
  • Adrenal Androgen Excess: The deficiency results in overproduction of adrenal androgens, which can cause virilization in females and precocious puberty in males.
  • Salt-Wasting Crisis Risk: Severe forms can lead to life-threatening electrolyte imbalances (hyponatremia, hyperkalemia) due to severe aldosterone deficiency.
  • Newborn Screening: Often detected through routine newborn screening programs, which test for elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone levels.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Salt-Wasting Crisis (Classic CAH): Infants may present with poor feeding, persistent vomiting, lethargy, dehydration, and hypotension.
  • Ambiguous Genitalia in Females (Classic CAH): At birth, external genitalia may appear masculinized, with an enlarged clitoris and partially fused labia resembling a scrotum.
  • Precocious Puberty in Males (Classic CAH): Early development of secondary sexual characteristics such as an enlarged penis, pubic hair, and accelerated growth in childhood.
  • Rapid Growth & Bone Age Advancement: Both sexes may experience accelerated growth spurts in early childhood, leading to premature epiphyseal fusion and short adult stature if untreated.
  • Non-Classic CAH Symptoms: May include premature adrenarche (early pubic hair/body odor), hirsutism (excessive hair growth), severe acne, and menstrual irregularities in adolescent females.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Lifelong Hormone Replacement: Patients require lifelong glucocorticoid (e.g., hydrocortisone) and often mineralocorticoid (e.g., fludrocortisone) replacement therapy.
  • Stress Dosing Protocol: Increased glucocorticoid doses are critical during periods of illness, fever, surgery, or significant physical stress to prevent adrenal crisis.
  • Emergency Hydrocortisone Kit: Patients and caregivers must carry an emergency injectable hydrocortisone kit and be trained in its administration.
  • Regular Endocrine Monitoring: Consistent follow-up with a pediatric endocrinologist for monitoring hormone levels (e.g., 17-OHP, renin), electrolytes, and growth parameters.
  • Avoidance of Adrenal Crisis: Vigilance for signs of adrenal insufficiency (e.g., persistent vomiting, severe lethargy, weakness, abdominal pain) and prompt medical intervention.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Supplementation (Salt-Wasting CAH): Infants and children with salt-wasting CAH often require supplemental sodium chloride, especially during hot weather, illness, or increased activity.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintaining good fluid intake is essential, particularly when ill or during periods of increased physical exertion, to prevent dehydration.
  • Consistent Meal Timing: Glucocorticoid medications are often taken with meals to minimize gastrointestinal upset and ensure consistent absorption.
  • Balanced Nutrition: A well-balanced diet supports overall growth and development; no specific food restrictions are typically imposed for CAH itself beyond managing sodium for salt-wasting forms.
  • Monitoring Electrolytes: Dietary considerations should always align with electrolyte balance, especially sodium and potassium levels, as guided by the medical team.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Caregiver Education: Parents and primary caregivers must receive thorough education on medication administration, stress dosing, and recognizing the early signs of adrenal crisis.
  • Emergency Action Plan: Develop and share a clear emergency action plan with all relevant individuals, including contact information for healthcare providers and emergency services.
  • Medical Alert Identification: Patients should wear a medical alert bracelet or carry a card indicating their CAH diagnosis and the critical need for emergency glucocorticoids.
  • School & Daycare Notification: Inform school nurses, teachers, and daycare providers about the child's condition, medication regimen, and emergency protocols.
  • Regular Medical Reviews: Ensure scheduled appointments with the endocrine team are kept to review treatment efficacy, adjust dosages, and address any emerging concerns.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis & Management: Crucial for preventing life-threatening salt-wasting crises and minimizing the long-term effects of androgen excess, particularly virilization in females.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Optimal care involves a team including pediatric endocrinologists, geneticists, surgeons (if genitoplasty is indicated), and psychosocial support specialists.
  • Optimizing Hormone Replacement: The goal is to achieve a delicate balance of hormone replacement to support normal growth, pubertal development, and fertility while preventing adrenal crises.
  • Long-Term Surveillance: Continuous monitoring is essential to detect and manage potential complications such as adrenal rest tumors, fertility issues, and bone health concerns.
  • Psychosocial Support: Addressing the psychological impact of a chronic condition, ambiguous genitalia, and body image concerns is vital for the patient's overall well-being.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Newborn Screening Follow-up: Understand the protocol for confirming elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone levels and initiating prompt diagnostic workup.
  • Adrenal Crisis Management: Be proficient in recognizing the signs and symptoms of adrenal crisis and initiating emergency treatment, including intramuscular hydrocortisone administration.
  • Medication Adherence Education: Educate families on the importance of consistent medication administration, proper dosing, and the critical need for stress dosing during illness.
  • Growth & Pubertal Assessment: Accurately monitor and document growth velocity, bone age, and pubertal staging to assess treatment effectiveness and identify potential complications.
  • Patient & Family Empowerment: Provide comprehensive teaching on disease self-management, emergency preparedness, and resources for ongoing support.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: E25.0 (Congenital adrenogenital disorders associated with enzyme deficiency), E25.8 (Other adrenogenital disorders), E25.9 (Adrenogenital disorder, unspecified).
  • Key Hormones Involved: Cortisol, Aldosterone, Androgens (e.g., testosterone, androstenedione), 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP), Renin.
  • Diagnostic Tests: Newborn screening (17-OHP), ACTH stimulation test, Karyotyping (for ambiguous genitalia), Electrolyte panel, Plasma renin activity.
  • Treatment Modalities: Glucocorticoid replacement (hydrocortisone, prednisone), Mineralocorticoid replacement (fludrocortisone), Sodium chloride supplementation, Genitoplasty (for females with severe virilization).
  • Genetic Basis: Autosomal recessive inheritance, most commonly involving mutations in the CYP21A2 gene (21-hydroxylase deficiency).
