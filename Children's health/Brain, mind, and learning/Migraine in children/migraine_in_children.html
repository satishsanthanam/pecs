Migraine in children

🏠 Home / Children's health / Brain, mind, and learning

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Pediatric Migraine Characteristics: Migraines in children often present differently than in adults, with shorter attack durations and more frequent bilateral pain.
  • Prevalence and Impact: Affects a significant number of children and adolescents, impacting school performance, social activities, and overall quality of life.
  • Genetic Predisposition: A strong family history of migraine is common, suggesting a genetic component in many cases.
  • Common Triggers: Stress, lack of sleep, dehydration, certain foods, bright lights, loud noises, and changes in weather are frequent precipitants.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Headache Pain: Often described as throbbing or pounding, moderate to severe intensity, which worsens with physical activity.
  • Nausea and Vomiting: Very common accompanying symptoms, sometimes leading to dehydration.
  • Sensory Sensitivities: Photophobia (sensitivity to light) and phonophobia (sensitivity to sound) are hallmark features.
  • Abdominal Migraine: A variant primarily seen in children, characterized by recurrent episodes of abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting, often without significant headache.
  • Aura Symptoms: Visual disturbances (e.g., flashing lights, zigzag lines), sensory changes (e.g., numbness, tingling), or speech difficulties preceding the headache phase.
  • Prodromal Symptoms: Mood changes, fatigue, neck stiffness, yawning, or food cravings hours or days before the headache onset.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Trigger Identification and Avoidance: Maintain a headache diary to identify and minimize exposure to personal migraine triggers.
  • Medication Overuse Headache: Educate families on the risks of frequent use of acute pain medications, which can worsen headache frequency.
  • Safety During Aura: If a child experiences aura, ensure they are in a safe environment, especially if visual or motor disturbances occur.
  • Hydration and Regular Meals: Emphasize consistent fluid intake and avoiding skipped meals to prevent blood sugar fluctuations.
  • Emergency Care Indicators: Seek immediate medical attention for sudden, severe headache, headache with fever and stiff neck, or new neurological deficits.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Identify Trigger Foods: Common dietary triggers include aged cheeses, processed meats, chocolate, caffeine, artificial sweeteners, and certain food additives (e.g., MSG).
  • Maintain Consistent Hydration: Encourage regular water intake throughout the day to prevent dehydration, a known migraine trigger.
  • Avoid Skipping Meals: Promote regular meal schedules to stabilize blood sugar levels, as hypoglycemia can precipitate migraine attacks.
  • Limit Caffeine Intake: While caffeine can sometimes alleviate headaches, withdrawal can trigger migraines, and excessive intake can be problematic.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a diet rich in whole foods, fruits, and vegetables to support overall health and potentially reduce migraine frequency.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Create a Quiet Environment: During an attack, provide a dark, quiet room for the child to rest, minimizing sensory input.
  • Administer Medications Promptly: Give acute migraine medications at the first sign of a headache, as directed by the physician, for best efficacy.
  • Monitor Symptoms and Response: Observe the child's symptoms, pain level, and response to treatment, documenting for physician review.
  • School Communication: Inform school nurses and teachers about the child's migraine condition and any necessary accommodations or emergency protocols.
  • Emotional Support: Provide reassurance and comfort, as migraines can be frightening and debilitating for children.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Thorough Diagnostic Workup: A comprehensive history and physical examination are crucial to diagnose pediatric migraine and rule out secondary headache causes.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Management involves a combination of acute abortive therapies and, for frequent or severe cases, preventive medications tailored to the child's age and specific needs.
  • Non-Pharmacological Strategies: Recommend behavioral therapies such as biofeedback, cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and stress management techniques.
  • Education and Empowerment: Educate families about migraine pathophysiology, triggers, and the importance of a consistent lifestyle to manage the condition effectively.
  • Referral to Specialists: Consider referral to a pediatric neurologist for complex cases, refractory migraines, or when atypical symptoms are present.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology Overview: Understand the neurovascular theory of migraine, involving cortical spreading depression and activation of the trigeminal nervous system.
  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct a detailed headache history, including frequency, intensity, duration, associated symptoms, and impact on daily life.
  • Pain Management Principles: Apply age-appropriate pain assessment tools and implement pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions for acute and preventive care.
  • Patient and Family Education: Educate on medication administration, trigger avoidance, lifestyle modifications, and when to seek medical attention.
  • School Health Collaboration: Facilitate communication between healthcare providers, families, and school personnel to ensure appropriate support and accommodations for the child.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICHD-3 Diagnostic Criteria: Refer to the International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition, criteria adapted for pediatric migraine diagnosis.
  • Pharmacological Interventions: Acute treatments include NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen), triptans (e.g., sumatriptan, zolmitriptan) for older children/adolescents, and antiemetics.
  • Preventive Medications: Options may include topiramate, propranolol, amitriptyline, or cyproheptadine, chosen based on side effect profiles and comorbidities.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Consider other headache types (tension-type, cluster), secondary headaches (e.g., due to infection, tumor, trauma), and other neurological conditions.
  • Neuroimaging Guidelines: Understand indications for neuroimaging (CT/MRI) in pediatric headache, typically reserved for red flag symptoms or atypical presentations.
