Depression in children and teens – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Children's health / Mental health

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Pediatric Depression: Depression is a serious medical illness, not a character flaw or a phase, affecting mood, thoughts, and behavior in children and teens.
  • Treatable Condition: With appropriate treatment, including therapy and sometimes medication, children and teens can recover and manage their symptoms effectively.
  • Importance of Adherence: Consistent follow-through with the prescribed treatment plan post-discharge is crucial for preventing relapse and promoting long-term well-being.
  • Safety Planning Review: The individualized safety plan developed during hospitalization or outpatient care must be reviewed and understood by the patient and family.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Sadness or Irritability: Watch for a mood that is consistently sad, empty, or irritable, lasting most of the day, nearly every day.
  • Loss of Interest or Pleasure: Observe for a marked decrease in enjoyment or participation in activities previously found pleasurable (anhedonia).
  • Significant Changes in Appetite or Weight: Monitor for unexplained weight loss or gain, or a noticeable increase or decrease in appetite.
  • Sleep Disturbances: Look for insomnia (difficulty sleeping) or hypersomnia (excessive sleeping) nearly every day.
  • Fatigue or Loss of Energy: Note if the child/teen experiences persistent tiredness, even after adequate rest.
  • Feelings of Worthlessness or Guilt: Listen for expressions of excessive or inappropriate guilt, or feelings of being worthless.
  • Difficulty Concentrating: Observe for a diminished ability to think, concentrate, or make decisions.
  • Thoughts of Death or Self-Harm: Any mention or indication of thoughts about death, dying, self-harm, or suicide requires immediate clinical attention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Adherence: Ensure all prescribed medications are taken exactly as directed, without skipping doses or altering amounts.
  • Therapy Session Attendance: Prioritize and ensure consistent attendance at all scheduled psychotherapy appointments.
  • Environmental Safety: Immediately remove or secure all potential means of self-harm from the home environment, including firearms, sharp objects, and all medications.
  • Supervision Levels: Maintain appropriate levels of supervision, especially if there was a history of suicidal ideation or attempts; never leave the child/teen unsupervised if they are at high risk.
  • Crisis Plan Activation: Understand and be prepared to activate the individualized crisis plan, including emergency contact numbers and steps to take if symptoms worsen or suicidal thoughts return.
  • Substance Avoidance: Strictly prohibit the use of alcohol, illicit drugs, or non-prescribed substances, as these can exacerbate depressive symptoms and interact dangerously with medications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Medication Interactions: Be aware of any specific food or beverage interactions with prescribed psychotropic medications (e.g., grapefruit juice with certain medications). Consult the pharmacist for detailed information.
  • Consistent Nutrition: Encourage regular, balanced meals to support overall physical and mental health, as erratic eating patterns can impact mood and energy levels.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake, as dehydration can sometimes mimic or worsen symptoms of fatigue and poor concentration.
  • Caffeine and Sugar Moderation: Advise moderation of caffeine and high-sugar foods, as excessive intake can sometimes contribute to anxiety or mood instability in sensitive individuals.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Vigilant Monitoring: Closely observe the child/teen for any changes in mood, behavior, sleep patterns, or expressions of hopelessness or self-harm.
  • Medication Management: Take responsibility for administering medications, monitoring for side effects, and ensuring refills are obtained on time.
  • Supportive Environment: Foster a calm, predictable, and supportive home environment that encourages open communication and emotional expression.
  • Open Communication with Healthcare Team: Maintain regular contact with the child/teen's psychiatrist, therapist, and primary care provider, reporting any concerns or changes promptly.
  • Emergency Contacts Accessibility: Keep a readily accessible list of all emergency contact numbers, including the crisis hotline, therapist, psychiatrist, and primary care physician.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Critical Follow-Up: Adherence to all scheduled follow-up appointments with mental health professionals is paramount for ongoing assessment, medication management, and therapeutic progress.
  • Medication Education: Understand that psychotropic medications may take several weeks to show full effect and require careful monitoring for efficacy and side effects. Do not discontinue without medical advice.
  • Therapeutic Modalities: Emphasize the vital role of evidence-based psychotherapies (e.g., CBT, DBT, family therapy) as a cornerstone of treatment, often more effective when combined with medication.
  • Holistic Approach: Encourage a comprehensive approach involving family support, school collaboration, and community resources to create a robust support system for the child/teen.
  • Early Intervention for Worsening Symptoms: Instruct families to contact the clinical team immediately if there is any escalation of depressive symptoms, new onset of suicidal ideation, or concerning behavioral changes.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Patient Education: Provide thorough education to families on the signs and symptoms of depression, the importance of treatment adherence, and how to utilize the crisis plan.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Ensure accurate medication reconciliation upon discharge, verifying dosages, frequency, and potential side effects with the family.
  • Ongoing Safety Assessment: Continuously assess for suicide risk and reinforce safety planning strategies with both the patient and their caregivers.
  • Resource Provision: Furnish families with a comprehensive list of local and national mental health resources, support groups, and crisis hotlines.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Meticulously document all discharge teaching, patient/family understanding, and any remaining questions or concerns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Refer to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5) for specific diagnostic criteria for Major Depressive Disorder and other depressive disorders in children and adolescents.
  • Pharmacotherapy Guidelines: Consult evidence-based guidelines for the use of antidepressants in pediatric populations, noting specific considerations for SSRIs (e.g., fluoxetine, escitalopram) and black box warnings regarding increased suicidality risk in youth.
  • Psychotherapeutic Interventions: Review the efficacy of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), and Interpersonal Psychotherapy for Adolescents (IPT-A) as primary treatment modalities.
  • Risk Factor Identification: Understand common risk factors for pediatric depression, including genetic predisposition, family history of mental illness, trauma, chronic medical conditions, and co-occurring anxiety or ADHD.
  • Crisis Intervention Protocols: Familiarize with local and national crisis intervention protocols, including the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and emergency psychiatric services.
