Undescended testes

🏠 Home / Children's health / Newborns

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Undescended testes, or cryptorchidism, occurs when one or both testes fail to descend into the scrotum during fetal development.
  • Prevalence: Affects approximately 1-4% of full-term male infants and up to 30% of premature male infants.
  • Spontaneous Descent: In many cases, the testes will descend spontaneously within the first 3-6 months of life.
  • Clinical Significance: If left uncorrected, it can lead to increased risks of infertility, testicular cancer, testicular torsion, and inguinal hernia.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Empty Scrotum: The primary clinical finding is the inability to palpate one or both testes within the scrotal sac during examination.
  • Asymmetry: One side of the scrotum may appear less developed or smaller than the other if only one testis is undescended.
  • Palpable Mass in Groin: In some cases, the undescended testis may be palpable in the inguinal canal or groin area.
  • Associated Conditions: May co-occur with other congenital anomalies such as hypospadias or an inguinal hernia.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Regular Monitoring: Close observation by a pediatrician is essential during the first 6 months to track for spontaneous descent.
  • Timely Referral: If descent does not occur by 6 months of age, referral to a pediatric urologist or surgeon is crucial for evaluation.
  • Avoid Manipulation: Parents should be advised against attempting to manually push the testis into the scrotum, as this can cause injury.
  • Post-Surgical Care: If orchiopexy is performed, adhere strictly to post-operative instructions for wound care, pain management, and activity restrictions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Operative Fasting: If surgical correction (orchiopexy) is planned, strict adherence to pre-operative fasting guidelines for infants is critical to prevent aspiration.
  • Post-Operative Feeding: Following anesthesia, reintroduce oral feeds gradually, starting with clear fluids and progressing to regular formula or breast milk as tolerated.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate hydration for the infant, especially if there is any post-operative discomfort or fever.
  • Comfort Feeding: Provide comfort feeds as needed, ensuring the infant is positioned safely and comfortably, particularly after surgery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Parental Education: Educate parents on the importance of follow-up appointments and what to observe regarding the infant's scrotum.
  • Signs of Complication: Instruct parents to seek immediate medical attention for signs of testicular torsion (sudden severe pain, swelling, redness in the groin/scrotum) or incarcerated hernia (bulge that cannot be reduced, vomiting, irritability).
  • Activity Restrictions: Post-surgery, advise on temporary activity restrictions to prevent strain on the surgical site, such as avoiding vigorous play or straddling toys.
  • Hygiene: Emphasize gentle hygiene around the surgical site to prevent infection, following specific instructions from the healthcare team.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: A thorough scrotal examination at birth and subsequent well-child visits is paramount for early identification.
  • Optimal Timing for Orchiopexy: Surgical correction is generally recommended between 6 and 12 months of age to optimize fertility potential and reduce cancer risk.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Distinguish true undescended testes from retractile testes, which can be manually brought into the scrotum and remain there without tension.
  • Long-Term Follow-up: Patients with a history of undescended testes require lifelong monitoring for testicular cancer, including self-examination education at puberty.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Newborn Assessment Skill: Master the technique for palpating the testes in the newborn, ensuring a warm environment and gentle approach.
  • Parental Support: Provide empathetic support and clear, concise education to parents regarding the condition, its implications, and the treatment plan.
  • Post-Operative Care: Focus on pain assessment and management, wound care, monitoring for complications (bleeding, infection), and ensuring adequate hydration and nutrition.
  • Discharge Teaching: Reinforce home care instructions, including medication administration, activity restrictions, signs of complications, and follow-up appointment schedules.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: Q53.1 (Unilateral undescended testis), Q53.2 (Bilateral undescended testes), Q53.9 (Undescended testis, unspecified).
  • Surgical Procedure: Orchiopexy (surgical repositioning and fixation of the testis into the scrotum).
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Primarily clinical physical examination. Imaging (e.g., ultrasound, MRI) may be used for non-palpable testes but is often not definitive.
  • Associated Syndromes: Prader-Willi syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome, Noonan syndrome, VACTERL association.
  • Hormonal Therapy: Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG) historically used, but its efficacy is limited and not widely recommended as primary treatment.
