# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Purpose of Crutches:** Crutches are used to help a child walk by taking weight off an injured leg or foot, allowing it to heal properly.
* **Proper Fitting is Key:** Incorrectly fitted crutches can cause discomfort, skin irritation, or even nerve damage. Always ensure they are adjusted to the child's height.
* **Adult Supervision:** Young children require constant supervision when using crutches to prevent falls and ensure correct technique.
* **Gradual Introduction:** Introduce crutch use in a safe, open space with clear pathways before navigating more challenging environments.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Pain with Weight-Bearing:** Significant pain in the leg, ankle, or foot when attempting to stand or walk.
* **Swelling and Bruising:** Visible swelling, bruising, or deformity following an injury such as a sprain, fracture, or muscle strain.
* **Inability to Bear Weight:** Complete inability or extreme difficulty for the child to put any weight on the affected limb.
* **Limping or Gait Changes:** A noticeable change in the child's walking pattern, indicating discomfort or instability.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Clear Pathways:** Ensure all walking areas at home and school are free from rugs, toys, and other obstacles to prevent tripping.
* **Non-Slip Footwear:** The child should always wear well-fitting, non-slip shoes (e.g., sneakers) to maintain traction.
* **Stair Safety:** Avoid stairs initially. If unavoidable, teach the 'up with the good, down with the bad' technique with adult assistance.
* **Crutch Tip Inspection:** Regularly check the rubber tips on the bottom of the crutches for wear and tear; replace them if they are worn smooth.
* **Axillary Padding:** Ensure the crutch pads under the arms are not pressing directly into the armpits, which can cause nerve damage (crutch palsy). Weight should be borne through the hands.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Hydration for Healing:** Encourage ample fluid intake to support overall healing and prevent constipation, which can be common with reduced mobility and certain pain medications.
* **Nutrient-Rich Diet:** Provide a diet rich in calcium, Vitamin D, and protein to support bone repair and tissue regeneration.
* **Fiber Intake:** Increase dietary fiber through fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to maintain regular bowel movements due to decreased activity.
* **Avoid Excessive Sugars:** Limit highly processed foods and sugary drinks, which can contribute to inflammation and hinder the healing process.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Supervise Technique:** Continuously observe the child's crutch technique to ensure they are using them correctly and safely.
* **Assist with Stairs and Curbs:** Provide direct physical assistance when the child needs to navigate stairs, curbs, or uneven surfaces.
* **Monitor for Skin Irritation:** Regularly check the child's armpits and hands for redness, blistering, or skin breakdown from crutch use.
* **Reinforce Weight-Bearing Status:** Consistently remind the child of their prescribed weight-bearing restrictions (e.g., non-weight bearing, touch-down weight bearing).
* **Encourage Breaks:** Advise the child to take frequent breaks to rest and prevent fatigue, especially during longer periods of mobility.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Adhere to Weight-Bearing Orders:** Strictly follow the prescribed weight-bearing status to ensure proper healing and prevent re-injury.
* **Pain Management:** Administer prescribed pain medication as directed and report any uncontrolled pain to the medical team.
* **Watch for Complications:** Be vigilant for signs of complications such as increased pain, swelling, numbness, tingling, or changes in skin color in the affected limb.
* **Follow-Up Appointments:** Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments for reassessment of the injury and crutch use.
* **Physical Therapy Referral:** A referral to physical therapy may be necessary to teach proper crutch gait, strengthen muscles, and regain full function.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Crutch Measurement & Fitting:** Crucial to measure from the axilla to a point 6 inches lateral to the foot, ensuring 2-3 finger widths between the axillary pad and the armpit. Handgrips should allow for a 20-30 degree elbow bend.
* **Gait Training:** Educate on various crutch gaits (e.g., 2-point, 3-point, 4-point, swing-through) based on the child's weight-bearing status and strength.
* **Skin Integrity Assessment:** Regularly assess skin in the axillary region, palms, and wrists for pressure areas, redness, or breakdown.
* **Pain Assessment & Management:** Utilize age-appropriate pain scales (e.g., Wong-Baker FACES) to assess pain and evaluate the effectiveness of interventions.
* **Patient & Family Education:** Provide clear, concise instructions on crutch use, safety, activity restrictions, and signs of complications to both the child and caregivers.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Types of Crutches:** Axillary crutches (underarm) are common for short-term use; forearm (Lofstrand) crutches offer more independence for long-term use.
* **Weight-Bearing Statuses:** Non-Weight Bearing (NWB), Touch-Down Weight Bearing (TDWB), Partial Weight Bearing (PWB), Weight Bearing As Tolerated (WBAT).
* **Crutch Palsy:** Neuropathy of the radial or ulnar nerve caused by prolonged pressure on the axilla from improperly fitted crutches.
* **Energy Expenditure:** Crutch walking significantly increases energy expenditure compared to normal ambulation, especially in children.
* **Rehabilitation Protocol:** Crutch use is often part of a broader rehabilitation protocol that includes physical therapy for strengthening, flexibility, and balance training.
