Acetaminophen poisoning

🏠 Home / Children's health / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for children

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Leading Cause: Acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose is a primary cause of acute liver failure in children, often due to accidental ingestion or dosing errors.
  • Dosing Confusion: Many cases arise from incorrect measurement, using multiple products containing acetaminophen, or misunderstanding different concentrations (e.g., infant drops vs. children's liquid).
  • Toxic Threshold: Even slightly exceeding the recommended dose can be harmful, especially in young children whose livers are still developing.
  • Delayed Onset: Initial symptoms can be mild or absent, leading to a dangerous delay in seeking medical attention and initiating treatment.
  • Specific Antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is the effective antidote, with its efficacy significantly higher when administered early after ingestion.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Early Stage (0-24 hours): Non-specific symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, pallor, lethargy, and anorexia may be present or entirely absent.
  • Intermediate Stage (24-72 hours): Signs of liver damage may emerge, including right upper quadrant abdominal pain, elevated liver enzymes (AST, ALT), and potentially jaundice or dark urine.
  • Late Stage (>72 hours): Severe cases can progress to hepatic encephalopathy (confusion, coma), coagulopathy (bleeding disorders), renal failure, and metabolic acidosis.
  • Fulminant Failure: In critical situations, multi-organ failure and death can occur if not promptly and effectively treated.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Precise Dosing: Always use the calibrated measuring device (syringe, dropper, or cup) provided with the medication; never use household spoons.
  • Ingredient Check: Carefully read all medication labels to ensure no other products being administered to the child contain acetaminophen.
  • Weight-Based Dosing: Administer doses strictly according to the child's current weight or age, as directed by a healthcare professional or product label.
  • Secure Storage: Keep all medications, especially acetaminophen, in a locked cabinet or out of reach and sight of children at all times.
  • Emergency Contacts: Have the Poison Control Center number (e.g., 1-800-222-1222) and emergency services readily accessible.
  • Avoid Double Dosing: Do not give an additional dose if a previous one was vomited without first consulting a medical professional.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • NPO Status: Children presenting with significant nausea, vomiting, or altered mental status due to poisoning may require NPO (nil per os) status to prevent aspiration, particularly if gastric decontamination is considered.
  • Intravenous Hydration: Aggressive intravenous fluid administration is often necessary to maintain hydration, support renal function, and manage electrolyte imbalances, especially with severe vomiting or developing liver injury.
  • Nutritional Support: In cases of severe liver damage or prolonged hospitalization, specialized nutritional support, such as parenteral nutrition, may be required to meet metabolic demands.
  • GI Rest: Following initial decontamination or during acute phases of liver injury, a period of gastrointestinal rest may be advised, with gradual reintroduction of clear fluids as tolerated.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Contact: If acetaminophen poisoning is suspected, call Poison Control or emergency services immediately, even if the child appears asymptomatic.
  • Do Not Induce Vomiting: Never attempt to induce vomiting without explicit instructions from a medical professional, as this can cause further harm.
  • Gather Information: Bring the medication bottle, any remaining pills, and any vomit to the emergency department for accurate assessment.
  • Time and Amount: Provide the most accurate possible information regarding the time of ingestion and the estimated amount of acetaminophen taken, as this is crucial for treatment decisions.
  • Continuous Monitoring: Closely observe the child for any changes in behavior, increased lethargy, persistent vomiting, or new abdominal pain.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Rapid Assessment: Prompt medical evaluation is paramount, including a detailed history of ingestion (time, amount, formulation, co-ingestants).
  • Serum Levels: Obtain serum acetaminophen levels and liver function tests (AST, ALT) to assess the severity and guide treatment using the Rumack-Matthew Nomogram.
  • N-acetylcysteine (NAC) Therapy: Initiate NAC administration as soon as possible, ideally within 8 hours of ingestion, to prevent or minimize hepatotoxicity.
  • Supportive Care: Provide comprehensive supportive care, including fluid and electrolyte management, antiemetics, and close monitoring of vital signs, coagulation, and neurological status.
  • Liver Transplant Evaluation: In cases of fulminant hepatic failure unresponsive to NAC, early consultation for liver transplantation is critical.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Parental Education: Educate parents/caregivers on safe medication storage practices, accurate dosing techniques, and the critical dangers of acetaminophen overdose.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Perform thorough medication reconciliation to identify all acetaminophen-containing products a child may be receiving, preventing inadvertent overdose.
  • Vigilant Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, intake and output, neurological status, and observe for any signs of liver failure (e.g., jaundice, bleeding, altered mental status).
  • NAC Administration Protocol: Understand and adhere to the proper administration protocols for N-acetylcysteine (oral or intravenous), including monitoring for potential adverse reactions like anaphylactoid responses.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide empathetic psychosocial support to families, particularly in situations involving intentional overdose or severe clinical outcomes.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Hepatotoxicity: Liver damage caused by toxic substances, a primary concern in acetaminophen poisoning.
  • N-acetylcysteine (NAC): The specific antidote for acetaminophen overdose, which replenishes hepatic glutathione stores.
  • Rumack-Matthew Nomogram: A widely used graph that plots serum acetaminophen concentration against time since ingestion to predict the risk of hepatotoxicity.
  • Glutathione: A critical endogenous antioxidant in the liver, which becomes depleted in acetaminophen overdose, leading to the accumulation of toxic metabolites.
  • NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine): The highly reactive and toxic metabolite of acetaminophen responsible for causing hepatocellular necrosis.
  • Activated Charcoal: A gastrointestinal decontaminant that may be considered if administered within 1-2 hours of ingestion, though its use is time-sensitive and often debated.
