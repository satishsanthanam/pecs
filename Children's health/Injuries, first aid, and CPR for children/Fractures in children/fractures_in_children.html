Fractures in children

🏠 Home / Children's health / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for children

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Unique Bone Characteristics: Children's bones are more flexible and porous than adult bones, leading to different fracture patterns like greenstick or torus fractures.
  • Growth Plate Involvement: Fractures involving the epiphyseal plates (growth plates) are critical as they can affect future bone growth and development.
  • Rapid Healing: Pediatric bones generally heal faster than adult bones due to higher metabolic activity and excellent remodeling potential.
  • Common Causes: Falls during play, sports injuries, and accidental trauma are frequent causes of fractures in children.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Pain: Intense pain at the site of injury, often worsening with movement or touch.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Visible swelling and discoloration (bruising) around the affected area.
  • Deformity: An unnatural angle, bend, or shortening of the limb, indicating a displaced fracture.
  • Inability to Use Limb: Refusal or inability to bear weight on a leg or move an arm, especially in younger children.
  • Guarding: The child may protect the injured area, resisting any attempt to move or touch it.
  • Crepitus: A grating or crackling sensation or sound when the injured area is moved (though manipulation should be avoided).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Immobilization: Stabilize the injured limb in the position found using a splint or sling to prevent further damage.
  • Avoid Manipulation: Do not attempt to straighten or realign the limb, as this can worsen the injury or cause nerve/vascular damage.
  • RICE Protocol (Initial): Apply Rest, Ice (wrapped), Compression (gentle), and Elevation to reduce swelling and pain while awaiting medical attention.
  • Seek Prompt Medical Care: Always consult a physician immediately for suspected fractures to ensure proper diagnosis and treatment.
  • Monitor for Neurovascular Compromise: Watch for signs of impaired circulation or nerve function, such as numbness, tingling, pallor, or coldness in the extremity distal to the injury.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Calcium Intake: Ensure a diet rich in calcium (dairy products, fortified plant milks, leafy greens) to support bone mineralization during healing.
  • Sufficient Vitamin D: Promote Vitamin D intake (fatty fish, fortified foods, sunlight exposure) as it is essential for calcium absorption and bone health.
  • Protein for Tissue Repair: Provide ample protein (lean meats, poultry, fish, beans, lentils) to support tissue repair and bone matrix formation.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration with water to support overall metabolic processes and nutrient transport for healing.
  • Avoid Excessive Sugars/Processed Foods: Limit foods that offer little nutritional value and may displace nutrient-dense options crucial for recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • First Aid Application: Apply basic first aid, including gentle immobilization and pain management, before transport to a medical facility.
  • Pain Management: Administer age-appropriate pain relief as directed by a healthcare professional, ensuring the child is comfortable.
  • Cast Care Education: Follow all instructions for cast care, including keeping it dry, checking for tightness, and monitoring for signs of infection or skin irritation.
  • Activity Restrictions: Adhere strictly to activity restrictions and weight-bearing limitations prescribed by the orthopedic specialist.
  • Watch for Complications: Be vigilant for signs of complications such as compartment syndrome (severe pain, pallor, paresthesia, pulselessness, paralysis), infection, or nerve damage.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Ensure all scheduled follow-up appointments with the orthopedic surgeon are attended for monitoring healing and cast changes.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: A thorough clinical examination and appropriate imaging (typically X-rays) are crucial for accurate diagnosis and classification of pediatric fractures.
  • Growth Plate Assessment: Special attention must be paid to fractures involving growth plates (Salter-Harris classifications) due to their potential impact on future growth.
  • Conservative vs. Surgical Management: Most pediatric fractures are managed conservatively with reduction and casting, but some complex or displaced fractures may require surgical intervention.
  • Pain Control: Effective pain management is paramount, utilizing age-appropriate analgesics and non-pharmacological methods.
  • Remodeling Potential: Children's bones have excellent remodeling potential, often allowing for correction of minor angulation over time, especially in younger children.
  • Long-term Follow-up: Regular follow-up is essential to monitor healing, ensure proper alignment, and address any potential growth disturbances.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Neurovascular Assessment (5 Ps): Routinely assess for Pain, Pallor, Paresthesia, Pulselessness, and Paralysis distal to the injury, especially with casts or splints.
  • Pain Assessment in Children: Utilize age-appropriate pain scales (e.g., FLACC, Wong-Baker FACES) to accurately assess and manage pain.
  • Cast Care Education: Educate parents/caregivers on proper cast care, including signs of complications (e.g., foul odor, fever, excessive swelling, numbness).
  • Developmental Considerations: Adapt care and education based on the child's developmental stage and ability to understand instructions.
  • Compartment Syndrome Recognition: Understand the early signs and symptoms of compartment syndrome and the urgency of reporting them.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide emotional support to the child and family, addressing anxieties related to injury, treatment, and recovery.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Fracture Types: Greenstick fracture, Torus (buckle) fracture, Salter-Harris fracture (growth plate), spiral fracture, transverse fracture, oblique fracture, comminuted fracture.
  • Imaging Modalities: Standard X-rays (AP and lateral views), sometimes oblique views; MRI for soft tissue or complex growth plate injuries; CT for complex articular fractures.
  • Treatment Principles: Reduction (closed or open), immobilization (casting, splinting, external fixation, internal fixation with pins/plates), rehabilitation.
  • Complications: Compartment syndrome, malunion, nonunion, growth arrest (physeal arrest), osteomyelitis, nerve or vascular injury.
  • Bone Healing Stages: Hematoma formation, fibrocartilaginous callus formation, bony callus formation, bone remodeling.
  • Pediatric Orthopedic Specialists: Referral to a pediatric orthopedic surgeon is often necessary for complex or growth plate fractures.
