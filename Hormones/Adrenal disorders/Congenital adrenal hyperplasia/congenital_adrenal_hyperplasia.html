Congenital adrenal hyperplasia

🏠 Home / Hormones / Adrenal disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Inherited Disorder: Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH) is a group of inherited genetic disorders affecting the adrenal glands' ability to produce essential hormones.
  • Enzyme Deficiency: The most common form, 21-hydroxylase deficiency, impairs the production of cortisol and aldosterone, leading to an overproduction of adrenal androgens.
  • Hormonal Imbalance: Results in a deficiency of stress hormones (cortisol) and salt-regulating hormones (aldosterone), alongside an excess of male hormones (androgens).
  • Clinical Spectrum: Ranges from severe 'classic' forms (salt-wasting, simple virilizing) presenting in infancy to milder 'non-classic' forms manifesting later in life.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Newborns (Classic CAH): Ambiguous genitalia in females, salt-wasting crisis (vomiting, dehydration, lethargy, poor feeding, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia), failure to thrive.
  • Children & Adolescents: Precocious puberty, rapid growth spurt followed by short adult stature, severe acne, hirsutism (excess hair growth) in females, irregular menses in females, enlarged clitoris.
  • Adults (Non-classic CAH): Infertility, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)-like symptoms, premature pubarche, acne, and hirsutism.
  • Adrenal Crisis: Severe fatigue, weakness, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, low blood pressure, hypoglycemia, and potential loss of consciousness.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Lifelong Medication Adherence: Strict and consistent adherence to prescribed glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement therapy is paramount; never abruptly discontinue medication.
  • Stress Dosing Protocol: Increase glucocorticoid dosage during periods of illness, fever, surgery, or significant physical/emotional stress to prevent an adrenal crisis, as advised by an endocrinologist.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Always carry an emergency injectable hydrocortisone kit and wear medical alert identification specifying CAH and emergency steroid requirements.
  • Regular Endocrine Monitoring: Undergo frequent blood tests (e.g., 17-hydroxyprogesterone, renin activity, electrolytes, adrenal androgens) and clinical evaluations to adjust medication and monitor for complications.
  • Salt-Wasting Crisis Prevention: Closely monitor infants and young children with salt-wasting CAH for signs of dehydration and electrolyte imbalance, especially during illness or hot weather.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Sodium Intake (Salt-Wasting CAH): Infants and young children with salt-wasting CAH may require supplemental sodium, particularly during illness, hot weather, or increased activity, under medical guidance.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain sufficient fluid intake, especially during illness, fever, or increased physical exertion, to prevent dehydration and support overall physiological function.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a generally healthy, balanced diet to support growth and development; no specific dietary restrictions are typically required beyond general wellness, unless advised for specific comorbidities.
  • Avoid Unproven Supplements: Exercise caution and consult with a healthcare provider before using any herbal remedies or dietary supplements that claim to influence adrenal function.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Recognize Adrenal Crisis: Be vigilant for symptoms of adrenal insufficiency (e.g., persistent vomiting, lethargy, severe weakness, abdominal pain, low blood pressure) and seek immediate emergency medical care.
  • Emergency Hydrocortisone Administration: Understand and be prepared to administer emergency injectable hydrocortisone as instructed by the medical team in crisis situations.
  • Medication Management: Ensure consistent and timely administration of daily medications (glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids) and be proficient in applying stress-dosing protocols.
  • Medical Alert Identification: Confirm the patient wears or carries medical alert identification clearly stating their CAH diagnosis and the need for emergency steroid treatment.
  • Growth and Development Monitoring: Regularly observe and report any concerns regarding the patient's growth, pubertal development, or psychological well-being to the healthcare provider.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis & Intervention: Crucial for preventing life-threatening adrenal crises in newborns and minimizing long-term complications associated with hormonal imbalances.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Optimal management requires a collaborative approach involving pediatric endocrinologists, geneticists, urologists, psychologists, and specialized nursing staff.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Therapy must be meticulously tailored to the specific type of CAH, patient age, and individual needs, with careful titration of hormone replacement to achieve biochemical control.
  • Genetic Counseling: Essential for affected individuals and their families to understand inheritance patterns, reproductive risks, and family planning options.
  • Long-Term Surveillance: Continuous monitoring for potential complications such as adrenal rest tumors, bone density issues, metabolic disturbances, and psychosocial challenges is vital throughout the patient's life.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient & Family Education: Provide comprehensive education on medication adherence, stress dosing, emergency protocols, and recognition of adrenal crisis symptoms.
  • Newborn Screening Interpretation: Understand the significance of elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone levels in newborn screening for early CAH detection and prompt referral.
  • Vital Sign & Electrolyte Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, particularly blood pressure, and electrolyte balance, especially during periods of illness or stress, to detect impending adrenal crisis.
  • Psychosocial Support: Recognize and address the psychosocial impact of CAH, including issues related to ambiguous genitalia, body image, and chronic illness management, providing appropriate support and referrals.
  • Medication Administration Competency: Demonstrate proficiency in administering prescribed glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement, including emergency injectable hydrocortisone.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Autosomal recessive disorder, most commonly due to a deficiency of the 21-hydroxylase enzyme (encoded by the CYP21A2 gene), leading to impaired cortisol and aldosterone synthesis and accumulation of adrenal androgen precursors.
  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Elevated serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) levels, often confirmed by an ACTH stimulation test, and genetic testing for CYP21A2 mutations.
  • Therapeutic Modalities: Glucocorticoid replacement (e.g., hydrocortisone, prednisone, dexamethasone) to suppress ACTH and reduce androgen production; mineralocorticoid replacement (fludrocortisone) for salt-wasting forms.
  • Key Monitoring Parameters: Serum 17-OHP, androstenedione, testosterone, plasma renin activity, electrolytes, blood pressure, growth velocity, and bone age.
  • Potential Complications: Adrenal crisis, short adult stature, precocious puberty, infertility, adrenal rest tumors, metabolic syndrome, and decreased bone mineral density.
