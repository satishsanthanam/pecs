Growth hormone treatment in adults

🏠 Home / Hormones / Growth hormone

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of Treatment: Growth hormone (GH) treatment in adults is primarily for replacement therapy in individuals with confirmed adult growth hormone deficiency (GHD).
  • Key Indications: GHD often results from pituitary disease, cranial irradiation, or can be a continuation of childhood-onset GHD.
  • Anticipated Benefits: Treatment aims to improve body composition (reducing fat mass, increasing lean body mass), enhance bone mineral density, normalize lipid profiles, boost exercise capacity, and improve overall quality of life.
  • Administration Method: Growth hormone is typically administered via daily subcutaneous injections, requiring patient education on proper technique.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Symptoms of GHD: Clinical indications prompting GH treatment include persistent fatigue, reduced energy levels, impaired concentration, increased visceral adiposity, decreased muscle strength, reduced bone mineral density, and dyslipidemia.
  • Potential Treatment Side Effects: Patients may experience peripheral edema, arthralgia (joint pain), myalgia (muscle pain), carpal tunnel syndrome, paresthesia (numbness/tingling), and headaches, especially during initial titration.
  • Glucose Metabolism Changes: Growth hormone can influence glucose metabolism, potentially leading to hyperglycemia or exacerbating pre-existing diabetes.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Absolute Contraindications: Growth hormone treatment is contraindicated in the presence of active malignancy, active proliferative or preproliferative diabetic retinopathy, and acute critical illness (e.g., following open-heart surgery, multiple trauma, or acute respiratory failure).
  • Essential Monitoring: Regular monitoring of Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1) levels, thyroid function, glucose metabolism (HbA1c, fasting glucose), and lipid profiles is crucial throughout therapy.
  • Dosage Titration: Initiate treatment with a low dose and titrate slowly based on IGF-1 levels and clinical response to minimize the risk and severity of side effects.
  • Diabetes Management: Patients with diabetes require close monitoring of blood glucose levels and potential adjustments to antidiabetic medications due to GH's impact on insulin sensitivity.
  • Intracranial Hypertension: Monitor for signs and symptoms of benign intracranial hypertension, such as severe headache, visual changes, or papilledema.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Glucose Management: A balanced diet with controlled carbohydrate intake is advisable, particularly for patients with pre-existing glucose intolerance or diabetes, given GH's potential impact on blood sugar.
  • Hydration Status: Maintain adequate hydration, especially if experiencing fluid retention or edema as a side effect of treatment.
  • Nutrient Support: Ensure sufficient protein intake to support muscle mass and bone health, which are key targets for improvement with GH therapy.
  • Sodium Intake: Monitor and potentially restrict sodium intake if fluid retention or peripheral edema becomes a significant concern during treatment.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education on proper subcutaneous injection technique, safe storage of medication, and recognition of potential side effects.
  • Adherence to Follow-ups: Emphasize the critical importance of attending all scheduled physician appointments for ongoing monitoring, dose adjustments, and assessment of treatment efficacy and safety.
  • Reporting Adverse Effects: Instruct patients to promptly report any new or worsening symptoms, particularly severe headaches, visual disturbances, significant swelling, or signs of allergic reaction.
  • Drug Interactions: Advise patients to inform all healthcare providers about their GH treatment and any other medications, as GH can interact with corticosteroids, insulin, and thyroid hormones.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Rigorous Diagnosis: A definitive diagnosis of adult GHD, typically confirmed by appropriate stimulation tests, is paramount before initiating growth hormone replacement therapy.
  • Individualized Therapy: Treatment protocols are highly individualized, necessitating careful dose titration and continuous assessment of clinical response and biochemical markers like IGF-1.
  • Long-term Commitment: Growth hormone replacement is generally a long-term therapy, requiring consistent patient adherence and ongoing medical supervision to achieve and maintain therapeutic benefits.
  • Risk-Benefit Analysis: Physicians must continually evaluate the benefits of improved body composition, bone density, and quality of life against the potential risks, side effects, and contraindications for each patient.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Injection Technique Training: Nurses are instrumental in teaching and reinforcing correct subcutaneous injection techniques, including site rotation, to ensure optimal absorption and minimize local reactions.
  • Adherence Counseling: Provide counseling on the importance of consistent medication adherence and discuss strategies to integrate daily injections into the patient's routine.
  • Side Effect Management: Educate patients on common side effects, such as fluid retention or joint pain, and offer practical advice for symptom management and when to seek medical attention.
  • Monitoring Parameters: Understand the significance of key laboratory parameters (IGF-1, glucose, thyroid function) and their role in guiding dose adjustments and assessing treatment safety and efficacy.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Mechanism of Action: Recombinant human growth hormone (somatropin) replaces deficient endogenous GH, stimulating the production of Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1), which mediates many of its anabolic and metabolic effects.
  • Pharmacokinetics: Administered subcutaneously, somatropin typically reaches peak serum concentrations within 3-6 hours, with an elimination half-life of approximately 2-3 hours.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Adult GHD is diagnosed based on clinical features combined with biochemical confirmation, often utilizing dynamic stimulation tests such as the insulin tolerance test (ITT), GHRH-arginine test, or glucagon stimulation test.
  • Therapeutic Goal: The primary therapeutic goal is to normalize IGF-1 levels to the age- and sex-adjusted reference range, avoiding supraphysiological concentrations to minimize adverse effects.
