Intrauterine devices (IUDs)

🏠 Home / Hormones / Birth control

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Intrauterine devices (IUDs) are small, T-shaped contraceptive devices inserted into the uterus to prevent pregnancy.
  • Types: They are broadly categorized into hormonal IUDs (releasing progestin) and non-hormonal copper IUDs.
  • Efficacy: IUDs are highly effective, with a failure rate of less than 1% per year, making them one of the most reliable forms of reversible contraception.
  • Duration: Depending on the type, IUDs can provide effective contraception for 3 to 10 years, or even longer for some copper IUDs.
  • Mechanism of Action (Hormonal): Hormonal IUDs thicken cervical mucus, thin the uterine lining, and may suppress ovulation in some users.
  • Mechanism of Action (Copper): Copper IUDs create an inflammatory reaction in the uterus that is toxic to sperm and eggs, preventing fertilization.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Indications for IUD: Desire for long-acting reversible contraception (LARC), management of heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) with hormonal IUDs, or contraception without systemic hormones (copper IUD).
  • Common Post-Insertion: Mild to moderate cramping and spotting are common for a few days to weeks after insertion.
  • Menstrual Changes (Hormonal IUD): Many users experience lighter, shorter periods or amenorrhea (absence of periods) over time. Irregular spotting may occur initially.
  • Menstrual Changes (Copper IUD): Periods may become heavier, longer, and more painful, especially in the first few months after insertion.
  • Symptoms of Concern: Severe abdominal pain, fever, unusual or foul-smelling vaginal discharge, heavy bleeding with clots, or feeling the IUD string is longer or shorter than usual.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Contraindications: IUDs are contraindicated in cases of active pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), current pregnancy, unexplained vaginal bleeding, certain uterine abnormalities, or known pelvic infection.
  • STI Risk: IUDs do not protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Consistent condom use is recommended for STI prevention.
  • Insertion Risks: Rare but serious risks include uterine perforation during insertion (0.1-0.2% incidence) and expulsion of the device.
  • Pre-Insertion Screening: Screening for STIs (e.g., Chlamydia, Gonorrhea) may be recommended prior to insertion, especially in individuals at higher risk.
  • Post-Insertion Monitoring: Patients should be advised on how to check for IUD strings and to report any signs of expulsion or complications promptly.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Insertion Comfort: Consider a light meal before the procedure to help prevent lightheadedness or nausea, especially if you are prone to vasovagal responses.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration before and after the procedure for general well-being.
  • Pain Management: If over-the-counter pain relievers (e.g., ibuprofen) are used for post-insertion cramping, ensure they are taken with food to minimize gastrointestinal upset.
  • Alcohol Consumption: Avoid alcohol consumption if you are taking prescribed pain medication, as it can interact and increase side effects.
  • No Direct Restrictions: There are no specific dietary restrictions that directly impact the efficacy or safety of an IUD once it is in place.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • PAINS Acronym: Be aware of the warning signs: Period late, abnormal spotting or bleeding; Abdominal pain, pain with intercourse; Infection exposure, abnormal discharge; Not feeling well, fever, chills; String missing, shorter or longer.
  • String Checks: Regularly check for the presence and length of the IUD strings, typically after each menstrual period, to ensure the device is in place.
  • Seek Medical Attention: Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience severe pain, fever, heavy bleeding, unusual discharge, or suspect pregnancy.
  • Follow-up Appointment: A follow-up visit is usually recommended 4-6 weeks after insertion to confirm proper placement and address any concerns.
  • Emergency Contraception: A copper IUD can be inserted as emergency contraception within 5 days of unprotected intercourse.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Shared Decision-Making: Discuss all contraceptive options thoroughly with patients, considering their medical history, lifestyle, and reproductive goals.
  • Counseling on Side Effects: Provide clear expectations regarding potential side effects, particularly changes in menstrual bleeding patterns, which are common and vary by IUD type.
  • Risk-Benefit Analysis: Emphasize the high efficacy and long-term benefits of IUDs while also discussing rare but serious risks such as perforation or infection.
  • STI Prevention: Reinforce that IUDs do not protect against STIs and counsel on safe sex practices or dual method use if appropriate.
  • Patient Education: Ensure patients understand how to check their IUD strings and when to seek urgent medical care for potential complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients on IUD types, mechanisms, expected side effects, and signs of complications (PAINS acronym).
  • Pre-Procedure Support: Assist with patient preparation, including pain management options (e.g., NSAIDs prior to insertion), and emotional support.
  • Post-Insertion Care: Monitor for immediate post-insertion complications (e.g., vasovagal reaction), provide post-procedure instructions, and schedule follow-up.
  • String Check Instruction: Teach patients how to correctly perform self-checks for IUD strings and what to do if strings are not felt or seem altered.
  • Documentation: Accurately document IUD type, lot number, insertion date, and any patient education provided.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Levonorgestrel-Releasing IUDs (LNG-IUDs): Release varying doses of levonorgestrel (e.g., Mirena, Liletta, Kyleena, Skyla), primarily acting locally on the endometrium and cervical mucus.
  • Copper IUD (ParaGard): Contains a copper wire that elicits a sterile inflammatory reaction in the uterus, creating a spermicidal environment.
  • Pearl Index: Efficacy rates are typically <1.0 for both hormonal and copper IUDs, indicating high effectiveness in preventing pregnancy.
  • Contraindications (Detailed): Pregnancy, active pelvic infection (PID, cervicitis), uterine anomalies distorting the cavity, certain gynecologic cancers, Wilson's disease (for copper IUD), or severe liver disease (for LNG-IUDs).
  • Complications: Uterine perforation, expulsion, pelvic inflammatory disease (primarily if STIs are present at insertion), ectopic pregnancy (if pregnancy occurs), and changes in bleeding patterns.
  • Mechanism of Action (Cellular): Copper ions enhance local prostaglandin production and increase leukocyte infiltration, creating a cytotoxic environment for gametes. Levonorgestrel causes decidualization of the endometrium, glandular atrophy, and thickening of cervical mucus.
