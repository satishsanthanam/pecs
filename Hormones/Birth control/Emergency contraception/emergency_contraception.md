# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Purpose:** Emergency contraception (EC) is used to prevent pregnancy after unprotected sexual intercourse or contraceptive failure.
* **Time Sensitivity:** EC pills are most effective when taken as soon as possible, typically within 72 hours (3 days) for levonorgestrel and up to 120 hours (5 days) for ulipristal acetate.
* **Mechanism:** EC primarily works by delaying or inhibiting ovulation, preventing fertilization. It does not terminate an existing pregnancy.
* **Types:** Available forms include levonorgestrel-only pills (e.g., Plan B One-Step), ulipristal acetate pills (e.g., Ella), and the copper intrauterine device (IUD), which can be inserted up to 5 days after unprotected sex.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Indications for Use:** Unprotected sexual intercourse, condom breakage or slippage, missed oral contraceptive pills, diaphragm/cervical cap displacement, or sexual assault.
* **Potential Side Effects (Pill):** Nausea, vomiting, fatigue, headache, dizziness, breast tenderness, and abdominal pain are common but usually mild and temporary.
* **Menstrual Changes:** EC can cause temporary changes to the menstrual cycle, such as an earlier or later period, or spotting.
* **IUD Insertion Discomfort:** Temporary cramping or discomfort may occur during and immediately after copper IUD insertion.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Not Routine Contraception:** EC is not intended for regular birth control due to higher hormone doses and potential for less efficacy than ongoing methods.
* **STI Protection:** EC does not protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Consistent barrier methods are essential for STI prevention.
* **Contraindications (Ulipristal Acetate):** Should not be used if already pregnant or if there is a known allergy to the components. Caution with severe asthma.
* **Drug Interactions:** Certain medications (e.g., enzyme-inducing anticonvulsants, St. John's Wort, some antiretrovirals) can reduce the effectiveness of EC pills.
* **Ectopic Pregnancy:** If a pregnancy occurs despite EC use, there is a slightly increased risk of ectopic pregnancy. Seek medical attention for severe abdominal pain.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Administration:** EC pills can be taken with or without food. Taking them with a meal or snack may help reduce nausea.
* **Vomiting Protocol:** If vomiting occurs within 2-3 hours of taking an EC pill, contact a healthcare provider as another dose may be needed.
* **Hydration:** Maintain adequate hydration, especially if experiencing nausea or vomiting after taking the pill.
* **No Specific Food Restrictions:** There are generally no specific food or drink restrictions directly related to the efficacy of EC pills, beyond general medication guidelines.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Prompt Action:** Advise patients to seek medical advice or obtain EC as soon as possible after unprotected intercourse to maximize effectiveness.
* **Pregnancy Test:** Recommend a pregnancy test if the next menstrual period is delayed by more than a week or is unusually light.
* **Ongoing Contraception:** Counsel patients on initiating or resuming a regular, effective method of contraception immediately after EC use.
* **STI Screening:** Encourage STI screening, especially if there was a risk of exposure during the unprotected sexual encounter.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Timely Access:** Emphasize the critical importance of timely access to EC, as efficacy decreases with delay.
* **Patient Counseling:** Provide comprehensive counseling on all available EC options, their mechanisms, efficacy rates, and potential side effects, allowing for informed patient choice.
* **Distinction from Abortion:** Clearly explain that EC prevents pregnancy and is distinct from medical abortion.
* **Holistic Care:** Integrate EC discussions within broader sexual and reproductive health counseling, including STI prevention and long-term contraception planning.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pharmacology Review:** Understand the distinct pharmacological profiles of levonorgestrel (progestin-only) and ulipristal acetate (selective progesterone receptor modulator).
* **Patient Education:** Educate patients on correct administration, potential side effects, what to do if vomiting occurs, and the importance of follow-up.
* **Accessibility & Dispensing:** Be aware of local regulations regarding over-the-counter access for levonorgestrel EC and prescription requirements for ulipristal acetate.
* **Sensitive Communication:** Approach discussions about EC with empathy, non-judgment, and cultural sensitivity, especially in cases of sexual assault.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Efficacy Data:** Levonorgestrel EC is approximately 75-89% effective, while ulipristal acetate is generally more effective, especially when taken later (up to 120 hours). Copper IUD is >99% effective.
* **Mechanism of Action:** Levonorgestrel primarily inhibits or delays ovulation. Ulipristal acetate also delays ovulation, even when LH surge has begun, and may alter endometrial receptivity.
* **BMI Impact:** Studies suggest reduced efficacy of levonorgestrel EC in individuals with higher body mass index (BMI >25-30 kg/m²), with ulipristal acetate potentially being more effective in this population.
* **Contraindications:** Absolute contraindication for all hormonal EC is known pregnancy. Ulipristal acetate is contraindicated in severe uncontrolled asthma.
