Adrenal cancer

🏠 Home / Hormones / Adrenal cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Rare Malignancy: Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) is a rare but aggressive cancer originating in the outer layer (cortex) of the adrenal glands.
  • Hormonal Activity: ACC can be 'functional' (producing excess hormones like cortisol, aldosterone, androgens, or estrogens) or 'non-functional' (not producing hormones).
  • Clinical Presentation: Often presents with symptoms related to hormone overproduction or due to the mass effect of a growing tumor.
  • Prognosis Factors: Prognosis is highly dependent on the stage at diagnosis, tumor size, and completeness of surgical resection.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Cushing's Syndrome Features: Weight gain, central obesity, 'moon face', 'buffalo hump', purple striae, hypertension, diabetes, muscle weakness.
  • Virilization/Feminization: In females, hirsutism, deepening voice, clitoromegaly; in males, gynecomastia, testicular atrophy.
  • Conn's Syndrome Features: Hypertension, hypokalemia, muscle weakness, fatigue, polyuria.
  • Mass Effect Symptoms: Abdominal pain or fullness, palpable abdominal mass, back pain, early satiety.
  • Non-specific Symptoms: Unexplained weight loss, fatigue, fever, loss of appetite.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Prompt Evaluation of Adrenal Masses: Any newly discovered adrenal mass (incidentaloma) requires thorough biochemical and imaging evaluation to rule out malignancy and hormonal activity.
  • Pre-operative Hormonal Control: For functional tumors, pre-operative management to normalize hormone levels (e.g., with alpha-blockers for pheochromocytoma, or steroid synthesis inhibitors for Cushing's) is crucial to prevent perioperative complications.
  • Adrenal Insufficiency Risk: Patients undergoing unilateral adrenalectomy for a functional tumor may experience temporary adrenal insufficiency in the remaining gland, requiring perioperative and potentially long-term steroid replacement.
  • Surgical Expertise: Adrenalectomy for ACC requires an experienced surgical team due to the potential for local invasion and the need for complete tumor removal.
  • Genetic Screening: Consider genetic counseling and testing for patients with a family history of ACC or associated syndromes (e.g., Li-Fraumeni syndrome, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome, MEN1).

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines for several hours prior to planned surgery to prevent aspiration.
  • Post-operative Fluid & Food Reintroduction: Gradual reintroduction of clear liquids, then soft foods, progressing to a regular diet as tolerated post-surgery, monitoring for nausea or vomiting.
  • Hormone-Related Dietary Adjustments: If experiencing hypertension due to hormone excess, a low-sodium diet may be recommended; for diabetes, carbohydrate-controlled meals are important.
  • Nutritional Support During Treatment: Maintain adequate caloric and protein intake, especially during chemotherapy or radiation, to support recovery and minimize treatment side effects.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Steroid Replacement Education: Thoroughly educate patients on the importance of strict adherence to steroid replacement therapy post-adrenalectomy, including stress-dosing protocols.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Instruct patients and caregivers to monitor for signs of adrenal crisis (severe fatigue, weakness, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain) or recurrence.
  • Medication Side Effects: Provide detailed information on potential side effects of adjuvant therapies like mitotane, including gastrointestinal upset, neurological symptoms, and adrenal insufficiency.
  • Psychosocial Support: Offer resources for psychological support and counseling, as a cancer diagnosis and chronic hormone management can be emotionally challenging.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Multidisciplinary Management: Optimal care for ACC requires a collaborative approach involving endocrinologists, endocrine surgeons, medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, and pathologists.
  • Diagnostic Challenges: Differentiating benign adrenal adenomas from ACC can be complex, often requiring a combination of imaging characteristics, hormonal workup, and sometimes biopsy (though fine-needle aspiration is generally discouraged due to seeding risk).
  • Primary Treatment: Complete surgical resection is the cornerstone of treatment for localized ACC, aiming for R0 (no residual tumor) margins.
  • Adjuvant Therapy: Adjuvant mitotane is often recommended post-surgery, especially for higher-risk or advanced disease, due to its adrenolytic and anti-tumor effects.
  • Surveillance: Lifelong surveillance with regular imaging and biochemical monitoring is crucial due to the high risk of recurrence.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Post-operative Assessment: Closely monitor vital signs, fluid balance, pain levels, and surgical site for signs of hemorrhage or infection.
  • Hormone Replacement Management: Understand the principles of corticosteroid replacement, including appropriate dosing and timing, and educate patients on self-administration.
  • Mitotane Administration & Monitoring: Be aware of mitotane's narrow therapeutic index and significant side effects, requiring careful patient education and monitoring for toxicity.
  • Patient Education on Adrenal Crisis: Teach patients and families how to recognize symptoms of adrenal crisis and the importance of carrying emergency hydrocortisone.
  • Emotional Support: Provide empathetic care and connect patients with support groups or counseling services to help manage the psychological impact of cancer and chronic illness.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathology: Weiss score (histological criteria for malignancy), Ki-67 proliferative index.
  • Staging: TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) staging system for adrenocortical carcinoma.
  • Biochemical Markers: Serum cortisol, DHEA-S, 17-hydroxyprogesterone, aldosterone, renin, urinary free cortisol, fractionated metanephrines (to rule out pheochromocytoma).
  • Imaging Modalities: Contrast-enhanced CT or MRI of the abdomen/pelvis, PET-CT for staging and recurrence detection.
  • Pharmacology: Mitotane (adrenolytic agent), chemotherapy regimens (e.g., EDP-M: etoposide, doxorubicin, cisplatin, mitotane), steroid replacement (hydrocortisone, fludrocortisone).
  • Genetic Syndromes: Li-Fraumeni syndrome, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome, Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 1 (MEN1).
