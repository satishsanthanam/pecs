# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Advance directives are legal documents that allow individuals to make decisions about their medical care in advance, should they become unable to communicate their wishes.
* **Purpose in Cancer Care:** Essential for cancer patients, especially those with advanced or metastatic disease, to ensure their end-of-life preferences are honored.
* **Key Documents:** Primarily include a Living Will (specifies medical treatments to accept or refuse) and a Durable Power of Attorney for Healthcare (appoints a healthcare agent to make decisions).
* **Timeliness:** Best discussed and completed early in the disease trajectory, while the patient has full decision-making capacity, and reviewed periodically as the condition evolves.
* **Empowerment:** Provides patients with a sense of control over their future medical care and reduces the burden of decision-making on family members during a crisis.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Progressive Disease Trajectory:** Worsening cancer symptoms such as intractable pain, severe fatigue, cachexia, or organ dysfunction indicating advanced, incurable disease.
* **Declining Functional Status:** Significant decrease in performance status (e.g., ECOG, Karnofsky scores), increasing dependence for activities of daily living.
* **Cognitive Impairment Risk:** Potential for future delirium, encephalopathy, or other cognitive deficits due to disease progression, brain metastases, or treatment side effects.
* **Prognostic Discussions:** When physicians initiate conversations about limited life expectancy or transition to palliative/hospice care.
* **Patient/Family Distress:** Anxiety or uncertainty expressed by the patient or family regarding future medical interventions and quality of life.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Legal Validity:** Ensure documents are properly witnessed and/or notarized according to state-specific laws to be legally binding.
* **Regular Review:** Revisit and update advance directives periodically, especially after significant changes in health status, diagnosis, or personal values.
* **Open Communication:** Share copies of completed directives with your healthcare agent, family members, primary care physician, and all relevant medical providers.
* **Accessibility:** Ensure the documents are readily available in your medical record and accessible to the healthcare team in an emergency.
* **Avoid Coercion:** Discussions about advance directives must occur in an environment free from pressure, ensuring the patient's decisions are truly autonomous.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Artificial Nutrition & Hydration:** Advance directives can specify preferences regarding the initiation or withdrawal of feeding tubes (e.g., PEG, NG) or intravenous fluids when unable to eat or drink.
* **Comfort Feeding:** Clarify that declining artificial nutrition does not preclude offering comfort feeding, such as small sips of preferred liquids or favorite foods, if the patient can safely swallow and desires them.
* **Swallowing Difficulties:** Directives may address interventions for dysphagia, such as texture-modified diets or speech therapy evaluations, and when to transition to comfort measures only.
* **End-of-Life Hydration:** Patients can specify wishes regarding IV hydration in the final stages of life, balancing potential benefits with risks like fluid overload.
* **Oral Care:** Emphasize the importance of meticulous oral hygiene, regardless of nutritional intake, for comfort and prevention of complications.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Respect Patient Autonomy:** Understand and honor the patient's stated wishes as outlined in their advance directives, even if they differ from your own preferences.
* **Advocate for Wishes:** Be prepared to communicate the patient's decisions to the medical team, especially if the patient is unable to speak for themselves.
* **Seek Clarity:** If there are ambiguities in the directive or new situations arise, engage in open dialogue with the healthcare team to interpret the patient's likely intent.
* **Emotional Support:** Recognize the emotional toll of these decisions; seek support for yourself and other family members from social workers, chaplains, or support groups.
* **Documentation:** Ensure that the healthcare team has access to the most current version of the patient's advance directives.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Proactive Discussion:** Initiate conversations about advance directives early in the disease course, integrating them into routine care planning for cancer patients.
* **Clarify Prognosis:** Provide clear, compassionate, and realistic information about the patient's prognosis and the likely trajectory of their illness to inform decision-making.
* **Explain Medical Options:** Detail the benefits and burdens of various life-sustaining treatments, ensuring the patient understands the implications of their choices.
* **Document Thoroughly:** Ensure that advance directives are scanned into the electronic health record and that discussions about them are clearly documented.
* **Palliative Care Integration:** Recommend palliative care consultations to facilitate these complex discussions and provide comprehensive symptom management and support.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Facilitator Role:** Nurses are often at the forefront of identifying patients who could benefit from advance directive discussions and initiating these conversations.
* **Patient Education:** Educate patients and families about the types of advance directives, their purpose, and the process for completion.
* **Advocacy:** Serve as patient advocates, ensuring that the patient's wishes, as expressed in their advance directives, are respected and implemented by the healthcare team.
* **Ethical Considerations:** Understand the ethical principles (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence) underpinning advance directives and navigate potential conflicts.
* **Resource Provision:** Provide patients with access to appropriate forms, legal resources, and support services for completing and understanding advance directives.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Living Will:** A legal document outlining specific medical treatments a person does or does not want in end-of-life situations.
* **Durable Power of Attorney for Healthcare (DPOAHC):** Designates a healthcare agent to make medical decisions if the patient loses capacity.
* **Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST/MOLST):** A medical order that translates patient preferences into actionable physician orders, often for those with serious illness.
* **Patient Self-Determination Act (PSDA):** Federal law requiring healthcare institutions to inform patients of their right to make decisions about their medical care, including advance directives.
* **Decision-Making Capacity:** The ability of a patient to understand information, appreciate consequences, reason, and communicate a choice regarding their medical care.
