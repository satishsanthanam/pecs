Multiple myeloma

🏠 Home / Cancer / Multiple myeloma

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Plasma Cell Cancer: Multiple myeloma is a cancer of plasma cells, a type of white blood cell found in the bone marrow.
  • Abnormal Protein Production: Malignant plasma cells produce abnormal antibodies (M-protein) that can accumulate in the blood and urine.
  • Systemic Impact: The disease primarily affects bones, kidneys, and the immune system, leading to bone destruction, kidney impairment, anemia, and increased susceptibility to infections.
  • Variable Progression: While often slow-growing, multiple myeloma can be aggressive, requiring individualized and ongoing treatment strategies.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Bone Pain & Fractures: Persistent pain, especially in the back or ribs, and pathological fractures due to bone lesions.
  • Fatigue & Weakness: Often a result of anemia (low red blood cell count) or kidney dysfunction.
  • Kidney Problems: Symptoms like swelling in legs, shortness of breath, or changes in urination due to impaired kidney function.
  • Recurrent Infections: Increased frequency of bacterial or viral infections due to a weakened immune system.
  • Hypercalcemia Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, constipation, excessive thirst, confusion, or muscle weakness due to high calcium levels in the blood.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Bone Health Management: Avoid high-impact activities and heavy lifting to minimize the risk of pathological fractures. Utilize prescribed bone-strengthening agents.
  • Infection Prevention: Practice meticulous hand hygiene, avoid crowded places, and report any signs of infection (fever, chills) immediately to your healthcare provider.
  • Kidney Function Monitoring: Maintain adequate hydration and avoid nephrotoxic medications unless specifically approved by your physician. Regular kidney function tests are vital.
  • Medication Adherence: Strictly follow the prescribed treatment regimen, including chemotherapy, immunomodulatory drugs, and supportive medications, to optimize outcomes and manage side effects.
  • Fall Risk Assessment: Be aware of increased fall risk due to bone fragility, neuropathy, or treatment-related fatigue. Ensure a safe home environment.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration Focus: Maintain consistent fluid intake, especially water, to support kidney function and help manage hypercalcemia.
  • Nutrient-Dense Foods: Prioritize a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains to support overall health and immune function.
  • Calcium & Vitamin D: Ensure adequate intake of calcium and Vitamin D, often through supplements, to support bone health as advised by your physician.
  • Managing Treatment Side Effects: Adapt diet to manage common side effects like nausea (small, frequent meals), constipation (fiber-rich foods, fluids), or diarrhea (BRAT diet, avoid spicy/fatty foods).
  • Food Safety: If immunocompromised from treatment, avoid raw or undercooked meats, fish, eggs, and unpasteurized dairy products to prevent foodborne illness.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Symptom Reporting: Immediately report any new or worsening bone pain, fever, unusual bleeding/bruising, or changes in mental status to the medical team.
  • Medication Schedule Adherence: Ensure all medications, including oral chemotherapies and supportive care drugs, are taken precisely as prescribed.
  • Hydration Status Monitoring: Observe for signs of dehydration or fluid overload and encourage consistent fluid intake as directed.
  • Infection Surveillance: Be vigilant for any signs of infection, such as fever, chills, redness, or swelling, and seek immediate medical attention.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Have a clear understanding of when to seek emergency care and keep all relevant medical contacts readily accessible.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Detection Challenges: Multiple myeloma often presents with non-specific symptoms, making early diagnosis challenging but crucial for improved prognosis.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Therapy is highly personalized, considering disease stage, risk stratification, patient age, comorbidities, and prior treatments.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Optimal management involves a team approach, including hematologist-oncologists, radiation oncologists, nephrologists, and orthopedic specialists.
  • Continuous Monitoring: Regular blood tests, urine tests, imaging, and bone marrow assessments are essential to monitor disease activity and treatment response.
  • Supportive Care Integration: Comprehensive care includes managing pain, preventing bone complications, addressing renal dysfunction, and providing infection prophylaxis.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by clonal proliferation of malignant plasma cells in the bone marrow, leading to monoclonal immunoglobulin production and end-organ damage.
  • CRAB Criteria: Understand the diagnostic and clinical significance of HyperCalcemia, Renal insufficiency, Anemia, and Bone lesions.
  • Nursing Interventions: Focus on pain assessment and management, infection control protocols, hydration status monitoring, fall prevention strategies, and comprehensive patient education on treatment side effects and self-care.
  • Diagnostic Workup: Familiarity with serum/urine protein electrophoresis (SPEP/UPEP), immunofixation, free light chain assays, bone marrow biopsy, and skeletal surveys.
  • Treatment Modalities: Knowledge of common drug classes (proteasome inhibitors, IMiDs, monoclonal antibodies), high-dose chemotherapy with autologous stem cell transplant, and radiation therapy.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Code: C90.00 (Multiple myeloma, not having achieved remission).
  • Key Biomarkers: M-protein (monoclonal protein), serum free light chain ratio (kappa/lambda), beta-2 microglobulin, albumin, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH).
  • Staging Systems: Revised International Staging System (R-ISS) utilizing serum albumin, beta-2 microglobulin, LDH, and cytogenetic abnormalities.
  • Common Therapies: Bortezomib, Lenalidomide, Dexamethasone (VRd regimen), Carfilzomib, Pomalidomide, Daratumumab, Isatuximab.
  • Major Complications: Pathological fractures, hypercalcemic crisis, acute kidney injury, spinal cord compression, recurrent infections, amyloidosis.
