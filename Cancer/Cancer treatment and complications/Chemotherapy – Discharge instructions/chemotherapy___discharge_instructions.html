Chemotherapy – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Cancer / Cancer treatment and complications

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Follow-Up Appointments: Ensure you attend all scheduled follow-up visits and laboratory tests to monitor your recovery and treatment response.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including antiemetics, pain relievers, and growth factors, exactly as directed by your healthcare team.
  • Side Effect Management: Understand common chemotherapy side effects and strategies for managing them at home. Report any severe or worsening symptoms promptly.
  • Infection Risk: Your immune system may be weakened, increasing your risk of infection. Practice strict hygiene and avoid exposure to sick individuals.
  • Chemotherapy Waste: Handle bodily fluids (urine, stool, vomit) and any items contaminated with them carefully for 48-72 hours post-treatment, as they may contain chemotherapy drugs.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Fever: A temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher is an emergency. Contact your healthcare provider immediately.
  • Uncontrolled Nausea/Vomiting: Persistent nausea or vomiting despite taking antiemetic medications, leading to dehydration or inability to keep food down.
  • Severe Fatigue: Extreme tiredness that interferes with daily activities and does not improve with rest.
  • Bleeding or Bruising: Unusual bleeding from gums, nosebleeds, blood in urine/stool, or excessive bruising without injury.
  • Signs of Infection: Redness, swelling, pus, or warmth at any catheter site, new cough, sore throat, or burning with urination.
  • Significant Pain: New or worsening pain that is not relieved by prescribed pain medication.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Infection Prevention: Wash hands frequently with soap and water or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer. Avoid crowded places and contact with people who are ill.
  • Oral Hygiene: Use a soft toothbrush and rinse your mouth frequently with a non-alcoholic mouthwash to prevent mucositis and infection.
  • Skin Care: Keep skin clean and moisturized. Protect skin from sun exposure, especially if photosensitivity is a side effect of your chemotherapy.
  • Avoid Injury: Use an electric shaver instead of a razor. Be careful with sharp objects to prevent cuts and bleeding.
  • Medication Interactions: Inform your healthcare provider about all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies you are taking to avoid potential interactions.
  • Safe Handling of Chemotherapy Waste: Use gloves when handling bodily fluids for 48-72 hours post-treatment. Flush toilets twice after use. Wash linens separately if soiled.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths, diluted juices) throughout the day to prevent dehydration and help flush toxins.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eat small, bland meals or snacks every 2-3 hours to manage nausea and maintain energy levels.
  • Food Safety: Avoid raw or undercooked meats, fish, eggs, and unpasteurized dairy products. Wash fruits and vegetables thoroughly.
  • Managing Taste Changes: Experiment with different foods and seasonings. Use plastic utensils if you experience a metallic taste.
  • Fiber Intake: Increase fiber intake gradually with whole grains, fruits, and vegetables to manage constipation, a common side effect.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit spicy, acidic, or very hot/cold foods if you have mouth sores or gastrointestinal irritation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Know the emergency contact numbers for the healthcare team and be prepared to call if the patient exhibits concerning symptoms.
  • Medication Assistance: Assist the patient with medication schedules and administration, ensuring correct dosages and times.
  • Observation for Side Effects: Monitor the patient closely for signs of infection, severe nausea, bleeding, or changes in mental status.
  • Chemotherapy Waste Handling: Wear gloves when assisting with personal care or cleaning up bodily fluids for 48-72 hours after treatment. Dispose of waste according to hospital instructions.
  • Emotional Support: Provide emotional support and encourage rest. Recognize that fatigue and mood changes are common.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Open Communication: Maintain open and honest communication with your oncology team. Report all symptoms and concerns, no matter how minor they seem.
  • Adherence to Plan: Strict adherence to your prescribed medication regimen and follow-up schedule is crucial for optimal outcomes and managing potential complications.
  • Prioritize Rest: Allow your body adequate time to rest and recover. Avoid overexertion, especially during periods of peak fatigue.
  • Hydration is Key: Emphasize consistent fluid intake to support kidney function and mitigate dehydration-related side effects.
  • Know When to Call: Understand the specific symptoms that warrant immediate medical attention, such as fever, uncontrolled bleeding, or severe pain.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Discharge Teaching Assessment: Assess patient and caregiver understanding of discharge instructions, including medication schedules, side effect management, and emergency protocols.
  • Neutropenic Precautions: Educate on the critical importance of neutropenic precautions, including hand hygiene, avoiding sick contacts, and food safety.
  • Antiemetic Regimen: Review the prescribed antiemetic schedule, emphasizing prophylactic use and rescue medications for breakthrough nausea.
  • Oral Mucositis Care: Instruct on proper oral hygiene, including soft toothbrushes, non-irritating mouthwashes, and pain management for mucositis.
  • Resource Provision: Provide contact information for the oncology clinic, emergency services, and support groups.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address potential psychosocial impacts of chemotherapy, including fatigue, anxiety, and body image changes, and offer appropriate referrals.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Myelosuppression Monitoring: Emphasize the need for regular Complete Blood Count (CBC) monitoring to assess nadir and recovery of white blood cells, red blood cells, and platelets.
  • Febrile Neutropenia Protocol: Review institutional protocols for managing febrile neutropenia, including prompt administration of broad-spectrum antibiotics.
  • Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV): Differentiate between acute, delayed, and anticipatory CINV and the pharmacological strategies for each (e.g., 5-HT3 antagonists, NK1 receptor antagonists, corticosteroids).
  • Peripheral Neuropathy Assessment: Instruct on monitoring for signs of chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) and strategies for symptom management.
  • Extravasation Management: Review the signs and symptoms of extravasation and the immediate nursing interventions required for vesicant agents.
  • Drug-Specific Toxicities: Highlight specific organ toxicities associated with the patient's chemotherapy regimen (e.g., cardiotoxicity with anthracyclines, nephrotoxicity with cisplatin).
