Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML)

🏠 Home / Cancer / Leukemia

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML): A type of cancer that starts in the blood-forming cells of the bone marrow, characterized by the overproduction of myeloid cells.
  • Philadelphia Chromosome (Ph+): The hallmark of CML, resulting from a specific genetic translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22, creating the BCR-ABL fusion gene.
  • Disease Phases: CML typically progresses through a chronic phase, an accelerated phase, and a blast crisis, each with distinct clinical features and treatment considerations.
  • Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs): The primary treatment for CML, targeting the BCR-ABL protein to control disease progression and induce remission.
  • Lifelong Management: CML is often a chronic condition requiring continuous monitoring and TKI therapy for many patients.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Fatigue and Weakness: Common due to anemia or the systemic effects of the disease.
  • Unexplained Weight Loss: Significant and unintentional reduction in body weight.
  • Night Sweats: Excessive sweating during sleep, often soaking clothes and bedding.
  • Abdominal Fullness or Pain: Caused by an enlarged spleen (splenomegaly), leading to early satiety.
  • Easy Bruising or Bleeding: Due to abnormal platelet function or low platelet counts.
  • Bone or Joint Pain: Can occur, especially in advanced phases, due to bone marrow expansion.
  • Fever: Persistent low-grade fever not attributable to infection.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Adherence to TKI Therapy: Missing doses or inconsistent intake can lead to treatment failure, disease progression, and development of resistance.
  • Monitoring for Side Effects: Be vigilant for TKI-specific adverse effects such as fluid retention, myelosuppression, gastrointestinal issues, skin rashes, and cardiac complications.
  • Drug Interactions: Many TKIs interact with other medications, including antacids, proton pump inhibitors, certain antibiotics, antifungals, and herbal supplements; always consult a physician before taking new drugs.
  • Pregnancy and Breastfeeding: TKIs are generally contraindicated during pregnancy and breastfeeding; effective contraception is essential for women of childbearing potential.
  • Infection Prevention: Due to potential myelosuppression, practice meticulous hand hygiene, avoid sick contacts, and report any signs of infection promptly.
  • Regular Clinical and Molecular Monitoring: Consistent follow-up appointments and blood tests, including BCR-ABL transcript levels, are vital to assess treatment response and detect resistance.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support kidney function and help manage potential hyperuricemia from high cell turnover.
  • Grapefruit and Grapefruit Juice: Avoid consumption as it can interfere with the metabolism of certain TKIs (e.g., imatinib, nilotinib) by inhibiting CYP3A4 enzymes, potentially increasing drug levels and side effects.
  • Antacids and Acid Reducers: Separate administration of antacids or proton pump inhibitors from certain TKIs (e.g., dasatinib, nilotinib) as they require an acidic environment for optimal absorption.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: May help manage gastrointestinal side effects like nausea and dyspepsia commonly associated with TKI therapy.
  • Avoid Raw or Undercooked Foods: If experiencing myelosuppression, minimize exposure to potential foodborne pathogens by avoiding raw meats, fish, eggs, and unwashed produce.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Reporting of Symptoms: Immediately report any new or worsening symptoms, especially fever, unusual bleeding, severe pain, or signs of infection, to your healthcare team.
  • Medication Management: Keep an accurate record of all medications, including over-the-counter drugs, supplements, and herbal remedies, and review with your physician regularly.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know the signs and symptoms that warrant immediate medical attention and have a clear plan for contacting your care team or seeking emergency care.
  • Avoid Self-Adjustment of Doses: Never alter the dosage or schedule of your TKI medication without explicit instruction from your prescribing physician.
  • Vaccination Status: Discuss appropriate vaccinations with your doctor, especially if immunocompromised, while avoiding live vaccines.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early and Accurate Diagnosis: Timely identification of CML and the Philadelphia chromosome is paramount for initiating effective, targeted therapy.
  • Individualized TKI Selection: The choice of TKI is tailored based on patient comorbidities, disease phase, risk stratification, and potential drug interactions.
  • Rigorous Molecular Monitoring: Regular quantitative PCR for BCR-ABL1 transcript levels is critical to assess treatment response, detect suboptimal response, and guide therapeutic adjustments.
  • Management of Resistance and Intolerance: Strategies for switching TKIs or considering alternative therapies like allogeneic stem cell transplant are crucial for patients who fail initial TKI therapy.
  • Long-Term Quality of Life: Focus on managing TKI side effects, addressing psychosocial needs, and supporting patients in maintaining a high quality of life throughout their chronic treatment journey.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education on TKIs: Instruct patients on proper TKI administration, potential side effects, and the importance of strict adherence and reporting adverse events.
  • Monitoring Vital Signs and Labs: Regularly assess complete blood counts (CBC), liver and renal function tests, and electrolyte levels to monitor for myelosuppression and organ toxicity.
  • Symptom Management Strategies: Provide interventions for common TKI side effects such as antiemetics for nausea, pain management, and strategies for fluid retention.
  • Infection Control Protocols: Educate patients and caregivers on infection prevention measures, especially during periods of neutropenia.
  • Psychosocial Support: Assess for and address patient anxiety, depression, and coping mechanisms related to a chronic cancer diagnosis and long-term treatment.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Philadelphia Chromosome (Ph): A reciprocal translocation between the long arms of chromosome 9 and 22, denoted t(9;22)(q34;q11).
  • BCR-ABL1 Fusion Gene: The oncogenic product of the Philadelphia chromosome, encoding a constitutively active tyrosine kinase.
  • Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs): A class of drugs including imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, bosutinib, and ponatinib, targeting the BCR-ABL1 protein.
  • Molecular Response: Measured by quantitative PCR (qPCR) for BCR-ABL1 transcript levels, typically expressed on the International Scale (IS).
  • European LeukemiaNet (ELN) Guidelines: Widely used clinical guidelines for the diagnosis, risk stratification, and management of CML.
  • Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (allo-HSCT): A curative option for CML, primarily considered for TKI failure or advanced disease phases.
  • Sokal, Hasford, and EUTOS Scores: Prognostic scoring systems used to stratify CML patients at diagnosis based on clinical and hematological parameters.
