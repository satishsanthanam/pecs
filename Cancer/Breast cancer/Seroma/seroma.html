Seroma

🏠 Home / Cancer / Breast cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A seroma is a collection of serous fluid that builds up under the skin at a surgical site, commonly occurring after breast cancer surgery such as mastectomy, lumpectomy, or axillary dissection.
  • Formation: It forms in the 'dead space' left after tissue removal, where lymphatic fluid and serum can accumulate.
  • Prevalence: Seromas are one of the most common complications following breast cancer surgery.
  • Nature: While generally benign, they can cause discomfort, delay wound healing, and in some cases, become infected.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Swelling or Bulge: A noticeable lump or swelling at or near the surgical incision site.
  • Fluid Sensation: A feeling of fullness, heaviness, or 'sloshing' under the skin.
  • Pain or Tenderness: Localized discomfort or pain, which may worsen with movement or pressure.
  • Skin Changes: The skin over the seroma may appear taut, shiny, or slightly discolored.
  • Infection Signs: Redness, warmth, increased pain, fever, or pus-like drainage indicate a potential infection requiring immediate medical attention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Post-Operative Drain Care: If drains are in place, meticulously follow instructions for emptying and caring for them to prevent fluid accumulation.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and activities that put strain on the surgical site as advised by your surgeon.
  • Compression Garments: Wear prescribed compression bras or garments consistently to help reduce fluid accumulation and support healing.
  • Infection Monitoring: Regularly inspect the surgical site for any signs of infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or new pain.
  • Prompt Reporting: Report any new or worsening swelling, pain, or signs of infection to your healthcare provider without delay.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support overall healing and tissue repair.
  • Protein Intake: Ensure sufficient protein consumption to aid in wound healing and tissue regeneration.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a diet rich in vitamins and minerals to bolster the immune system and support recovery.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate fiber to prevent constipation, which can cause straining and discomfort at the surgical site.
  • Anti-Inflammatory Foods: While not directly preventing seroma, consuming foods with anti-inflammatory properties can support overall post-surgical recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Self-Drainage Prohibition: Never attempt to drain a seroma yourself, as this significantly increases the risk of infection and other complications.
  • Infection Alert: Seek immediate medical attention if you develop a fever, chills, increased pain, spreading redness, or pus from the surgical site.
  • Follow-Up Adherence: Attend all scheduled post-operative appointments for monitoring and potential seroma management.
  • Compression Consistency: Wear compression garments as directed; inconsistent use can lead to increased fluid accumulation.
  • Medication Compliance: Take all prescribed medications, including antibiotics if indicated, exactly as instructed.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Common Complication: Seromas are a very common and expected complication after breast surgery, particularly with extensive tissue removal or lymph node dissection.
  • Management Approach: Most small seromas resolve spontaneously. Larger or symptomatic seromas often require aspiration (needle drainage) in the clinic.
  • Recurrence Risk: Seromas can recur after aspiration, and multiple drainage procedures may be necessary.
  • Infection Risk: Each aspiration carries a small risk of introducing infection, so strict aseptic technique is crucial.
  • Impact on Treatment: Large or persistent seromas can sometimes delay adjuvant therapies like radiation, necessitating active management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients on the signs and symptoms of seroma, proper wound care, and when to seek medical attention.
  • Aseptic Aspiration: Master aseptic technique for seroma aspiration, including skin preparation and post-procedure care.
  • Drain Management: Monitor and record drain output, color, and consistency, ensuring proper function and patient understanding of home care.
  • Assessment Skills: Regularly assess the surgical site for swelling, tenderness, skin changes, and signs of infection.
  • Compression Therapy: Instruct patients on the correct application and importance of compression garments to minimize fluid accumulation.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Formation of a fluid-filled cavity due to disruption of lymphatic and vascular channels, leading to accumulation of plasma and lymph in a surgically created dead space.
  • Risk Factors: Larger surgical dissection, axillary lymph node dissection, higher body mass index (BMI), use of electrocautery, and certain types of reconstructive surgery.
  • Diagnosis: Primarily clinical examination; ultrasound can confirm fluid collection and rule out other masses.
  • Management Options: Expectant management for small, asymptomatic seromas; serial needle aspiration; compression therapy; rarely, sclerotherapy or surgical excision for refractory cases.
  • Potential Complications: Infection (seroma cellulitis or abscess), skin flap necrosis, delayed wound healing, lymphedema, and interference with radiation therapy or implant integrity.
