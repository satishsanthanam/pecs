# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Procedure Overview:** Lumpectomy, also known as breast-conserving surgery or partial mastectomy, is a surgical procedure to remove a cancerous tumor (lump) and a small margin of surrounding healthy tissue from the breast, while preserving the majority of the breast tissue.
* **Primary Goal:** The main objective is to remove early-stage breast cancer effectively, often followed by radiation therapy to eliminate any remaining cancer cells.
* **Indications:** Typically performed for localized breast cancers that are small enough to be removed with clear margins while maintaining an acceptable cosmetic outcome.
* **Lymph Node Assessment:** Often includes a sentinel lymph node biopsy to check if cancer cells have spread to the nearest lymph nodes in the armpit.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Palpable Breast Lump:** The most common indication is the discovery of a new lump or mass in the breast or armpit that may be painless.
* **Breast Pain:** Persistent pain in a specific area of the breast or nipple that does not resolve.
* **Skin Changes:** Dimpling, puckering, redness, scaling, or thickening of the breast skin.
* **Nipple Alterations:** Nipple inversion, discharge (especially bloody), or scaling around the nipple.
* **Swelling:** Swelling of all or part of the breast, even if no distinct lump is felt.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Pre-operative Medication Review:** Disclose all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies to your surgeon, especially blood thinners (e.g., aspirin, warfarin, NSAIDs) which may need to be stopped prior to surgery.
* **Smoking Cessation:** Advised to stop smoking several weeks before surgery to improve healing and reduce complication risks.
* **Avoid Alcohol:** Refrain from alcohol consumption for at least 24-48 hours before surgery.
* **Post-operative Activity Restrictions:** Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and repetitive arm movements on the affected side for several weeks post-surgery to promote healing and prevent complications.
* **Infection Monitoring:** Watch for signs of infection at the incision site, including increased redness, swelling, warmth, pus, or fever.
* **Lymphedema Risk:** Be aware of the potential for lymphedema (swelling) in the arm or hand on the affected side, especially if lymph nodes were removed.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Pre-operative Fasting:** Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines, typically no food or drink for 6-8 hours before surgery, and clear liquids for 2 hours, as instructed by the surgical team.
* **Post-operative Hydration:** Begin with clear liquids after surgery and gradually advance to light, easily digestible foods as tolerated to prevent nausea and promote bowel function.
* **Nausea Management:** Avoid heavy, greasy, or spicy foods immediately after surgery, especially if experiencing post-anesthesia nausea.
* **Constipation Prevention:** Increase fiber intake and ensure adequate fluid consumption to counteract potential constipation from pain medications.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Pre-surgical Instructions:** Follow all specific instructions regarding bathing, skin preparation, and medication timing provided by the surgical team.
* **Transportation Arrangement:** Ensure a responsible adult is available to drive you home after surgery and assist with initial recovery due to the effects of anesthesia.
* **Pain Management:** Take prescribed pain medication as directed to manage discomfort and facilitate recovery. Do not wait for pain to become severe.
* **Wound Care:** Keep the incision site clean and dry. Follow specific instructions for dressing changes and showering.
* **Drain Management:** If a surgical drain is placed, understand how to empty it, measure output, and care for the site as instructed by your nurse.
* **Report Concerns:** Immediately report any signs of infection, excessive bleeding, sudden swelling, or severe pain to your healthcare provider.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Multidisciplinary Care:** Lumpectomy is part of a comprehensive breast cancer treatment plan, often involving collaboration with medical oncologists, radiation oncologists, and pathologists.
* **Margin Status:** Achieving clear surgical margins (no cancer cells at the edge of the removed tissue) is crucial for local control and may necessitate re-excision if margins are positive.
* **Adjuvant Therapy:** Radiation therapy is almost always recommended after lumpectomy to reduce the risk of local recurrence. Systemic therapies (chemotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy) may also be indicated based on pathology results.
* **Oncoplastic Techniques:** For larger tumors or smaller breasts, oncoplastic surgery combines cancer removal with plastic surgery techniques to optimize cosmetic outcomes.
* **Psychosocial Support:** Encourage patients to seek support from breast cancer support groups, counselors, or social workers to address emotional and psychological impacts.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pre-operative Education:** Provide thorough patient education on the surgical procedure, expected recovery, pain management, and potential complications.
* **Post-operative Assessment:** Monitor vital signs, pain levels, incision site for signs of infection or hematoma, and drain output (if applicable).
* **Mobility and Positioning:** Encourage early ambulation to prevent deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pneumonia. Assist with comfortable positioning to reduce tension on the incision.
* **Lymphedema Prevention:** Educate patients on exercises, skin care, and precautions for the affected arm to minimize lymphedema risk.
* **Emotional Support:** Offer empathetic listening and provide resources for psychological support, recognizing the significant emotional impact of a cancer diagnosis and surgery.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **ICD-10 Codes:** C50 (Malignant neoplasm of breast), Z85.3 (Personal history of malignant neoplasm of breast).
* **CPT Codes:** 19301 (Partial mastectomy, lumpectomy, wide excision of lesion of breast).
* **Pathology Report Key Elements:** Tumor size, type, grade, lymphovascular invasion, hormone receptor status (ER/PR), HER2 status, and surgical margin status.
* **Pre-operative Localization:** Techniques such as wire localization, radioactive seed localization, or magnetic seed localization are used for non-palpable lesions.
* **Adjuvant Therapy Guidelines:** NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network) guidelines provide evidence-based recommendations for post-lumpectomy radiation and systemic therapies.
