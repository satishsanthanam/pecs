Non-small cell lung cancer

🏠 Home / Cancer / Lung cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Prevalence: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases.
  • Subtypes: The main types include adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma, each with distinct characteristics and treatment approaches.
  • Risk Factors: Smoking is the leading cause, but exposure to secondhand smoke, radon gas, asbestos, and certain industrial chemicals also significantly increase risk.
  • Early Detection: Symptoms often do not appear until the cancer is advanced, making early detection challenging but crucial for better outcomes.
  • Treatment Advances: Significant progress has been made in targeted therapies and immunotherapies, offering personalized treatment options based on specific genetic mutations.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Cough: A new cough that doesn't go away or a change in a chronic cough.
  • Chest Pain: Pain that worsens with deep breathing, coughing, or laughing.
  • Shortness of Breath: Feeling breathless or having difficulty breathing, especially during activity.
  • Hoarseness: A change in voice that persists for more than a few weeks.
  • Weight Loss & Fatigue: Unexplained weight loss and persistent tiredness or weakness.
  • Hemoptysis: Coughing up blood or rust-colored sputum.
  • Recurrent Infections: Frequent bouts of bronchitis or pneumonia.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Smoking Cessation: The most effective measure to prevent NSCLC and improve prognosis for those diagnosed.
  • Radon Testing: Test homes for radon gas, a colorless, odorless radioactive gas that is a leading cause of lung cancer in non-smokers.
  • Occupational Safety: Use appropriate protective equipment and follow safety protocols in workplaces with exposure to carcinogens like asbestos or certain chemicals.
  • Regular Check-ups: Discuss lung cancer screening options with a physician, especially for high-risk individuals (e.g., current or former heavy smokers).
  • Environmental Awareness: Minimize exposure to air pollution and other environmental toxins.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Support: Focus on a high-calorie, high-protein diet to combat weight loss and muscle wasting often associated with cancer and its treatments.
  • Managing Nausea: Consume small, frequent meals, bland foods, and avoid strong odors to alleviate treatment-induced nausea.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially during chemotherapy or radiation, to prevent dehydration and support kidney function.
  • Soft Foods for Dysphagia: If experiencing difficulty swallowing (dysphagia) due to treatment, opt for soft, pureed, or liquid diets.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit spicy, acidic, or very hot foods if experiencing mouth sores (mucositis) or esophageal irritation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Symptom Monitoring: Patients and caregivers must vigilantly monitor for new or worsening symptoms, including pain, dyspnea, fever, or changes in mental status, and report them promptly.
  • Medication Adherence: Strict adherence to prescribed medications, including chemotherapy, targeted therapies, and supportive care drugs, is vital for treatment efficacy and symptom control.
  • Infection Prevention: Due to potential immunosuppression from treatment, practice meticulous hand hygiene, avoid crowded places, and report any signs of infection immediately.
  • Emotional Support: Recognize and address the psychological impact of a cancer diagnosis; encourage seeking support from family, friends, support groups, or mental health professionals.
  • Advance Care Planning: Engage in discussions about advance directives and end-of-life care preferences with the healthcare team and family.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Effective management of NSCLC requires a collaborative effort involving oncologists, thoracic surgeons, radiation oncologists, pulmonologists, and palliative care specialists.
  • Personalized Treatment: Treatment decisions are highly individualized, based on the cancer's stage, histological subtype, molecular profile (e.g., EGFR, ALK, PD-L1 status), and the patient's overall health.
  • Molecular Testing: Comprehensive genomic profiling is critical to identify actionable mutations that can guide the use of targeted therapies and immunotherapies.
  • Clinical Trials: Patients should be evaluated for eligibility in clinical trials, which offer access to cutting-edge treatments and contribute to advancing cancer care.
  • Prognosis & Quality of Life: Open and honest communication about prognosis, potential treatment side effects, and strategies to maintain quality of life throughout the disease trajectory is paramount.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct thorough patient assessments, including respiratory status, pain levels, nutritional intake, and psychosocial well-being, to identify needs and guide interventions.
  • Treatment Education: Educate patients and families on their specific treatment plan, potential side effects, self-care strategies, and when to seek urgent medical attention.
  • Symptom Management: Implement evidence-based nursing interventions for common symptoms such as dyspnea, pain, fatigue, nausea, and mucositis.
  • Oncologic Emergencies: Be vigilant for and prepared to manage oncologic emergencies like superior vena cava syndrome, spinal cord compression, or hypercalcemia.
  • Psychosocial Support: Provide emotional support, facilitate access to counseling services, and connect patients with support groups to help them cope with the challenges of NSCLC.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Histological Subtypes: Adenocarcinoma, Squamous Cell Carcinoma, Large Cell Carcinoma, NSCLC-NOS (Not Otherwise Specified).
  • Staging: TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) staging system, crucial for prognosis and treatment planning.
  • Molecular Markers: EGFR mutations, ALK rearrangements, ROS1 fusions, BRAF mutations, MET amplification, RET fusions, PD-L1 expression.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Chest X-ray, CT scan, PET scan, MRI, bronchoscopy with biopsy, CT-guided biopsy, mediastinoscopy, EBUS (Endobronchial Ultrasound).
  • Treatment Modalities: Surgery (lobectomy, pneumonectomy, segmentectomy), Radiation Therapy (SBRT, conventional), Chemotherapy (platinum-based regimens), Targeted Therapy (TKIs), Immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitors like pembrolizumab, nivolumab).
