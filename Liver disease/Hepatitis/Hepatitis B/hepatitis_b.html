Hepatitis B

🏠 Home / Liver disease / Hepatitis

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Viral Infection: Hepatitis B is a viral infection that primarily affects the liver, causing both acute and chronic disease.
  • Transmission Routes: Transmitted through contact with infected blood or body fluids, including sexual contact, sharing needles, and from mother to child during birth.
  • Global Health Concern: A major global health problem, it can lead to chronic infection, increasing the risk of developing cirrhosis, liver failure, and liver cancer.
  • Preventable: Highly preventable through vaccination.
  • Asymptomatic Nature: Many individuals, especially those with chronic infection, may not experience symptoms for years.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Acute Hepatitis B: May include fatigue, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain (especially in the upper right quadrant), dark urine, clay-colored stools, and jaundice (yellowing of skin and eyes).
  • Chronic Hepatitis B: Often asymptomatic for decades. When symptoms appear, they can be non-specific like persistent fatigue, or signs of advanced liver disease such as ascites (fluid in the abdomen), peripheral edema, spider angiomas, easy bruising, or confusion (hepatic encephalopathy).
  • Clinical Indications: Elevated liver enzymes (ALT, AST), unexplained fatigue, recent exposure to infected blood or body fluids, or being part of a high-risk population (e.g., healthcare workers, IV drug users, individuals from endemic regions) prompt screening.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Vaccination: Ensure complete vaccination for all infants, children, and at-risk adults. This is the most effective preventive measure.
  • Safe Practices: Avoid sharing personal items such as razors, toothbrushes, and nail clippers. Do not share needles or drug injection equipment.
  • Sexual Health: Practice safe sex by consistently and correctly using barrier methods like condoms to prevent transmission.
  • Blood Safety: Healthcare professionals must adhere to universal precautions when handling blood and body fluids. Ensure blood products are screened for HBV.
  • Maternal-Fetal Transmission: Pregnant women should be screened for Hepatitis B. Infants born to HBsAg-positive mothers must receive Hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG) and the first dose of the Hepatitis B vaccine within 12 hours of birth.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Alcohol Restriction: Strict avoidance of alcohol is paramount, as it can accelerate liver damage and progression to cirrhosis.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a well-balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to support overall liver health.
  • Limit Processed Foods: Reduce intake of high-fat, high-sugar, and highly processed foods to minimize the metabolic burden on the liver.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake to support kidney function and overall well-being.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: For individuals experiencing nausea or poor appetite, consuming smaller, more frequent meals may be beneficial.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Regular Monitoring: Patients with chronic Hepatitis B require regular medical follow-ups, including liver function tests, viral load measurements, and screening for liver cancer (e.g., ultrasound, alpha-fetoprotein).
  • Medication Adherence: If prescribed antiviral therapy, strict adherence to the medication regimen is crucial for suppressing viral replication and preventing disease progression.
  • Avoid Hepatotoxic Agents: Consult a physician before taking any new medications, herbal remedies, or supplements, as some can be harmful to the liver.
  • Report New Symptoms: Promptly report any new or worsening symptoms such as increased jaundice, abdominal swelling, unexplained bleeding, or changes in mental status to your healthcare provider.
  • Family Screening: Encourage family members and close contacts to get screened and vaccinated for Hepatitis B.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis: Early detection of Hepatitis B is critical for effective management and preventing long-term complications like cirrhosis and liver cancer.
  • Vaccination Imperative: The Hepatitis B vaccine is highly effective and safe; it is a cornerstone of public health to prevent this serious liver disease.
  • Treatment Advances: Significant advancements in antiviral therapies can effectively suppress the virus, improve liver function, and reduce the risk of disease progression in chronic cases.
  • Holistic Management: Management involves not only antiviral treatment but also lifestyle modifications, regular monitoring, and addressing co-morbidities.
  • Patient Education: Empowering patients with knowledge about their condition, transmission, and prevention strategies is vital for disease control and public health.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a crucial role in educating patients and their families about Hepatitis B transmission, prevention (vaccination, safe practices), and the importance of treatment adherence and follow-up care.
  • Vaccine Administration: Understand the Hepatitis B vaccine schedule, proper administration techniques, and contraindications.
  • Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): Be knowledgeable about PEP protocols for accidental needle sticks or other exposures to HBV-infected blood.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Assess patients for signs and symptoms of liver decompensation, such as ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding, and jaundice.
  • Emotional Support: Provide empathetic support to patients dealing with a chronic illness, addressing potential stigma and psychological impact.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • HBsAg (Hepatitis B surface antigen): Indicates active Hepatitis B infection (acute or chronic).
  • Anti-HBs (Hepatitis B surface antibody): Indicates immunity to Hepatitis B, either from vaccination or successful recovery from past infection.
  • HBcAb (Hepatitis B core antibody): Indicates past or current Hepatitis B infection. IgM anti-HBc suggests acute infection; IgG anti-HBc suggests chronic or resolved infection.
  • HBV DNA: Measures the viral load, indicating active viral replication and infectivity. Used to monitor treatment response.
  • HBeAg (Hepatitis B e-antigen): Indicates high levels of viral replication and infectivity.
  • Antiviral Agents: Key medications include Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), Entecavir, Lamivudine, and Pegylated interferon alfa-2a.
  • Liver Biopsy/Elastography: Diagnostic tools used to assess the degree of liver inflammation, fibrosis, and cirrhosis.
