Hip fracture

🏠 Home / Senior health / Osteoporosis

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A hip fracture is a break in the upper part of the femur (thigh bone), specifically near the hip joint.
  • Common Cause: Most frequently caused by falls in older adults, especially those with underlying osteoporosis.
  • Clinical Impact: Represents a significant cause of morbidity, loss of independence, and increased mortality rates in the senior population.
  • Treatment: Requires surgical intervention in the vast majority of cases to stabilize the fracture and facilitate recovery.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Pain: Intense pain in the hip or groin area, often exacerbated by movement.
  • Inability to Bear Weight: Complete or partial inability to stand or put weight on the affected leg.
  • Bruising and Swelling: Visible bruising and swelling around the hip and upper thigh region.
  • Leg Shortening: The injured leg may appear shorter than the uninjured leg.
  • External Rotation: The injured leg often turns outward (externally rotates) compared to the uninjured leg.
  • Immobility: Difficulty or inability to move the leg, even slightly.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Implement comprehensive home safety assessments to remove hazards, ensure adequate lighting, and install grab bars.
  • Bone Density Screening: Regular DEXA scans for at-risk individuals, particularly post-menopausal women and older adults, to detect and manage osteoporosis.
  • Nutritional Support: Ensure adequate daily intake of calcium and Vitamin D through diet or supplements to support bone health.
  • Medication Review: Regularly review all medications with a physician to identify and minimize those that may increase fall risk (e.g., sedatives, certain antihypertensives).
  • Assistive Devices: Encourage the use of walkers, canes, or other mobility aids as appropriate to enhance stability.
  • Post-Surgical Care: Adhere strictly to weight-bearing restrictions and physical therapy protocols to prevent re-injury and promote healing.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Strict adherence to NPO (nil per os) guidelines as instructed by the anesthesia team prior to surgery.
  • Post-Op Fluid Reintroduction: Gradual reintroduction of clear liquids, progressing to a soft diet, then regular diet as tolerated post-surgery.
  • High Protein Intake: Encourage protein-rich foods to support wound healing, muscle repair, and overall recovery.
  • Fiber for Bowel Regularity: Ensure adequate fiber intake to prevent constipation, which is common due to immobility and opioid pain medication.
  • Calcium & Vitamin D: Continue or initiate dietary intake of calcium and Vitamin D-rich foods (e.g., dairy, fortified cereals, leafy greens) to support bone mineralization.
  • Hydration: Maintain excellent hydration with water and other clear fluids to prevent dehydration and support recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Do Not Move Patient: If a hip fracture is suspected, do not attempt to move the patient; stabilize the leg as much as possible and call emergency medical services immediately.
  • Keep Patient Warm: Cover the patient with a blanket to prevent hypothermia while awaiting medical assistance.
  • Monitor for Shock: Observe for signs of shock (e.g., rapid pulse, pale skin, confusion) and report immediately to emergency personnel.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed pain medication as directed to manage severe pain, especially post-operatively.
  • Post-Op Complications: Closely monitor for signs of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, urinary tract infections, and delirium.
  • Weight-Bearing Restrictions: Strictly enforce post-operative weight-bearing limitations as instructed by the surgeon and physical therapist.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prompt Surgical Intervention: Early surgical fixation is paramount for optimal outcomes, reducing complications and facilitating rehabilitation.
  • Individualized Approach: Surgical choice (e.g., internal fixation, hemiarthroplasty, total hip arthroplasty) depends on fracture type, patient age, and bone quality.
  • Early Mobilization: Crucial for preventing post-operative complications such as DVT, pneumonia, and pressure ulcers.
  • Osteoporosis Management: Addressing underlying osteoporosis with pharmacotherapy (e.g., bisphosphonates) and lifestyle modifications is essential to prevent future fractures.
  • Comprehensive Rehabilitation: A structured physical and occupational therapy program is vital for regaining strength, mobility, and independence.
  • Risk Factor Modification: Physicians should counsel patients on fall prevention strategies and medication adjustments to mitigate future fracture risk.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-Op Assessment: Conduct thorough neurovascular assessments of the affected limb, pain assessment, and review of comorbidities.
  • Pain Management: Implement a multimodal pain management plan, including pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions, and regularly assess pain levels.
  • Wound Care: Monitor surgical incision for signs of infection (redness, swelling, drainage, warmth) and perform dressing changes as ordered.
  • DVT Prophylaxis: Administer prescribed anticoagulants, apply sequential compression devices (SCDs) or anti-embolism stockings (TED hose), and encourage ankle pumps.
  • Early Ambulation: Assist with out-of-bed activities and ambulation as soon as medically cleared, using appropriate assistive devices and fall precautions.
  • Patient Education: Educate patient and family on post-operative care, weight-bearing restrictions, signs of complications, and discharge planning.
  • Delirium Monitoring: Assess for and manage post-operative delirium, especially in elderly patients, using validated screening tools.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Fracture Types: Femoral neck fracture, intertrochanteric fracture, subtrochanteric fracture.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: X-ray (initial), MRI (for occult fractures), CT scan (for surgical planning).
  • Surgical Procedures: Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF), Hemiarthroplasty, Total Hip Arthroplplasty (THA).
  • ICD-10 Codes: S72.0 (Fracture of neck of femur), S72.1 (Pertrochanteric fracture), S72.2 (Subtrochanteric fracture).
  • Risk Factors: Osteoporosis, advanced age, female gender, previous falls, certain medications (e.g., corticosteroids), poor vision, neurological conditions, low body mass index.
  • Complications: Nonunion, avascular necrosis, infection, DVT, pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, pressure ulcers, delirium, loss of independence.
