# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Life-Threatening Reaction:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) are severe, potentially fatal mucocutaneous reactions.
* **Drug-Induced Origin:** Most cases are triggered by medications, making careful drug management crucial, especially in seniors with polypharmacy.
* **Rapid Progression:** Characterized by widespread blistering, epidermal detachment, and severe mucosal involvement.
* **Spectrum of Severity:** SJS involves less than 10% body surface area (BSA) detachment, SJS/TEN overlap 10-30% BSA, and TEN more than 30% BSA.
* **Medical Emergency:** Requires immediate hospitalization, often in a burn unit or intensive care unit, due to high mortality rates and extensive organ involvement.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Prodromal Phase:** Often begins with non-specific flu-like symptoms such as fever, malaise, myalgia, and arthralgia, preceding skin lesions by several days.
* **Skin Manifestations:** Widespread erythematous macules, targetoid lesions, rapidly progressing to flaccid blisters and extensive epidermal detachment.
* **Mucosal Involvement:** Painful erosions and crusting affecting at least two mucosal sites, commonly oral, ocular, and genital.
* **Ocular Symptoms:** Severe conjunctivitis, photophobia, blurred vision, and potential corneal erosions.
* **Systemic Signs:** Tachycardia, hypotension, and signs of organ dysfunction (e.g., renal impairment, respiratory distress) due to systemic inflammation and fluid loss.
* **Severe Pain:** Patients experience excruciating pain due to extensive skin and mucosal damage.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Medication Reconciliation:** Meticulously review all current and recently started medications, especially within the preceding 8 weeks, for potential culprits.
* **High-Risk Medications:** Be vigilant with drugs commonly implicated, including allopurinol, sulfonamide antibiotics, anti-epileptics (e.g., carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin), and certain NSAIDs.
* **Early Symptom Recognition:** Educate patients and caregivers to promptly report any new rash, blistering, or mucosal pain, particularly if accompanied by fever or flu-like symptoms.
* **Avoid Re-exposure:** Absolutely critical to identify and permanently discontinue the causative drug and all chemically related compounds to prevent recurrence.
* **Genetic Screening:** Consider pharmacogenetic testing (e.g., HLA-B*1502 for carbamazepine in certain Asian populations, HLA-B*5801 for allopurinol in specific ethnic groups) where indicated.
* **Hydration and Nutrition:** Proactively manage fluid and electrolyte balance and ensure adequate nutritional support, often intravenously or via enteral feeding, due to severe oral pain.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Soft/Liquid Diet:** Due to severe and painful oral and pharyngeal erosions, patients typically require a soft, bland, or liquid diet.
* **Nutritional Support:** Many patients will require enteral (e.g., nasogastric tube) or parenteral nutrition if oral intake is severely compromised or impossible.
* **Hydration Management:** Aggressive intravenous fluid replacement is essential to compensate for significant fluid loss through denuded skin and impaired oral intake.
* **Avoid Irritants:** Restrict acidic, spicy, hot, or coarse foods and beverages that can exacerbate oral and esophageal lesions.
* **Temperature Control:** Prefer cool or lukewarm foods and liquids to minimize discomfort and irritation to damaged mucous membranes.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Immediate Transfer:** Patients with suspected SJS/TEN require urgent transfer to a specialized burn unit or intensive care unit for expert management.
* **Offending Drug Withdrawal:** Prompt and complete discontinuation of all non-essential medications, especially the suspected causative agent, is the most critical intervention.
* **Supportive Care Focus:** Meticulous wound care, fluid and electrolyte balance, pain control, and aggressive infection prevention are paramount.
* **Ocular Care:** Regular ophthalmology consultation and intensive ocular lubrication are vital to prevent long-term vision impairment.
* **Temperature Regulation:** Monitor and manage body temperature closely, as extensive skin loss impairs thermoregulation.
* **Psychological Support:** Provide comprehensive emotional and psychological support for patients and their families coping with a severe, disfiguring, and life-threatening illness.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **High Index of Suspicion:** Maintain a high index of suspicion for SJS/TEN in any patient presenting with an acute, widespread rash and mucosal involvement, particularly following new medication exposure.
* **Timely Diagnosis:** Rapid diagnosis and immediate withdrawal of the offending drug are the most crucial factors influencing patient outcomes.
* **Multidisciplinary Team:** Effective management necessitates a collaborative approach involving dermatologists, intensivists, ophthalmologists, and burn specialists.
* **Prognosis Awareness:** Be aware of the significant mortality rates, especially in TEN, and the potential for severe long-term sequelae affecting multiple organ systems.
* **Patient Education:** Emphasize thorough patient and family education regarding the identified causative agent and the absolute necessity of avoiding future exposure to prevent recurrence.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Comprehensive Assessment:** Perform meticulous skin and mucosal assessments, documenting the extent of blistering and epidermal detachment (e.g., using the SCORTEN score).
* **Aseptic Wound Care:** Implement strict aseptic techniques for wound care, treating denuded areas similarly to severe burns to prevent secondary infections.
* **Fluid Balance Monitoring:** Accurately monitor intake and output, recognizing the significant fluid and electrolyte losses through extensive skin damage.
* **Pain Management:** Administer prescribed analgesics effectively, utilizing a multi-modal approach to manage the severe pain associated with SJS/TEN.
* **Infection Surveillance:** Closely monitor for signs of infection, including fever, changes in wound appearance, and elevated inflammatory markers.
* **Psychosocial Support:** Provide empathetic care, addressing the patient's and family's emotional distress and anxiety related to the severe nature of the illness.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pathophysiology:** Characterized by widespread keratinocyte apoptosis, primarily mediated by cytotoxic T lymphocytes and granulysin, leading to epidermal detachment.
* **SCORTEN Score:** A validated prognostic tool used to estimate mortality risk in SJS/TEN based on seven clinical parameters (age, malignancy, heart rate, BSA detachment, serum urea, glucose, bicarbonate).
* **Histopathology:** Biopsy typically reveals full-thickness epidermal necrosis with a sparse lymphocytic infiltrate at the dermo-epidermal junction.
* **Immunomodulatory Therapies:** While supportive care is primary, various immunomodulatory agents (e.g., IVIG, cyclosporine, systemic corticosteroids) have been explored, with varying and often controversial evidence of efficacy.
* **Long-Term Sequelae:** Common complications include chronic dry eyes, corneal scarring, synechiae, skin dyspigmentation, scarring, nail dystrophy, and mucosal strictures.
* **Pharmacogenomics:** Specific HLA alleles (e.g., HLA-B*1502, HLA-B*5801) are strongly associated with an increased risk of SJS/TEN induced by certain drugs in genetically predisposed individuals.
