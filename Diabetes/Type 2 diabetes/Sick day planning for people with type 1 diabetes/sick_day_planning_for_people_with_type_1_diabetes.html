Sick day planning for people with type 1 diabetes

🏠 Home / Diabetes / Type 2 diabetes

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Increased Risk During Illness: Even minor illnesses can significantly impact blood glucose levels and increase the risk of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) in individuals with Type 1 diabetes.
  • Preparation is Crucial: Having a pre-defined sick day plan is essential for managing diabetes effectively during illness and preventing serious complications.
  • Never Stop Insulin: Insulin doses should never be discontinued, even if food intake is reduced, as the body's insulin needs often increase during stress and illness.
  • Frequent Monitoring: Blood glucose and ketone monitoring become critically important during illness to guide insulin adjustments and identify potential issues early.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Hyperglycemia: Blood glucose levels remaining high despite usual insulin doses.
  • Ketones in Urine or Blood: Presence of moderate to large ketones, indicating insufficient insulin.
  • Nausea and Vomiting: Common during illness, can lead to dehydration and difficulty maintaining nutrition.
  • Abdominal Pain: A potential symptom of developing diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).
  • Fever: Indicates infection, which can elevate blood glucose levels and insulin requirements.
  • Unusual Fatigue or Weakness: General malaise associated with illness and potential metabolic imbalance.
  • Fruity Breath Odor: A distinct smell indicating high ketone levels, characteristic of DKA.
  • Rapid, Deep Breathing (Kussmaul Respirations): The body's attempt to correct acidosis, a sign of severe DKA.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of sugar-free fluids (water, broth, diet soda) to prevent dehydration, especially if experiencing fever, vomiting, or diarrhea.
  • Insulin Adjustment Protocols: Understand and follow your personalized sick day insulin adjustment plan, which often involves increasing basal and/or bolus insulin doses.
  • Regular Ketone Monitoring: Test for ketones every 2-4 hours if blood glucose is consistently above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) or if feeling unwell.
  • Emergency Contact Information: Keep your healthcare team's contact details readily accessible, along with a list of your current medications and insulin regimen.
  • Over-the-Counter Medication Review: Consult your doctor or pharmacist about safe over-the-counter medications for illness, avoiding those with high sugar content or ingredients that may interact with diabetes medications.
  • Know When to Seek Medical Help: Be aware of specific criteria for contacting your doctor or seeking emergency care, such as persistent vomiting, large ketones, inability to keep fluids down, or signs of severe DKA.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Prioritize Sugar-Free Fluids: Focus on consuming ample amounts of water, unsweetened tea, diet beverages, or clear broths to prevent dehydration.
  • Small, Frequent Carbohydrate Intake: If unable to eat regular meals, consume small, frequent portions of carbohydrate-containing fluids or easily digestible foods (e.g., juice, regular soda, crackers, soup) to prevent hypoglycemia, especially if taking insulin.
  • Avoid Sugary Drinks (if high BG): If blood glucose levels are elevated, avoid sugary drinks unless specifically treating hypoglycemia.
  • Easily Digestible Foods: When able to eat, opt for bland, easily digestible foods like toast, rice, or plain crackers.
  • Continue Carbohydrate Counting: Even with reduced intake, continue to estimate carbohydrate content for appropriate insulin dosing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Familiarize with the Plan: Ensure all family members or caregivers are fully aware of the individual's sick day plan, including insulin adjustments and emergency contacts.
  • Assist with Monitoring: Provide support with blood glucose and ketone monitoring, especially if the individual is too unwell to do so independently.
  • Recognize DKA Signs: Be vigilant for symptoms of diabetic ketoacidosis (e.g., fruity breath, rapid breathing, severe abdominal pain, confusion) and know when to call emergency services.
  • Encourage Fluid Intake: Actively encourage and offer fluids to prevent dehydration, which can worsen hyperglycemia.
  • Medication Administration Support: Understand how to assist with insulin administration or glucagon if necessary and as instructed by the healthcare provider.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Personalized Sick Day Plan: Every patient with Type 1 diabetes requires a personalized sick day management plan developed in collaboration with their endocrinologist or diabetes care team.
  • Insulin is Non-Negotiable: Reiterate that insulin administration must continue, even during illness and reduced food intake, as stress hormones increase insulin requirements.
  • Early Intervention Prevents DKA: Emphasize that prompt recognition of illness symptoms, coupled with diligent blood glucose and ketone monitoring, is critical to prevent the progression to DKA.
  • Open Communication: Instruct patients to contact their healthcare provider if they are uncertain about insulin adjustments, experience persistent vomiting, or have moderate to large ketones.
  • Regular Plan Review: Advise patients to review and update their sick day plan periodically, especially with changes in their insulin regimen, health status, or lifestyle.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology of Illness: Understand that illness-related stress hormones (e.g., cortisol, glucagon, catecholamines) can significantly increase hepatic glucose production and peripheral insulin resistance, leading to hyperglycemia.
  • DKA Management Principles: Review the core principles of DKA management, including aggressive fluid resuscitation, continuous intravenous insulin infusion, and careful electrolyte monitoring and replacement.
  • Patient Education Role: Nurses are pivotal in educating patients and their families on sick day rules, including the importance of monitoring, appropriate insulin adjustments, and clear guidelines for seeking medical attention.
  • Comprehensive Assessment Skills: Develop strong clinical assessment skills to identify early signs of dehydration, worsening hyperglycemia, and impending DKA in patients with Type 1 diabetes during acute illness.
  • Insulin Pump Specifics: Understand the unique sick day protocols for patients using insulin pumps, including temporary basal rate adjustments and troubleshooting potential pump site issues or malfunctions.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ADA Standards of Care: Refer to the American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes for comprehensive guidelines on sick day management for Type 1 diabetes.
  • Ketone Thresholds: Clinical guidelines typically recommend ketone testing if blood glucose levels exceed 250-300 mg/dL (13.9-16.7 mmol/L) or if symptoms of illness are present.
  • Insulin Sensitivity Factor (ISF) Adjustment: Recognize that illness can decrease insulin sensitivity, potentially requiring an increase in the Insulin Sensitivity Factor (ISF) or correction factor for bolus insulin.
  • Basal Insulin Augmentation: Protocols often involve increasing basal insulin rates by 10-20% or more during illness, guided by blood glucose and ketone trends, to counteract increased insulin resistance.
  • Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) Definition: A severe, acute complication characterized by hyperglycemia (>250 mg/dL), ketonemia/ketonuria, and metabolic acidosis (pH <7.3, bicarbonate <18 mEq/L).
