Diabetic ketoacidosis

🏠 Home / Diabetes / Type 2 diabetes

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Acute Complication: Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a severe, life-threatening complication of diabetes, characterized by hyperglycemia, ketosis, and metabolic acidosis.
  • Insulin Deficiency: It results from a profound lack of insulin, leading the body to break down fat for energy, producing ketones as a byproduct.
  • Type 2 Diabetes Context: While more common in Type 1 diabetes, DKA can occur in Type 2 diabetes, especially during severe stress, infection, or with certain medications (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors).
  • Medical Emergency: DKA requires immediate medical attention and hospitalization for proper management and prevention of severe complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Excessive Thirst & Urination: Polydipsia and polyuria are common initial signs due to high blood glucose.
  • Nausea & Vomiting: Often accompanied by abdominal pain, which can be severe and mimic an acute abdomen.
  • Fruity Breath Odor: A distinct sweet or fruity smell on the breath, caused by acetone, a type of ketone.
  • Rapid, Deep Breathing: Kussmaul respirations, the body's attempt to compensate for metabolic acidosis by expelling carbon dioxide.
  • Fatigue & Weakness: Generalized malaise and lethargy due to dehydration and metabolic derangements.
  • Confusion & Altered Mental Status: Can progress to stupor or coma if DKA is left untreated.
  • Dehydration: Dry mouth, decreased skin turgor, and sunken eyes are common physical signs.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Glucose Monitoring: Regularly check blood glucose levels, especially during illness, stress, or when experiencing unusual symptoms.
  • Ketone Testing: Test for ketones in urine or blood when blood glucose is consistently high (e.g., >250-300 mg/dL) or during illness.
  • Sick Day Rules Adherence: Follow established sick day management plans for diabetes, including continuing insulin/medications, monitoring glucose/ketones, and maintaining hydration.
  • Prompt Medical Consultation: Seek immediate medical attention if DKA symptoms develop or if ketone levels are high.
  • Medication Adherence: Never skip insulin doses or other prescribed diabetes medications without consulting a healthcare provider.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially during illness, to prevent dehydration.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-DKA Prevention: Consistent carbohydrate counting and meal planning are crucial to maintain stable blood glucose and prevent the conditions that lead to DKA.
  • During Acute DKA: Oral intake is typically restricted due to nausea, vomiting, and altered mental status; intravenous fluids are administered for rehydration.
  • Post-Stabilization: Gradual reintroduction of clear liquids and easily digestible foods once nausea subsides and metabolic parameters improve.
  • Hydration Focus: Emphasize continuous hydration with water or sugar-free fluids to prevent dehydration, a key factor in DKA development.
  • Electrolyte Balance: Dietary considerations post-DKA will focus on restoring electrolyte balance, often guided by medical team recommendations.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Assessment: Rapidly assess for signs of DKA, including vital signs, mental status, and breath odor.
  • Notify Physician: Alert the attending physician immediately upon suspicion of DKA for urgent medical intervention.
  • Fluid Resuscitation: Initiate intravenous fluid therapy as prescribed, typically with isotonic saline, to correct dehydration.
  • Insulin Administration: Prepare and administer intravenous insulin infusion as ordered, carefully monitoring blood glucose levels.
  • Electrolyte Monitoring: Closely monitor serum electrolytes, particularly potassium, and prepare for replacement as needed.
  • Frequent Monitoring: Continuously monitor blood glucose, ketones, arterial blood gases, and neurological status.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Recognition is Key: Prompt diagnosis and aggressive management are critical to prevent severe morbidity and mortality associated with DKA.
  • Comprehensive Treatment: Management involves a multi-pronged approach: fluid replacement, insulin therapy, electrolyte correction, and addressing precipitating factors.
  • Identify Triggers: Always investigate and treat the underlying cause of DKA, such as infection, insulin non-adherence, or other acute stressors.
  • Patient Education: Emphasize robust patient education on sick day management, ketone monitoring, and the importance of medication adherence to prevent recurrence.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for potential complications during treatment, including cerebral edema, hypokalemia, and acute kidney injury.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pathophysiology Review: Understand the cascade of insulin deficiency leading to increased counter-regulatory hormones, lipolysis, ketogenesis, and subsequent metabolic acidosis.
  • Fluid Management: Master the principles of IV fluid resuscitation in DKA, including types of fluids and rates of administration based on patient status.
  • Insulin Drip Titration: Learn to safely prepare and titrate continuous intravenous insulin infusions based on blood glucose targets and protocols.
  • Electrolyte Imbalance: Recognize the signs and symptoms of hypokalemia and hyperkalemia, and understand the rationale for potassium replacement in DKA.
  • Neurological Assessment: Perform frequent neurological assessments to detect early signs of cerebral edema, a rare but devastating complication.
  • Patient Education Role: Educate patients and families on DKA prevention, sick day management, and when to seek emergency care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Diagnostic Criteria: Typically includes blood glucose >250 mg/dL, arterial pH <7.3, serum bicarbonate <18 mEq/L, and moderate to large ketonuria/ketonemia.
  • Precipitating Factors: Common triggers include infection (e.g., pneumonia, UTI), insulin omission/non-adherence, new-onset diabetes, myocardial infarction, stroke, pancreatitis, and certain drugs (e.g., corticosteroids, SGLT2 inhibitors).
  • Treatment Goals: Correct dehydration, resolve hyperglycemia, reverse acidosis, restore electrolyte balance, and identify/treat the underlying cause.
  • Insulin Protocol: Usually involves an initial bolus followed by a continuous intravenous infusion of regular insulin.
  • Fluid Replacement: Initial fluid choice is typically 0.9% NaCl, transitioning to 0.45% NaCl once sodium levels are stable, and dextrose-containing fluids when glucose falls below 200-250 mg/dL.
  • Anion Gap: DKA is characterized by a high anion gap metabolic acidosis, which helps differentiate it from other causes of acidosis.
