Femur fracture

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • High-Energy Trauma: Femur fractures are typically caused by significant force, such as motor vehicle accidents, falls from height, or direct impact.
  • Severe Pain & Dysfunction: Patients experience intense pain, inability to bear weight, and often visible deformity of the thigh.
  • Risk of Complications: There is a high risk of significant blood loss, hypovolemic shock, and potential neurovascular injury.
  • Emergency Intervention: Requires immediate medical attention, stabilization, and often surgical repair for optimal outcomes.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Intense Localized Pain: Severe, sharp pain in the thigh that worsens with any movement or attempt to bear weight.
  • Inability to Bear Weight: Complete loss of function in the affected leg, making standing or walking impossible.
  • Limb Deformity: Visible shortening, angulation, or external rotation of the injured leg compared to the uninjured side.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Rapid onset of significant edema and ecchymosis around the fracture site due to internal bleeding.
  • Open Wound: Presence of a laceration or break in the skin, indicating an open fracture with increased risk of infection.
  • Neurovascular Compromise: Numbness, tingling, pallor, coolness, or diminished/absent pulses distal to the injury (requires urgent assessment).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immobilization: Do not attempt to move the patient or manipulate the injured limb; stabilize it in the position found using a splint or improvised support.
  • Bleeding Control: For open fractures, apply direct pressure with a sterile dressing to control bleeding and elevate the limb if safe.
  • Shock Monitoring: Continuously monitor for signs of hypovolemic shock (tachycardia, hypotension, altered mental status) and prepare for fluid resuscitation.
  • Pain Management: Administer appropriate analgesia as soon as medically feasible to alleviate severe pain.
  • Neurovascular Assessment: Regularly assess distal pulses, capillary refill, sensation, and motor function to detect any compromise.
  • Infection Prevention: For open fractures, cover with a sterile dressing and ensure prompt administration of prophylactic antibiotics.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • NPO Status: Maintain nil per os (NPO) status upon admission, especially if surgical intervention is anticipated, to prevent aspiration.
  • Intravenous Hydration: Intravenous fluids will be initiated to maintain hydration, correct electrolyte imbalances, and address potential blood loss.
  • Post-Operative Progression: Gradual reintroduction of oral fluids and a light diet post-surgery, advancing to a regular diet as tolerated and bowel function returns.
  • Nutritional Support for Healing: Encourage a diet rich in protein, Vitamin C, Vitamin D, and calcium post-operatively to support bone healing and tissue repair.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Emergency Call: Contact 911 or your local emergency services without delay.
  • Avoid Movement: Instruct the injured person and bystanders not to move the affected limb or the patient unnecessarily.
  • Support and Comfort: Provide reassurance, keep the patient warm, and maintain a calm environment while awaiting professional medical help.
  • Monitor Vital Signs: Observe the patient's breathing, level of consciousness, and skin color for any changes until paramedics arrive.
  • Information Gathering: Be prepared to provide emergency responders with details about the mechanism of injury and any pre-existing medical conditions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Primary Survey & Resuscitation: Prioritize ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation) and manage life-threatening injuries first, especially in polytrauma patients.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Obtain AP and lateral X-rays of the entire femur, including the hip and knee joints; CT scans may be necessary for complex fractures or associated injuries.
  • Orthopedic Consultation: Early consultation with an orthopedic surgeon is critical, as most femur fractures require surgical fixation (e.g., intramedullary nailing).
  • Pain Management Strategy: Implement a robust pain management plan, often involving intravenous opioids and regional nerve blocks.
  • DVT Prophylaxis: Initiate deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis as soon as medically appropriate to prevent thromboembolic complications.
  • Rehabilitation Planning: Develop a comprehensive post-operative rehabilitation plan focusing on early mobilization, weight-bearing restrictions, and physical therapy.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Initial Assessment Focus: Conduct thorough neurovascular assessments (5 Ps: Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis) and monitor for signs of hypovolemic shock.
  • Immobilization & Transfer: Understand and apply appropriate splinting techniques (e.g., traction splints) and ensure safe patient handling during transfers.
  • Fluid & Electrolyte Management: Monitor IV fluid administration, urine output, and electrolyte levels, recognizing signs of fluid imbalance.
  • Post-Operative Care: Manage surgical drains, perform meticulous wound care, administer prescribed analgesia, and assist with early mobilization protocols.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on pain management, weight-bearing precautions, signs of infection, and the importance of adherence to physical therapy.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: S72.3 (Fracture of shaft of femur), S72.4 (Fracture of lower end of femur), S72.0 (Fracture of neck of femur).
  • Surgical Procedures: Intramedullary nailing (IMN), Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF) with plates and screws, external fixation.
  • Potential Complications: Fat embolism syndrome, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), non-union, malunion, infection, compartment syndrome, sciatic nerve injury.
  • Anatomical Landmarks: Femoral head, neck, greater trochanter, lesser trochanter, femoral shaft, medial and lateral condyles.
  • Pharmacological Interventions: Opioid analgesics, NSAIDs, prophylactic antibiotics (for open fractures), anticoagulants (e.g., LMWH for DVT prophylaxis).
