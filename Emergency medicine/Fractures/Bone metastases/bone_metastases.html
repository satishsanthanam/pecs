Bone metastases

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Bone metastases occur when cancer cells spread from their primary site to the bone, commonly from breast, prostate, lung, kidney, and thyroid cancers.
  • Emergency Presentation: Often presents in the emergency department due to severe pain or a pathological fracture, which is a fracture occurring through bone weakened by disease rather than trauma.
  • Clinical Significance: Can lead to significant morbidity, including severe pain, hypercalcemia, spinal cord compression, and impaired mobility.
  • Prognosis Impact: While often indicating advanced disease, timely management can significantly improve quality of life and prevent severe complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Bone Pain: Persistent, deep, aching pain, often worse at night or with weight-bearing, not relieved by rest.
  • Sudden Onset Pain/Deformity: Indicative of a pathological fracture, even with minimal trauma.
  • Neurological Deficits: Weakness, numbness, tingling, or bowel/bladder dysfunction, suggesting spinal cord compression.
  • Hypercalcemia Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, constipation, excessive thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, confusion, or altered mental status.
  • Generalized Symptoms: Unexplained weight loss, fatigue, or fever, common in advanced cancer.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pathological Fracture Prevention: Handle patients with known or suspected bone metastases with extreme care during transfers, examinations, and positioning to prevent iatrogenic fractures.
  • Spinal Cord Compression Assessment: Immediately assess for new or worsening neurological symptoms; this is a medical emergency requiring urgent imaging and intervention.
  • Hypercalcemia Management: Monitor serum calcium levels closely and initiate aggressive hydration and appropriate pharmacotherapy as indicated.
  • Pain Control: Ensure adequate and timely pain management, often requiring opioid analgesics, and consider adjuvant therapies.
  • Weight-Bearing Restrictions: Implement strict non-weight-bearing or protected weight-bearing protocols for limbs with high fracture risk or confirmed fractures.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration for Hypercalcemia: Encourage generous fluid intake (oral or intravenous) to promote renal calcium excretion and prevent dehydration.
  • Nutritional Support: Provide high-calorie, high-protein nutritional support to combat cachexia and support healing, especially post-fracture or during active treatment.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Recommend increased fiber intake to manage constipation, a common side effect of opioid pain medications.
  • Calcium/Vitamin D Considerations: While generally important for bone health, in cases of hypercalcemia, dietary calcium restriction may be considered under medical guidance.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Advise smaller, more frequent meals to manage nausea and improve overall intake, particularly for patients undergoing chemotherapy or radiation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Gentle Handling: Always use log-rolling techniques for spinal precautions and ensure adequate support for all limbs during movement to prevent fractures.
  • Immobilization: Apply appropriate splints or braces to stabilize suspected or confirmed pathological fractures before and during transport.
  • Pain Assessment: Regularly assess pain intensity and effectiveness of analgesia, advocating for adjustments as needed.
  • Fall Prevention: Implement strict fall prevention strategies, including bed alarms, call bell within reach, and assistance with ambulation.
  • Patient Education: Educate the patient and family on the fragility of bones, signs of worsening symptoms (especially neurological), and the importance of adherence to activity restrictions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prompt Diagnosis: Utilize appropriate imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI, bone scan, PET) to confirm bone metastases and assess fracture risk or spinal cord compression.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Collaborate with oncology, orthopedic surgery, radiation oncology, and palliative care to formulate a comprehensive treatment plan.
  • Treatment Goals: Focus on pain relief, prevention of skeletal-related events (SREs), stabilization of fractures, preservation of neurological function, and optimization of quality of life.
  • Bone-Modifying Agents: Consider bisphosphonates or denosumab to reduce SREs and manage hypercalcemia.
  • Radiation Therapy: Often highly effective for pain control and local tumor control in bone metastases.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Pain Assessment: Utilize validated pain scales (e.g., Numeric Rating Scale, Wong-Baker FACES) and assess pain characteristics (PQRST).
  • Neurological Monitoring: Perform frequent neurological checks, including motor strength, sensation, and reflex assessment, especially for spinal involvement.
  • Fluid Balance Management: Monitor intake and output, urine specific gravity, and signs of dehydration or fluid overload, particularly with hypercalcemia.
  • Safe Patient Mobility: Educate patients on safe transfer techniques and the use of assistive devices to minimize stress on affected bones.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient and family anxiety, fear, and grief related to advanced cancer and its complications.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Skeletal-Related Events (SREs): Pathological fracture, spinal cord compression, hypercalcemia requiring intervention, and radiation/surgery to bone.
  • Imaging Modalities: Plain radiographs, Computed Tomography (CT), Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), Bone Scintigraphy (Technetium-99m bone scan), Positron Emission Tomography (PET) scan.
  • Pharmacological Interventions: Opioid analgesics, NSAIDs, corticosteroids, bisphosphonates (e.g., zoledronic acid), denosumab, targeted therapies, chemotherapy.
  • Surgical Stabilization: Intramedullary nailing, plate fixation, vertebroplasty, kyphoplasty for impending or actual pathological fractures.
  • Radiation Therapy: External beam radiation, stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) for localized pain relief and tumor control.
