Hip fracture

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A break in the upper part of the femur (thigh bone), near the hip joint.
  • Common Causes: Often results from falls in older adults, especially those with osteoporosis, but can also occur from high-impact trauma in younger individuals.
  • Urgency: Considered a medical emergency requiring prompt medical attention and often surgical intervention.
  • Risk Factors: Advanced age, osteoporosis, female gender, certain medications, and chronic medical conditions increase susceptibility.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Pain: Intense pain in the hip or groin area, often worsening with movement or weight-bearing.
  • Inability to Bear Weight: Difficulty or complete inability to stand or put weight on the affected leg.
  • Deformity: The injured leg may appear shorter than the uninjured leg and may be externally rotated (turned outward).
  • Swelling and Bruising: Visible swelling, tenderness, and bruising around the hip and upper thigh.
  • Limited Mobility: Significant restriction in the range of motion of the hip joint.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Immobilization: Avoid moving the patient unnecessarily to prevent further displacement or injury to surrounding tissues.
  • Fall Prevention: Implement home safety modifications (e.g., clear pathways, adequate lighting, grab bars) for at-risk individuals.
  • Osteoporosis Management: Address underlying osteoporosis with appropriate medication and lifestyle changes to reduce future fracture risk.
  • Post-operative DVT Prophylaxis: Strict adherence to deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prevention protocols, including anticoagulants and early mobilization, is critical.
  • Pain Control: Administer appropriate analgesia promptly to manage severe pain while awaiting medical intervention.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines before surgery to prevent aspiration.
  • Post-operative Hydration: Gradual reintroduction of clear fluids, progressing to a soft diet as tolerated, typically after bowel function returns.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Emphasize protein, calcium, and Vitamin D intake to support bone healing and muscle recovery during rehabilitation.
  • Fiber Intake: Encourage adequate fiber and fluid intake to prevent constipation, a common side effect of immobility and opioid pain medication.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Do Not Attempt to Reset: Never try to manipulate or straighten the fractured limb; this can cause further damage.
  • Call Emergency Services: Immediately contact emergency medical services (e.g., 911 or local equivalent) for prompt professional help.
  • Keep Patient Comfortable: Provide blankets to keep the patient warm and support the injured leg with pillows in the position of least pain, if possible, without moving it.
  • Monitor for Shock: Observe for signs of shock, such as pallor, rapid pulse, or confusion, and report to emergency responders.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prompt Diagnosis: Early diagnosis via X-ray is essential for determining fracture type and guiding treatment.
  • Surgical Intervention: Most hip fractures require surgical repair, such as internal fixation, hemiarthroplasty, or total hip arthroplasty, depending on fracture location and patient factors.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: A comprehensive approach involving orthopedics, geriatrics, anesthesia, physical therapy, and social work optimizes outcomes.
  • Aggressive Rehabilitation: Post-operative physical therapy is crucial for regaining strength, mobility, and independence, often starting within days of surgery.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Neurovascular Assessment: Regularly assess the neurovascular status of the affected limb (pulses, sensation, motor function, capillary refill).
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics, assess pain levels, and monitor for adverse effects, especially with opioids.
  • Mobility Assistance: Assist with safe transfers and ambulation, adhering strictly to weight-bearing restrictions and using appropriate assistive devices.
  • Wound Care: Monitor surgical incision for signs of infection (redness, swelling, drainage, fever) and perform dressing changes as ordered.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on post-operative care, fall prevention strategies, and the importance of adherence to rehabilitation exercises.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Fracture Types: Femoral neck fracture (intracapsular), intertrochanteric fracture (extracapsular), subtrochanteric fracture.
  • Classifications: Garden classification (for femoral neck fractures), AO/OTA classification (comprehensive system).
  • Surgical Procedures: Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF), Hemiarthroplasty (partial hip replacement), Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA).
  • Common Complications: Avascular necrosis (especially with femoral neck fractures), non-union, infection, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), delirium.
  • Bone Density Testing: Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) scan for osteoporosis diagnosis.
