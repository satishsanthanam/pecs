Wrist fracture

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A break in one or more of the bones of the wrist, most commonly the distal radius.
  • Common Causes: Often results from a fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH injury), direct trauma, or high-impact sports injuries.
  • Vulnerable Populations: More prevalent in children and the elderly due to bone fragility (osteoporosis in older adults).
  • Immediate Action: Seek urgent medical attention for proper diagnosis and management to prevent long-term complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Pain: Intense pain immediately following the injury, often worsening with movement or pressure.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Rapid onset of localized swelling and discoloration around the wrist joint.
  • Deformity: Visible angulation or displacement of the wrist, sometimes described as a 'dinner fork' or 'bayonet' deformity.
  • Limited Motion: Inability or extreme difficulty to move the wrist or fingers due to pain and structural damage.
  • Numbness or Tingling: Sensation changes in the hand or fingers, indicating potential nerve compression or damage.
  • Tenderness: Extreme sensitivity to touch over the fractured area.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immobilization: Immediately immobilize the injured wrist using a splint, sling, or even a rolled-up newspaper and tape to prevent further injury.
  • Elevation: Keep the injured wrist elevated above heart level to help reduce swelling and pain.
  • Avoid Weight-Bearing: Do not attempt to put any weight or pressure on the injured wrist.
  • Neurovascular Checks: Monitor for signs of compromised circulation or nerve function, such as increasing numbness, tingling, pallor, or coldness in the fingers.
  • Pain Management: Administer over-the-counter pain relievers if appropriate, but avoid NSAIDs if surgery is anticipated soon.
  • Fall Prevention: Implement strategies to prevent falls, especially in elderly patients, to reduce the risk of future fractures.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Bone Healing Nutrients: Ensure adequate intake of calcium (dairy, leafy greens), Vitamin D (fortified foods, sunlight), and protein (lean meats, legumes) to support bone repair and recovery.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration with water to support overall physiological processes, especially important if pain medications are prescribed.
  • Pre-Surgical Fasting: If surgical intervention is planned, adhere strictly to pre-operative fasting guidelines (typically NPO for 6-8 hours prior to anesthesia).
  • Post-Sedation Reintroduction: Following any sedation or anesthesia, reintroduce clear fluids gradually before progressing to solid foods as tolerated.
  • Avoid Inflammatory Foods: Limit processed foods, excessive sugar, and unhealthy fats that may hinder the healing process.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Do Not Manipulate: Never attempt to straighten or realign a visibly deformed wrist; this can cause further damage to nerves, blood vessels, or soft tissues.
  • Ice Application: Apply ice packs wrapped in a cloth to the injured area for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours to reduce swelling and pain, avoiding direct skin contact.
  • Monitor for Compartment Syndrome: Be vigilant for signs of increasing pain disproportionate to the injury, tightness, pallor, or paresthesia, which could indicate compartment syndrome.
  • Emergency Transport: Ensure prompt and safe transport to an emergency department for definitive diagnosis and treatment.
  • Medication Adherence: Follow all prescribed pain management and anti-inflammatory medication instructions carefully.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: A thorough clinical examination combined with X-rays (AP, lateral, oblique views) is crucial for identifying the fracture type, displacement, and involvement of articular surfaces. CT scans may be necessary for complex fractures.
  • Treatment Modalities: Treatment ranges from closed reduction and cast immobilization for stable, non-displaced fractures to open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) with plates and screws for unstable, displaced, or intra-articular fractures.
  • Rehabilitation: Early, guided rehabilitation with physical or occupational therapy is vital post-immobilization or surgery to restore range of motion, strength, and function, preventing stiffness and chronic pain.
  • Complication Awareness: Potential complications include malunion, nonunion, post-traumatic arthritis, nerve injury (e.g., median nerve compression), and complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS).
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on cast care, activity restrictions, signs of complications, and the importance of adherence to rehabilitation protocols for optimal outcomes.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Neurovascular Assessment: Perform frequent '5 Ps' neurovascular checks (Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis) distal to the injury, especially after cast application or reduction.
  • Pain Management: Assess pain levels using appropriate scales and administer analgesics as prescribed, evaluating their effectiveness.
  • Cast/Splint Care Education: Instruct patients on how to care for their cast/splint, including keeping it dry, not inserting objects, and recognizing signs requiring medical attention (e.g., foul odor, severe pain, numbness).
  • Activity of Daily Living (ADL) Support: Assist patients with ADLs and provide adaptive strategies for maintaining independence with one arm immobilized.
  • Discharge Planning: Provide comprehensive discharge instructions covering wound care (if surgical), medication management, follow-up appointments, and rehabilitation referrals.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: S52.5 (Colles' fracture), S52.6 (Smith's fracture), S62.0 (Fracture of scaphoid bone of wrist), S52.52 (Barton's fracture).
  • Common Fracture Types: Colles' fracture (dorsal displacement), Smith's fracture (volar displacement), Barton's fracture (intra-articular with carpal displacement), Scaphoid fracture (often subtle, high risk of nonunion).
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Plain radiographs (AP, lateral, oblique views), CT scan (for complex intra-articular fractures or surgical planning), MRI (for occult fractures like scaphoid or ligamentous injuries).
  • Surgical Procedures: Closed Reduction and Percutaneous Pinning (CRPP), Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF) with volar or dorsal plating, External Fixation.
  • Potential Complications: Carpal Tunnel Syndrome, Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS), Malunion, Nonunion, Tendon Rupture, Post-traumatic Arthritis.
