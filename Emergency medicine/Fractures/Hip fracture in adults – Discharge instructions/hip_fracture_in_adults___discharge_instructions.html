Hip fracture in adults – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Fracture: Your hip fracture required surgical repair. Recovery is a gradual process that demands patience and strict adherence to medical and rehabilitation instructions.
  • Weight-Bearing Status: Your surgeon has specified your weight-bearing restrictions. This is crucial for proper healing and preventing damage to the surgical repair. Ensure you understand if it's non-weight bearing, touch-down weight bearing, partial weight bearing, or weight bearing as tolerated.
  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medications as directed to manage discomfort. Do not wait for pain to become severe before taking medication. Report any uncontrolled pain to your healthcare provider.
  • Follow-Up Appointments: Attending all scheduled follow-up appointments with your surgeon and physical therapist is essential to monitor healing and progress.
  • Rehabilitation is Key: Active participation in physical and occupational therapy is vital for regaining strength, mobility, and independence.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Increased or Unrelieved Pain: Report any sudden increase in pain, or pain that is not managed by your prescribed medication, especially if it's sharp or new.
  • Signs of Infection: Watch for redness, warmth, swelling, pus-like drainage, or foul odor from your surgical incision.
  • Fever or Chills: A temperature above 100.4°F (38°C) or persistent chills can indicate an infection.
  • Calf Pain or Swelling: Unilateral leg swelling, tenderness, or pain in your calf could be a sign of a deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
  • Shortness of Breath or Chest Pain: Sudden difficulty breathing, chest pain, or rapid heart rate could indicate a pulmonary embolism (PE), a serious complication.
  • Numbness or Tingling: Any new or worsening numbness, tingling, or weakness in your affected leg or foot should be reported immediately.
  • Loss of Balance or Dizziness: Report any persistent dizziness or significant loss of balance, which could increase fall risk.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Remove throw rugs, ensure adequate lighting, use assistive devices (walker, crutches) as instructed, and keep pathways clear. Avoid slippery surfaces.
  • Incision Care: Keep your surgical incision clean and dry. Follow specific instructions for dressing changes. Do not soak the incision in baths or pools until cleared by your surgeon.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including pain relievers, blood thinners (if prescribed), and any other chronic medications, exactly as directed. Do not stop blood thinners without consulting your doctor.
  • Hip Precautions: If you had a hip replacement, adhere strictly to any specific hip precautions (e.g., avoiding bending past 90 degrees, crossing legs, or internal rotation) to prevent dislocation.
  • Assistive Device Use: Always use your walker, crutches, or cane as instructed by your physical therapist. Ensure proper fit and technique.
  • Avoid Driving: Do not drive until cleared by your surgeon, especially if you are taking opioid pain medication or cannot safely operate the vehicle.
  • Gradual Activity Increase: Do not overexert yourself. Gradually increase activity levels as tolerated and as advised by your physical therapist.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths) to prevent dehydration, which can contribute to constipation and fatigue.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein (lean meats, fish, beans, dairy) for tissue repair, calcium (dairy, leafy greens) and Vitamin D (fortified foods, sunlight exposure) for bone healing.
  • Fiber Intake: Increase dietary fiber (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) to prevent constipation, which is common with reduced mobility and opioid pain medication.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Alcohol can interfere with medication effectiveness, impair balance, and hinder the healing process.
  • Limit Smoking: Smoking significantly impairs bone healing and increases the risk of complications. Cessation is strongly encouraged.
  • Monitor Blood Sugar: If diabetic, maintain strict blood sugar control, as high blood sugar can impede wound healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assist with Mobility: Provide support and supervision during transfers (bed to chair, toileting) and ambulation, especially in the initial weeks post-discharge.
  • Medication Management: Help organize and administer medications as prescribed, ensuring correct dosages and timings.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for any signs of infection, DVT, PE, or other concerning symptoms listed above, and know when to contact the healthcare team.
  • Safe Home Environment: Ensure the home is free of clutter, well-lit, and that necessary assistive devices are readily accessible. Modify the environment to prevent falls.
  • Emotional Support: Recognize that recovery can be emotionally challenging. Offer encouragement and support, and report any signs of depression or anxiety to the medical team.
  • Transportation: Arrange for transportation to follow-up appointments and therapy sessions.
  • Personal Care Assistance: Provide assistance with bathing, dressing, and other activities of daily living as needed, respecting the patient's privacy and promoting independence where possible.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Commitment to Rehabilitation: Your active engagement in physical and occupational therapy is paramount for a successful recovery and regaining functional independence.
  • Long-Term Bone Health: Discuss with your physician about bone density testing (DEXA scan) and potential treatments for osteoporosis, as this is a common underlying factor in hip fractures.
  • Realistic Expectations: Recovery from a hip fracture is a marathon, not a sprint. Celebrate small victories, but understand that full recovery can take several months to a year.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact your healthcare team with any questions, concerns, or new symptoms. Early intervention can prevent minor issues from becoming major complications.
  • Preventing Future Falls: Proactively address any risk factors for falls in your home and lifestyle to prevent future injuries.
  • Medication Review: Ensure all your medications are reviewed by your physician to identify any that might increase fall risk or interact negatively.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Discharge Teaching: Reinforce all aspects of discharge instructions, including medication regimen, wound care, activity restrictions, and signs/symptoms to report, ensuring patient and caregiver comprehension.
  • Pain Assessment and Management: Regularly assess pain levels using appropriate scales and evaluate the effectiveness of analgesic interventions, adjusting as needed in collaboration with the physician.
  • Mobility and Transfer Training: Provide hands-on demonstration and return demonstration of safe transfer techniques and proper use of assistive devices to ensure patient and caregiver competency.
  • DVT/PE Prophylaxis Education: Educate on the importance of prescribed anticoagulants, early ambulation, and leg exercises to prevent venous thromboembolism.
  • Wound Care Principles: Teach aseptic technique for incision care, emphasizing hand hygiene, recognition of infection signs, and appropriate dressing changes.
  • Nutritional Support: Counsel on dietary choices that promote healing and prevent constipation, emphasizing protein, calcium, Vitamin D, and fiber.
  • Psychosocial Support: Assess for and address any psychological impacts of the fracture and recovery, such as anxiety, depression, or fear of falling, and provide appropriate referrals.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Hip Fracture Classification: Common types include femoral neck fractures (intracapsular), intertrochanteric fractures, and subtrochanteric fractures (extracapsular), influencing surgical approach and prognosis.
  • Surgical Interventions: May include Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF) with screws/plates, hemiarthroplasty (partial hip replacement), or total hip arthroplasty (THA), depending on fracture type and patient factors.
  • Post-Operative Complications: Potential complications include non-union, malunion, avascular necrosis (especially with femoral neck fractures), infection, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), and prosthetic dislocation (for arthroplasty).
  • Anticoagulation Therapy: Often prescribed post-operatively to prevent DVT and PE, commonly low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) or oral anticoagulants, requiring careful monitoring.
  • Osteoporosis Management: A significant risk factor for hip fractures; post-fracture evaluation and treatment for osteoporosis are crucial for secondary prevention.
  • Rehabilitation Protocols: Structured physical and occupational therapy programs are tailored to the surgical repair and patient's functional status, progressing from bed mobility to ambulation and functional activities.
  • Weight-Bearing Status (WBS): Determined by the surgeon based on fracture stability and surgical fixation, ranging from non-weight bearing (NWB) to weight bearing as tolerated (WBAT).
