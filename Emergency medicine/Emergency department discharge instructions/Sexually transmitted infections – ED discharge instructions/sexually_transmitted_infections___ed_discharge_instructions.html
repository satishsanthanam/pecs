Sexually transmitted infections – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Diagnosis: You have been diagnosed with a sexually transmitted infection (STI). It is crucial to understand the specific infection, its treatment, and how to prevent further transmission.
  • Importance of Treatment Adherence: Complete the entire course of medication as prescribed, even if symptoms improve. Stopping early can lead to treatment failure, resistance, and recurrence.
  • Partner Notification and Treatment: It is vital to inform all recent sexual partners so they can be tested and treated. This prevents re-infection and stops the spread of the STI in the community.
  • Risk of Re-infection: You can get an STI again if you have unprotected sex with an infected partner, even after successful treatment.
  • Follow-up Care: Schedule and attend all recommended follow-up appointments with your primary care provider or a specialized clinic to ensure the infection has cleared and to discuss long-term prevention strategies.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Unusual Genital Discharge: Any new or worsening discharge from the penis, vagina, or anus that is discolored, foul-smelling, or increased in volume.
  • Genital Sores or Rashes: The appearance of new blisters, ulcers, warts, or rashes on or around the genitals, anus, or mouth.
  • Painful Urination (Dysuria): Burning or discomfort during urination, which may indicate a urinary tract infection or an STI affecting the urethra.
  • Pelvic or Abdominal Pain: Persistent pain in the lower abdomen or pelvis, which could indicate a more serious infection like Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) in women.
  • Testicular Pain or Swelling: Discomfort, tenderness, or swelling in one or both testicles, potentially indicating epididymitis or orchitis.
  • Rectal Symptoms: Pain, discharge, bleeding, or itching in the anal or rectal area, especially after anal sex.
  • Fever and Chills: Systemic symptoms like fever, chills, body aches, or swollen lymph nodes, which can accompany certain STIs.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Abstinence During Treatment: Avoid all sexual activity (vaginal, anal, oral) until you and your partner(s) have completed treatment and your healthcare provider confirms the infection has cleared.
  • Consistent Condom Use: Once sexual activity resumes, consistently and correctly use barrier methods (condoms, dental dams) to reduce the risk of future STI transmission.
  • Avoid Sharing Personal Items: Do not share towels, undergarments, or other personal items that may come into contact with infected bodily fluids or sores.
  • Regular Screening: Consider regular STI screening, especially if you have multiple partners or engage in high-risk sexual behaviors.
  • Vaccination: Discuss available vaccinations, such as the HPV vaccine, with your healthcare provider to prevent certain STIs.
  • Hygiene Practices: Maintain good personal hygiene, but avoid harsh soaps or douches that can irritate genital tissues and potentially worsen symptoms or disrupt natural flora.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support overall health and kidney function, especially if experiencing fever or systemic symptoms.
  • Alcohol Restriction with Specific Medications: If prescribed metronidazole (Flagyl) or tinidazole, strictly avoid alcohol consumption during treatment and for at least 24-72 hours after the last dose, as it can cause severe nausea, vomiting, and abdominal cramps.
  • Supportive Nutrition: Focus on a balanced diet to support your immune system during recovery, though no specific dietary restrictions are universally required for all STIs.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Return to ED for Worsening Symptoms: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience worsening pain, high fever (over 101°F or 38.3°C), severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, new or spreading rashes, or difficulty breathing.
  • Allergic Reaction Signs: Watch for signs of an allergic reaction to medication, such as hives, rash, swelling of the face/lips/tongue, or difficulty breathing. Seek emergency care immediately if these occur.
  • Medication Adherence is Non-Negotiable: Do NOT stop your medication early, even if you feel better. Incomplete treatment can lead to resistant infections and chronic complications.
  • Ensure Partner Treatment: Your partner(s) must also be treated to prevent re-infection and further spread. Provide them with the necessary information or resources for testing and treatment.
  • Avoid Self-Treatment: Do not attempt to treat yourself with over-the-counter remedies or unprescribed medications, as this can mask symptoms or delay effective treatment.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Care is Key: An STI diagnosis requires a holistic approach, including effective treatment, partner management, and education on prevention.
  • Long-Term Health Implications: Untreated STIs can lead to serious long-term health issues, including infertility, chronic pain, certain cancers, and increased susceptibility to HIV.
  • Open Communication: Be honest with your healthcare provider about your sexual history to ensure accurate diagnosis and appropriate management.
  • Empowerment Through Education: Understanding STIs empowers you to make informed decisions about your sexual health and protect yourself and your partners.
  • Public Health Responsibility: Partner notification and treatment are not just for your health but are crucial public health measures to control STI prevalence.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Priority: Nurses play a critical role in educating patients on medication adherence, safe sex practices, and the importance of partner notification.
  • Emotional Support: Provide empathetic and non-judgmental support to patients, addressing potential stigma and anxiety associated with an STI diagnosis.
  • Resource Provision: Connect patients with local public health clinics, sexual health services, and counseling resources for ongoing care and support.
  • Follow-up Coordination: Assist patients in scheduling follow-up appointments and understanding the necessity of post-treatment testing.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Ensure thorough and accurate documentation of patient education, discharge instructions, and referrals.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Common STIs: Chlamydia trachomatis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Treponema pallidum (Syphilis), Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV), Human Papillomavirus (HPV), Trichomonas vaginalis.
  • Diagnostic Methods: Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAAT) for bacterial STIs, serology for Syphilis and HIV, viral culture or PCR for HSV, microscopy for Trichomonas.
  • Treatment Regimens: Specific antibiotic or antiviral therapies based on the identified pathogen and local resistance patterns (e.g., azithromycin/doxycycline for Chlamydia, ceftriaxone for Gonorrhea, penicillin for Syphilis).
  • Public Health Reporting: Certain STIs (e.g., Gonorrhea, Chlamydia, Syphilis, HIV) are reportable diseases to public health authorities to facilitate surveillance and contact tracing.
  • CDC Guidelines: Refer to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) STI Treatment Guidelines for current evidence-based recommendations on diagnosis, treatment, and prevention.
