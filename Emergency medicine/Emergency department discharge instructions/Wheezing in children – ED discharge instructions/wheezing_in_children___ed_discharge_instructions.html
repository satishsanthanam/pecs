Wheezing in children – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Wheezing: Wheezing is a high-pitched whistling sound heard during breathing, often indicating narrowed airways in the lungs.
  • Common Causes: In children, wheezing is frequently caused by viral infections (like bronchiolitis or common cold), asthma, or allergies.
  • Airway Inflammation: The narrowing of airways is usually due to inflammation and mucus production, making it harder for air to pass through.
  • Follow-up Care: It is crucial to follow up with your child's pediatrician or a specialist for ongoing management and to identify underlying causes.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Cough: Persistent or worsening cough, often dry or productive of clear mucus.
  • Rapid Breathing (Tachypnea): Your child may be breathing faster than usual.
  • Shortness of Breath: Difficulty catching breath, especially during activity or feeding.
  • Retractions: Visible pulling in of the skin between the ribs, above the collarbone, or below the rib cage with each breath.
  • Nasal Flaring: Widening of the nostrils with each breath, indicating increased effort.
  • Difficulty Feeding: Infants may struggle to feed due to breathing difficulties, leading to poor intake.
  • Irritability or Lethargy: Changes in your child's usual behavior, such as increased fussiness or unusual tiredness.
  • Cyanosis: Bluish discoloration around the lips or fingertips, indicating severe oxygen deprivation (requires immediate medical attention).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Adherence: Administer all prescribed medications (e.g., bronchodilators, steroids) exactly as directed by the emergency physician.
  • Proper Inhaler/Nebulizer Use: Ensure correct technique for using metered-dose inhalers with spacers or nebulizers; improper use reduces effectiveness.
  • Trigger Avoidance: Identify and minimize exposure to potential triggers such as tobacco smoke, pet dander, dust mites, and strong odors.
  • Environmental Control: Maintain a clean, dust-free home environment and consider using air purifiers if allergens are a concern.
  • Hand Hygiene: Practice frequent hand washing to prevent the spread of viral infections that can exacerbate wheezing.
  • Avoid Over-the-Counter Cough Suppressants: Do not use cough suppressants in young children without explicit medical advice, as they can be harmful.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Encourage Hydration: Offer frequent small amounts of clear fluids (water, diluted juice, oral rehydration solution) to help thin secretions and prevent dehydration.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: If your child is experiencing breathing difficulties, offer smaller, more frequent meals to reduce the effort associated with eating large portions.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit exposure to foods or drinks that may trigger reflux or worsen coughing, such as very cold beverages or highly acidic foods, if they seem to exacerbate symptoms.
  • Monitor Feeding Tolerance: Observe for signs of increased respiratory distress during feeding and adjust feeding methods as needed (e.g., shorter feeding times, smaller volumes).

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Worsening Symptoms: Continuously observe your child for any signs of increased breathing difficulty, such as faster breathing, deeper retractions, or bluish discoloration.
  • Emergency Return Criteria: Return to the Emergency Department immediately if your child experiences severe shortness of breath, difficulty speaking or crying, bluish lips or face, extreme lethargy, or if medications are not improving symptoms.
  • Medication Schedule: Keep a clear record of medication times and doses to ensure proper administration and avoid missed doses.
  • Comfort Measures: Keep your child calm and comfortable; crying can worsen breathing difficulties. Elevate the head of the bed slightly if tolerated.
  • Follow-up Appointment: Schedule and attend the recommended follow-up appointment with your child's primary care provider or pulmonologist within the specified timeframe.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough evaluation is essential to differentiate between viral-induced wheezing, asthma, and other respiratory conditions.
  • Asthma Action Plan: For children with diagnosed asthma, ensure parents understand and have a written asthma action plan for managing exacerbations at home.
  • Education on Red Flags: Clearly communicate the specific signs of respiratory distress that warrant an immediate return to the ED.
  • Medication Review: Review all prescribed medications, including their purpose, dosage, frequency, and potential side effects, ensuring parents are confident in administration.
  • Long-term Management: Emphasize the importance of ongoing primary care and specialist follow-up for chronic wheezing to optimize control and prevent future ED visits.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Respiratory Assessment: Perform a comprehensive respiratory assessment including respiratory rate, effort, oxygen saturation, auscultation for adventitious sounds, and presence of retractions or nasal flaring.
  • Patient/Family Education: Educate caregivers on proper medication administration techniques (e.g., spacer use, nebulizer setup), symptom monitoring, and when to seek emergency care.
  • Discharge Planning: Ensure discharge instructions are clear, concise, and culturally sensitive, providing written materials and demonstrating techniques as needed.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Verify all prescribed medications against the child's home medication list and ensure no contraindications or drug interactions.
  • Follow-up Coordination: Facilitate scheduling of follow-up appointments with primary care or specialty clinics and provide contact information.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address parental anxiety and provide reassurance while reinforcing the importance of vigilance and adherence to the care plan.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Bronchiolitis: Acute viral infection of the lower respiratory tract, primarily affecting infants, often caused by RSV, leading to inflammation and wheezing.
  • Asthma: Chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airflow obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and wheezing.
  • Bronchodilators: Medications (e.g., albuterol) that relax the smooth muscles of the airways, opening them up and easing breathing.
  • Corticosteroids: Anti-inflammatory medications (e.g., prednisolone, dexamethasone) used to reduce airway swelling and mucus production.
  • Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV): A common respiratory virus that is a leading cause of bronchiolitis and pneumonia in infants and young children.
  • Spacers: Devices used with metered-dose inhalers to improve drug delivery to the lungs, especially in children.
  • Pulse Oximetry: Non-invasive method to monitor oxygen saturation (SpO2) in the blood, indicating respiratory efficiency.
  • Tachypnea: Abnormally rapid breathing, a common sign of respiratory distress in children.
