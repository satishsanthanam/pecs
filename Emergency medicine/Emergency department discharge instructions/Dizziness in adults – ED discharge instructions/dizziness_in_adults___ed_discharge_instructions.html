Dizziness in adults – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • ED Evaluation: Your visit to the Emergency Department focused on ruling out life-threatening causes of dizziness, such as stroke, heart conditions, or severe infections.
  • Common Causes: Dizziness is a symptom, not a disease, and can stem from various benign conditions including inner ear problems (e.g., BPPV, labyrinthitis), orthostatic hypotension, dehydration, medication side effects, or anxiety.
  • Follow-Up Importance: While serious conditions have been excluded at this time, ongoing dizziness requires follow-up with your primary care provider to identify and manage the underlying cause.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • New or Worsening Headache: Especially if sudden, severe, or "the worst headache of your life."
  • Weakness or Numbness: Sudden onset of weakness or numbness on one side of the body or face.
  • Vision Changes: Sudden double vision, loss of vision, or difficulty seeing.
  • Speech Difficulty: Slurred speech, difficulty finding words, or understanding others.
  • Difficulty Walking: Sudden loss of balance, inability to walk, or severe unsteadiness.
  • Chest Pain or Shortness of Breath: New or worsening chest discomfort, palpitations, or difficulty breathing.
  • Loss of Consciousness: Fainting or passing out.
  • Persistent Vomiting: Inability to keep fluids down.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Move slowly when changing positions, especially when getting out of bed or standing up. Use handrails on stairs and avoid rugs or clutter that could cause tripping.
  • Avoid Driving & Machinery: Do not drive or operate heavy machinery until your dizziness has completely resolved and you feel stable and alert.
  • Medication Review: Discuss all current medications with your primary care provider, as some drugs can cause or worsen dizziness. Avoid taking new over-the-counter medications without consulting a healthcare professional.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake unless otherwise instructed by your doctor, as dehydration can exacerbate dizziness.
  • Safe Environment: Ensure good lighting in your home, especially at night. Consider using a cane or walker if unsteadiness is significant.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of water and clear fluids throughout the day to prevent dehydration, which can worsen dizziness.
  • Avoid Caffeine & Alcohol: Limit or avoid caffeine and alcoholic beverages, as they can dehydrate you and potentially exacerbate dizziness or inner ear symptoms.
  • Regular Meals: Eat regular, balanced meals to prevent low blood sugar (hypoglycemia), which can cause lightheadedness.
  • Sodium Intake: If diagnosed with Meniere's disease or similar inner ear conditions, your doctor may recommend a low-sodium diet; otherwise, maintain a balanced intake.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Worsening Symptoms: Observe the patient closely for any new or worsening symptoms listed in the 'Associated Symptoms' section.
  • Ensure Safety: Help the patient with ambulation, ensure a clear path, and assist with getting up from sitting or lying positions to prevent falls.
  • Medication Adherence: Assist the patient in understanding and adhering to any prescribed medications and follow-up appointments.
  • Emergency Contact: Be prepared to call emergency services (e.g., 911) if the patient experiences any red-flag symptoms or a sudden decline in condition.
  • Support & Reassurance: Provide emotional support and reassurance, as dizziness can be frightening and anxiety-provoking.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Exclusion of Acute Threats: Your ED evaluation has focused on excluding acute, life-threatening causes of dizziness. The tests performed were reassuring for these conditions at this time.
  • Importance of Follow-Up: It is crucial to follow up with your primary care physician within 1-3 days, or as advised, to further investigate the underlying cause of your dizziness and develop a long-term management plan.
  • Symptomatic Management: While awaiting further evaluation, focus on safety measures and symptom management as outlined in your discharge instructions.
  • Medication Review: Be prepared to discuss all your current medications, including over-the-counter drugs and supplements, with your PCP, as medication adjustments may be necessary.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Dizziness requires a thorough history (onset, duration, triggers, associated symptoms) and physical exam (neurological, cardiovascular, vestibular).
  • Red Flag Identification: Nurses must be vigilant for "red flag" symptoms (e.g., focal neurological deficits, severe headache, chest pain) indicating serious underlying pathology.
  • Fall Risk Assessment: Implement and educate patients on fall prevention strategies, especially for elderly or frail individuals discharged with dizziness.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Review all medications for potential side effects or interactions contributing to dizziness. Educate patients on proper medication use.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear, concise discharge instructions on symptom monitoring, safety precautions, and the importance of follow-up care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Differential Diagnoses: Orthostatic hypotension, Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV), Meniere's disease, vestibular neuritis/labyrinthitis, stroke (posterior circulation), cardiac arrhythmias, dehydration, medication toxicity, anxiety disorders.
  • Diagnostic Workup: May include orthostatic vital signs, EKG, complete blood count (CBC), electrolytes, glucose, CT head/MRI brain (if neurological deficits or red flags), Dix-Hallpike maneuver (for BPPV).
  • Management Principles: Symptomatic relief (antiemetics, antihistamines, benzodiazepines for acute vertigo), fall prevention, identification and treatment of underlying cause, patient education.
  • Vestibular Rehabilitation: A specialized therapy often recommended for chronic dizziness or specific vestibular disorders.
