Taking medicines – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understand Your Prescriptions: Always clarify the name, dosage, frequency, and purpose of each new medication before leaving the ED.
  • Medication List: Keep an updated list of all current medications, including over-the-counter drugs, supplements, and herbal remedies, to share with all healthcare providers.
  • Adherence is Key: Take medications exactly as prescribed, even if you start feeling better, to ensure full recovery and prevent recurrence.
  • Discharge Summary Review: Carefully review your discharge summary for medication changes, new prescriptions, and follow-up instructions.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Allergic Reactions: Watch for hives, rash, itching, swelling of the face/lips/tongue, or difficulty breathing, which require immediate medical attention.
  • Unexpected Side Effects: Report any new or worsening symptoms such as severe nausea, dizziness, unusual fatigue, or changes in mood to your doctor or pharmacist.
  • Lack of Improvement: If your original symptoms do not improve or worsen after starting the medication, contact your healthcare provider.
  • Drug-Specific Adverse Events: Be aware of specific adverse effects listed for your particular medications (e.g., stomach upset with NSAIDs, muscle pain with statins).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Do Not Share Medications: Never share your prescription medications with others, and do not take medications prescribed for someone else.
  • Avoid Double Dosing: If you miss a dose, do not take two doses at once unless specifically instructed by your doctor or pharmacist. Follow instructions for missed doses.
  • Drug Interactions: Inform your doctor and pharmacist about all other medications, supplements, and herbal products you are taking to prevent harmful interactions.
  • Safe Storage: Store medications in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and out of reach of children and pets. Do not store in the bathroom medicine cabinet due to humidity.
  • Expiration Dates: Check expiration dates and safely dispose of expired or unused medications according to local guidelines.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Food Interactions: Some medications must be taken with food to reduce stomach upset (e.g., NSAIDs), while others require an empty stomach for optimal absorption (e.g., levothyroxine).
  • Grapefruit Warning: Be aware that grapefruit and grapefruit juice can interact with many medications, including certain statins, blood pressure drugs, and immunosuppressants.
  • Alcohol Restrictions: Avoid alcohol when taking medications that can cause drowsiness, liver damage, or other adverse reactions (e.g., opioids, benzodiazepines, metronidazole).
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration unless otherwise instructed, as some medications require sufficient fluid intake to prevent kidney issues.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Medication Reconciliation: Assist the patient in creating and maintaining an accurate, up-to-date list of all medications, including dosage and frequency.
  • Dispensing Assistance: Help ensure prescriptions are filled promptly and correctly, and understand how to administer medications if the patient requires assistance.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Closely monitor the patient for any new or worsening symptoms, side effects, or signs of allergic reaction, and know when to seek medical help.
  • Appointment Reminders: Help the patient schedule and attend follow-up appointments with their primary care physician or specialists.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Open Communication: Always feel comfortable asking your doctor or pharmacist questions about your medications, including their purpose, potential side effects, and how to take them.
  • Follow-Up Care: Adhere to all recommended follow-up appointments to monitor your condition and adjust medication regimens as needed.
  • Report Concerns: Do not hesitate to report any concerns, adverse effects, or difficulties with medication adherence to your healthcare provider.
  • Pharmacist as a Resource: Utilize your pharmacist as a valuable resource for medication information, potential interactions, and proper usage.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Priority: Emphasize comprehensive patient education on discharge medications, including teach-back methods to confirm understanding.
  • Medication Reconciliation Process: Understand the critical role of medication reconciliation at admission, transfer, and discharge to prevent errors.
  • Adverse Drug Event Recognition: Be vigilant in assessing for and recognizing potential adverse drug events and allergic reactions, educating patients on what to report.
  • Resource Utilization: Guide patients to reliable resources for medication information (e.g., pharmacist, trusted websites) and reinforce the importance of adherence.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Medication Adherence Strategies: Explore behavioral and educational interventions to improve patient compliance with prescribed medication regimens.
