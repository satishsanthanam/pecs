Sunburn – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Sunburn Severity: Sunburn is skin damage caused by ultraviolet (UV) radiation, ranging from mild redness to severe blistering.
  • Healing Process: Mild sunburn typically heals within a few days, while severe burns with blistering can take weeks and require careful management.
  • Risk of Complications: Severe sunburn increases the risk of dehydration, heatstroke, and secondary skin infections.
  • Long-Term Effects: Repeated severe sunburns significantly increase the risk of premature skin aging and skin cancer, including melanoma.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Primary Manifestations: Redness, warmth, tenderness, and pain in affected skin areas.
  • Blister Formation: Development of fluid-filled blisters, indicating a second-degree burn, which should not be popped.
  • Peeling Skin: Subsequent peeling of the damaged skin as it heals, typically occurring a few days after the initial burn.
  • Systemic Symptoms: Fever, chills, headache, nausea, or dizziness may indicate severe sunburn or heat-related illness requiring medical attention.
  • Signs of Infection: Increased pain, swelling, redness, pus, or red streaks spreading from a blister site.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Further Sun Exposure: Stay indoors or in shaded areas, especially during peak UV hours (10 AM to 4 PM), until the sunburn has healed.
  • Protective Clothing: Wear loose-fitting, tightly woven clothing, wide-brimmed hats, and UV-protective sunglasses when outdoors.
  • Sunscreen Application: Apply broad-spectrum sunscreen with an SPF of 30 or higher to all exposed skin, reapplying every two hours or after swimming/sweating.
  • Avoid Irritants: Do not apply petroleum jelly, butter, or other greasy products to sunburned skin, as they can trap heat and worsen the burn.
  • Do Not Pop Blisters: Leave blisters intact to protect the underlying skin and prevent infection. If a blister breaks, gently clean the area and apply a sterile dressing.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of water, clear broths, and electrolyte-rich fluids (e.g., sports drinks, oral rehydration solutions) to prevent dehydration, especially with blistering.
  • Avoid Dehydrating Beverages: Limit or avoid alcohol, excessive caffeine, and sugary drinks, which can contribute to fluid loss.
  • Nutrient-Rich Foods: Consume a diet rich in fruits and vegetables to support skin healing and provide antioxidants.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: If experiencing nausea or discomfort, opt for small, frequent, bland meals.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Worsening Symptoms: Watch for signs of infection (pus, increasing redness, fever), severe pain unresponsive to medication, or spreading blisters.
  • Signs of Dehydration: Decreased urination, dry mouth, lethargy, or dizziness warrant immediate medical re-evaluation.
  • Fever Management: If fever develops (above 101°F or 38.3°C), seek medical advice, especially if accompanied by chills or confusion.
  • Medication Adherence: Follow all instructions for pain relievers (e.g., ibuprofen, acetaminophen) and topical treatments as prescribed or recommended by the ED physician.
  • When to Return to ED: Return immediately if you experience severe blistering over a large area, signs of infection, severe pain, high fever, confusion, or signs of heatstroke.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Pain Management: Use over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or acetaminophen as directed to manage discomfort.
  • Cool Compresses: Apply cool, damp cloths to the affected areas for 10-15 minutes several times a day to soothe the skin.
  • Moisturize Skin: Apply aloe vera gel or a fragrance-free moisturizer to damp skin after a cool shower or bath to help retain moisture and reduce peeling.
  • Avoid Picking or Peeling: Allow the skin to shed naturally to prevent further damage and potential scarring.
  • Future Prevention: Emphasize consistent sun protection practices to prevent recurrence and reduce long-term skin damage risks.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients on proper wound care for blisters, signs of infection, and the importance of hydration.
  • Pain Assessment: Regularly assess pain levels and effectiveness of pain management strategies.
  • Hydration Monitoring: Encourage fluid intake and monitor for signs of dehydration, especially in pediatric and elderly patients.
  • Topical Application Guidance: Instruct on the correct application of topical agents (e.g., aloe vera, hydrocortisone cream) and avoidance of irritants.
  • Discharge Planning: Ensure patients understand when to seek follow-up care and the importance of long-term sun protection.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: UV radiation (primarily UVB) causes DNA damage in keratinocytes, leading to an inflammatory response and apoptosis.
  • Classification: Typically classified as a first-degree burn (epidermis only) or superficial second-degree burn (epidermis and superficial dermis) if blistering occurs.
  • Management Principles: Focus on pain control, hydration, prevention of infection, and promotion of skin healing.
  • Pharmacology: NSAIDs for inflammation and pain, topical corticosteroids for severe inflammation (short-term), and emollients for skin barrier support.
  • Complications: Increased risk of actinic keratoses, basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma with cumulative exposure.
