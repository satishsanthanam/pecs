Motor vehicle crash (adult) – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Potential for Delayed Symptoms: Injuries from a motor vehicle crash may not be immediately apparent. It is crucial to monitor for new or worsening symptoms over the next several days to weeks.
  • Importance of Follow-Up: You must follow up with your primary care provider or a specialist as instructed by the emergency department physician. This is vital for ongoing assessment and management of your injuries.
  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medications exactly as directed. Do not exceed the recommended dose and avoid combining with other sedatives or alcohol.
  • Activity Restrictions: Limit strenuous activities, heavy lifting, and sudden movements as advised. Gradual return to normal activities should be guided by your healthcare provider.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Worsening Headache or Confusion: Any increase in headache severity, dizziness, blurred vision, difficulty concentrating, memory problems, or changes in consciousness.
  • New or Worsening Neck/Back Pain: Severe or radiating pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness in your arms or legs.
  • Abdominal Pain or Swelling: Persistent or worsening pain in your abdomen, distension, blood in urine or stool, or unexplained fainting.
  • Chest Pain or Shortness of Breath: Difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, coughing up blood, or rapid heart rate.
  • Extremity Changes: Increased swelling, severe pain, inability to move a limb, coldness, paleness, or tingling in an injured extremity.
  • Persistent Nausea or Vomiting: Inability to keep fluids down, especially if accompanied by headache or dizziness.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Strenuous Activities: Refrain from heavy lifting, vigorous exercise, or activities that could exacerbate your injuries until cleared by a healthcare professional.
  • Medication Safety: Adhere strictly to the prescribed medication regimen. Do not share medications, and avoid alcohol while taking pain relievers or sedatives.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive if you are experiencing significant pain, dizziness, impaired judgment, or if you are taking medications that cause drowsiness.
  • Monitor for Changes: Continuously observe yourself for any new or worsening symptoms. Keep a symptom diary if helpful.
  • Concussion Protocol: If a concussion is suspected, ensure adequate cognitive and physical rest. Avoid screens, loud noises, and mentally demanding tasks initially.
  • Seek Support: Arrange for a responsible adult to stay with you for at least the first 24-48 hours, especially if a head injury was sustained, to monitor for changes.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths, electrolyte solutions) to prevent dehydration, especially if experiencing nausea or vomiting.
  • Light, Bland Meals: If experiencing nausea or stomach upset, start with small, frequent meals of bland foods such as toast, crackers, rice, or applesauce.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Strictly avoid alcohol consumption, as it can interact negatively with many medications and impair your recovery.
  • Limit Caffeine and Sugary Drinks: Reduce intake of caffeine and highly sugary beverages, which can sometimes exacerbate headaches or anxiety.
  • Medication-Specific Dietary Advice: Follow any specific dietary instructions provided for your prescribed medications (e.g., taking certain medications with food).

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Close Observation: Monitor the patient closely for any changes in their level of consciousness, worsening headache, persistent vomiting, unusual behavior, or new neurological deficits.
  • Medication Adherence: Assist the patient in taking their prescribed medications correctly and on schedule. Keep track of doses and times.
  • Emergency Contact Readiness: Be prepared to seek immediate medical attention if the patient develops any concerning symptoms listed in the 'Associated Symptoms' section.
  • Safe Environment: Ensure the patient has a safe and quiet environment for rest and recovery, free from potential hazards.
  • Support and Assistance: Provide practical assistance with daily activities as needed and offer emotional support during their recovery.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: While a thorough evaluation was performed in the ED, some injuries may not manifest immediately. Vigilance for evolving symptoms is paramount.
  • Adherence to Follow-Up: Timely follow-up with appropriate specialists (e.g., orthopedics, neurology, physical therapy) is critical for optimal recovery and to prevent long-term complications.
  • Pain Management Strategy: Utilize prescribed analgesics judiciously. Consider non-pharmacological pain relief methods in conjunction with medication.
  • Rest and Rehabilitation: Emphasize the importance of adequate rest and gradual, guided rehabilitation to facilitate healing and restore function.
  • Psychological Impact: Acknowledge the potential for psychological distress post-MVC. Encourage seeking support if symptoms of anxiety, depression, or PTSD arise.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Systematically review all discharge instructions, ensuring patient comprehension regarding medication, activity restrictions, and red flag symptoms.
  • Pain Assessment and Management: Educate patients on using a pain scale, the expected duration of pain, and the proper administration and potential side effects of analgesics.
  • Injury-Specific Care: Provide specific instructions for managing contusions (RICE protocol), minor lacerations (wound care), and concussion symptoms (cognitive rest).
  • Resource Provision: Furnish patients with contact information for follow-up appointments, primary care, and emergency services. Discuss available support groups or counseling if appropriate.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Meticulously document all patient education provided, the patient's demonstrated understanding, and any barriers to learning or adherence.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Common MVC Injuries: Whiplash-associated disorders (WAD), concussion/mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), musculoskeletal sprains/strains, contusions, fractures, internal organ injury (e.g., splenic laceration, pneumothorax).
  • Diagnostic Modalities: X-ray (fractures), CT scan (head, C-spine, abdomen/pelvis for trauma), MRI (ligamentous injury, soft tissue damage, spinal cord assessment).
  • Pharmacological Interventions: NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen), acetaminophen, opioid analgesics (short-term), muscle relaxants (cyclobenzaprine, tizanidine).
  • Potential Complications: Post-concussion syndrome, chronic pain syndromes, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), delayed hemorrhage, compartment syndrome, deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
  • Referral Pathways: Orthopedics, neurosurgery, physical therapy, occupational therapy, pain management, psychology/psychiatry, social work.
