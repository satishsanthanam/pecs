COVID-19 in pregnancy – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Increased Risk in Pregnancy: Pregnant individuals may experience more severe COVID-19 illness compared to non-pregnant individuals, including a higher risk of complications such as preterm birth.
  • Importance of Monitoring: Close monitoring of both maternal symptoms and fetal well-being is crucial during and after COVID-19 infection.
  • Isolation Protocols: Strict adherence to home isolation guidelines is essential to prevent further transmission within the household and community.
  • Vaccination Status: Discuss your COVID-19 vaccination status and any booster doses with your healthcare provider for personalized guidance.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Worsening Respiratory Distress: Seek immediate medical attention for new or worsening shortness of breath, difficulty breathing, or persistent chest pain or pressure.
  • Signs of Hypoxia: Watch for new confusion, inability to wake or stay awake, or pale, gray, or blue-colored skin, lips, or nail beds, indicating low oxygen levels.
  • Pregnancy-Specific Concerns: Report any decreased fetal movement, vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, or signs of preterm labor (e.g., regular contractions, fluid leakage) immediately.
  • General COVID-19 Symptoms: Monitor for fever (100.4°F or 38°C or higher), persistent cough, fatigue, muscle aches, headache, sore throat, congestion, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, or new loss of taste or smell.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Home Isolation: Remain isolated at home for at least 10 days from symptom onset (or positive test if asymptomatic), and until fever-free for 24 hours without fever-reducing medication, and other symptoms are improving.
  • Symptom and Oxygen Monitoring: Monitor your temperature twice daily and, if provided, use a pulse oximeter to check oxygen saturation (SpO2). Report readings below 95% or any significant drops to your provider.
  • Hydration and Rest: Ensure adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration and prioritize rest to support your body's recovery.
  • Medication Management: Take acetaminophen (Tylenol) as directed for fever or body aches. Avoid NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen) unless specifically advised by your obstetrician.
  • Hand Hygiene and Masking: Practice frequent hand washing with soap and water for at least 20 seconds. Wear a well-fitting mask if you must be around others in your home.
  • Avoid Contact with High-Risk Individuals: Do not have close contact with unvaccinated individuals, immunocompromised persons, or those with underlying health conditions during your isolation period.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids such as water, clear broths, electrolyte solutions, and diluted fruit juices to prevent dehydration, especially if you have fever, vomiting, or diarrhea.
  • Easily Digestible Foods: Opt for bland, easily digestible foods like toast, rice, applesauce, bananas, and plain chicken or vegetable broth if experiencing nausea or loss of appetite.
  • Nutrient-Dense Options: When appetite allows, focus on nutrient-rich foods to support your immune system and fetal development, such as fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains.
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit spicy, greasy, or heavily processed foods that may exacerbate gastrointestinal symptoms or discomfort during illness.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Caregiver Protection: Any household members providing care should wear a well-fitting mask, practice rigorous hand hygiene, and maintain physical distance when possible.
  • Monitor for Worsening Symptoms: Attendants should be vigilant for any signs of the patient's condition worsening, especially respiratory distress or changes in mental status.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know when to call 911 or seek immediate medical attention for the patient (e.g., severe shortness of breath, persistent chest pain, new confusion).
  • Household Isolation: If possible, the patient should use a separate bedroom and bathroom. If not, ensure good ventilation and frequent disinfection of shared spaces.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Urgent OB/GYN Follow-up: Schedule an urgent follow-up appointment with your obstetrician or midwife within 24-48 hours to assess maternal and fetal well-being.
  • Individualized Risk Assessment: Your specific risk factors and pregnancy stage will guide further management and monitoring. Adhere strictly to all follow-up recommendations.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications as directed and do not discontinue them without consulting your healthcare provider.
  • Mental Health Support: COVID-19 in pregnancy can be stressful. Do not hesitate to discuss any anxiety or depression with your healthcare provider, who can offer resources and support.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Patient Education: Ensure the patient understands all discharge instructions, including symptom monitoring, isolation protocols, and when to seek emergency care.
  • Fetal Movement Counting: Educate the patient on how to perform fetal movement counts and the importance of reporting any decrease in activity.
  • Resource Provision: Provide contact information for their OB/GYN, local health department, and telehealth services for ongoing support.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Review all current medications, including over-the-counter drugs, to ensure no contraindications or interactions with pregnancy or COVID-19 treatment.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CDC Guidelines for Pregnant People: Refer to the latest Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidance on COVID-19 for pregnant and recently pregnant people.
  • ACOG Recommendations: Consult American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) practice advisories regarding COVID-19 in pregnancy for clinical management.
  • Pulse Oximetry Interpretation: Understand that SpO2 readings below 95% or a drop of 3% or more from baseline may indicate hypoxemia requiring medical evaluation.
  • Preterm Labor Signs: Review the clinical signs and symptoms of preterm labor to ensure prompt recognition and intervention.
