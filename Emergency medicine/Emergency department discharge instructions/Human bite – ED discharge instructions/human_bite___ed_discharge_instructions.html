Human bite – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • High Infection Risk: Human bites carry a significant risk of infection due to the diverse bacteria present in the human mouth. Prompt and thorough treatment is crucial.
  • Wound Care Importance: Meticulous wound care as instructed is vital to prevent complications and promote healing.
  • Follow-up is Key: A follow-up appointment with your primary care provider or a specialist is essential to monitor healing and ensure no infection develops.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Increasing Pain: Worsening pain at the bite site beyond the initial injury.
  • Redness & Swelling: Spreading redness, warmth, or increased swelling around the wound.
  • Pus or Discharge: Any new or increasing pus, foul-smelling discharge, or fluid oozing from the wound.
  • Fever & Chills: Development of a fever (temperature over 100.4°F or 38°C) or chills, indicating a systemic infection.
  • Red Streaks: Red streaks extending from the wound towards the body, which can indicate cellulitis or lymphangitis.
  • Limited Movement: Difficulty moving the affected body part, especially if the bite is near a joint or tendon.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Wound Cleaning: Gently wash the wound with soap and water daily, or as directed by your healthcare provider. Pat dry with a clean cloth.
  • Dressing Changes: Change wound dressings as instructed, using sterile techniques to prevent contamination.
  • Antibiotic Adherence: Take all prescribed antibiotics exactly as directed, even if you start feeling better. Do not stop early.
  • Pain Management: Use over-the-counter pain relievers (e.g., acetaminophen, ibuprofen) as needed for discomfort, following dosage instructions.
  • Avoid Manipulation: Do not pick at, squeeze, or otherwise manipulate the wound, as this can introduce bacteria or delay healing.
  • Tetanus Status: Ensure your tetanus vaccination is up-to-date. If you received a tetanus shot in the ED, be aware of potential arm soreness.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration & Nutrition: Maintain good hydration and a balanced diet to support your body's healing and immune response, particularly important while on antibiotics.
  • Medication with Food: If prescribed oral antibiotics, take them as directed, often with food to minimize stomach upset and improve absorption.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol while taking antibiotics, as it can interfere with medication effectiveness and potentially worsen side effects.
  • Smoking Cessation: If you smoke, consider reducing or stopping, as smoking can impair wound healing and increase infection risk.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Return to ED: Return to the Emergency Department immediately if you develop any signs of worsening infection (increasing pain, redness, swelling, pus, fever), or if the wound re-opens or bleeds excessively.
  • Complete Antibiotics: It is critical to complete the entire course of antibiotics, even if symptoms improve, to prevent antibiotic resistance and recurrence of infection.
  • Follow-up Appointment: Attend your scheduled follow-up appointment with your primary care provider or specialist to ensure proper healing and address any concerns.
  • Hand Hygiene: Always wash your hands thoroughly before and after touching the wound or changing dressings.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Serious Nature: Human bites are not trivial injuries and carry a significant risk of serious infection, including cellulitis, abscess formation, and even osteomyelitis or septic arthritis if near bone or joint.
  • Prophylactic Antibiotics: Prophylactic antibiotics are often prescribed due to the polymicrobial nature of human bite infections, targeting common oral flora.
  • Close Monitoring: Close monitoring for signs of infection is paramount. Early intervention for developing complications can prevent more severe outcomes.
  • Tetanus & Rabies: Tetanus prophylaxis is standard. Rabies prophylaxis is generally not indicated for human bites but is crucial for animal bites.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Focus: Emphasize clear, concise discharge instructions regarding wound care, medication adherence, and signs/symptoms of infection requiring immediate re-evaluation.
  • Wound Assessment: Instruct patients on how to visually assess their wound for changes in color, size, drainage, and surrounding skin integrity.
  • Medication Teaching: Review antibiotic name, dosage, frequency, duration, potential side effects, and importance of completion.
  • Documentation: Document all patient education provided, patient's understanding, and any questions asked or concerns raised.
  • Follow-up Reinforcement: Stress the importance of follow-up care and provide contact information for their primary care provider or clinic.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Common Pathogens: *Eikenella corrodens*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus* species, and anaerobes are frequently isolated from human bite wounds.
  • Antibiotic Regimen: Amoxicillin-clavulanate (Augmentin) is a common first-line choice due to its broad spectrum against typical human oral flora. Other options include doxycycline, moxifloxacin, or clindamycin plus a fluoroquinolone for penicillin-allergic patients.
  • Tetanus Prophylaxis: Administer Tdap or Td based on vaccination history and wound characteristics, according to ACIP guidelines.
  • Wound Management: Bites are often left open to heal by secondary intention to reduce the risk of abscess formation, especially if presenting late or showing signs of infection.
  • Complications: Potential complications include cellulitis, abscess, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, tenosynovitis, and rarely, viral transmission (e.g., Hepatitis B/C, HIV).
