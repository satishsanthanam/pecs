Abdominal pain in adults – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Diagnosis: You were evaluated for abdominal pain. While a specific cause may or may not have been identified, your condition was stable for discharge.
  • Importance of Follow-Up: It is crucial to follow up with your primary care physician or a specialist as directed by the emergency department staff to further investigate the cause of your pain or monitor your recovery.
  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medication as directed. Do not exceed the recommended dose.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, and intense exercise for at least 24-48 hours, or as advised by the ED physician.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Worsening Pain: If your abdominal pain significantly increases in severity, becomes unbearable, or shifts to a new location.
  • Persistent Vomiting: Inability to keep down fluids or food, or vomiting blood (hematemesis).
  • Fever and Chills: A temperature greater than 100.4°F (38°C) accompanied by chills.
  • Changes in Bowel Habits: New onset of severe constipation, inability to pass gas or stool, or bloody/tarry stools (melena).
  • Abdominal Distension: Your abdomen becomes noticeably swollen, hard, or tender to touch.
  • Signs of Dehydration: Decreased urination, dry mouth, dizziness, or lightheadedness.
  • Jaundice: Yellowing of the skin or eyes.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications exactly as instructed. Do not share medications or take unprescribed drugs.
  • Avoid NSAIDs (Unless Advised): Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen) can sometimes irritate the stomach lining or worsen certain conditions; consult your physician before use.
  • Alcohol and Tobacco: Avoid alcohol consumption and tobacco use, as these can exacerbate gastrointestinal symptoms and hinder healing.
  • Monitoring Symptoms: Keep a detailed log of your pain level, associated symptoms, and any changes you observe to discuss with your follow-up provider.
  • Emergency Return: Do not hesitate to return to the Emergency Department immediately if you experience any of the 'Associated Symptoms' listed above.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Start with Clear Liquids: Begin with small sips of clear liquids (water, clear broth, diluted juice) and gradually advance as tolerated.
  • Bland Diet Progression: Once clear liquids are tolerated, introduce bland, easy-to-digest foods such as toast, crackers, rice, applesauce, and plain chicken.
  • Avoid Irritants: Steer clear of fatty, greasy, spicy, acidic, or highly processed foods, as well as caffeine and carbonated beverages, which can irritate the digestive system.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration by drinking plenty of fluids, especially if you have experienced vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than large ones to reduce the burden on your digestive system.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Symptom Recognition: Attendants should be vigilant for any worsening of the patient's pain or the development of new concerning symptoms, especially those listed under 'Associated Symptoms'.
  • Medication Assistance: Ensure the patient takes prescribed medications correctly and on schedule.
  • Support and Comfort: Provide a quiet, comfortable environment for rest and recovery. Assist with light activities if needed.
  • Emergency Transport: Be prepared to transport the patient back to the Emergency Department immediately if their condition deteriorates or if they exhibit red flag symptoms.
  • Follow-Up Reminders: Help the patient schedule and remember their follow-up appointments.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Evaluation: Your ED visit provided an acute assessment and stabilization. Abdominal pain can have numerous causes, and further outpatient evaluation is often necessary to establish a definitive diagnosis.
  • Risk Stratification: Discharge indicates that acute life-threatening conditions were ruled out or managed in the ED, but ongoing monitoring is vital.
  • Personalized Care Plan: Adhere to the specific follow-up instructions provided, as these are tailored to your individual presentation and suspected etiology.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact your primary care provider or return to the ED if you have concerns or if your symptoms change.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Discharge Teaching Focus: Emphasize red flag symptoms requiring ED return, medication reconciliation, dietary progression, and the importance of follow-up care.
  • Pain Assessment: Educate patients on using a pain scale and documenting pain characteristics (location, quality, radiation, timing, severity) for follow-up.
  • Hydration Status: Instruct patients and caregivers on monitoring for signs of dehydration, especially with vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Medication Education: Review specific medication instructions, potential side effects, and drug-food interactions.
  • Cultural Competency: Tailor discharge instructions to the patient's health literacy level and cultural background, ensuring understanding.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Differential Diagnoses: Common ED considerations for abdominal pain include gastritis, gastroenteritis, constipation, irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), diverticulitis, cholecystitis, appendicitis, urinary tract infection (UTI), renal colic, and gynecological issues.
  • Diagnostic Workup: ED evaluation often involves complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), urinalysis, lipase, lactate, and imaging studies such as ultrasound or CT scan, depending on clinical suspicion.
  • Risk Factors for Serious Pathology: Age (extremes), comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, immunosuppression), specific pain characteristics (e.g., sudden onset, severe, radiating), and associated symptoms (e.g., fever, hemodynamic instability) guide further investigation.
  • Follow-up Modalities: May include outpatient gastroenterology consultation, repeat imaging, endoscopy, colonoscopy, or further laboratory testing.
