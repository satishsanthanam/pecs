Eczema – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Eczema is a Chronic Condition: Understand that eczema (atopic dermatitis) is a long-term inflammatory skin condition characterized by dry, itchy skin and rashes.
  • Acute Flare-Up Management: Your visit to the Emergency Department addressed an acute worsening of your eczema symptoms, often involving intense itching, redness, and sometimes infection.
  • Ongoing Management is Key: ED treatment provides immediate relief, but consistent daily skin care and adherence to a long-term management plan are crucial to prevent future flare-ups.
  • Identify Your Triggers: Learning what irritates your skin (e.g., certain soaps, fabrics, allergens, stress) is vital for prevention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Intense Pruritus (Itching): Often severe, especially at night, leading to sleep disturbance and potential skin damage from scratching.
  • Erythema and Inflammation: Red, inflamed patches of skin, which can appear darker on some skin tones.
  • Dry, Scaly Skin: Patches of dry, thickened, or scaly skin, particularly in areas like the bends of elbows and knees, neck, and face.
  • Oozing and Crusting: During acute flares, skin may weep clear fluid and then form crusts, indicating inflammation or potential secondary infection.
  • Lichenification: Thickened, leathery skin resulting from chronic scratching and rubbing.
  • Signs of Infection: Increased pain, warmth, swelling, pus, or fever, which may have prompted the ED visit.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Strict Medication Adherence: Apply all prescribed topical medications (e.g., corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors) exactly as directed by the ED physician. Do not stop prematurely unless advised.
  • Moisturize Regularly: Apply a thick, fragrance-free emollient or moisturizer at least twice daily, especially within three minutes of bathing, to lock in moisture.
  • Avoid Known Triggers: Stay away from irritants like harsh soaps, detergents, perfumes, certain fabrics (wool), and extreme temperatures. Manage stress effectively.
  • Prevent Scratching: Keep fingernails short and clean. Consider wearing cotton gloves at night to minimize scratching damage.
  • Watch for Infection: Monitor for signs of worsening infection such as spreading redness, increased pain, warmth, pus, or fever. Seek medical attention if these occur.
  • Gentle Skin Care: Use lukewarm water for short showers or baths. Pat skin dry gently rather than rubbing.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Adequate Hydration: Ensure sufficient fluid intake throughout the day to support overall skin health and barrier function.
  • Monitor for Food Triggers: If you suspect specific foods exacerbate your eczema, discuss this with your primary care physician or a dermatologist. Do not implement restrictive diets without medical guidance.
  • Avoid Unproven Supplements: Consult with your healthcare provider before taking any dietary supplements marketed for eczema, as their efficacy and safety may not be established.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a generally healthy, balanced diet to support overall well-being, which indirectly benefits skin health.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Return to ED for Worsening Symptoms: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience spreading redness, increased pain, warmth, pus, fever, or if your eczema becomes severely widespread and unmanageable.
  • Signs of Allergic Reaction: If you develop difficulty breathing, swelling of the face/throat, or widespread hives, return to the ED immediately.
  • Uncontrolled Itching/Pain: If prescribed medications are not controlling your itching or pain, contact your primary care provider or dermatologist.
  • Follow-Up Appointment: Schedule a follow-up appointment with your primary care physician or a dermatologist within 1-2 weeks to establish a long-term management plan.
  • Medication Questions: If you have questions about your prescribed medications or experience adverse effects, contact your pharmacy or healthcare provider.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Eczema Requires Ongoing Care: Eczema is a chronic condition that requires consistent management, not just during flare-ups. Adherence to your treatment plan is paramount.
  • Personalized Treatment Plans: Your eczema management plan will be tailored to your specific needs, severity, and triggers. Regular follow-up allows for adjustments.
  • Preventing Complications: Effective management helps prevent secondary infections, sleep disturbances, and the psychological impact of chronic itching.
  • Quality of Life Improvement: Our goal is to control your symptoms, reduce flare-ups, and significantly improve your quality of life.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to communicate any concerns, side effects, or lack of improvement with your healthcare team.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Priority: Educate patients on proper application techniques for topical medications, the importance of emollients, and trigger identification/avoidance.
  • Assessment for Complications: Regularly assess for signs of secondary bacterial (e.g., impetigo) or viral (e.g., eczema herpeticum) infections, and the psychological impact of chronic pruritus.
  • Holistic Care Approach: Address not only physical symptoms but also the impact on sleep, mental health, and quality of life, offering support and resources.
  • Referral Pathways: Understand when to refer patients to dermatology, allergy specialists, or mental health services for comprehensive management.
  • Reinforce Discharge Instructions: Ensure patients and caregivers understand all discharge instructions, including medication schedules, follow-up plans, and red flag symptoms.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Involves skin barrier dysfunction, immune dysregulation (Th2-mediated inflammation), and genetic predisposition (e.g., filaggrin mutations).
  • Common Triggers: Irritants (soaps, detergents), allergens (dust mites, pollen, pet dander, certain foods), stress, temperature changes, low humidity.
  • Pharmacological Management: Topical corticosteroids (various potencies), topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus), antihistamines (for pruritus), systemic corticosteroids (short courses for severe flares), antibiotics (for secondary bacterial infections).
  • Non-Pharmacological Interventions: Regular emollient use, wet wrap therapy, bleach baths, phototherapy, trigger avoidance.
  • Differential Diagnoses: Psoriasis, contact dermatitis (allergic/irritant), seborrheic dermatitis, fungal infections, scabies.
  • Potential Complications: Secondary bacterial infections (Staphylococcus aureus), viral infections (Herpes simplex leading to eczema herpeticum), sleep disturbance, anxiety, depression.
