Hip pain – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Diagnosis: Your hip pain has been evaluated in the Emergency Department. Common causes include muscle strain, ligament sprain, contusion, bursitis, or an exacerbation of underlying arthritis. Serious injuries like fractures have been ruled out or managed.
  • Pain Management: You have been provided with initial pain relief and instructions for ongoing management. Adhere strictly to medication schedules and dosages.
  • Activity Modification: Rest is crucial for initial healing. Avoid activities that worsen your pain. Gradual return to normal activities will be guided by your follow-up provider.
  • Importance of Follow-Up: It is vital to schedule and attend your follow-up appointment with your primary care physician or an orthopedic specialist as instructed by the ED team to ensure proper healing and long-term management.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent or Worsening Pain: If your pain significantly increases despite medication, or if new, severe pain develops.
  • Inability to Bear Weight: If you suddenly cannot put any weight on the affected leg, or if walking becomes impossible.
  • New Numbness or Tingling: Any new or worsening numbness, tingling, or weakness in your leg or foot.
  • Signs of Infection: Redness, warmth, increased swelling, pus drainage, or fever (temperature over 100.4°F or 38°C) around the hip area.
  • Significant Swelling or Bruising: Rapidly increasing swelling or bruising around the hip or thigh.
  • Loss of Bowel or Bladder Control: Though rare, this is an emergency and requires immediate re-evaluation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Use assistive devices (crutches, walker) as instructed. Ensure your home environment is free of tripping hazards (rugs, clutter). Use handrails on stairs.
  • Medication Safety: Take all prescribed medications exactly as directed. Do not exceed recommended doses. Be aware of potential side effects and drug interactions. Avoid alcohol while taking pain medications.
  • Ice and Heat Application: Apply ice packs to the affected area for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours for the first 24-48 hours to reduce swelling. After 48 hours, you may alternate with heat if it provides comfort.
  • Proper Body Mechanics: Avoid twisting at the hip. When moving, keep your body aligned. Use your legs, not your back, to lift objects.
  • Avoid Strenuous Activity: Refrain from heavy lifting, running, jumping, or prolonged standing/walking until cleared by your follow-up provider.
  • Monitor for Complications: Watch for any signs of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) such as calf pain, swelling, or tenderness, especially if you have reduced mobility.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration for Healing: Maintain adequate fluid intake, primarily water, to support overall healing and prevent constipation, especially if taking opioid pain medications.
  • Medication with Food: If prescribed NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen), take them with food or milk to minimize stomach irritation and reduce the risk of gastrointestinal upset.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol, especially while taking pain medications, as it can increase the risk of side effects, liver damage, and sedation.
  • Nutrient-Rich Foods: Focus on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains to provide essential nutrients for tissue repair and recovery.
  • Fiber Intake: Increase dietary fiber to prevent constipation, a common side effect of opioid pain relievers.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assist with Mobility: Help the patient with safe ambulation, especially on stairs or uneven surfaces, ensuring proper use of assistive devices.
  • Medication Reminders: Assist the patient in adhering to their medication schedule and dosage, and help them understand potential side effects.
  • Monitor for Warning Signs: Be vigilant for any worsening symptoms, new neurological deficits, signs of infection, or inability to bear weight, and know when to seek immediate medical attention.
  • Support Follow-Up Care: Help schedule and transport the patient to their follow-up appointments with primary care or specialists.
  • Environmental Safety: Ensure the home environment is safe, well-lit, and free of obstacles to prevent falls.
  • Emotional Support: Provide reassurance and support, as pain and limited mobility can be frustrating and emotionally challenging.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Initial ED Assessment: Your hip pain was thoroughly assessed in the ED to rule out acute, life-threatening conditions or significant fractures. Imaging (e.g., X-rays) was performed as clinically indicated.
  • Diagnosis and Management: The current diagnosis is likely a musculoskeletal injury or exacerbation of a chronic condition. The focus of ED care is acute pain control and safe discharge with a clear follow-up plan.
  • Importance of Adherence: Strict adherence to discharge instructions, including medication regimen and activity restrictions, is crucial for optimal recovery and preventing complications.
  • Follow-Up is Key: The ED visit is a starting point. A definitive diagnosis and long-term management plan often require further evaluation by your primary care physician or an orthopedic specialist. Do not delay your follow-up.
  • When to Return: Understand the specific 'red flag' symptoms that warrant an immediate return to the Emergency Department.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pain Assessment & Reassessment: Utilize a standardized pain scale (e.g., 0-10) to assess pain levels at discharge and educate patients on self-assessment. Emphasize the importance of reporting changes.
  • Mobility & Assistive Devices: Provide clear instructions and demonstrate proper use of crutches, walkers, or canes. Assess patient's ability to ambulate safely before discharge.
  • Medication Education: Review all prescribed medications, including dosage, frequency, purpose, potential side effects, and interactions. Highlight NSAID precautions (take with food) and opioid safety (constipation, sedation).
  • RICE Protocol: Educate on the RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) protocol for acute musculoskeletal injuries, adapting as appropriate for hip pain.
  • Discharge Planning & Referrals: Ensure patient understands the importance of follow-up appointments and provide contact information for primary care or specialist referrals. Document all patient education.
  • Warning Signs Education: Thoroughly review all 'return to ED' criteria with the patient and any accompanying family members, ensuring comprehension.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Differential Diagnoses for Hip Pain: Musculoskeletal strain/sprain, contusion, trochanteric bursitis, osteoarthritis exacerbation, avascular necrosis (AVN), stress fracture, femoral neck fracture, sacroiliac joint dysfunction, referred pain (e.g., lumbar spine).
  • Imaging Modalities: Plain radiographs (X-rays) are initial for fracture exclusion. MRI may be indicated for soft tissue injury, AVN, occult fracture, or labral tears. CT scan for complex fractures.
  • Pharmacological Management: NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen), acetaminophen, muscle relaxants (cyclobenzaprine), short-course opioids (e.g., hydrocodone/acetaminophen) for severe acute pain.
  • Non-Pharmacological Interventions: RICE therapy, activity modification, physical therapy referral, assistive device prescription.
  • Clinical Assessment Pearls: Gait analysis, range of motion assessment (flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, internal/external rotation), palpation for tenderness, special tests (e.g., Faber test, Trendelenburg sign).
  • Red Flag Symptoms: Acute inability to bear weight, severe deformity, open fracture, neurovascular compromise, signs of septic arthritis (fever, warmth, severe pain with passive motion), cauda equina syndrome.
