Shoulder pain – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Pain: Your shoulder pain has been evaluated in the Emergency Department, and serious life-threatening conditions have been ruled out. Common causes include muscle strains, ligament sprains, tendinitis, or bursitis.
  • Expected Recovery: Most shoulder injuries improve with rest, proper pain management, and activity modification. Full recovery can take several weeks to months depending on the specific injury.
  • Importance of Follow-up: It is crucial to follow up with your primary care physician or an orthopedic specialist for ongoing assessment and management to ensure proper healing and prevent chronic issues.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent or Worsening Pain: Pain that does not improve with prescribed medication or rest, or pain that significantly increases in intensity.
  • New Numbness or Tingling: Any new or worsening sensation of pins and needles, or loss of feeling in your arm or hand.
  • Significant Weakness: Inability to lift your arm, or a noticeable decrease in strength compared to before the injury.
  • Signs of Infection: Redness, warmth, increased swelling, or pus drainage around the shoulder area, especially if accompanied by fever or chills.
  • New Deformity: Any visible change in the shape or alignment of your shoulder joint.
  • Inability to Move Arm: Complete inability to move your arm at the shoulder joint.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Rest and Activity Modification: Avoid activities that aggravate your shoulder pain. Limit overhead movements, heavy lifting, and repetitive tasks.
  • Ice Application: Apply ice packs to the affected area for 15-20 minutes, 3-4 times a day, especially during the first 48-72 hours, to reduce swelling and pain. Ensure a barrier (e.g., cloth) between ice and skin.
  • Proper Sling Use: If a sling was provided, wear it as instructed to immobilize and support the shoulder, preventing further injury. Remove it periodically for gentle elbow and hand movements to prevent stiffness, as advised by your physician.
  • Medication Adherence: Take prescribed pain relievers and anti-inflammatory medications exactly as directed. Do not exceed recommended doses.
  • Avoid Re-injury: Be mindful of your movements to prevent falls or sudden jerks that could re-injure the shoulder.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Hydration: Drink plenty of water throughout the day to support overall healing and prevent constipation, which can be a side effect of some pain medications.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol, especially while taking pain medications, as it can interact dangerously and increase side effects like drowsiness or liver damage.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins to provide essential nutrients for tissue repair and recovery.
  • Monitor Medication Interactions: Be aware of any specific dietary restrictions or recommendations provided by your pharmacist regarding your prescribed medications.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • When to Return to ED: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience sudden, severe, unbearable pain, new significant weakness or numbness, signs of infection (fever, spreading redness), or a new deformity.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Schedule an appointment with your primary care provider or an orthopedic specialist within 3-5 days, or as specifically instructed by the ED physician.
  • Medication Side Effects: If you experience severe side effects from your medications (e.g., extreme drowsiness, severe stomach upset, allergic reaction), contact your physician or pharmacist immediately.
  • Driving Restrictions: Avoid driving if you are taking opioid pain medications or if your shoulder injury significantly impairs your ability to safely operate a vehicle.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: While the ED provides acute care, a definitive diagnosis and long-term management plan often require further evaluation, potentially including imaging like MRI and consultation with an orthopedic specialist.
  • Pain Management Strategy: Effective pain control is vital for recovery. This may involve a combination of over-the-counter and prescription medications, along with non-pharmacological methods like ice and rest.
  • Importance of Rehabilitation: For many shoulder injuries, physical therapy is a critical component of recovery, helping to restore range of motion, strength, and function. Adherence to a prescribed exercise program is paramount.
  • Realistic Expectations: Recovery from shoulder injuries can be a gradual process. Patience and consistent adherence to treatment recommendations are key to achieving the best possible outcome.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Focus: Emphasize RICE therapy, proper sling application and removal, medication administration and potential side effects, and clear instructions for follow-up care.
  • Pain Assessment: Teach patients how to self-assess their pain levels and identify when pain is worsening or not responding to treatment.
  • Red Flag Recognition: Educate on specific 'red flag' symptoms (e.g., neurovascular compromise, signs of infection, sudden loss of function) that necessitate immediate re-evaluation.
  • Functional Limitations: Discuss expected temporary functional limitations and strategies for adapting daily activities to prevent re-injury.
  • Resource Provision: Provide contact information for follow-up appointments, pharmacy, and emergency services.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Common Diagnoses: Rotator cuff tendinitis, impingement syndrome, AC joint sprain, bursitis, muscle strain, minor fracture.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: X-ray (to rule out fracture/dislocation), Ultrasound (for soft tissue), MRI (for detailed soft tissue assessment).
  • Pharmacological Interventions: NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen), Acetaminophen, Opioid analgesics (short-term for severe pain), Muscle relaxants.
  • Non-Pharmacological Interventions: RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation), Immobilization (sling), Physical Therapy, Activity Modification.
  • Referral Pathways: Orthopedic surgery, Physical therapy, Primary care physician.
