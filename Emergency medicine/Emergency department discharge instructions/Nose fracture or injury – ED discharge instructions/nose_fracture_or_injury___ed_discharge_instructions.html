Nose fracture or injury – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Nasal Fracture Confirmation: You have sustained a fracture or injury to your nose. While some fractures may not require immediate surgical intervention, proper care is crucial for healing.
  • Expected Swelling & Bruising: Significant swelling and bruising around the nose and eyes are common and may worsen over the first 24-48 hours. This will gradually subside over several weeks.
  • Pain Management: Mild to moderate pain is expected. Manage this with prescribed or over-the-counter pain relievers as directed.
  • Follow-up is Essential: A follow-up appointment with an Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT) specialist or your primary care physician is critical within 3-7 days to assess healing and determine if further intervention (e.g., reduction) is needed.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Increased or Uncontrolled Bleeding: Persistent or heavy bleeding from the nose that does not stop after 15-20 minutes of direct pressure.
  • Severe or Worsening Pain: Pain that is not relieved by medication or significantly increases in intensity.
  • Difficulty Breathing Through Nose: New or worsening obstruction of nasal passages making breathing difficult.
  • Clear Fluid from Nose: Any clear, watery discharge from the nose, especially if it increases when bending forward, could indicate a cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leak.
  • Vision Changes: Double vision, blurred vision, or any new visual disturbances.
  • Signs of Infection: Fever, increased redness, warmth, pus-like discharge from the nose, or worsening tenderness.
  • Changes in Nose Shape: Noticeable new deformity or collapse of the nasal bridge.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Protect Your Nose: Avoid any activities that could lead to further injury to your nose for at least 6-8 weeks, including contact sports, rough play, or situations where you might fall.
  • Avoid Blowing Nose Forcefully: Gently dab any nasal discharge. Forceful nose blowing can dislodge clots, increase bleeding, or worsen swelling.
  • Ice Application: Apply ice packs to the nose and surrounding areas for 15-20 minutes every 1-2 hours for the first 24-48 hours to reduce swelling. Wrap ice in a cloth to prevent skin damage.
  • Elevate Head: Keep your head elevated, especially when sleeping, for the first few days to help reduce swelling. Use extra pillows.
  • Avoid Hot Showers/Baths: For the first 24-48 hours, avoid very hot showers or baths as they can increase swelling and bleeding.
  • Do Not Pick or Probe: Refrain from picking your nose or inserting anything into the nostrils.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications as directed, especially pain relievers and any antibiotics if prescribed.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Soft Diet Initially: Consume soft, easy-to-chew foods for the first few days to minimize facial movement and discomfort.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good hydration by drinking plenty of fluids, such as water, clear broths, and juices.
  • Avoid Very Hot or Spicy Foods: Extremely hot or spicy foods may irritate nasal passages or increase discomfort if there is any oral involvement.
  • Limit Alcohol: Avoid alcohol consumption as it can thin the blood and potentially increase bleeding or interfere with pain medications.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Return: Return to the Emergency Department immediately if you experience any of the 'Associated Symptoms' listed above, especially uncontrolled bleeding, severe difficulty breathing, clear fluid from the nose, or vision changes.
  • Follow-up Appointment: Ensure you schedule and attend your follow-up appointment with an ENT specialist or your primary care physician as advised. This is crucial for monitoring healing and addressing any potential complications.
  • Medication Instructions: Take all medications exactly as prescribed. Do not exceed recommended dosages.
  • Activity Restrictions: Strictly adhere to activity restrictions to prevent re-injury or worsening of the fracture.
  • Caregiver Awareness: If a caregiver is present, ensure they are aware of these instructions and can assist in monitoring for warning signs.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: While initial assessment in the ED addresses acute concerns, a definitive management plan often requires an ENT specialist to evaluate for septal hematoma, significant displacement, or other complex injuries.
  • Functional & Cosmetic Outcomes: Proper follow-up ensures optimal functional (breathing) and cosmetic outcomes. Untreated displaced fractures can lead to chronic nasal obstruction or cosmetic deformities.
  • Realistic Recovery Timeline: Full healing of nasal bones typically takes 6-8 weeks, but swelling can persist for several months. Patience and adherence to instructions are key.
  • Septal Hematoma Risk: Be vigilant for signs of septal hematoma (swelling of the nasal septum), which requires urgent drainage to prevent cartilage necrosis.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Nurses play a vital role in reinforcing discharge instructions, ensuring patient comprehension of pain management, activity restrictions, and warning signs.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Ongoing assessment of pain levels and effectiveness of analgesia is crucial. Educate on non-pharmacological pain relief methods (e.g., ice, head elevation).
  • Monitoring for Complications: Instruct patients and caregivers on how to monitor for signs of infection, CSF leak, or septal hematoma, and when to seek immediate medical attention.
  • Wound Care (if applicable): If there are external lacerations, provide clear instructions on wound cleaning and dressing changes.
  • Follow-up Coordination: Assist patients in understanding the importance of and scheduling their follow-up appointments.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Nasal Bone Fracture Classification: Often categorized by displacement (displaced vs. non-displaced), comminution, and involvement of the nasal septum.
  • Septal Hematoma: Collection of blood between the perichondrium and cartilage of the nasal septum, requiring urgent incision and drainage to prevent pressure necrosis of the cartilage.
  • Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Rhinorrhea: Leakage of CSF from the nose, indicating a breach in the skull base, often associated with high-impact trauma. Requires urgent neurosurgical evaluation.
  • Nasal Reduction: Procedure to realign displaced nasal bones, typically performed within 7-14 days post-injury before bone consolidation. Can be open or closed.
  • Imaging Modalities: While clinical examination is often sufficient, CT scans may be used to assess complex fractures, orbital involvement, or suspected CSF leaks. Plain X-rays have limited utility for nasal fractures.
