Minor contusion – ED discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Emergency department discharge instructions

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding a Contusion: A contusion, commonly known as a bruise, results from blunt trauma that damages small blood vessels under the skin, leading to blood leakage into surrounding tissues.
  • Expected Healing: Most minor contusions resolve within 1 to 3 weeks, with discoloration progressing from red/purple to green, yellow, and brown before fading.
  • Pain Management: Mild to moderate pain is expected and can be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.
  • Swelling: Localized swelling is common due to fluid accumulation and blood extravasation.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Pain: Tenderness and discomfort at the site of injury, often worsening with pressure or movement.
  • Discoloration (Ecchymosis): Initial reddish-purple hue, evolving to blue, green, and yellow over several days as hemoglobin breaks down.
  • Swelling: Visible or palpable enlargement of the affected area due to fluid and blood accumulation.
  • Limited Range of Motion: Mild restriction in movement if the contusion is near a joint or muscle, due to pain and swelling.
  • Tenderness to Touch: Increased sensitivity when the injured area is palpated.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • RICE Protocol: Apply Rest, Ice, Compression, and Elevation to the injured area for the first 24-48 hours to minimize swelling and pain.
  • Ice Application: Apply ice packs wrapped in a cloth for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours during waking hours.
  • Compression: Use a mild compression bandage if advised, ensuring it is not too tight to avoid impairing circulation.
  • Elevation: Keep the injured body part elevated above heart level whenever possible to reduce swelling.
  • Avoid Re-injury: Protect the contused area from further trauma during the healing process.
  • Pain Relievers: Use acetaminophen or ibuprofen as directed for pain relief; avoid aspirin due to its anticoagulant effects.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support overall physiological processes and tissue healing.
  • No Specific Restrictions: Generally, no specific dietary restrictions are required for a minor contusion.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Limit alcohol consumption, as it can potentially thin the blood and exacerbate bruising or swelling.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in vitamins and minerals to support the body's natural healing mechanisms.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Worsening Pain: Return to the ED if pain becomes severe, unmanageable with prescribed medication, or significantly worsens.
  • Increased Swelling or Redness: Seek immediate medical attention if swelling rapidly increases, or if the area becomes excessively red, warm, or develops pus, indicating potential infection.
  • Numbness or Tingling: Report any new onset of numbness, tingling, or weakness distal to the injury, which could suggest nerve compression.
  • Fever: Consult a physician if you develop a fever, especially if accompanied by worsening local symptoms.
  • Inability to Move Limb: If the contusion is near a joint and you experience new or increased inability to move the limb, seek re-evaluation.
  • Persistent Symptoms: If symptoms do not improve significantly within 1-2 weeks, or if new concerning symptoms develop, follow up with your primary care provider.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Benign Nature: Minor contusions are typically benign injuries that heal completely with appropriate self-care.
  • Adherence to RICE: Consistent application of the RICE protocol is paramount for optimal recovery and symptom management.
  • Pain Management Strategy: Utilize over-the-counter analgesics as directed, and understand that some discomfort is normal during the healing phase.
  • Watch for Complications: While rare, be vigilant for signs of complications such as expanding hematoma, compartment syndrome, or infection, and seek prompt medical advice if these occur.
  • Gradual Return to Activity: Gradually reintroduce normal activities as pain subsides, avoiding activities that exacerbate symptoms.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Crucial role in educating patients on the RICE protocol, expected healing timeline, and signs/symptoms warranting re-evaluation.
  • Pain Assessment: Regular assessment of pain using a validated scale and evaluating the effectiveness of pain management strategies.
  • Wound Care (if applicable): If skin integrity is compromised (e.g., abrasion with contusion), provide instructions for wound cleaning and dressing changes.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Instruct patients to monitor for signs of infection (redness, warmth, pus), expanding hematoma, or neurovascular compromise.
  • Documentation: Thoroughly document patient education, discharge instructions, and patient's understanding of care.
  • Follow-up Planning: Assist in scheduling follow-up appointments with primary care or specialists if indicated.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: S00-S99 (Injury, poisoning and certain other consequences of external causes), specific to the anatomical site of the contusion (e.g., S90.32XA for Contusion of left ankle, initial encounter).
  • Pathophysiology: Blunt force trauma leading to capillary and venule rupture, extravasation of blood into interstitial tissues, resulting in ecchymosis and localized edema.
  • Pharmacology: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) like ibuprofen, acetaminophen (paracetamol) for pain relief.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Must differentiate from fractures, sprains, strains, hematomas, and compartment syndrome.
  • RICE Protocol: Acronym for Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation – standard first-line treatment for acute soft tissue injuries.
  • Clinical Assessment: Palpation for tenderness, swelling, crepitus (if fracture suspected), assessment of range of motion and neurovascular status.
