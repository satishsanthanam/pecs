How to use a shoulder sling

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for children

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of a Sling: Immobilizes and supports an injured arm, shoulder, or collarbone to prevent further injury and promote healing.
  • Common Indications: Fractures (clavicle, humerus), dislocations (shoulder, elbow), severe sprains, or post-surgical support.
  • Child-Specific Considerations: Ensure proper fit for a child's anatomy; monitor closely for skin irritation or neurovascular compromise.
  • First Aid Principle: A sling is a temporary measure to stabilize an injury until professional medical help can be obtained.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Pain: Sharp or dull pain in the shoulder, arm, or collarbone, often worsening with movement.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Visible edema and discoloration around the injured area.
  • Deformity: Any visible misalignment, unusual bump, or abnormal contour of the shoulder or arm.
  • Limited Range of Motion: Difficulty or inability to move the affected arm or shoulder without significant pain.
  • Numbness or Tingling: Sensation changes in the hand or fingers, indicating potential nerve involvement.
  • Weakness: Inability to grip or move the hand/fingers with normal strength.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Neurovascular Assessment: Always check for pulse, capillary refill, sensation, and motor function in the hand and fingers before and after applying the sling.
  • Proper Fit: Ensure the elbow is bent at a 90-degree angle, and the hand is slightly elevated above the elbow to prevent swelling.
  • Skin Integrity: Monitor for pressure points, redness, blistering, or skin breakdown, especially under the armpit and around the neck.
  • Avoid Over-Tightening: The sling should be snug enough to support but not restrictive, allowing for adequate circulation.
  • Regular Repositioning: Encourage slight movement of fingers and wrist (if not contraindicated) to prevent stiffness and promote circulation.
  • Padding: Use padding under the knot at the neck to prevent discomfort and skin irritation.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Encourage adequate fluid intake to support overall healing and prevent dehydration, especially if pain medications are used.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on foods rich in protein, Vitamin C, and calcium to aid tissue repair and bone healing.
  • Pain Medication Considerations: If pain medication is prescribed, ensure adequate food intake to prevent stomach upset or nausea.
  • Constipation Prevention: Increased immobility can lead to constipation; encourage fiber-rich foods and sufficient fluids.
  • Avoid Sugary Drinks: Limit excessive sugar intake which can contribute to inflammation and hinder the healing process.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Initial Assessment: Before applying, assess the injury for open wounds, severe bleeding, or obvious fractures requiring immediate advanced care.
  • Support the Arm: Gently support the injured arm throughout the application process to minimize movement and pain.
  • Secure Knot: Ensure the knot is secure but positioned away from the spine and padded to prevent discomfort.
  • Educate the Child/Parent: Clearly explain the purpose of the sling, how to care for it, and signs to watch for (e.g., increased pain, numbness, color changes).
  • Follow-up Care: Emphasize the critical importance of seeking professional medical evaluation after initial first aid for definitive diagnosis and treatment.
  • Monitor for Changes: Continuously observe the child for any changes in pain, sensation, or circulation.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Definitive Diagnosis: A sling is a temporary measure; a definitive diagnosis (e.g., X-ray, MRI) is crucial to guide further treatment.
  • Pain Management: Prescribe appropriate analgesia, considering the child's age, weight, and specific injury.
  • Rehabilitation Plan: Outline a clear plan for immobilization duration, gradual mobilization, and potential physical therapy.
  • Complication Monitoring: Advise parents on signs of complications such as compartment syndrome, nerve damage, or infection.
  • Growth Plate Considerations: Be mindful of potential growth plate injuries in children and their long-term implications for bone development.
  • Referral: Consider referral to an orthopedic specialist for complex fractures or dislocations.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Crucial role in educating patients and caregivers on sling application, care, and signs of complications.
  • Pain Assessment: Utilize age-appropriate pain scales (e.g., Wong-Baker FACES scale) for accurate pain assessment in children.
  • Regular Neurovascular Checks: Perform and document frequent neurovascular assessments of the affected extremity.
  • Skin Care Protocol: Implement protocols for skin assessment and care under and around the sling to prevent pressure injuries.
  • Mobility Assistance: Assist with activities of daily living while maintaining sling integrity and patient comfort.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document sling application, neurovascular status, patient education, and any observed changes.
