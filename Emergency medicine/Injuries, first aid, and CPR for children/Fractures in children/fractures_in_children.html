Fractures in children

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for children

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Pediatric Fracture Basics: Fractures in children involve unique considerations due to their growing bones, which are more flexible and have open growth plates (epiphyses).
  • Common Types: Greenstick fractures (incomplete break), torus or buckle fractures (compression injury), and physeal fractures (involving the growth plate) are common in children.
  • Growth Plate Significance: Injuries to the growth plate (physis) can affect future bone growth and limb length, necessitating careful management.
  • Causes: Common mechanisms include falls from height, sports injuries, direct trauma, and, in some cases, non-accidental trauma (child abuse).

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Localized Pain: Severe pain at the site of injury, often exacerbated by movement or touch.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Visible edema and discoloration around the affected area.
  • Deformity: An unnatural angulation or shortening of the limb, though sometimes subtle.
  • Inability to Use Limb: Reluctance or inability to move the injured extremity or bear weight.
  • Tenderness: Exquisite pain upon palpation over the fracture site.
  • Crepitus: A grating or crackling sensation felt or heard with movement, indicating bone fragments rubbing together.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Immobilization: Splint the injured limb in the position found to prevent further damage and reduce pain.
  • Do Not Attempt Reduction: Never try to straighten or realign a suspected fracture yourself.
  • Neurovascular Assessment: Regularly check the limb distal to the injury for color, warmth, sensation, and movement to detect potential nerve or blood vessel compromise.
  • Pain Management: Administer age-appropriate analgesia as soon as possible to alleviate distress.
  • Child Abuse Screening: Maintain a high index of suspicion for non-accidental trauma, especially with inconsistent injury mechanisms or unexplained fractures.
  • Open Fracture Care: For open fractures (bone piercing the skin), cover with a sterile dressing and ensure tetanus prophylaxis is considered.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Bone Healing Nutrition: Ensure adequate intake of calcium (dairy, fortified foods), Vitamin D (sunlight, fortified foods, supplements), and protein (lean meats, legumes) to support bone repair and recovery.
  • Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake to support overall health and metabolic processes crucial for healing.
  • Fiber Intake: Encourage fiber-rich foods to prevent constipation, which can be a side effect of immobility and certain pain medications.
  • Avoidance of Empty Calories: Limit highly processed foods and excessive sugars that offer little nutritional value and may displace nutrient-dense options essential for recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Medical Attention: Seek immediate medical care for any suspected fracture, especially if there is severe pain, deformity, open wounds, or signs of neurovascular compromise.
  • Follow-Up Care: Adhere strictly to all follow-up appointments with orthopedic specialists to monitor healing and address any complications.
  • Cast/Splint Care: Keep casts dry and clean. Report any signs of skin irritation, foul odor, numbness, increased pain, or swelling to the healthcare provider immediately.
  • Activity Restrictions: Ensure the child follows prescribed activity restrictions to allow proper healing and prevent re-injury.
  • Medication Adherence: Administer all prescribed pain medications and other treatments as directed.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Accurate Diagnosis: Radiographic imaging (X-rays) is crucial for confirming the presence, type, and location of a fracture, including growth plate involvement.
  • Treatment Modalities: Management ranges from closed reduction and casting to surgical intervention, depending on fracture stability, displacement, and growth plate involvement.
  • Growth Plate Monitoring: Long-term follow-up is essential for physeal fractures to monitor for potential growth disturbances or angular deformities.
  • Pain Management Strategy: A multi-modal approach to pain management, including pharmacological and non-pharmacological methods, is vital for pediatric patients.
  • Referral Considerations: Prompt referral to a pediatric orthopedic surgeon is indicated for complex fractures, open fractures, growth plate injuries, or those requiring surgical intervention.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Neurovascular Assessment (5 P's): Thoroughly assess Pain, Pallor, Pulses, Paresthesia, and Paralysis distal to the injury site.
  • Pain Assessment in Children: Utilize age-appropriate pain scales (e.g., FLACC for non-verbal, Wong-Baker FACES for verbal children) for accurate assessment.
  • Parental Education: Provide comprehensive education on cast care, signs of complications, activity restrictions, and medication administration.
  • Compartment Syndrome Monitoring: Be vigilant for signs of compartment syndrome, a limb-threatening condition requiring immediate intervention.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address the child's and family's anxiety and provide emotional support throughout the injury and recovery process.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Salter-Harris Classification: A system for classifying physeal (growth plate) fractures, critical for prognosis and treatment planning.
  • Fracture Types: Greenstick, Torus (Buckle), Transverse, Oblique, Spiral, Comminuted, and Avulsion fractures.
  • Imaging Modalities: Plain radiographs (X-rays) are the primary diagnostic tool; MRI may be used for complex growth plate injuries or suspected ligamentous damage.
  • Reduction Techniques: Closed reduction (manipulation without surgery) and Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF) are common methods to align bone fragments.
  • Potential Complications: Malunion, nonunion, growth arrest, limb length discrepancy, angular deformity, osteomyelitis (for open fractures), and compartment syndrome.
