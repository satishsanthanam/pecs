Motor vehicle crash – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for children

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Observation is Key: Closely monitor your child for any new or worsening symptoms for at least 48-72 hours post-discharge.
  • Delayed Symptoms: Some injuries, especially concussions or internal injuries, may not manifest immediately. Be vigilant for changes in behavior, pain, or physical condition.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Ensure you attend all scheduled follow-up appointments with your pediatrician, specialists (e.g., orthopedics, neurology), or physical therapy as directed by the emergency physician.
  • Emotional Impact: Children may experience emotional distress or anxiety following a crash. Provide reassurance and seek professional help if emotional symptoms persist.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Head Injury Concerns: Seek immediate medical attention for persistent or worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion, excessive drowsiness, difficulty waking, slurred speech, or unequal pupil size.
  • Abdominal Pain: Watch for increasing abdominal pain, distension, tenderness, blood in urine or stool, or persistent nausea.
  • Musculoskeletal Worsening: Return to the ED if there is increasing pain, swelling, redness, inability to bear weight, or inability to move an injured limb.
  • General Deterioration: Be alert for fever, lethargy, unusual irritability, difficulty breathing, or any symptom that causes significant concern.
  • Changes in Gait/Balance: Observe for new unsteadiness, dizziness, or difficulty walking.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Rest and Activity Restriction: Ensure your child gets adequate physical and cognitive rest. Limit strenuous activities, sports, rough play, and excessive screen time as advised.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed pain medication as directed. Do not exceed recommended dosages. For over-the-counter pain relief, use acetaminophen or ibuprofen as appropriate for age and weight, avoiding aspirin due to Reye's syndrome risk in children.
  • Car Seat Safety: If the car seat was occupied during the crash, it must be replaced, even if there is no visible damage, according to manufacturer and NHTSA guidelines. Ensure proper installation of the new car seat.
  • Medication Adherence: Give all medications exactly as prescribed. Do not stop medications early unless instructed by a healthcare provider.
  • Environmental Safety: Ensure a safe home environment to prevent further injury during recovery, especially if mobility is impaired.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Encourage frequent sips of clear fluids (water, diluted juice, electrolyte solutions) to prevent dehydration, especially if your child experienced vomiting.
  • Light, Bland Diet: Start with easily digestible, bland foods such as toast, crackers, rice, applesauce, bananas, and plain pasta.
  • Avoid Irritants: For the first 24-48 hours, avoid greasy, spicy, highly acidic, or sugary foods that might upset the stomach or exacerbate nausea.
  • Gradual Reintroduction: Gradually reintroduce your child's normal diet as tolerated, paying attention to any food sensitivities or discomfort.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • When to Return to ED: Return immediately to the Emergency Department if your child exhibits any of the 'Associated Symptoms' listed above, or if you have any significant concerns about their well-being.
  • Medication Administration: Understand the dosage, frequency, and potential side effects of all prescribed medications. Keep a record of when doses are given.
  • Observation Period: Maintain close observation of your child, especially during sleep, for at least 48 hours. Wake them periodically as instructed by the physician to check responsiveness.
  • Emergency Contacts: Keep emergency contact numbers readily available, including your pediatrician, the ED, and 911.
  • Emotional Support: Provide a calm and supportive environment. Acknowledge your child's feelings and offer comfort.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Recovery: Recovery from a motor vehicle crash involves not just physical healing but also emotional and psychological well-being. Support your child holistically.
  • Patience is Crucial: Healing takes time. Avoid rushing your child back into full activity, even if they appear to be feeling better. Adhere to all activity restrictions.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact your healthcare provider with any questions or concerns, no matter how minor they may seem. Early intervention can prevent complications.
  • Child Advocacy: Ensure your child's safety and well-being are paramount. Report any concerns regarding the circumstances of the crash to appropriate authorities if necessary.
  • Preventative Measures: Reiterate the importance of proper car seat use, seatbelt safety, and safe driving practices to prevent future incidents.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Discharge Education: Nurses play a critical role in providing clear, concise, and empathetic discharge instructions to caregivers, ensuring understanding of red flag symptoms and follow-up care.
  • Pain Assessment in Children: Utilize age-appropriate pain scales (e.g., FLACC, Wong-Baker FACES) for ongoing assessment and effective pain management.
  • Neurological Monitoring: Educate parents on basic neurological checks (e.g., alertness, pupil response, motor function) and when to escalate concerns.
  • Child Development Considerations: Tailor activity restrictions and recovery plans to the child's developmental stage, recognizing the impact of injury on play and learning.
  • Trauma-Informed Care: Approach care with an understanding of the potential psychological trauma experienced by the child and family, fostering a sense of safety and trust.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Concussion Protocol: Standardized guidelines for managing mild traumatic brain injury, including cognitive rest and gradual return-to-activity.
  • Polytrauma Management: Coordinated care for patients with multiple significant injuries affecting different body systems.
  • Delayed Presentation of Injuries: Clinical phenomenon where symptoms or signs of an injury become apparent hours or days after the initial trauma.
  • Child Passenger Safety (CPS): Guidelines and regulations concerning the safe transportation of children in motor vehicles, including car seat types and installation.
  • Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) in Children: A potential psychological sequela of traumatic events, requiring vigilance for symptoms and appropriate referral.
