How to use crutches

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for children

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Proper Fit is Paramount: Incorrectly fitted crutches can lead to nerve damage (crutch palsy) in the armpit.
  • Weight Bearing Through Hands: Always support your body weight through your hands and wrists, not your armpits, to prevent injury.
  • Aid for Mobility: Crutches are essential assistive devices for individuals with lower limb injuries or post-surgical recovery.
  • Gait Training is Key: Learning the correct walking patterns (gait) with crutches is vital for safety and efficient movement.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Lower Limb Pain: Acute or chronic pain in the leg, ankle, or foot that prevents weight bearing.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Visible signs of injury or inflammation in the affected limb.
  • Instability or Weakness: Inability to maintain balance or support body weight on the injured leg.
  • Numbness/Tingling in Hands/Arms: A potential symptom of improper crutch use, indicating nerve compression in the axilla.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Correct Crutch Adjustment: Ensure the top of the crutch is 1-2 inches below the armpit and handgrips allow a 20-30 degree elbow bend.
  • Avoid Axillary Pressure: Never lean on the crutch pads with your armpits; this can cause nerve damage (brachial plexus injury).
  • Clear Pathways: Remove all tripping hazards like rugs, loose cords, or clutter from walking areas.
  • Non-Slip Footwear: Always wear sturdy, supportive shoes with good traction to prevent slips and falls.
  • Stair Safety: Use handrails when available; lead with the unaffected leg when going up, and the crutches/affected leg when going down.
  • Surface Awareness: Be extremely cautious on wet, slippery, or uneven surfaces.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake, especially with increased physical exertion from crutch use.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on foods high in protein, calcium, and Vitamin D to support bone healing if an injury is present.
  • Fiber for Regularity: Increase fiber intake to prevent constipation, which can be common with reduced mobility and certain pain medications.
  • Avoidance of Impairing Substances: Limit alcohol or sedatives that could impair balance and increase fall risk while using crutches.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Constant Supervision for Children: Children using crutches require close supervision, especially on stairs or unfamiliar terrain.
  • Home Environment Assessment: Ensure the home is free of obstacles, well-lit, and has non-slip surfaces.
  • Assistance with Transfers: Be prepared to assist the individual with standing, sitting, and navigating challenging areas.
  • Monitor for Fatigue: Watch for signs of exhaustion, which can increase the risk of falls, and encourage frequent rest breaks.
  • Reinforce Proper Technique: Gently remind and correct any improper crutch use, such as leaning on armpits.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Strict Adherence to Weight-Bearing Status: Follow your physician's specific instructions regarding how much weight, if any, you can place on the injured limb.
  • Professional Fitting: Always have crutches properly fitted and adjusted by a healthcare professional to prevent complications.
  • Pain Management Strategy: Discuss effective pain control options to ensure comfort and facilitate mobility during recovery.
  • Regular Follow-Up: Attend all scheduled appointments to monitor healing progress and adjust mobility aids or weight-bearing status as needed.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Priority: Nurses are crucial in teaching and reinforcing proper crutch technique, gait patterns, and safety measures.
  • Comprehensive Assessment: Assess patient's strength, balance, coordination, cognitive ability, and skin integrity (especially axillary and hand areas).
  • Gait Training Demonstration: Instruct and supervise various gait patterns (e.g., 2-point, 3-point, 4-point, swing-through) based on the patient's weight-bearing status.
  • Complication Prevention: Educate patients on signs of nerve compression (crutch palsy) and skin breakdown, emphasizing proper body mechanics.
  • Environmental Safety Check: Conduct home safety assessments and provide recommendations for modifications to prevent falls.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Brachial Plexus Injury (Crutch Palsy): Neuropathy resulting from sustained pressure on the brachial plexus in the axilla.
  • Gait Patterns: Specific walking techniques with crutches, including 2-point, 3-point (non-weight bearing), 4-point, and swing-through gaits.
  • Weight-Bearing Status (WBS): Clinical classifications such as Non-Weight Bearing (NWB), Toe-Touch Weight Bearing (TTWB), Partial Weight Bearing (PWB), Weight Bearing As Tolerated (WBAT), and Full Weight Bearing (FWB).
  • Assistive Devices: Medical equipment designed to aid mobility, including axillary crutches, forearm (Lofstrand) crutches, walkers, and canes.
  • Orthopedic Rehabilitation: The process of restoring function and mobility after musculoskeletal injury or surgery, often involving crutch use.
