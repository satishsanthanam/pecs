Fracture in adults – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for adults

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Fracture: A fracture is a break in the bone. Proper healing requires immobilization and adherence to your treatment plan.
  • Immobilization Device: You will likely have a cast, splint, or brace. This device is crucial for keeping the bone stable and allowing it to heal correctly.
  • Follow-Up Appointments: It is vital to attend all scheduled follow-up appointments with your orthopedic specialist to monitor healing progress and adjust your care as needed.
  • Recovery Timeline: Bone healing varies by fracture type, location, and individual health, typically taking several weeks to months. Full recovery may involve physical therapy.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Increased or Uncontrolled Pain: Pain that worsens despite medication or becomes severe and constant, especially if accompanied by new swelling or discoloration.
  • Numbness or Tingling: Any new or worsening sensation of numbness, tingling, or 'pins and needles' in the limb distal to the fracture site.
  • Coolness or Pallor: The affected limb or digits feeling unusually cool to the touch or appearing pale, indicating potential circulation issues.
  • Fever and Chills: Development of a fever (temperature above 100.4°F or 38°C) accompanied by chills, which could signal an infection.
  • Foul Odor or Discharge: Any foul smell, pus, or unusual discharge from under the cast or around an open wound.
  • Inability to Move Digits: New difficulty or inability to move fingers or toes in the affected limb.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Cast/Splint Care: Keep your cast or splint dry at all times. Do not insert anything inside the cast to scratch an itch. Avoid putting weight on the injured limb unless specifically instructed by your doctor.
  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medication as directed. Do not exceed the recommended dose. Report any severe side effects or inadequate pain control to your physician.
  • Activity Restrictions: Strictly adhere to all activity restrictions provided by your healthcare team. Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, or sports until cleared by your orthopedic specialist.
  • Elevation: Elevate the injured limb above heart level as much as possible, especially during the first few days, to reduce swelling.
  • Skin Integrity: Regularly inspect the skin around the edges of the cast or splint for redness, irritation, or pressure sores.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive while taking opioid pain medication or if your fracture significantly impairs your ability to safely operate a vehicle.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Bone Healing Nutrients: Ensure adequate intake of calcium (e.g., dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens) and Vitamin D (e.g., fatty fish, fortified foods, sunlight exposure) to support bone repair.
  • Protein Intake: Consume sufficient protein (e.g., lean meats, poultry, fish, beans, lentils, tofu) to aid in tissue repair and recovery.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration by drinking plenty of water throughout the day, which supports overall bodily functions and can help prevent constipation.
  • Fiber for Constipation: Increase dietary fiber (e.g., whole grains, fruits, vegetables) to prevent constipation, which can be a common side effect of immobility and certain pain medications.
  • Avoid Alcohol and Smoking: Refrain from alcohol consumption and smoking, as both can impair bone healing and increase the risk of complications.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Department Visit: Seek immediate medical attention at the nearest emergency department if you experience sudden, severe, or uncontrolled pain; new or worsening numbness/tingling; significant discoloration or coolness of the limb; or signs of infection (high fever, pus from wound).
  • Cast/Splint Issues: Return to the emergency department or urgent care if your cast feels too tight, causes severe pain, or if you notice new cracks or damage to the cast that compromises its integrity.
  • Loss of Sensation/Movement: Any sudden loss of sensation or inability to move fingers or toes in the affected limb requires immediate medical evaluation.
  • Unexplained Bleeding: For open fractures, any new or excessive bleeding from the wound site should prompt immediate medical review.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence is Key: Your commitment to the prescribed treatment plan, including immobilization, medication, and activity restrictions, is paramount for optimal bone healing and preventing complications.
  • Physical Therapy: Once cleared, engaging in physical therapy is crucial for restoring strength, flexibility, and function to the injured limb. Do not skip these sessions.
  • Long-Term Outlook: While most fractures heal well, some may require further intervention. Report any persistent pain, deformity, or functional limitations during your recovery.
  • Preventing Re-injury: Discuss strategies with your care team to prevent future fractures, especially if underlying conditions like osteoporosis are present.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Neurovascular Assessment: Perform regular '5 Ps' neurovascular checks (Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis) distal to the fracture site to monitor for compartment syndrome or nerve damage.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on proper cast/splint care, signs of complications (infection, neurovascular compromise), pain management strategies, and the importance of follow-up.
  • Mobility Assistance: Assist patients with safe ambulation using assistive devices (crutches, walker) and reinforce weight-bearing restrictions.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Review all discharge medications, ensuring the patient understands dosage, frequency, potential side effects, and drug interactions, especially for analgesics.
  • Wound Care: For open fractures, provide clear instructions on wound dressing changes, signs of infection, and maintaining sterility.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Fracture Types: Common classifications include closed (simple), open (compound), comminuted, greenstick, spiral, transverse, oblique, and pathological fractures.
  • Immobilization Techniques: Methods include plaster or fiberglass casts, splints (e.g., sugar tong, posterior), braces, and external fixators.
  • Complications: Potential complications include compartment syndrome, non-union, malunion, delayed union, osteomyelitis, nerve damage, vascular injury, and deep vein thrombosis (DVT).
  • Analgesia: Pain management typically involves NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen), acetaminophen, and sometimes opioid analgesics for severe pain, with careful consideration of side effects.
  • Radiographic Evaluation: X-rays are the primary diagnostic tool, with CT scans or MRI sometimes used for complex fractures or soft tissue assessment.
  • Bone Healing Stages: Involves hematoma formation, fibrocartilaginous callus formation, bony callus formation, and bone remodeling.
