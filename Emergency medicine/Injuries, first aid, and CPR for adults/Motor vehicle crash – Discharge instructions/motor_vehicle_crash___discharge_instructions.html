Motor vehicle crash – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for adults

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Delayed Symptoms: It is common for new or worsening symptoms, such as pain, stiffness, or headaches, to appear hours or even days after a motor vehicle crash. Monitor your body closely.
  • Follow-Up Care: Adhere strictly to all scheduled follow-up appointments with your primary care physician, specialists (e.g., orthopedics, neurology), or physical therapy as directed by the emergency department.
  • Pain Management: Understand your prescribed pain medication regimen, including dosage, frequency, and potential side effects. Do not exceed recommended doses.
  • Activity Restrictions: You may have temporary restrictions on lifting, strenuous activity, or driving. Follow these guidelines to prevent further injury and promote healing.
  • Emotional Impact: It is normal to experience emotional distress, anxiety, or difficulty sleeping after a traumatic event. Do not hesitate to discuss these feelings with your healthcare provider.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Worsening Pain: Any significant increase in pain that is not relieved by prescribed medication, or new pain in an area not previously affected.
  • Neurological Changes: New or worsening headache, dizziness, blurred vision, difficulty speaking, confusion, memory problems, numbness, tingling, or weakness in any limb.
  • Signs of Internal Injury: Severe abdominal pain, persistent nausea or vomiting, blood in urine or stool, or unexplained bruising.
  • Breathing Difficulties: Shortness of breath, chest pain, or difficulty taking a deep breath.
  • Signs of Infection (if wounds present): Redness, swelling, warmth, pus, or fever around any cuts or abrasions.
  • Loss of Consciousness: Any episode of fainting or loss of awareness.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Safety: Take all medications exactly as prescribed. Do not share medications. Avoid alcohol and other sedatives while taking opioid pain relievers.
  • Avoid Driving: Do not drive if you are taking sedating medications, experiencing dizziness, or have significant pain that impairs your ability to operate a vehicle safely. Follow your physician's specific instructions regarding return to driving.
  • Activity Modification: Avoid heavy lifting, sudden movements, or activities that exacerbate your pain. Gradually increase activity as tolerated and advised by your healthcare provider.
  • Wound Care: If you have any cuts or abrasions, keep them clean and dry. Change dressings as instructed and watch for signs of infection.
  • Fall Prevention: Be cautious when moving, especially if you are dizzy or on pain medication. Ensure your home environment is free of tripping hazards.
  • Avoid Self-Medication: Do not take over-the-counter medications without consulting your doctor, especially if you are already on prescription drugs, to avoid adverse interactions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake, primarily water, to support healing and prevent constipation, which can be a side effect of some pain medications.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Consume a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins to support tissue repair and overall recovery.
  • Fiber Intake: Increase dietary fiber through whole grains, fruits, and vegetables to help prevent constipation, especially if taking opioid pain relievers.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Strictly avoid alcohol, particularly while taking pain medications, as it can interact dangerously and impair judgment.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: If experiencing nausea or decreased appetite, try eating smaller, more frequent meals throughout the day.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Changes: Closely observe the patient for any new or worsening symptoms, especially neurological changes, increased pain, or signs of infection.
  • Medication Assistance: Help the patient adhere to their medication schedule and ensure they understand dosage and potential side effects.
  • Safe Environment: Assist in creating a safe home environment, removing obstacles, and providing support during ambulation if needed.
  • Emotional Support: Offer emotional support and encourage the patient to express their feelings. Be aware of signs of distress or depression.
  • Emergency Contact: Know when to seek emergency medical attention and have contact information for the healthcare team readily available.
  • Transportation: Provide transportation for follow-up appointments and avoid allowing the patient to drive if they are impaired or restricted.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prioritize Recovery: Your body needs time to heal. Do not rush your recovery process. Listen to your body and follow all medical advice diligently.
  • Open Communication: Report all symptoms, concerns, and medication side effects to your healthcare provider promptly. Do not minimize your discomfort.
  • Gradual Return to Activity: A gradual and supervised return to daily activities and work is crucial. Pushing too hard too soon can lead to setbacks.
  • Mental Health Awareness: The psychological impact of a crash can be significant. Seek professional help if you experience persistent anxiety, depression, or post-traumatic stress symptoms.
  • Prevent Future Incidents: Reflect on the circumstances of the crash and consider ways to enhance safety in future driving or passenger experiences.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Pain Assessment: Educate patients on pain scales (e.g., 0-10) and the importance of regular pain assessment and reporting. Discuss non-pharmacological pain relief strategies.
  • Wound Care Education: Provide clear, step-by-step instructions for wound cleaning, dressing changes, and signs of infection to watch for.
  • Mobility and Activity Restrictions: Reinforce specific activity limitations, demonstrate safe body mechanics, and discuss the rationale behind these restrictions to enhance compliance.
  • Medication Reconciliation and Education: Review all discharge medications, including purpose, dose, frequency, side effects, and potential interactions, ensuring patient understanding.
  • Psychosocial Support: Assess for signs of emotional distress, anxiety, or PTSD. Provide resources for mental health support and encourage open communication.
  • Discharge Planning: Coordinate follow-up appointments, referrals to physical therapy or specialists, and ensure the patient has necessary equipment (e.g., crutches, braces) and understands how to use them.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: Common codes include S00-S99 for injuries to specific body regions, V00-V99 for external causes of morbidity (e.g., V43.6XXA for car occupant injured in collision with car, initial encounter).
  • Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS): Used for initial and ongoing assessment of neurological status, particularly for head injuries. Discharge criteria often include a GCS of 15.
  • Canadian C-Spine Rule/NEXUS Criteria: Clinical decision rules used to determine the need for cervical spine imaging in alert trauma patients.
  • Imaging Follow-up: Criteria for repeat imaging (e.g., X-rays, CT scans) based on initial findings, worsening symptoms, or specific injury types (e.g., unstable fractures).
  • Referral Pathways: Established protocols for referral to orthopedics, neurosurgery, physical therapy, occupational therapy, pain management clinics, or psychological services post-discharge.
  • Patient Education Materials: Standardized discharge instructions, often including diagrams for exercises, wound care, or medication schedules.
