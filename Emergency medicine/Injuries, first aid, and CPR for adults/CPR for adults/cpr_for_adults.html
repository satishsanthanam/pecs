CPR for adults

🏠 Home / Emergency medicine / Injuries, first aid, and CPR for adults

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of CPR: Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) is an emergency life-saving procedure performed when the heart stops beating. Immediate CPR can double or triple chances of survival after cardiac arrest.
  • Recognizing Cardiac Arrest: Look for unresponsiveness and absence of normal breathing (e.g., gasping, no breathing). Check for a pulse if trained, but prioritize compressions if uncertain.
  • Chain of Survival: Emphasizes early recognition and activation of emergency response, early high-quality CPR, rapid defibrillation, effective advanced life support, and integrated post-cardiac arrest care.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sudden Collapse: An individual may suddenly fall unconscious without warning.
  • Unresponsiveness: The person does not respond to verbal commands or gentle shaking.
  • Absence of Normal Breathing: Breathing may be absent or characterized by agonal gasps (infrequent, noisy gasps that are not effective breathing).
  • No Palpable Pulse: Inability to feel a pulse in major arteries (e.g., carotid or femoral) within 10 seconds.
  • Cyanosis: Bluish discoloration of the skin, especially around the lips and fingertips, indicating lack of oxygen.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Scene Safety First: Always ensure the environment is safe for both the rescuer and the victim before initiating CPR. Move the victim only if absolutely necessary for safety.
  • Proper Hand Placement: For adult CPR, place the heel of one hand in the center of the chest, on the lower half of the sternum, with the other hand on top.
  • Compression Depth and Rate: Aim for compressions at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep but no more than 2.4 inches (6 cm), at a rate of 100-120 compressions per minute.
  • Minimize Interruptions: Limit interruptions to chest compressions to less than 10 seconds.
  • Barrier Devices: Use a pocket mask or other barrier device during rescue breaths to protect against disease transmission, if available.
  • Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Orders: Respect valid DNR orders. If a patient has a DNR, CPR should not be initiated.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • No Pre-Procedure Dietary Restrictions for Rescuer: There are no specific dietary preparations or restrictions required for an individual performing CPR.
  • Aspiration Risk in Victim: If the victim has recently eaten, there is an increased risk of aspiration during resuscitation efforts, particularly during airway management and ventilation.
  • Post-Resuscitation Nutritional Support: Following successful resuscitation, dietary considerations will be managed by medical staff, often involving intravenous fluids and gradual reintroduction of oral intake based on neurological status and gastrointestinal function.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Activate Emergency Services: Immediately call 911 (or local emergency number) and retrieve an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) if available.
  • High-Quality Chest Compressions: Provide continuous, high-quality chest compressions, allowing for full chest recoil after each compression.
  • AED Use: Follow AED prompts as soon as it arrives. Apply pads and allow the AED to analyze the rhythm and deliver a shock if advised.
  • Rescue Breaths: If trained and willing, provide 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions (30:2 ratio). If untrained or unwilling, perform hands-only CPR.
  • Continue Until: Continue CPR until emergency medical services (EMS) arrive and take over, an AED is ready for use, the person shows signs of life, or you are too exhausted to continue.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Recognition is Key: Prompt identification of cardiac arrest and immediate activation of the emergency response system are critical for improving outcomes.
  • Importance of Training: Encourage all individuals, especially healthcare providers and caregivers, to undergo certified CPR training and regular refreshers.
  • Integrated Care: Successful resuscitation involves not only immediate CPR and defibrillation but also comprehensive post-cardiac arrest care to address underlying causes and optimize neurological recovery.
  • Team Approach: In a clinical setting, effective CPR is a team effort requiring clear roles, communication, and coordination among healthcare professionals.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Assessment Skills: Nurses must rapidly assess for unresponsiveness, absence of normal breathing, and pulse within 10 seconds to initiate CPR promptly.
  • CPR Technique Mastery: Proficiency in correct hand placement, compression depth, rate, and full chest recoil is essential for high-quality CPR.
  • Airway Management: Understanding and applying head-tilt/chin-lift or jaw-thrust maneuvers, and effective use of barrier devices for rescue breaths.
  • Team Dynamics and Communication: Participating effectively in resuscitation teams, understanding assigned roles, and communicating clearly during a code.
  • Post-Resuscitation Care: Monitoring vital signs, neurological status, and assisting with advanced interventions and documentation following Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC).

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • AHA/ERC Guidelines: Refer to the latest American Heart Association (AHA) or European Resuscitation Council (ERC) guidelines for up-to-date CPR protocols.
  • Compression-to-Ventilation Ratio: For single rescuer adult CPR, the ratio is 30 compressions to 2 breaths. For two rescuers, it remains 30:2 or continuous compressions with asynchronous ventilations in advanced settings.
  • AED Algorithm: Understand the basic AED algorithm: power on, attach pads, analyze rhythm, shock if advised, continue CPR.
  • Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS): For healthcare professionals, ACLS builds upon basic life support with advanced airway management, rhythm interpretation, and pharmacologic interventions.
  • Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC): Defined by the presence of a palpable pulse and measurable blood pressure, and often signs of consciousness.
