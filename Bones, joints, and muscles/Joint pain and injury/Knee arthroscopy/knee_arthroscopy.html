Knee arthroscopy

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Joint pain and injury

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Minimally Invasive Procedure: Knee arthroscopy uses a small incision and a camera (arthroscope) to visualize and treat problems inside the knee joint.
  • Common Indications: Used for diagnosing and treating various knee conditions like meniscal tears, ligament injuries (ACL, PCL), cartilage damage, and removal of loose bodies.
  • Benefits: Generally involves less pain, quicker recovery, and smaller scars compared to traditional open surgery.
  • Outpatient or Short Stay: Often performed as an outpatient procedure, allowing patients to return home the same day.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Knee Pain: Pain that doesn't improve with conservative treatments like rest, ice, compression, elevation (RICE), physical therapy, or medication.
  • Swelling and Stiffness: Chronic or recurrent swelling, difficulty bending or straightening the knee.
  • Clicking, Popping, or Locking: Sensations within the knee joint, especially during movement, indicating potential meniscal tears or loose bodies.
  • Instability or Giving Way: Feeling that the knee might buckle or give out, often associated with ligamentous injuries.
  • Difficulty with Weight-Bearing: Pain or discomfort when putting weight on the affected knee.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Assessment: Thorough medical evaluation, including blood tests, ECG, and chest X-ray, to ensure fitness for surgery and anesthesia.
  • Medication Review: Discuss all current medications, especially blood thinners (e.g., aspirin, warfarin, NSAIDs), which may need to be stopped several days before surgery.
  • Allergy Disclosure: Inform the medical team about any known allergies to medications, latex, or anesthesia.
  • Infection Prevention: Report any active infections (e.g., skin rash, cold, flu) prior to surgery, as these may necessitate postponement.
  • Post-operative Mobility: Arrange for assistance at home and transportation, as mobility will be restricted immediately after the procedure.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Strictly adhere to NPO (nil per os) guidelines, typically no food or drink for 6-8 hours before surgery, as instructed by the anesthesiologist.
  • Hydration Prior to Fasting: Ensure adequate hydration in the days leading up to the fasting period, unless otherwise advised.
  • Post-anesthesia Reintroduction: Start with clear liquids (water, broth, juice) after surgery, gradually progressing to light, easily digestible foods as tolerated.
  • Avoid Heavy or Fatty Foods: Initially avoid heavy, greasy, or spicy foods that may cause nausea or discomfort after anesthesia.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Pain Management: Follow prescribed pain medication regimen diligently to manage post-operative discomfort.
  • RICE Protocol: Apply ice packs to the knee for 20 minutes at a time, several times a day, elevate the leg, and use compression bandages as directed to reduce swelling.
  • Wound Care: Keep incision sites clean and dry; follow specific instructions for dressing changes and showering.
  • Weight-Bearing Restrictions: Adhere strictly to weight-bearing limitations and crutch use as advised by the surgeon.
  • Physical Therapy: Begin prescribed physical therapy exercises as soon as instructed to restore range of motion and strength.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic and Therapeutic Tool: Arthroscopy serves both to accurately diagnose intra-articular knee pathology and to provide definitive treatment.
  • Patient Selection: Careful patient selection is crucial for optimal outcomes, considering factors like age, activity level, and extent of damage.
  • Realistic Expectations: Patients should understand that recovery is a process, and full return to pre-injury activity levels may take several weeks to months, depending on the extent of repair.
  • Importance of Rehabilitation: Adherence to a structured rehabilitation program is paramount for successful recovery and long-term joint function.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Educate patients on the procedure, anesthesia, potential risks, and post-operative care expectations.
  • Pain Assessment: Regularly assess and manage post-operative pain using a validated pain scale and appropriate interventions.
  • Neurovascular Checks: Monitor the affected extremity for color, temperature, sensation, and movement (CMS checks) to detect potential complications.
  • Mobility Assistance: Assist patients with safe ambulation using crutches or walkers, ensuring proper technique and fall prevention.
  • Discharge Planning: Provide comprehensive discharge instructions, including wound care, medication schedule, activity restrictions, and follow-up appointments.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Arthroscope: A small fiber-optic camera inserted into the joint to visualize internal structures.
  • Meniscectomy: Surgical removal of damaged meniscal tissue.
  • Meniscal Repair: Surgical suturing of a torn meniscus.
  • Ligament Reconstruction: Repair or replacement of torn ligaments, such as the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL).
  • Chondroplasty: Surgical repair or smoothing of damaged articular cartilage.
  • Synovectomy: Removal of inflamed synovial tissue.
