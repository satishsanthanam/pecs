Hip fracture

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Bone health

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A hip fracture is a break in the upper part of the femur (thigh bone), near the hip joint.
  • Common Occurrence: Most frequently affects older adults, often as a result of a fall and underlying osteoporosis.
  • Severity: It is a serious injury that typically requires immediate surgical intervention and carries a significant risk of complications.
  • Impact: Can lead to a substantial loss of independence, increased morbidity, and mortality, especially in the elderly population.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Pain: Intense pain in the hip or groin area, which worsens with movement or weight-bearing.
  • Inability to Move: Difficulty or complete inability to stand, walk, or bear weight on the affected leg.
  • Leg Deformity: The injured leg may appear shorter than the uninjured leg and often turns outward (external rotation).
  • Bruising and Swelling: Visible bruising, swelling, or tenderness around the hip joint.
  • Restricted Movement: Significant limitation in the range of motion of the hip joint.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Implement comprehensive fall prevention strategies, including home safety modifications (e.g., removing tripping hazards, adequate lighting), use of assistive devices, and regular exercise to improve balance and strength.
  • Bone Health Management: Proactively manage osteoporosis through prescribed medications, adequate calcium and Vitamin D intake, and regular bone density screenings.
  • Pre-operative Assessment: Ensure thorough medical evaluation to optimize patient health before surgery, addressing comorbidities and medication adjustments.
  • Post-operative Care Adherence: Strictly follow post-surgical instructions regarding weight-bearing restrictions, physical therapy protocols, and wound care to prevent complications and promote healing.
  • DVT Prophylaxis: Comply with prescribed deep vein thrombosis (DVT) prevention measures, such as anticoagulants and early mobilization, as directed by the medical team.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines for food and fluids as instructed by the anesthesiologist prior to surgery.
  • Post-operative Protein Intake: Ensure a high-protein diet post-surgery to support wound healing, muscle repair, and overall recovery.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain sufficient fluid intake to prevent constipation, support kidney function, and aid in medication metabolism.
  • Calcium and Vitamin D: Incorporate foods rich in calcium and Vitamin D, or take supplements as prescribed, to support bone healing and maintain bone density.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Consume fiber-rich foods to prevent constipation, a common issue post-surgery due to immobility and pain medications.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Emergency Care: Transport the patient to an emergency department immediately if a hip fracture is suspected; avoid moving the patient unnecessarily.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed pain medication promptly and monitor its effectiveness, reporting any uncontrolled pain to the medical team.
  • Mobility Assistance: Provide careful assistance with all transfers and ambulation, strictly adhering to weight-bearing precautions and using appropriate assistive devices.
  • Pressure Injury Prevention: Implement regular repositioning schedules and use pressure-relieving devices to prevent skin breakdown and pressure injuries.
  • Emotional Support: Offer reassurance and emotional support, as a hip fracture can be a traumatic event leading to fear, anxiety, and potential depression.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Surgical Imperative: Most hip fractures necessitate surgical intervention, either internal fixation or arthroplasty, to restore stability and facilitate early mobilization.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Optimal outcomes require a collaborative effort involving orthopedic surgeons, anesthesiologists, geriatricians, physical therapists, and occupational therapists.
  • Rehabilitation Focus: Post-operative rehabilitation is paramount for regaining strength, mobility, and functional independence; patient adherence is critical.
  • Osteoporosis Management: Addressing underlying osteoporosis is crucial to prevent future fractures and should be initiated or optimized post-fracture.
  • Complication Vigilance: Close monitoring for post-operative complications such as DVT, pulmonary embolism, infection, and delirium is essential for patient safety.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Perform thorough neurovascular assessments of the affected extremity, pain assessments, and functional status evaluations regularly.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Monitor vital signs, surgical site for signs of infection, intake/output, and mental status, especially for signs of delirium.
  • Pain Management: Administer analgesics as prescribed, evaluate pain relief, and utilize non-pharmacological pain interventions.
  • Early Mobilization: Assist with early and progressive mobilization as per physician orders and physical therapy recommendations to prevent complications.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and caregivers on medication regimens, wound care, signs of complications, fall prevention, and the importance of rehabilitation.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Fracture Types: Femoral neck fracture (intracapsular), intertrochanteric fracture (extracapsular), subtrochanteric fracture.
  • Surgical Procedures: Open Reduction Internal Fixation (ORIF), Hemiarthroplasty (partial hip replacement), Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA).
  • Common Complications: Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT), Pulmonary Embolism (PE), pneumonia, pressure injuries, urinary tract infections, delirium, non-union, avascular necrosis.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: X-ray (initial), MRI (for occult fractures), CT scan (for complex fractures or surgical planning).
  • Risk Factors: Advanced age, osteoporosis, falls, female gender, certain medications (e.g., corticosteroids), poor vision, neurological conditions, malnutrition.
