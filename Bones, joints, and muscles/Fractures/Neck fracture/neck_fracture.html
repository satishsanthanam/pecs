Neck fracture

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Cervical Spine Fracture: A neck fracture refers to a break in one or more of the seven bones (vertebrae) that form the cervical spine, located in the neck.
  • High-Impact Trauma: These fractures are typically caused by significant trauma, such as falls, motor vehicle accidents, or sports injuries, leading to hyperflexion, hyperextension, compression, or rotation forces.
  • Spinal Cord Injury Risk: Due to the close proximity of the spinal cord, neck fractures carry a high risk of associated neurological damage, including paralysis or even death, making immediate and careful management critical.
  • Stability Assessment: Fractures are classified based on their stability, which dictates treatment approach and the potential for further spinal cord compromise.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Neck Pain: Intense pain in the neck, often exacerbated by movement, is a primary indicator.
  • Limited Range of Motion: Inability or extreme difficulty to move the head or neck due to pain and potential instability.
  • Neurological Deficits: Numbness, tingling, weakness, or paralysis in the arms, legs, or torso, indicating potential spinal cord involvement.
  • Muscle Spasms: Involuntary tightening of neck and shoulder muscles as a protective mechanism.
  • Deformity or Swelling: Visible changes in the neck's contour or localized swelling may be present in severe cases.
  • Headache: May accompany neck pain, especially if there's associated head trauma.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Immobilization: Any suspected neck fracture requires immediate and rigid immobilization of the cervical spine using a hard cervical collar and a backboard to prevent further injury.
  • Avoid Movement: Do not attempt to move the patient's head or neck under any circumstances until medical professionals have assessed and stabilized the spine.
  • Emergency Medical Services (EMS): Promptly call for emergency medical assistance (e.g., 911 or local equivalent) for expert handling and transport.
  • Airway Management: While maintaining spinal precautions, ensure the patient's airway is clear and breathing is adequate, as respiratory compromise can occur with high cervical injuries.
  • Log-Rolling Technique: If the patient must be moved (e.g., to prevent aspiration), a multi-person log-roll technique must be used to maintain spinal alignment.
  • Contraindications for Movement: Avoid any maneuvers that could cause flexion, extension, or rotation of the cervical spine.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Initial NPO Status: Patients with suspected or confirmed neck fractures are typically kept nil per os (NPO) in anticipation of potential surgical intervention or sedation for diagnostic imaging.
  • Swallowing Assessment: Post-stabilization, a swallowing assessment may be required, especially if neurological deficits affect cranial nerves, before oral intake is permitted.
  • Soft/Liquid Diet Progression: If swallowing is safe, a gradual progression from clear liquids to a soft diet may be initiated, avoiding foods that require extensive chewing.
  • Hydration Monitoring: Ensure adequate hydration, initially via intravenous fluids, then orally as tolerated, to prevent dehydration.
  • Bowel Regularity: Address potential constipation due to immobility, pain medication, and dietary changes through appropriate fluid intake and stool softeners as prescribed.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Maintain Spinal Alignment: Continuously ensure the patient's head and neck are in a neutral, in-line position, especially during transfers or repositioning.
  • Monitor Neurological Status: Regularly assess and document changes in sensation, motor strength, and reflexes in all four extremities.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics promptly and assess their effectiveness, noting any adverse reactions.
  • Skin Integrity: Frequently inspect skin under cervical collars or halo vests for pressure points, redness, or breakdown, and provide meticulous skin care.
  • Psychological Support: Provide reassurance and emotional support to the patient and family, addressing anxieties related to the injury and recovery.
  • Educate on Restrictions: Clearly communicate all activity restrictions and the importance of adherence to the prescribed immobilization device.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Rapid Diagnostic Imaging: Prompt X-rays, CT scans, and MRI are crucial for accurate diagnosis, fracture classification, and assessment of spinal cord involvement.
  • Stabilization Priority: The primary goal is to stabilize the cervical spine, either non-surgically with external immobilization (e.g., halo vest, rigid collar) or surgically through fusion or fixation.
  • Neurological Assessment: Thorough and repeated neurological examinations are vital to detect any progression or improvement of deficits.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Management often involves neurosurgeons, orthopedic spine surgeons, neurologists, physical therapists, and occupational therapists.
  • Long-Term Rehabilitation: Extensive rehabilitation is often necessary to regain function and manage potential long-term neurological impairments.
  • Complication Vigilance: Watch for complications such as deep vein thrombosis, pneumonia, pressure ulcers, and autonomic dysreflexia in higher-level injuries.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Cervical Spine Assessment: Master the systematic assessment of the cervical spine, including palpation, range of motion (if safe), and neurological evaluation (sensory, motor, reflexes).
  • Immobilization Device Care: Learn proper application, adjustment, and care of cervical collars, halo vests, and other immobilization devices, including skin care protocols.
  • Pain and Spasm Management: Understand pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions for acute pain and muscle spasms associated with neck fractures.
  • Neurovascular Monitoring: Develop proficiency in frequent neurovascular checks of the upper and lower extremities to detect changes indicative of spinal cord compression or vascular compromise.
  • Bowel and Bladder Management: Implement protocols for managing neurogenic bladder and bowel, including catheterization and bowel regimens, as needed.
  • Patient Education: Prepare to educate patients and families on the nature of the injury, treatment plan, activity restrictions, and signs of complications.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • AO Spine Classification: A comprehensive system used to classify cervical spine fractures based on morphology, neurological status, and facet injury.
  • Spinal Cord Injury (SCI) Levels: Understanding the dermatomal and myotomal distribution of neurological deficits to localize the level of spinal cord injury.
  • Halo Vest Application: A method of external skeletal fixation providing rigid immobilization for unstable cervical fractures, requiring meticulous pin site care.
  • Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF): A common surgical procedure for stabilizing cervical fractures, involving removal of damaged disc/bone and fusion with a graft.
  • Posterior Cervical Fusion: Surgical approach involving fixation from the back of the neck, often used for more complex or unstable fractures.
  • Methylprednisolone Protocol: Historical and debated use of high-dose corticosteroids in acute spinal cord injury to potentially reduce secondary injury.
  • Vertebral Artery Dissection: A potential complication of cervical trauma, requiring imaging like CT angiography for diagnosis.
