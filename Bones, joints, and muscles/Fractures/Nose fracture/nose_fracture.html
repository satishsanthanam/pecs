Nose fracture

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A nose fracture, or nasal bone fracture, is a break in the bone or cartilage of the nose, most commonly affecting the nasal bones.
  • Common Causes: Typically results from direct trauma to the face, such as falls, sports injuries, motor vehicle accidents, or altercations.
  • Immediate Signs: Often presents with immediate pain, swelling, and tenderness over the bridge of the nose, sometimes accompanied by a visible deformity.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Pain and Tenderness: Acute pain, especially upon touching the nose, and generalized tenderness around the nasal area.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Significant swelling of the nose and surrounding facial tissues, often with periorbital ecchymosis (black eyes).
  • Nosebleed (Epistaxis): Common occurrence due to the rich blood supply of the nasal mucosa, ranging from mild to severe.
  • Nasal Deformity: A noticeable change in the shape of the nose, such as a crooked appearance or flattening of the nasal bridge.
  • Difficulty Breathing: Obstruction of one or both nostrils due to swelling, blood clots, or displacement of nasal structures.
  • Crepitus: A crackling sensation or sound when the nose is gently palpated, indicating bone fragments rubbing together.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Manipulation: Do not attempt to straighten or manipulate the nose yourself, as this can worsen the injury or cause further damage.
  • Cold Compresses: Apply ice packs wrapped in a cloth to the nose and surrounding areas for 15-20 minutes every 1-2 hours to reduce swelling and pain.
  • Head Elevation: Keep the head elevated, especially when resting or sleeping, to minimize swelling and facilitate drainage.
  • Avoid Strenuous Activity: Refrain from activities that could increase blood pressure or risk further trauma, such as heavy lifting or contact sports, until cleared by a physician.
  • Monitor for Septal Hematoma: Be vigilant for signs of a septal hematoma (a collection of blood in the nasal septum), which appears as a bluish, boggy swelling inside the nose and requires urgent medical drainage to prevent cartilage necrosis.
  • Pain Management: Use over-the-counter pain relievers like acetaminophen or ibuprofen as directed, avoiding aspirin which can increase bleeding.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Soft Food Diet: Opt for soft, easy-to-chew foods if jaw movement or chewing causes discomfort or pain due to facial swelling.
  • Avoid Hot Liquids: Initially, avoid very hot beverages or foods that could increase local blood flow and potentially exacerbate swelling or bleeding.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to support healing and overall well-being, especially if experiencing nosebleeds.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from alcohol consumption, as it can thin the blood and potentially increase the risk of bleeding.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Care: Seek immediate medical attention if there is severe, uncontrollable bleeding, clear fluid draining from the nose (suggesting CSF leak), changes in vision, severe headache, or signs of a septal hematoma.
  • Do Not Pack Nose: Avoid inserting cotton or tissue deep into the nostrils to stop bleeding, as this can dislodge clots and worsen the situation. Gentle pressure on the soft part of the nose is preferred.
  • Monitor for Infection: Watch for signs of infection such as increased pain, redness, pus-like discharge, or fever, and report them to a healthcare provider.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Adhere strictly to all scheduled follow-up appointments with the ENT specialist or plastic surgeon for assessment and potential reduction of the fracture.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Timely Assessment: Early medical evaluation is crucial to accurately diagnose the extent of the fracture and rule out other facial injuries or complications like septal hematoma.
  • Reduction Window: Nasal bone fractures are typically reduced within 7-14 days of injury, before significant callus formation makes reduction more difficult.
  • Management Options: Treatment can range from conservative management for minor, non-displaced fractures to closed reduction (manual manipulation) or open reduction (surgical intervention) for more complex or displaced fractures.
  • Functional and Cosmetic Outcomes: The primary goals are to restore nasal airway patency and achieve an acceptable cosmetic appearance, minimizing long-term complications such as chronic nasal obstruction or cosmetic deformity.
  • Referral to Specialists: Patients often require referral to an Otolaryngologist (ENT) or a Plastic Surgeon for definitive management and follow-up.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Initial Assessment: Focus on ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation), assess for other facial or head injuries, and perform a thorough nasal examination (external and internal with a nasal speculum).
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics and educate the patient on non-pharmacological pain relief methods like cold compresses.
  • Patient Education: Instruct patients on post-injury care, including head elevation, avoiding nose blowing, proper epistaxis management, and signs of complications.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Regularly assess for signs of septal hematoma, CSF rhinorrhea, infection, or worsening nasal obstruction.
  • Pre- and Post-Procedure Care: Prepare patients for potential closed reduction, including explaining the procedure, managing anxiety, and providing post-reduction care instructions (e.g., cast/splint care).

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: S02.2 (Fracture of nasal bones), S02.21 (Displaced fracture of nasal bones), S02.22 (Nondisplaced fracture of nasal bones).
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Clinical diagnosis is often sufficient; X-rays (lateral and Waters views) or CT scans may be used to confirm fracture, assess displacement, or rule out other facial fractures.
  • Reduction Techniques:
    • Closed Reduction: Manual manipulation of the fractured nasal bones under local or general anesthesia, often using specialized instruments.
    • Open Reduction: Surgical approach for complex fractures, comminuted fractures, or those involving the septum, allowing direct visualization and fixation.
  • Potential Complications: Septal hematoma, deviated nasal septum, saddle nose deformity, chronic nasal obstruction, anosmia, cosmetic deformity, infection.
  • Anatomical Considerations: Nasal bones, nasal septum (vomer, perpendicular plate of ethmoid, quadrangular cartilage), nasal cartilages (upper and lower lateral cartilages).
