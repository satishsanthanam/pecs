Patella fracture

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Fractures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A patella fracture is a break in the kneecap, a small, flat, triangular bone located at the front of the knee joint.
  • Common Causes: Typically results from direct trauma to the knee, such as a fall onto the kneecap, a direct blow, or impact from a car dashboard during an accident.
  • Mechanism of Injury: Can also occur from indirect forces, like a sudden, forceful contraction of the quadriceps muscle when the knee is partially bent.
  • Severity Spectrum: Fractures range from stable, non-displaced cracks to severely comminuted (multiple fragments) or open fractures where the bone breaks through the skin.
  • Functional Impact: Significantly impairs the knee's ability to extend, bear weight, and perform daily activities due to disruption of the extensor mechanism.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Acute Pain: Immediate, severe pain in the front of the knee, often exacerbated by any attempt to move the leg.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Rapid onset of swelling (hemarthrosis) and discoloration around the knee due to internal bleeding.
  • Palpable Gap: A noticeable gap or depression may be felt in the kneecap, especially with displaced fractures.
  • Inability to Extend Leg: Difficulty or complete inability to straighten the leg against gravity, indicating disruption of the quadriceps mechanism.
  • Tenderness: Extreme tenderness to touch directly over the patella.
  • Deformity: Visible distortion or abnormal alignment of the kneecap in severe cases.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Immediate Immobilization: Avoid any movement of the injured knee immediately after the injury to prevent further displacement or damage.
  • Non-Weight Bearing: Strictly adhere to non-weight-bearing instructions on the affected leg until medically cleared.
  • Elevation: Elevate the injured leg above heart level to help reduce swelling and pain.
  • Ice Application: Apply ice packs (wrapped in a cloth) to the knee for 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours to manage swelling and discomfort.
  • Prompt Medical Evaluation: Seek immediate medical attention for accurate diagnosis and appropriate treatment planning.
  • Fall Prevention: Implement measures to prevent falls, especially in individuals with osteoporosis or balance issues, to reduce fracture risk.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Protein Intake: Ensure adequate protein consumption (lean meats, fish, legumes) to support tissue repair and bone matrix formation during healing.
  • Calcium and Vitamin D: Increase intake of calcium-rich foods (dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens) and Vitamin D (fatty fish, fortified foods, sunlight exposure) essential for bone mineralization.
  • Hydration: Maintain excellent hydration with water to support overall metabolic processes and aid in nutrient transport and waste elimination.
  • Fiber for Constipation: Increase dietary fiber (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) to prevent constipation, which can be a side effect of pain medications and reduced mobility.
  • Anti-inflammatory Foods: Incorporate foods rich in omega-3 fatty acids (salmon, flaxseed) and antioxidants (berries, colorful vegetables) to support the body's natural healing and reduce inflammation.
  • Limit Inflammatory Agents: Minimize consumption of highly processed foods, excessive sugars, and unhealthy fats that can promote inflammation and potentially hinder healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Response: For severe injuries, open fractures, or inability to bear any weight, call emergency medical services immediately.
  • Stabilization: If trained, apply a temporary splint to immobilize the knee in the position found, avoiding any attempt to straighten or manipulate the limb.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed pain relief as directed by a healthcare professional; monitor for effectiveness and side effects.
  • Wound Care (Open Fractures): For open fractures, cover the wound with a clean, sterile dressing to prevent infection and avoid direct pressure on exposed bone.
  • Post-Operative Monitoring: Closely monitor the patient for signs of infection, neurovascular compromise (numbness, tingling, pallor, coldness), and deep vein thrombosis (DVT) post-surgery.
  • Assistive Devices: Ensure proper use and fitting of crutches, walkers, or other assistive devices for safe non-weight-bearing ambulation.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnosis: Clinical examination, palpation for a patellar gap, and X-rays (AP, lateral, and skyline views) are crucial for confirming the diagnosis and classifying the fracture type.
  • Treatment Modalities: Non-surgical management (immobilization in a cast or brace) is considered for non-displaced or minimally displaced fractures with an intact extensor mechanism.
  • Surgical Intervention: Surgical repair (e.g., tension band wiring, screw fixation, partial or total patellectomy) is typically required for displaced fractures, comminuted fractures, or those disrupting the extensor mechanism.
  • Rehabilitation Protocol: A structured physical therapy program is paramount post-immobilization or surgery to restore knee range of motion, quadriceps strength, and functional mobility.
  • Potential Complications: Risks include knee stiffness, post-traumatic arthritis, non-union or malunion of the fracture, and hardware irritation or failure.
  • Prognosis: With appropriate treatment and diligent rehabilitation, most patients achieve good to excellent outcomes, though full recovery can take several months.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Neurovascular Assessment: Regularly assess distal pulses, capillary refill, sensation, and motor function of the affected limb to detect any neurovascular compromise.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Utilize pain scales to assess pain levels and administer prescribed analgesics, evaluating their effectiveness and monitoring for adverse effects.
  • Immobilization Care: Monitor skin integrity under casts or braces for pressure points, skin breakdown, or signs of compartment syndrome (e.g., severe pain, pallor, paresthesia).
  • Patient Education: Educate patients on activity restrictions, signs of complications (infection, DVT), proper wound care, and the importance of adherence to rehabilitation exercises.
  • Mobility Assistance: Provide instruction and assistance with safe transfers, ambulation with assistive devices (crutches, walker), and proper body mechanics.
  • Post-Operative Monitoring: Observe for signs of surgical site infection, excessive drainage, fever, and monitor vital signs closely.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: S82.0 (Fracture of patella). Specificity depends on laterality, displacement, and fracture type (e.g., S82.001A for displaced transverse fracture of right patella, initial encounter).
  • Imaging Modalities: Standard radiographs (AP, lateral, and sunrise/skyline views of the knee) are primary. CT scans may be used for complex or comminuted fractures to assess articular involvement.
  • Surgical Techniques: Common methods include tension band wiring, cerclage wiring, screw fixation, partial patellectomy (for highly comminuted fragments), or total patellectomy (rarely, for severe comminution).
  • Rehabilitation Phases: Typically involves initial immobilization, followed by progressive range of motion exercises, quadriceps strengthening (e.g., isometric exercises), and gradual weight-bearing progression.
  • Associated Injuries: May include quadriceps tendon rupture, patellar tendon rupture, or other ligamentous injuries of the knee, especially in high-energy trauma.
  • Classification Systems: Patella fractures can be classified by fracture pattern (transverse, longitudinal, comminuted, stellate, polar) and displacement.
