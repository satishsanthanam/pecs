Knee arthroscopy – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Pain Management: Expect some pain and discomfort for several days. Take prescribed pain medication as directed. Do not wait for pain to become severe before taking medication.
  • Swelling and Bruising: Swelling and bruising around the knee are common. Elevate your leg above heart level and apply ice packs for 20 minutes every 2-3 hours to help reduce these symptoms.
  • Activity Restrictions: Follow your surgeon's specific instructions regarding weight-bearing and activity levels. Crutches or a walker may be required initially.
  • Wound Care: Keep the incision sites clean and dry. You will receive specific instructions on when you can shower and how to care for your dressings.
  • Follow-up Appointment: A follow-up appointment is crucial to monitor your recovery and remove sutures or staples, typically within 7-14 days. Ensure you know the date and time.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Normal Post-operative Discomfort: Mild to moderate pain, swelling, and bruising around the knee are expected. Some numbness around the incision is also common.
  • Signs of Infection: Immediately contact your surgeon if you experience fever (oral temperature above 101°F or 38.3°C), increasing redness, warmth, severe pain, or pus draining from the incision sites.
  • Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) Symptoms: Seek urgent medical attention for new or worsening calf pain, tenderness, swelling, or redness in the leg, which could indicate a blood clot.
  • Nerve Impairment: Report any new or persistent numbness, tingling, or weakness in the foot or toes that was not present immediately after surgery.
  • Excessive Bleeding: Contact your doctor if the dressing becomes saturated with blood or if bleeding does not stop with direct pressure.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Weight-Bearing Restrictions: Adhere strictly to the prescribed weight-bearing status (e.g., non-weight-bearing, partial weight-bearing) to protect the healing joint and surgical repairs.
  • Fall Prevention: Use assistive devices (crutches, walker) as instructed and ensure your home environment is free of tripping hazards (e.g., loose rugs, cords). Avoid stairs if possible or use them with extreme caution.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including pain relievers and any prophylactic antibiotics or anticoagulants, exactly as directed. Do not share medications.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive while taking narcotic pain medication or until cleared by your surgeon, especially if the surgery was on your right knee.
  • Avoid Immersion: Do not submerge the surgical site in water (e.g., baths, hot tubs, swimming pools) until the incisions are fully healed and cleared by your surgeon to prevent infection.
  • Physical Therapy Compliance: Begin and diligently follow your prescribed physical therapy exercises. These are vital for restoring strength, flexibility, and function.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Post-Anesthesia Nausea: Start with clear liquids and light foods (e.g., crackers, toast, broth) to prevent nausea and vomiting, especially in the first 24 hours post-anesthesia.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake to prevent dehydration, which can be exacerbated by pain medications and reduced activity.
  • Constipation Management: Pain medications can cause constipation. Increase fiber intake through fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, and consider a stool softener as recommended by your physician.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from consuming alcohol while taking pain medications, as this can lead to dangerous interactions and increased sedation.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein for tissue repair and vitamins/minerals to support healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Medication Assistance: Help the patient remember to take medications on schedule and monitor for side effects.
  • Mobility Support: Assist with safe transfers and ambulation, ensuring the patient uses assistive devices correctly and avoids falls.
  • Wound Observation: Monitor incision sites daily for signs of infection (redness, swelling, discharge, warmth) and report any concerns to the medical team.
  • Ice and Elevation: Assist with proper application of ice packs and elevation of the leg to manage swelling.
  • Transportation: Ensure safe transportation for follow-up appointments and in case of emergencies.
  • Emotional Support: Provide encouragement and support during the recovery process, which can be physically and emotionally challenging.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Rehabilitation: Successful recovery hinges on strict adherence to the prescribed physical therapy regimen. Consistency is key to regaining full function.
  • Long-Term Expectations: While arthroscopy is minimally invasive, full recovery can take several weeks to months. Manage expectations and avoid rushing the healing process.
  • Activity Modification: Listen to your body and gradually increase activity levels. Avoid high-impact or twisting movements until cleared, even after initial recovery.
  • Preventative Measures: Discuss strategies to prevent re-injury, such as strengthening exercises, proper body mechanics, and appropriate footwear.
  • Report Concerns Promptly: Do not hesitate to contact our office with any questions or concerns, especially if you experience worsening pain, signs of infection, or new neurological symptoms.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Discharge Readiness Assessment: Evaluate patient's pain control, ability to ambulate safely with assistive devices, understanding of discharge instructions, and presence of a responsible adult for transport and home care.
  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Provide clear, concise verbal and written instructions on wound care, medication regimen, activity restrictions, DVT prevention, and signs/symptoms requiring immediate medical attention.
  • Pain Assessment and Management: Educate on the use of a pain scale, the importance of scheduled pain medication, and non-pharmacological pain relief methods (ice, elevation).
  • Mobility and Safety: Demonstrate proper use of crutches/walker, emphasize fall prevention strategies, and assess home environment safety if possible.
  • Post-operative Complication Monitoring: Instruct patient and caregiver on recognizing and reporting signs of infection, DVT, nerve damage, or excessive bleeding.
  • Follow-up Coordination: Confirm the patient has a scheduled follow-up appointment and understands its importance.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CPT Codes: Common codes include 29870-29889 for various arthroscopic procedures of the knee.
  • ICD-10 Codes: Relevant codes for post-operative care and complications, e.g., T81.4 (Infection following a procedure), I82.4 (Acute embolism and thrombosis of other deep veins of lower extremity).
  • DVT Prophylaxis: May include pharmacological agents (e.g., low molecular weight heparin) and mechanical methods (e.g., sequential compression devices, early ambulation).
  • RICE Protocol: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation – fundamental for managing post-operative swelling and pain.
  • Knee Bracing: Specific brace types (e.g., hinged knee brace, immobilizer) may be prescribed depending on the surgical repair (e.g., ligament reconstruction, meniscal repair).
  • Pain Management Regimen: Often involves a multimodal approach including NSAIDs, acetaminophen, and short-term opioids.
