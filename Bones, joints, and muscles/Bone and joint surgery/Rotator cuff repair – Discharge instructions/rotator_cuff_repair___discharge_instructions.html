Rotator cuff repair – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Recovery: Rotator cuff repair is a significant procedure requiring diligent post-operative care for optimal healing and function.
  • Expected Timeline: Full recovery can take 6-12 months, with initial weeks focused on protection and pain management, followed by progressive rehabilitation.
  • Adherence is Key: Strictly follow all discharge instructions, including sling use, activity restrictions, and physical therapy, to prevent re-injury and ensure successful outcomes.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Expected Post-Op Discomfort: Mild to moderate pain, swelling, and bruising around the shoulder and upper arm are common and should gradually improve.
  • Signs of Infection: Report immediately if you experience fever (over 101°F or 38.3°C), increasing redness, warmth, severe swelling, or pus-like drainage from the incision site.
  • Neurological Changes: Contact your physician if you develop new or worsening numbness, tingling, or weakness in your hand or fingers.
  • Uncontrolled Pain: Seek medical advice if your pain is severe and not adequately managed by prescribed pain medication.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Sling Immobilization: Wear your prescribed sling continuously, including during sleep, for the duration instructed by your surgeon, typically 4-6 weeks, removing only for exercises or hygiene as directed.
  • Activity Restrictions: Absolutely no lifting, pushing, pulling, or reaching overhead with the operative arm. Avoid sudden movements, external rotation, or internal rotation beyond prescribed limits.
  • Incision Care: Keep the incision clean and dry. Do not submerge the incision in baths, hot tubs, or swimming pools until cleared by your surgeon, usually after staples/sutures are removed and the wound is fully closed.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive while wearing a sling or while taking opioid pain medication. You must have full control of your operative arm and be off sedating medications.
  • Fall Prevention: Use caution when ambulating, especially if taking pain medication that may cause dizziness or drowsiness. Ensure clear pathways at home.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration for Healing: Maintain adequate fluid intake (water, clear broths) to support tissue healing and prevent constipation, a common side effect of pain medications.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein (lean meats, fish, beans), vitamins (especially C and D), and minerals (calcium, zinc) to promote wound healing and tissue repair.
  • Managing Constipation: Incorporate fiber-rich foods (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) and consider a stool softener as prescribed or recommended to counteract opioid-induced constipation.
  • Alcohol Avoidance: Refrain from consuming alcohol while taking prescription pain medications, as this can lead to dangerous interactions and increased sedation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assistance with Daily Activities: The patient will require significant assistance with dressing, bathing, meal preparation, and other activities of daily living for several weeks post-surgery.
  • Medication Management: Ensure all prescribed medications, especially pain relievers, are administered on schedule to maintain comfort and facilitate rest.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Be vigilant for any signs of infection (fever, increased redness, warmth, drainage) or other concerning symptoms and report them to the medical team promptly.
  • Safe Environment: Help maintain a safe home environment, removing tripping hazards and ensuring easy access to necessities to prevent falls or accidental use of the operative arm.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Commit to Rehabilitation: Your active participation in physical therapy is paramount for regaining strength, flexibility, and full function of your shoulder. Do not skip sessions or exercises.
  • Manage Pain Proactively: Take prescribed pain medication as directed, especially before physical therapy sessions, to optimize your ability to participate. Do not wait for pain to become severe.
  • Patience and Persistence: Recovery from rotator cuff repair is a marathon, not a sprint. Be patient with your progress, celebrate small victories, and remain persistent with your rehabilitation program.
  • Open Communication: Do not hesitate to contact your surgeon's office with any questions, concerns, or unexpected symptoms. Early intervention can prevent complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Reinforce Patient Education: Systematically review and reinforce all discharge instructions with the patient and their primary caregiver, focusing on sling use, activity restrictions, and pain management strategies.
  • Wound Assessment: Instruct on proper incision care, emphasizing signs of infection (erythema, warmth, purulent drainage, dehiscence) and when to seek medical attention.
  • Neurovascular Monitoring: Educate on monitoring for changes in sensation, motor function, and circulation (color, temperature, capillary refill) in the affected extremity, especially distal to the surgical site.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Ensure the patient understands the purpose, dosage, frequency, and potential side effects of all prescribed discharge medications, particularly analgesics and anti-inflammatories.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Surgical Approach: Rotator cuff repair typically involves arthroscopic or open techniques to reattach torn tendons (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) to the humeral head using suture anchors.
  • Rehabilitation Phases: Post-operative rehabilitation progresses through distinct phases: initial immobilization (0-6 weeks), passive range of motion (PROM), active assisted range of motion (AAROM), active range of motion (AROM), and progressive strengthening (typically 12+ weeks).
  • Potential Complications: Risks include re-tear of the repaired cuff, adhesive capsulitis (frozen shoulder), infection, nerve injury (e.g., axillary nerve), deltoid detachment, and persistent pain.
  • Pain Management Protocol: A multimodal approach often includes opioid analgesics for acute pain, NSAIDs (if not contraindicated), acetaminophen, regional nerve blocks (e.g., interscalene block), and cryotherapy.
