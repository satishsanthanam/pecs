# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Total Knee Arthroplasty (TKA)**: Surgical procedure to replace damaged knee joint surfaces with artificial components (prostheses).
* **Partial Knee Arthroplasty (PKA)**: Replaces only the most damaged compartment of the knee, preserving healthy bone and cartilage.
* **Primary Goal**: Alleviate severe knee pain, improve mobility, and correct deformity caused by arthritis or injury.
* **Common Indications**: Osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and avascular necrosis.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Persistent Knee Pain**: Severe, chronic pain not relieved by conservative treatments (medication, physical therapy, injections).
* **Stiffness and Reduced Mobility**: Difficulty bending or straightening the knee, impacting daily activities like walking, climbing stairs, or standing.
* **Swelling and Inflammation**: Recurrent or chronic swelling around the knee joint.
* **Grinding or Catching Sensation**: Audible or palpable crepitus during knee movement.
* **Knee Deformity**: Bow-legged (varus) or knock-kneed (valgus) deformity that significantly affects gait and function.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Pre-operative Medical Clearance**: Thorough evaluation by primary care physician and specialists to ensure fitness for surgery, managing chronic conditions like diabetes or heart disease.
* **Medication Review**: Discontinuation of blood thinners (e.g., warfarin, aspirin, NSAIDs) as advised by the surgeon, typically 7-10 days prior to surgery, to minimize bleeding risk.
* **Infection Prevention**: Prophylactic antibiotics administered before surgery; meticulous wound care post-operatively to prevent surgical site infection.
* **Fall Prevention**: Post-surgery, use assistive devices (walker, crutches) as instructed; modify home environment to remove tripping hazards.
* **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) Prophylaxis**: Adherence to prescribed anticoagulants, early mobilization, and use of compression stockings or sequential compression devices.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Pre-operative Fasting**: Strict NPO (nil per os) after midnight on the day of surgery, or as directed by the anesthesiologist, to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
* **Post-operative Hydration**: Gradual reintroduction of clear liquids, then soft foods, progressing to a regular diet as tolerated, to prevent nausea and constipation.
* **Nutrient-Rich Diet**: Emphasize protein for tissue repair, vitamins (especially C and D) and minerals (calcium) for bone healing, and fiber to prevent constipation from pain medication.
* **Avoid Inflammatory Foods**: Limit highly processed foods, excessive sugars, and unhealthy fats that may hinder the healing process.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Assistance with ADLs**: Be prepared to assist the patient with activities of daily living (dressing, bathing, toileting) in the initial post-operative period.
* **Medication Management**: Help monitor and administer pain medications and other prescribed drugs, noting any side effects.
* **Mobility Support**: Provide physical support during transfers and ambulation, ensuring safe use of assistive devices and adherence to weight-bearing restrictions.
* **Wound Care Observation**: Monitor the surgical incision for signs of infection (redness, swelling, warmth, discharge, increasing pain) and report to the medical team.
* **Physical Therapy Adherence**: Facilitate and encourage consistent participation in prescribed physical therapy exercises, which are crucial for recovery.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Patient Selection**: Knee replacement is considered when severe knee pain and functional limitations significantly impact quality of life, and conservative treatments have failed.
* **Realistic Expectations**: Patients should understand that while surgery aims to relieve pain and improve function, full restoration of pre-injury activity levels may not always be achieved.
* **Post-operative Rehabilitation**: Adherence to a structured physical therapy program is paramount for optimal recovery, range of motion, and strength.
* **Long-term Management**: Regular follow-up appointments are essential to monitor prosthesis longevity, detect complications, and manage any ongoing symptoms.
* **Risk-Benefit Discussion**: A thorough discussion of potential risks (infection, DVT, nerve damage, prosthesis loosening) versus expected benefits is crucial for informed consent.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pre-operative Education**: Educate patients on the surgical process, pain management expectations, post-operative exercises, and use of assistive devices.
* **Pain Assessment & Management**: Implement a multi-modal pain management strategy, regularly assess pain levels, and administer analgesics as prescribed.
* **Neurovascular Checks**: Perform frequent neurovascular assessments of the affected extremity (color, temperature, sensation, motor function, pulses) to detect complications like compartment syndrome or nerve injury.
* **Mobility & DVT Prophylaxis**: Encourage early ambulation, assist with exercises, and ensure proper use of DVT prophylaxis measures (e.g., compression stockings, anticoagulants).
* **Wound Care & Infection Monitoring**: Monitor the surgical incision for signs of infection, maintain sterile dressing changes, and educate patients on home wound care.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Arthroplasty**: Surgical reconstruction or replacement of a joint.
* **Prosthesis**: An artificial device that replaces a missing body part or augments a damaged part, in this case, the knee joint components.
* **Femoral Component**: The metal cap that covers the end of the thigh bone (femur).
* **Tibial Component**: The metal tray with a polyethylene insert that covers the top of the shin bone (tibia).
* **Patellar Component**: A plastic button that replaces the kneecap (patella) surface, if necessary.
* **Cemented vs. Uncemented**: Refers to how the prosthetic components are fixed to the bone; cemented uses bone cement, uncemented relies on bone ingrowth into a porous coating.
