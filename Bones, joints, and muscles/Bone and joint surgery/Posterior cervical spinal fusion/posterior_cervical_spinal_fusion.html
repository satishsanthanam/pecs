Posterior cervical spinal fusion

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Posterior cervical spinal fusion is a surgical procedure to permanently join two or more vertebrae in the neck from the back of the spine.
  • Primary Goal: To stabilize the cervical spine, decompress spinal nerves or the spinal cord, and alleviate pain and neurological symptoms caused by instability, degeneration, or trauma.
  • Instrumentation: Often involves the use of metal plates, screws, and rods to hold the vertebrae in place while the bone graft fuses.
  • Bone Grafting: Bone graft material (autograft from the patient or allograft from a donor) is placed between the vertebrae to promote fusion.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Chronic Neck Pain: Persistent, severe neck pain unresponsive to conservative treatments like physical therapy, medication, or injections.
  • Cervical Radiculopathy: Pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness radiating into the arm, hand, or fingers due to nerve root compression.
  • Cervical Myelopathy: Symptoms of spinal cord compression including gait disturbance, balance issues, loss of fine motor skills, hand clumsiness, and spasticity.
  • Spinal Instability: Sensation of the neck 'giving way' or severe pain with certain movements, often confirmed by dynamic imaging.
  • Progressive Neurological Deficits: Worsening weakness, sensory loss, or reflex changes indicating ongoing nerve or spinal cord damage.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Medication Review: Discontinue blood thinners, NSAIDs, and certain supplements as advised by the surgeon to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Smoking Cessation: Absolutely critical to stop smoking several weeks before and after surgery, as nicotine significantly impairs bone fusion.
  • Post-operative Cervical Immobilization: Strict adherence to wearing a cervical collar or brace as prescribed to protect the fusion site.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid lifting, twisting, bending the neck, and strenuous activities for several months post-surgery to prevent hardware failure or non-union.
  • Infection Prevention: Maintain meticulous wound care and report any signs of infection (redness, swelling, discharge, fever) immediately.
  • Avoid NSAIDs Post-op: Some surgeons recommend avoiding NSAIDs for a period after surgery as they can inhibit bone healing.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines for food and drink before surgery as instructed by the anesthesia team.
  • Post-operative Swallowing: Start with clear liquids, then advance to soft foods as tolerated, especially if experiencing dysphagia or sore throat from intubation.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake to support healing, prevent constipation, and aid in medication absorption.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on foods rich in protein, calcium, and Vitamin D to support bone healing and overall recovery.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Refrain from alcohol consumption during the recovery period, as it can interfere with medication and healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Pain Management: Follow the prescribed pain medication regimen diligently; do not wait for pain to become severe before taking medication.
  • Wound Monitoring: Keep the incision clean and dry. Report any excessive drainage, foul odor, increasing redness, or separation of the wound edges.
  • Neurological Changes: Immediately report any new or worsening numbness, tingling, weakness, or difficulty moving limbs.
  • Fever and Chills: These can be signs of infection and require prompt medical evaluation.
  • Constipation Management: Post-operative pain medications often cause constipation; use stool softeners or laxatives as prescribed.
  • Safe Mobility: Use proper body mechanics for getting in and out of bed, avoiding neck twisting or bending, often requiring assistance initially.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Realistic Expectations: Understand that spinal fusion is a major surgery with a significant recovery period, and complete pain relief is not always guaranteed.
  • Adherence to Post-operative Protocol: Strict compliance with activity restrictions, cervical collar use, and physical therapy is paramount for successful fusion and optimal outcomes.
  • Long-term Follow-up: Regular clinical assessments and imaging studies (X-rays) are essential to monitor fusion progress and identify potential complications.
  • Risk-Benefit Discussion: A thorough understanding of the potential benefits (pain relief, neurological improvement, stability) and risks (non-union, infection, nerve injury, adjacent segment disease) is crucial before proceeding.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: Emphasize the importance of maintaining a healthy weight and avoiding activities that place undue stress on the cervical spine long-term.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Provide comprehensive patient teaching on the surgical procedure, expected post-operative course, pain management strategies, and discharge planning.
  • Post-operative Assessment: Conduct frequent neurological assessments (motor, sensory, reflexes), vital signs monitoring, pain assessment, and wound site evaluation.
  • Cervical Collar Management: Ensure correct application and removal of the cervical collar, educating the patient on its purpose and wear schedule.
  • Mobility Assistance: Safely assist with log-rolling and ambulation, emphasizing spine precautions and proper body mechanics.
  • Complication Monitoring: Vigilantly monitor for signs of infection, CSF leak (clear drainage from wound), neurological deficits, and respiratory compromise.
  • Discharge Planning: Coordinate home care needs, medication reconciliation, follow-up appointments, and provide clear written instructions for self-care and activity restrictions.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Indications: Cervical instability (e.g., trauma, rheumatoid arthritis), degenerative disc disease with myelopathy or severe radiculopathy, cervical deformity, tumor, infection.
  • Surgical Approach: Posterior midline incision, often involving laminectomy or laminoplasty for decompression, followed by instrumentation and bone grafting.
  • Instrumentation: Typically involves lateral mass screws or pedicle screws connected by rods to provide immediate stability.
  • Bone Graft Options: Autograft (iliac crest, local bone), allograft (cadaveric bone), or synthetic bone graft substitutes.
  • Potential Complications: Non-union (pseudarthrosis), infection, nerve root injury, spinal cord injury, CSF leak, hardware failure, dysphagia, adjacent segment disease.
  • Post-operative Imaging: Serial plain radiographs are used to assess fusion progression; CT scans may be used to confirm fusion if non-union is suspected.
