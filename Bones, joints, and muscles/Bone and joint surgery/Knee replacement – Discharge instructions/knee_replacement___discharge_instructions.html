Knee replacement – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Discharge: These instructions are vital for a safe and effective recovery at home following your knee replacement surgery.
  • Expected Recovery Timeline: Recovery is a gradual process, and adherence to these guidelines will optimize your outcome, typically spanning several weeks to months.
  • Active Participation is Key: Your active involvement in your rehabilitation, including exercises and activity modifications, is crucial for regaining strength and mobility.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Normal Post-Operative Discomfort: Expect some pain, swelling, and bruising around the surgical site, which should gradually improve with time and prescribed pain management.
  • Signs of Infection: Report immediately if you experience fever (above 101°F or 38.3°C), chills, increasing redness, warmth, pus-like drainage from the incision, or severe, unremitting pain.
  • Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) Warning Signs: Seek urgent medical attention for sudden calf pain, tenderness, swelling, or redness in your leg, or sudden shortness of breath or chest pain.
  • Numbness or Tingling: Persistent or worsening numbness, tingling, or weakness in your foot or leg should be reported to your surgeon promptly.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Fall Prevention: Use prescribed assistive devices (walker, crutches) consistently. Ensure your home environment is free of tripping hazards like loose rugs, cords, or clutter.
  • Incision Care: Keep the incision clean and dry. Follow specific instructions for dressing changes. Do not submerge the incision in water (e.g., baths, pools) until cleared by your surgeon.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid twisting or pivoting on the operative leg. Do not lift heavy objects. Strictly follow your physical therapist's instructions for weight-bearing and range of motion exercises.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including pain relievers, antibiotics, and blood thinners, exactly as directed. Do not stop blood thinners without consulting your surgeon.
  • Driving Restrictions: Do not drive until cleared by your surgeon, typically when you are off narcotic pain medication and have regained sufficient strength, reaction time, and full control of the operative leg.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths) to aid recovery, support tissue healing, and prevent constipation, especially while taking pain medications.
  • Fiber-Rich Foods: Incorporate fruits, vegetables, and whole grains into your diet to prevent constipation, a common side effect of pain medication and reduced activity.
  • Balanced Nutrition for Healing: Focus on a balanced diet rich in lean protein for tissue repair and essential vitamins/minerals for overall healing and immune function.
  • Avoid Excessive Alcohol: Alcohol can interact with medications, impair judgment, and increase fall risk. Limit or avoid consumption during your recovery period.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Assistance with Daily Activities: Be prepared to assist the patient with personal care, meal preparation, and safe mobility during the initial recovery period at home.
  • Medication Management Support: Help the patient keep track of medication schedules and ensure proper administration, especially for pain relief and blood thinners.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Be vigilant for any signs of infection, DVT, or other concerning symptoms (e.g., sudden increase in pain, fever) and know when to contact the medical team.
  • Emotional Support and Encouragement: Provide consistent encouragement and support, as recovery can be challenging both physically and emotionally for the patient.
  • Home Safety Modifications: Ensure the home environment is safe, with clear pathways, grab bars in the bathroom, and an elevated toilet seat if recommended.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Commitment to Rehabilitation: Your dedication to physical therapy is paramount for restoring function, regaining strength, and achieving the best possible long-term outcome from your knee replacement.
  • Effective Pain Management: Manage your pain effectively to allow for full participation in therapy. Do not hesitate to discuss pain concerns or medication side effects with your medical team.
  • Long-Term Joint Health: Protect your new joint by avoiding high-impact activities, adhering to exercise guidelines, and maintaining a healthy weight to reduce stress on the prosthesis.
  • Regular Follow-ups: Adhere to all scheduled post-operative appointments with your orthopedic surgeon to monitor your progress, assess joint function, and address any concerns.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Reinforce Discharge Teaching: Systematically review all discharge instructions with the patient and caregiver, ensuring comprehension of medication schedules, wound care, activity restrictions, and warning signs.
  • Pain Assessment and Management Education: Educate on the importance of effective pain control for mobility and participation in physical therapy. Assess pain levels regularly and intervene with appropriate analgesia.
  • Early Ambulation and DVT Prophylaxis: Emphasize the critical role of early, supervised ambulation and strict adherence to anticoagulant therapy to prevent deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism.
  • Infection Surveillance and Prevention: Instruct on recognizing and reporting signs of surgical site infection, stressing meticulous hand hygiene and proper incision care techniques.
  • Resource Provision and Coordination: Provide clear contact information for the surgical team, physical therapy, and any necessary home health services, ensuring a smooth transition of care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Implant Information: Document the specific type, manufacturer, and size of the knee prosthesis implanted for future reference and potential imaging considerations.
  • Rehabilitation Protocol: Detail the prescribed post-operative physical therapy protocol, including frequency, duration, specific range-of-motion goals, strengthening exercises, and weight-bearing status.
  • Pharmacological Regimen: List all discharge medications, including analgesics, anticoagulants (e.g., rivaroxaban, apixaban), and prophylactic antibiotics, with precise dosages, administration instructions, and duration.
  • Follow-up Schedule: Specify dates for post-operative clinic visits with the orthopedic surgeon and initial physical therapy appointments, including contact details for scheduling changes.
  • Weight-Bearing Status: Clearly state the patient's prescribed weight-bearing status (e.g., weight-bearing as tolerated, partial weight-bearing) as determined by the surgeon, along with any specific limitations.
