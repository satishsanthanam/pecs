Knee arthroscopy

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Bone and joint surgery

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Minimally Invasive Procedure: Knee arthroscopy is a surgical technique that uses small incisions and a specialized camera (arthroscope) to visualize and treat problems inside the knee joint.
  • Diagnostic and Therapeutic: It can be used to diagnose the cause of knee pain, swelling, or instability, and to perform repairs or removals of damaged tissue.
  • Common Conditions Treated: Frequently performed for meniscal tears, anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction, cartilage defects, removal of loose bodies, and treatment of patellar issues.
  • Outpatient Setting: Often performed as an outpatient procedure, meaning patients can typically go home the same day after recovery from anesthesia.
  • Reduced Recovery Time: Compared to traditional open surgery, arthroscopy generally results in less pain, less joint stiffness, and a faster recovery.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Knee Pain: Chronic or acute pain in the knee joint that does not improve with conservative treatments like rest, ice, compression, elevation (RICE), medication, or physical therapy.
  • Swelling and Effusion: Accumulation of fluid within the knee joint, often accompanied by warmth and tenderness, indicating inflammation or injury.
  • Clicking, Catching, or Locking: Sensations within the knee that suggest a mechanical obstruction, such as a torn meniscus or a loose piece of cartilage.
  • Knee Instability or 'Giving Way': A feeling that the knee is unreliable or might buckle, often indicative of ligamentous injury, particularly an ACL tear.
  • Reduced Range of Motion: Difficulty fully straightening or bending the knee, which may be due to pain, swelling, or a mechanical block.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Medical Evaluation: Undergo a thorough medical assessment, including a review of medical history, current medications, and any allergies, to ensure fitness for surgery and anesthesia.
  • Medication Management: Discontinue blood-thinning medications (e.g., aspirin, NSAIDs, warfarin, novel oral anticoagulants) as instructed by the surgeon, typically 5-7 days prior to surgery, to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Infection Prevention: Report any signs of infection (e.g., fever, rash, skin lesions) near the surgical site to your healthcare provider immediately, as surgery may need to be postponed.
  • Smoking Cessation: Cease smoking several weeks before surgery, as it can impair healing, increase infection risk, and complicate anesthesia.
  • Post-operative Support: Arrange for a responsible adult to drive you home after the procedure and assist with daily activities during the initial recovery period.
  • Adherence to Instructions: Strictly follow all pre-operative and post-operative instructions regarding activity restrictions, wound care, and medication schedules.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines, typically refraining from food and drink for 6-8 hours before surgery, to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
  • Hydration Prior to Fasting: Ensure adequate hydration in the days leading up to surgery, but cease fluid intake as per fasting instructions.
  • Post-Anesthesia Reintroduction: Begin with clear liquids (water, clear broth, apple juice) after surgery, progressing slowly to light, easily digestible foods as tolerated to avoid nausea and vomiting.
  • Avoid Alcohol and Caffeine: Refrain from alcohol consumption for at least 24-48 hours post-surgery due to interactions with pain medications and potential dehydration. Limit caffeine intake initially.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet for Healing: Focus on a balanced diet rich in protein, vitamins (especially C and D), and minerals (calcium, zinc) to support tissue repair and reduce inflammation during recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Transportation Requirement: The patient will require a responsible adult to drive them home after the procedure due to the effects of anesthesia and pain medication.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Monitor the patient for signs of complications such as excessive bleeding from the surgical site, severe or uncontrolled pain, fever, chills, or numbness/tingling in the lower leg or foot.
  • Medication Assistance: Assist the patient with adhering to their prescribed pain medication schedule and any antibiotics, ensuring proper dosage and timing.
  • Mobility Support: Help the patient with safe ambulation using crutches or other assistive devices, reinforcing weight-bearing restrictions as instructed by the surgeon.
  • Ice and Elevation: Aid in the regular application of ice packs to the knee and ensure the leg is elevated above heart level to reduce swelling and discomfort.
  • Home Environment Safety: Ensure the home environment is safe, free of tripping hazards, and that necessary items are within easy reach to minimize movement and fall risk.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Individualized Treatment Plan: Each knee arthroscopy is tailored to the specific pathology identified and the patient's overall health, activity level, and recovery goals.
  • Realistic Expectations: Patients should have a clear understanding of the potential outcomes, recovery timeline, and the importance of active participation in their rehabilitation.
  • Rehabilitation is Paramount: Surgical success is highly dependent on diligent adherence to the prescribed physical therapy program, which is crucial for restoring strength, flexibility, and function.
  • Risk-Benefit Discussion: A thorough discussion of potential risks (e.g., infection, DVT, nerve damage, stiffness) versus the anticipated benefits of pain relief and improved knee function is essential.
  • Long-Term Knee Health: Advise patients on strategies to maintain knee health post-surgery, including appropriate exercise, weight management, and activity modification to prevent re-injury.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Provide comprehensive patient education on the procedure, anesthesia, pain management expectations, and post-operative care, including wound care and activity restrictions.
  • Neurovascular Assessment: Perform regular neurovascular checks (color, temperature, sensation, movement – CMS) of the operative extremity to monitor for signs of compromise.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics and assess pain levels frequently using appropriate pain scales, adjusting interventions as needed to ensure patient comfort.
  • Wound Care and Infection Control: Monitor surgical incision sites for signs of infection (redness, swelling, warmth, purulent discharge) and perform dressing changes using aseptic technique.
  • DVT Prophylaxis: Implement measures to prevent deep vein thrombosis (DVT), such as early ambulation, sequential compression devices (SCDs), or prophylactic anticoagulants as ordered.
  • Mobility and Rehabilitation Support: Assist patients with safe transfers and ambulation using assistive devices, reinforcing physical therapy exercises and weight-bearing precautions.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: M23.2 (Derangement of meniscus), S83.5 (Sprain of cruciate ligament of knee), M22.4 (Chondromalacia patellae), M25.56 (Pain in knee).
  • CPT Codes: 29870 (Arthroscopy, knee, diagnostic), 29880 (Arthroscopy, knee, meniscectomy), 29888 (Arthroscopy, knee, anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction), 29877 (Arthroscopy, knee, debridement/chondroplasty).
  • Anesthesia Options: General anesthesia, spinal anesthesia, or regional nerve block (e.g., femoral nerve block, adductor canal block).
  • Common Instruments: Arthroscope, light source, camera, video monitor, shaver, grasping forceps, scissors, probes, suture passers, radiofrequency ablation devices.
  • Potential Complications: Infection, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), nerve damage, persistent stiffness (arthrofibrosis), reflex sympathetic dystrophy (RSD), re-tear of repaired structures.
  • Rehabilitation Phases: Typically involves acute (pain/swelling control, early range of motion), subacute (progressive strengthening, proprioception), and return-to-activity phases, guided by physical therapy.
