Multiple sclerosis relapse in adults – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Bones, joints, and muscles / Muscle disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Relapse: A multiple sclerosis (MS) relapse, also known as an exacerbation or flare-up, is a sudden onset or worsening of neurological symptoms lasting at least 24 hours, in the absence of fever or infection.
  • Treatment Adherence: Strictly follow the prescribed treatment plan, which may include corticosteroids to shorten the duration and severity of the relapse.
  • Recovery Expectations: Recovery from a relapse varies; some symptoms may resolve completely, while others may persist. Patience, consistent rehabilitation, and adherence to your long-term management plan are crucial.
  • Follow-up Care: Schedule and attend all follow-up appointments with your neurologist, rehabilitation team, and other specialists as recommended.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • New or Worsening Weakness: Sudden or increased muscle weakness in one or more limbs, affecting mobility, balance, or coordination.
  • Sensory Changes: New or intensified numbness, tingling, burning sensations, or electric shock-like feelings (e.g., Lhermitte's sign) in various body parts.
  • Vision Disturbances: Blurred vision, double vision (diplopia), or pain with eye movement (optic neuritis) that is new or significantly worse.
  • Balance and Coordination Issues: Increased dizziness, vertigo, unsteadiness, or difficulty with walking (ataxia).
  • Severe Fatigue: Overwhelming tiredness disproportionate to activity, significantly impacting daily function and not relieved by rest.
  • Cognitive Changes: Noticeable difficulties with memory, concentration, word-finding, or processing information.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Side Effects: Be aware of potential side effects of prescribed medications, especially corticosteroids (e.g., mood changes, sleep disturbances, increased blood sugar). Report any severe or unusual effects to your healthcare provider.
  • Fall Prevention: Due to potential weakness, spasticity, or balance issues, use assistive devices as recommended (e.g., cane, walker) and ensure a clear, well-lit home environment free of tripping hazards.
  • Avoid Overheating: High body temperatures can temporarily worsen MS symptoms (Uhthoff's phenomenon). Stay cool, especially during exercise, hot weather, or when taking a hot bath/shower.
  • Infection Prevention: Avoid exposure to infections, as they can trigger or worsen relapses. Practice good hand hygiene, avoid sick individuals, and discuss recommended vaccinations with your doctor.
  • Stress Management: High stress levels can impact MS symptoms. Implement stress-reduction techniques such as mindfulness, meditation, gentle exercise, or seeking psychological support.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake throughout the day to prevent dehydration, support overall well-being, and manage potential constipation, especially if on certain medications.
  • Nutrient-Rich Foods: Focus on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains to support energy levels, immune function, and neurological health.
  • Fiber Intake: Increase dietary fiber to help manage potential bowel dysfunction, a common MS symptom. Include whole grains, legumes, fruits, and vegetables.
  • Limit Processed Foods and Sugars: Reduce intake of highly processed foods, excessive sugars, and unhealthy fats that can contribute to inflammation and energy fluctuations.
  • Vitamin D Supplementation: Discuss vitamin D supplementation with your neurologist, as it is often recommended for people with MS and plays a role in immune regulation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Keep emergency contact information for your neurologist, primary care physician, and local emergency services readily available.
  • Recognizing Worsening Symptoms: Seek immediate medical attention if you experience sudden, severe worsening of symptoms, new seizures, signs of infection (e.g., high fever, severe pain), or any concerning changes.
  • Caregiver Support: Educate family members and caregivers on how to recognize new or worsening relapse symptoms, assist with daily activities, and administer medications as needed.
  • Home Safety Assessment: Ensure the home environment is safe and accessible, especially if mobility is impaired. Remove throw rugs, ensure adequate lighting, and consider grab bars in bathrooms.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Long-term Disease Management: Emphasize the critical importance of continuing disease-modifying therapies (DMTs) as prescribed to reduce the frequency and severity of future relapses and slow disease progression.
  • Rehabilitation Services: Actively participate in prescribed physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy to regain function, manage symptoms, and improve quality of life.
  • Mental Health Support: MS can significantly impact mental health. Encourage seeking support from a therapist, counselor, or support groups if experiencing depression, anxiety, or adjustment difficulties.
  • Open Communication: Maintain open and honest communication with your entire healthcare team about all symptoms, concerns, medication side effects, and any changes in your condition.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Reinforce patient and family education regarding relapse identification, medication adherence, symptom management strategies, and the importance of follow-up care.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Regularly assess and document neurological symptoms, functional status (e.g., mobility, ADLs), pain levels, and any new complaints.
  • Medication Administration & Monitoring: Ensure correct administration of relapse treatments (e.g., IV corticosteroids) and closely monitor for adverse reactions and side effects.
  • Referral Coordination: Facilitate timely referrals to rehabilitation specialists (physical, occupational, speech therapists), social workers, and mental health professionals as indicated.
  • Fatigue Management Strategies: Educate patients on energy conservation techniques, pacing activities, and other strategies to manage MS-related fatigue effectively.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • MS Relapse Definition: An acute episode of neurological dysfunction attributable to MS, lasting at least 24 hours, in the absence of fever or infection, and often associated with new lesion activity on MRI.
  • Corticosteroid Therapy: High-dose intravenous or oral corticosteroids (e.g., methylprednisolone) are the standard acute treatment for relapses to reduce inflammation and shorten recovery time.
  • Disease-Modifying Therapies (DMTs): A class of long-term treatments aimed at reducing relapse frequency, severity, and progression of disability in MS (e.g., interferons, glatiramer acetate, oral agents, monoclonal antibodies).
  • Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS): A widely used quantitative scale to assess and monitor the level of disability in people with MS, ranging from 0 (normal neurological exam) to 10 (death due to MS).
  • Neurorehabilitation: A multidisciplinary approach involving physical, occupational, and speech therapy, among others, to optimize function, manage symptoms, and improve the quality of life for individuals with MS.
