How to put in a child's nasogastric tube

🏠 Home / Home health / Procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of NG Tube: A nasogastric (NG) tube is a flexible tube inserted through the nose, down the esophagus, and into the stomach to deliver nutrition, medication, or remove air/fluid.
  • Home Health Context: In a home setting, caregivers or parents are often trained by healthcare professionals to manage NG tube placement and care for their child.
  • Importance of Training: Proper technique is crucial to prevent complications such as respiratory distress, aspiration, or injury to the nasal passages or esophagus.
  • Child-Specific Considerations: Children require specific tube sizes and gentle handling due to their smaller and more delicate anatomy.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Poor Feeding/Failure to Thrive: Inability to consume adequate oral nutrition leading to insufficient weight gain or growth.
  • Dysphagia or Aspiration Risk: Difficulty swallowing safely, increasing the risk of food or liquid entering the lungs.
  • Severe Gastroesophageal Reflux: Persistent reflux that may require gastric decompression or specific medication delivery.
  • Neurological Impairment: Conditions affecting the ability to coordinate sucking, swallowing, and breathing.
  • Specific Medical Conditions: Certain illnesses or post-surgical states requiring temporary or long-term enteral feeding.
  • Tube Dislodgement Signs: Coughing, gagging, respiratory distress, abdominal distension, or visible tube displacement.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Correct Measurement: Always measure the tube from the tip of the nose to the earlobe, then down to the xiphoid process to ensure proper length for gastric placement.
  • Placement Confirmation: Confirm tube placement before every use by aspirating gastric contents and testing pH (should be acidic, pH < 5.5). X-ray confirmation is required for initial placement.
  • Hand Hygiene: Perform thorough handwashing with soap and water or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer before and after handling the tube.
  • Lubrication: Use a water-soluble lubricant on the tip of the tube to facilitate smooth insertion and minimize discomfort.
  • Child's Position: Position the child comfortably, often semi-reclined or held securely, to aid insertion and prevent aspiration.
  • Contraindications: Avoid NG tube insertion in children with severe facial trauma, esophageal atresia, recent esophageal surgery, or basilar skull fractures without physician consultation.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Formula Preparation: Prepare formula or medication exactly as prescribed by the physician or dietitian, ensuring correct concentration and temperature.
  • Flushing the Tube: Flush the NG tube with a small amount of sterile water (typically 3-5 mL for infants/children) before and after each feed or medication administration to prevent clogging.
  • Feeding Schedule: Adhere strictly to the prescribed feeding schedule and volume to prevent overfeeding, underfeeding, or gastric distension.
  • Positioning During Feeds: Keep the child in an upright or semi-upright position (at least 30-45 degrees) during feeding and for 30-60 minutes afterward to minimize reflux and aspiration risk.
  • Oral Care: Provide regular oral hygiene even when not feeding orally, as the tube can cause dryness and irritation.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Respiratory Distress: Immediately remove the tube if the child experiences coughing, choking, cyanosis, or severe respiratory distress during insertion or feeding.
  • Observe for Tube Migration: Regularly check the external marking on the tube at the nostril to ensure it has not migrated inward or outward.
  • Skin Care: Inspect the skin around the nostril daily for redness, irritation, or pressure sores from the tape or tube.
  • Emergency Contact: Keep emergency contact numbers for the healthcare provider, home health nurse, or emergency services readily accessible.
  • Signs of Intolerance: Watch for signs of feeding intolerance such as vomiting, abdominal distension, diarrhea, or significant gastric residuals.
  • Tube Patency: Ensure the tube remains patent; if it becomes clogged, attempt to flush with warm water using gentle pressure. Do not force.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prescription & Oversight: NG tube placement and feeding regimens must always be initiated and closely monitored by a physician.
  • Regular Reassessment: Periodic reassessment of the child's nutritional status, growth, and tolerance to enteral feeding is essential.
  • Complication Management: Physicians guide the management of potential complications, including tube dislodgement, infection, or feeding intolerance.
  • Caregiver Education: Emphasize comprehensive training for caregivers, ensuring they are proficient in insertion, care, and troubleshooting.
  • Transition Planning: Plan for eventual transition off the NG tube when the child's oral feeding abilities improve or alternative feeding methods are indicated.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Anatomical Considerations: Understand the differences in pediatric nasal and pharyngeal anatomy compared to adults, influencing tube size and insertion angle.
  • French Scale: Familiarize with the French (Fr) scale for tube sizing; smaller numbers indicate smaller diameters, appropriate for infants and young children.
  • Evidence-Based Placement: Prioritize pH testing of aspirate as the primary bedside method for confirming gastric placement, understanding the limitations of auscultation.
  • Family-Centered Care: Educate parents/caregivers thoroughly on the procedure, potential complications, and daily care, empowering them as active participants in their child's care.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document tube size, insertion depth, confirmation method, date/time of insertion, and any complications or patient responses.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Tube Types: Various types exist, including polyurethane and silicone, chosen based on duration of use and flexibility.
  • Insertion Steps: Key steps include patient positioning, measurement, lubrication, gentle insertion through the nostril, advancement while encouraging swallowing, and securing the tube.
  • Confirmation Methods: Primary methods include pH testing of gastric aspirate (pH < 5.5) and initial X-ray verification. Auscultation is unreliable for definitive placement.
  • Securing the Tube: Use hypoallergenic tape to secure the tube to the child's cheek or nose, ensuring it's snug but not causing pressure.
  • Troubleshooting Clogs: Attempt to clear clogs by flushing with warm water using a 10-20 mL syringe with gentle push-pull action. Enzyme solutions may be used under medical direction.
  • Tube Replacement: NG tubes are typically replaced every 2-4 weeks, or sooner if damaged, clogged, or dislodged.
