How to care for a suprapubic catheter

🏠 Home / Home health / Procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your SPC: A suprapubic catheter (SPC) is a tube inserted directly into the bladder through the abdominal wall, used to drain urine when the urethra is not an option.
  • Purpose of Care: Proper care prevents infection, skin irritation, blockages, and ensures the catheter functions effectively for continuous urine drainage.
  • Hygiene is Key: Meticulous hand hygiene before and after any interaction with the catheter or drainage system is paramount to prevent bacterial contamination.
  • Recognizing Issues: Be vigilant for signs of infection, catheter blockage, leakage around the stoma, or unusual pain, and report them promptly.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Signs of Infection: Fever, chills, increased pain or tenderness at the catheter site, redness, swelling, or pus-like discharge around the stoma.
  • Urinary Changes: Cloudy, foul-smelling urine, blood in the urine, or a sudden decrease in urine output despite adequate fluid intake.
  • Catheter Blockage: Abdominal discomfort or pain, bladder spasms, leakage of urine around the catheter, or no urine draining into the bag.
  • Skin Irritation: Persistent redness, rash, itching, or breakdown of skin around the catheter insertion site.
  • Bladder Spasms: Sudden, strong urges to urinate or cramping sensations in the lower abdomen, which may indicate bladder irritation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Hand Hygiene: Always wash hands thoroughly with soap and water or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer before and after touching the catheter or drainage system.
  • Secure the Catheter: Ensure the catheter is securely taped or fastened to the abdomen to prevent accidental pulling or dislodgement, which can cause trauma.
  • Drainage Bag Position: Keep the drainage bag below the level of the bladder at all times to prevent urine backflow and reduce the risk of infection.
  • Stoma Site Care: Clean the skin around the catheter insertion site daily with mild soap and water, or as directed by your healthcare provider, ensuring it is dry afterwards.
  • Avoid Kinks: Ensure the tubing is free of kinks or loops that could impede urine flow, checking regularly, especially when moving or changing positions.
  • Emptying the Bag: Empty the drainage bag when it is half to two-thirds full, or at least every 8 hours, using a clean container and avoiding contact between the spout and the container.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain a high fluid intake (e.g., 8-10 glasses of water daily) to help flush the bladder and catheter, reducing the risk of urinary tract infections and sediment buildup.
  • Cranberry Intake: Discuss with your doctor if cranberry juice or supplements are appropriate, as they may help prevent bacterial adherence to the bladder wall in some individuals.
  • Fiber-Rich Diet: Consume a diet rich in fiber to prevent constipation, as straining during bowel movements can put pressure on the bladder and catheter site.
  • Avoid Bladder Irritants: Limit intake of caffeine, alcohol, spicy foods, and acidic juices if they are noted to cause bladder irritation or discomfort.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Know when to contact emergency services (e.g., severe pain, sudden inability to drain urine, catheter dislodgement, heavy bleeding).
  • Reporting Changes: Immediately report any signs of infection, leakage, blockage, or significant discomfort to the healthcare team.
  • Proper Bag Changing: Follow strict aseptic technique when changing the drainage bag, ensuring all connections are secure and sterile.
  • Skin Assessment: Regularly inspect the skin around the stoma for any signs of redness, swelling, breakdown, or unusual discharge.
  • Patient Education: Educate the patient and other caregivers on the signs of complications and the importance of adhering to care instructions.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Regular Follow-ups: Schedule and attend all recommended follow-up appointments for catheter changes, site assessment, and overall management.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications, including antibiotics if indicated, exactly as directed to prevent or treat infections.
  • Irrigation Protocols: If bladder irrigation is prescribed, ensure the correct sterile solution and technique are used to prevent blockages and maintain catheter patency.
  • When to Seek Help: Do not attempt to reinsert a dislodged catheter yourself; seek immediate medical attention. Report persistent pain or fever promptly.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Aseptic Technique: Emphasize strict aseptic technique during all catheter care procedures, including dressing changes and drainage system manipulation, to minimize infection risk.
  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive patient and caregiver education on catheter care, complication recognition, and when to seek medical advice, using teach-back methods.
  • Documentation: Accurately document catheter insertion date, size, type, site assessment, urine characteristics, patient tolerance, and any interventions or complications.
  • Troubleshooting: Understand common catheter complications such as blockages, leakage, and infections, and implement appropriate nursing interventions.
  • Skin Integrity: Implement strategies to maintain skin integrity around the stoma, including proper cleaning, barrier creams, and appropriate dressing selection.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Catheter Change Frequency: Suprapubic catheters are typically changed every 4-12 weeks, depending on the catheter material, patient's condition, and manufacturer guidelines.
  • Dressing Protocols: Utilize sterile transparent dressings or gauze dressings as per institutional policy, ensuring the site is clean and dry before application.
  • Irrigation Solutions: If irrigation is necessary, use sterile normal saline or prescribed solutions, ensuring the volume instilled is fully aspirated to prevent bladder overdistension.
  • Infection Control: Adhere to CDC guidelines for catheter-associated urinary tract infection (CAUTI) prevention, even for suprapubic catheters, focusing on hygiene and closed drainage systems.
  • Managing Encrustation: For patients prone to catheter encrustation, consider specific catheter materials (e.g., silicone) and discuss bladder acidification strategies with the physician.
