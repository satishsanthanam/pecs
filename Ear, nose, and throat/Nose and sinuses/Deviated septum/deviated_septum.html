Deviated septum

🏠 Home / Ear, nose, and throat / Nose and sinuses

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A deviated septum occurs when the thin wall (nasal septum) between your nasal passages is displaced to one side, making one nasal passage smaller than the other.
  • Commonality: It is a very common condition, often present from birth or resulting from injury to the nose.
  • Impact on Airflow: Significant deviation can obstruct airflow, leading to difficulty breathing through the nose, especially during colds or allergies.
  • Severity: While many people have some degree of deviation, only significant deviations cause noticeable symptoms requiring intervention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Nasal Obstruction: Difficulty breathing through one or both nostrils, which can worsen with colds or allergies.
  • Nosebleeds (Epistaxis): The nasal septum's surface can become dry and cracked, increasing the risk of bleeding.
  • Facial Pain/Pressure: Rarely, a severely deviated septum can cause pressure on the nasal wall, leading to facial pain.
  • Headaches: Chronic nasal obstruction can sometimes contribute to tension-type headaches.
  • Recurrent Sinus Infections: Impaired drainage due to obstruction can predispose individuals to repeated sinus infections.
  • Snoring and Sleep Disturbances: Difficulty breathing through the nose can lead to mouth breathing during sleep, contributing to snoring or exacerbating sleep apnea.
  • Postnasal Drip: Altered airflow patterns can sometimes lead to irritation and increased mucus production.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Nasal Trauma: Protect the nose from injury, especially during sports or activities with a risk of facial impact, to prevent worsening existing deviation or causing new ones.
  • Manage Allergies/Colds: Effectively manage allergic rhinitis or common colds to reduce nasal swelling, which can exacerbate symptoms of a deviated septum.
  • Pre-Surgical Considerations: If septoplasty is planned, strictly follow pre-operative instructions regarding medication cessation (e.g., blood thinners, NSAIDs) to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Post-Surgical Care: Adhere to all post-operative guidelines, including avoiding strenuous activity, nose blowing, and heavy lifting, to ensure proper healing and prevent complications.
  • Humidification: Use saline nasal sprays or a humidifier to keep nasal passages moist, which can help reduce dryness and nosebleeds associated with a deviated septum.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Surgical Fasting: If septoplasty is scheduled, strictly adhere to the fasting instructions provided by the surgical team (typically NPO for 6-8 hours prior to surgery).
  • Post-Surgical Soft Diet: Following septoplasty, consume soft, bland foods that do not require extensive chewing for the first few days to minimize facial movement and discomfort.
  • Avoid Hot/Spicy Foods: Refrain from very hot or spicy foods post-surgery, as they can increase nasal congestion and potentially irritate healing tissues.
  • Maintain Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially post-surgery, to prevent dehydration and help keep nasal secretions thin, aiding comfort and healing.
  • Limit Alcohol: Avoid alcohol consumption, particularly before and immediately after surgery, as it can thin the blood and interfere with healing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Seek Professional Diagnosis: Do not self-diagnose; consult an Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT) specialist for accurate diagnosis and treatment recommendations if you suspect a deviated septum.
  • Understand Treatment Options: Be fully informed about both conservative management strategies and surgical options (septoplasty), including potential benefits, risks, and recovery expectations.
  • Post-Operative Monitoring: After septoplasty, monitor for signs of complications such as excessive bleeding, severe pain, fever, or signs of infection, and report them immediately to your surgeon.
  • Adhere to Medication Regimen: Take prescribed medications (e.g., pain relievers, antibiotics) exactly as directed to manage pain and prevent infection during recovery.
  • Avoid Nasal Manipulation: Refrain from picking your nose or forcefully blowing it, especially after surgery, to prevent dislodging splints or packing and to protect healing tissues.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Evaluation: A thorough nasal examination, often with a nasal endoscope, is crucial for assessing the degree and location of septal deviation and its impact on nasal airflow.
  • Symptom-Driven Treatment: Treatment is typically indicated when the deviated septum causes significant symptoms such as chronic nasal obstruction, recurrent sinusitis, or severe nosebleeds.
  • Conservative Management: Initial approaches may include nasal steroid sprays, antihistamines, or decongestants to manage associated inflammation and improve airflow without surgery.
  • Surgical Intervention (Septoplasty): For symptomatic patients unresponsive to conservative measures, septoplasty is the definitive surgical procedure to straighten the septum and improve nasal breathing.
  • Referral to ENT: Patients presenting with persistent nasal obstruction or related symptoms should be referred to an otolaryngologist for specialized evaluation and management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Educate patients on the anatomy of the nasal septum, the causes and symptoms of deviation, and the rationale behind conservative versus surgical management.
  • Pre-Operative Care: Provide clear instructions on pre-surgical fasting, medication adjustments, and what to expect on the day of septoplasty, including anesthesia considerations.
  • Post-Operative Assessment: Monitor vital signs, assess for pain, bleeding, and signs of infection post-septoplasty. Educate on proper nasal packing/splint care and removal.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics and educate patients on non-pharmacological pain relief methods, ensuring comfort during the recovery period.
  • Discharge Planning: Provide comprehensive discharge instructions covering wound care, activity restrictions, medication schedules, and when to seek urgent medical attention.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Code: J34.2 (Deviated nasal septum).
  • CPT Code (Septoplasty): 30520 (Septoplasty with or without ethmoidectomy; submucous resection, with or without cartilage scoring, contouring, or replacement with graft).
  • Anatomical Structures: Nasal septum (composed of cartilage and bone), vomer, perpendicular plate of the ethmoid bone, quadrangular cartilage.
  • Pathophysiology: Mechanical obstruction of nasal airflow due to structural deformity, leading to increased airway resistance and potential mucosal irritation.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Anterior rhinoscopy, nasal endoscopy, occasionally CT scan of paranasal sinuses to rule out concomitant sinus pathology.
