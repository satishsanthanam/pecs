Sinusitis in children

🏠 Home / Ear, nose, and throat / Head and neck infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Sinusitis in children is an inflammation of the paranasal sinuses, often following a viral upper respiratory infection, leading to prolonged or worsening symptoms.
  • Commonality: It is a frequent complication of the common cold, with bacterial sinusitis occurring in a small percentage of viral infections.
  • Causes: Primarily viral, but can become bacterial when mucus drainage is blocked, creating a breeding ground for bacteria.
  • Differentiation: Distinguish from a typical cold by the persistence of symptoms beyond 10 days, or worsening after initial improvement.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Nasal Discharge: Thick, yellow, green, or cloudy discharge lasting more than 10 days.
  • Nasal Congestion: Blocked nose making breathing difficult, especially at night.
  • Cough: Often worse at night due to postnasal drip, sometimes lasting for weeks.
  • Facial Pain/Pressure: May manifest as irritability or head-holding in younger children; older children might describe pain around the eyes, nose, or forehead.
  • Headache: Frontal headache, especially in older children.
  • Fever: Low-grade fever is common, but high fever can indicate a more severe infection.
  • Irritability & Fatigue: General malaise and reduced activity levels.
  • Halitosis: Bad breath due to bacterial overgrowth and postnasal drip.
  • Periorbital Swelling: Swelling around the eyes, particularly in the morning, can be a sign of more severe infection.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • When to Seek Care: Consult a physician if symptoms persist beyond 10 days, worsen after initial improvement, or include high fever, severe headache, eye swelling, or vision changes.
  • Environmental Irritants: Avoid exposure to secondhand smoke and other airborne irritants that can exacerbate sinus inflammation.
  • Hand Hygiene: Encourage frequent hand washing to prevent the spread of viral infections that can precede sinusitis.
  • Medication Adherence: If antibiotics are prescribed, ensure the child completes the entire course, even if symptoms improve, to prevent recurrence and antibiotic resistance.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Watch for signs of spreading infection such as severe eye pain, bulging eye, vision changes, stiff neck, or altered mental status.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Encourage ample fluid intake (water, clear broths, diluted juices) to help thin mucus and promote drainage.
  • Warm Fluids: Offer warm liquids like herbal teas or chicken broth, which can provide comfort and help soothe a sore throat often associated with postnasal drip.
  • Avoid Dehydrating Drinks: Limit caffeinated beverages and sugary sodas, which can contribute to dehydration.
  • Soft Foods: If the child has a sore throat or discomfort, offer soft, easy-to-swallow foods like soups, yogurt, or mashed fruits.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Accurate Diagnosis: Emphasize the importance of differentiating bacterial sinusitis from viral rhinosinusitis to avoid unnecessary antibiotic use.
  • Antibiotic Resistance: Counsel parents on the risks of antibiotic resistance and the importance of using antibiotics only when clinically indicated.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Instruct caregivers to closely monitor for any signs of worsening symptoms or development of complications, especially orbital or intracranial spread.
  • Comfort Measures: Advise on supportive care such as saline nasal rinses, humidifiers, and appropriate pain/fever reducers.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Diagnosis: Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on symptom duration and severity; imaging is generally not recommended for uncomplicated acute sinusitis.
  • Watchful Waiting: For mild-to-moderate symptoms, a period of watchful waiting with symptomatic treatment may be appropriate before initiating antibiotics.
  • Antibiotic Selection: Amoxicillin-clavulanate is often the first-line antibiotic for bacterial sinusitis, considering local resistance patterns.
  • Underlying Factors: Consider evaluating for underlying conditions such as allergies, adenoid hypertrophy, or immune deficiencies in cases of recurrent or refractory sinusitis.
  • ENT Referral: Refer to an otolaryngologist for recurrent acute sinusitis, chronic sinusitis, or suspected complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pediatric Assessment: Recognize that facial pain may be difficult to localize in younger children; observe for irritability, periorbital swelling, and persistent cough.
  • Supportive Care: Implement nursing interventions such as teaching proper saline nasal rinse techniques, encouraging fluid intake, and ensuring adequate rest.
  • Parent Education: Educate parents on medication administration (antibiotics, analgesics, decongestants if appropriate), symptom recognition, and when to seek follow-up care.
  • Developmental Considerations: Understand that sinus development varies with age, influencing symptom presentation and potential for specific sinus involvement.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Inflammation of the mucosal lining of the paranasal sinuses, leading to ostial obstruction and impaired mucociliary clearance, often triggered by viral infection.
  • Common Pathogens: Bacterial sinusitis is most commonly caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae* (non-typeable), and *Moraxella catarrhalis*.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis is diagnosed based on persistent symptoms (nasal discharge, cough) for >10 days without improvement, or worsening symptoms after initial improvement.
  • Treatment Guidelines: Initial management often involves symptomatic relief; antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin-clavulanate) are indicated for bacterial infections based on clinical criteria.
  • Complications: Potential complications include orbital cellulitis, subperiosteal abscess, osteomyelitis, and, rarely, intracranial complications like meningitis or brain abscess.
