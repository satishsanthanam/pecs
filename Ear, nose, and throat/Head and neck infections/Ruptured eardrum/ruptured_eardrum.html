Ruptured eardrum

🏠 Home / Ear, nose, and throat / Head and neck infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: A ruptured eardrum, or tympanic membrane perforation, is a hole or tear in the thin tissue separating the ear canal from the middle ear.
  • Common Causes: Middle ear infections (otitis media), barotrauma (sudden pressure changes), loud noises, or direct trauma to the ear.
  • Healing Process: Most small perforations heal spontaneously within a few weeks to months without intervention.
  • Potential Complications: Persistent hearing loss, recurrent middle ear infections, or the development of a cholesteatoma if left untreated.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sudden Ear Pain: Often sharp and intense, which may quickly subside after the rupture.
  • Ear Drainage: Clear, pus-filled, or bloody discharge from the ear canal.
  • Hearing Loss: Ranging from mild to moderate, typically conductive hearing loss.
  • Tinnitus: A ringing, buzzing, or roaring sound in the affected ear.
  • Vertigo: A sensation of spinning or dizziness, which can sometimes be accompanied by nausea.
  • Feeling of Fullness: A blocked or stuffy sensation in the ear.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Ear Protection from Water: Keep the ear dry by using a waterproof earplug or cotton ball coated with petroleum jelly during bathing or showering.
  • Avoid Inserting Objects: Never insert cotton swabs, fingers, or any other objects into the ear canal, as this can worsen the perforation or introduce infection.
  • No Forceful Nose Blowing: Avoid vigorous nose blowing, which can increase pressure in the middle ear and impede healing.
  • Prescription Medications Only: Use only ear drops or medications specifically prescribed by a healthcare provider for the ruptured eardrum.
  • Monitor for Infection: Watch for signs of worsening infection such as increased pain, fever, or purulent discharge, and seek medical attention promptly.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Maintain Adequate Hydration: Ensure sufficient fluid intake to support overall health and the body's healing processes.
  • Soft Food Consideration: If chewing causes discomfort or exacerbates ear pain, temporarily opt for softer foods that require less jaw movement.
  • Avoid Excessive Caffeine/Alcohol: While not directly impacting eardrum healing, these can sometimes exacerbate tinnitus or dizziness in sensitive individuals.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in vitamins and minerals to support immune function and tissue repair.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Medical Consultation: Seek urgent medical advice if experiencing severe pain, high fever, facial weakness, or persistent vertigo.
  • Avoid Self-Diagnosis/Treatment: Do not attempt to diagnose or treat a ruptured eardrum without professional medical guidance.
  • Report Changes Promptly: Inform your healthcare provider immediately about any changes in ear discharge, hearing, or pain levels.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Adhere strictly to all scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor healing and prevent complications.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Diagnosis is typically made via otoscopy, allowing direct visualization of the tympanic membrane.
  • Conservative Management: Many perforations heal spontaneously; treatment often involves watchful waiting, pain management, and antibiotics if infection is present.
  • Surgical Intervention: For non-healing perforations or significant hearing loss, surgical repair (tympanoplasty or myringoplasty) may be recommended.
  • Patient Education Focus: Emphasize the critical importance of keeping the ear dry and avoiding trauma during the healing period to prevent complications.
  • Hearing Assessment: Audiometry may be performed to assess the degree of hearing loss and monitor recovery.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Instruct patients on proper ear care, medication adherence, signs of infection, and the importance of follow-up.
  • Pain Management: Assess pain levels and administer prescribed analgesics, educating patients on their use and potential side effects.
  • Infection Monitoring: Observe and document characteristics of ear discharge, temperature, and patient symptoms indicative of infection.
  • Post-Surgical Care: For tympanoplasty patients, provide instructions on activity restrictions, wound care, and signs of post-operative complications.
  • Hearing Assessment: Conduct basic hearing screenings and report any significant changes to the physician.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: H72.xx (Perforation of tympanic membrane), specific subcodes for acute, chronic, or traumatic perforations.
  • Anatomy: Tympanic membrane (eardrum), malleus, incus, stapes, Eustachian tube.
  • Pathophysiology: Disruption of sound conduction, increased risk of middle ear infection due to loss of protective barrier.
  • Diagnostic Tools: Otoscopy, audiometry (to assess hearing loss), tympanometry (to assess middle ear function).
  • Surgical Interventions: Tympanoplasty (surgical repair of the eardrum), myringoplasty (grafting to repair the perforation).
  • Pharmacology: Oral antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin) for infection, topical antibiotic ear drops (e.g., ofloxacin, ciprofloxacin) if no contraindication, analgesics (e.g., ibuprofen, acetaminophen) for pain.
