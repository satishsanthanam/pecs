Laryngoscopy

🏠 Home / Ear, nose, and throat / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Laryngoscopy is a medical procedure used to visualize the larynx (voice box) and surrounding structures, including the vocal cords.
  • Diagnostic & Therapeutic Uses: It helps diagnose conditions like persistent hoarseness, swallowing difficulties, airway obstruction, and can be used to remove foreign bodies or take biopsies.
  • Types of Laryngoscopy: Can be indirect (using a mirror or flexible fiberoptic scope) or direct (using a rigid scope, often under general anesthesia).
  • Purpose of Examination: Allows for direct inspection of the vocal cords for nodules, polyps, paralysis, inflammation, or cancerous lesions.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Hoarseness (Dysphonia): Unexplained changes in voice quality lasting more than two weeks.
  • Difficulty Swallowing (Dysphagia): Sensation of food sticking or pain during swallowing.
  • Stridor or Noisy Breathing: High-pitched sound during inhalation, indicating airway narrowing.
  • Chronic Cough: A persistent cough without an identifiable cause.
  • Sensation of Foreign Body: Feeling of a lump or object stuck in the throat.
  • Throat Pain or Discomfort: Unexplained or persistent pain in the throat area.
  • Hemoptysis: Coughing up blood, which may originate from the laryngeal area.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • NPO Status: Strict adherence to 'nil per os' (nothing by mouth) guidelines for several hours prior to the procedure if sedation or general anesthesia is planned.
  • Allergy Disclosure: Inform the medical team of any known allergies, especially to anesthetics, sedatives, or latex.
  • Dental Assessment: Report any loose teeth, crowns, bridges, or dentures, as they may be at risk during rigid laryngoscopy.
  • Anticoagulant Use: Discuss all medications, particularly blood thinners, with your physician as they may need to be adjusted or temporarily stopped before the procedure due to bleeding risk.
  • Cervical Spine Issues: Alert the team to any history of neck injury or cervical spine instability, as positioning may need modification.
  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Be prepared for monitoring of vital signs, airway patency, and potential complications like laryngospasm or bleeding.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: For procedures requiring sedation or general anesthesia, typically no solid food for 6-8 hours and no clear liquids for 2-4 hours prior. Follow specific instructions from your care team.
  • Post-Procedure Fluid Reintroduction: Once the gag reflex has fully returned and you are alert, begin with sips of clear liquids (water, ice chips).
  • Gradual Food Progression: If tolerated, advance to soft, bland foods. Avoid hot, spicy, acidic, or rough-textured foods for the first 24-48 hours to prevent irritation to the throat.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration with cool, non-irritating fluids to soothe the throat and aid recovery.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Consent Process: Ensure you fully understand the procedure, potential risks, and benefits before signing the informed consent form.
  • Transportation Post-Sedation: Arrange for a responsible adult to drive you home if you receive sedation, as your judgment and reaction time will be impaired.
  • Expected Discomfort: A sore throat, mild hoarseness, or a sensation of a lump in the throat are common after laryngoscopy and usually resolve within a few days.
  • When to Seek Medical Attention: Report any severe pain, difficulty breathing, persistent bleeding, fever, or worsening symptoms immediately to your healthcare provider.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, and making important decisions for 24 hours post-sedation.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Thorough Evaluation: A comprehensive history and physical examination, including a focused head and neck exam, are crucial before recommending laryngoscopy.
  • Appropriate Technique Selection: The choice between indirect, flexible, or direct rigid laryngoscopy depends on the clinical indication, patient's condition, and desired diagnostic/therapeutic goals.
  • Risk-Benefit Discussion: Always engage in a detailed discussion with the patient regarding the potential risks (e.g., trauma, bleeding, aspiration, anesthetic complications) versus the benefits of the procedure.
  • Management of Findings: Be prepared to manage findings, which may include biopsy for histopathology, foreign body removal, or planning further interventions based on the diagnosis.
  • Post-Procedure Follow-up: Emphasize the importance of follow-up appointments to discuss results, monitor recovery, and plan any necessary ongoing treatment.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-Procedure Checklist: Verify NPO status, confirm informed consent, assess vital signs, document allergies, and ensure patient understanding of the procedure.
  • Patient Positioning: Assist with appropriate patient positioning (e.g., 'sniffing position' for direct laryngoscopy) to optimize laryngeal visualization and airway access.
  • Monitoring During Procedure: Continuously monitor vital signs, oxygen saturation, and patient comfort level, especially during flexible laryngoscopy or under sedation.
  • Post-Procedure Airway Management: Prioritize airway assessment, monitor for signs of respiratory distress, laryngospasm, or stridor. Ensure gag reflex has returned before oral intake.
  • Pain and Comfort Management: Assess and manage post-procedure throat pain with appropriate analgesics and provide comfort measures like ice chips or throat lozenges (once safe).
  • Patient Education: Educate the patient and family on expected post-procedure symptoms, signs of complications to report, and activity restrictions.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Anatomical Focus: Larynx, epiglottis, vocal cords (true and false), arytenoids, piriform sinuses.
  • Equipment: Flexible fiberoptic laryngoscope, rigid laryngoscope, light source, video monitor, biopsy forceps, suction apparatus.
  • Anesthesia Modalities: Topical anesthesia (e.g., lidocaine spray), local anesthesia, conscious sedation, general anesthesia.
  • Key Indications: Dysphonia, dysphagia, stridor, chronic cough, foreign body removal, laryngeal lesion biopsy, vocal cord assessment.
  • Potential Complications: Laryngospasm, dental trauma, mucosal trauma, bleeding, aspiration, infection, vocal cord injury, anesthetic reactions.
  • Diagnostic Yield: High for direct visualization of laryngeal pathology, often superior to indirect methods for detailed examination and intervention.
