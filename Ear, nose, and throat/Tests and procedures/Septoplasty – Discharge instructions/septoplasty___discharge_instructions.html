Septoplasty – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Ear, nose, and throat / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of Septoplasty: This procedure corrects a deviated nasal septum, improving airflow and breathing.
  • Recovery Timeline: Initial recovery typically takes a few days to a week, with full internal healing continuing for several weeks to months.
  • Expected Discomfort: Mild pain, congestion, and some bloody discharge are normal during the first few days post-surgery.
  • Nasal Packing/Splints: You may have soft packing or splints inside your nose to support the septum; these will be removed by your surgeon at a follow-up appointment.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Expected Post-Operative Symptoms: Mild to moderate nasal pain, congestion similar to a cold, slight bloody discharge, and facial pressure.
  • Signs of Infection: Increased pain, fever (above 101°F or 38.3°C), foul-smelling discharge, or significant redness around the nose.
  • Excessive Bleeding: Bright red blood that soaks through dressings rapidly or does not stop with gentle pressure.
  • Difficulty Breathing: Severe shortness of breath or persistent difficulty breathing through the mouth.
  • Vision Changes: Any changes in vision, eye pain, or swelling around the eyes.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, bending over, and nose blowing for at least 2-3 weeks to prevent bleeding and protect the surgical site.
  • Nasal Care: Do not pick or rub your nose. If prescribed, use saline nasal sprays gently to keep the nasal passages moist and aid in crust removal.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed pain medications and antibiotics as directed to manage discomfort and prevent infection. Avoid aspirin or NSAIDs unless specifically approved by your surgeon due to increased bleeding risk.
  • Head Elevation: Keep your head elevated, even while sleeping, for the first few days to reduce swelling and congestion.
  • Avoid Irritants: Stay away from smoke, dust, and allergens that could irritate your nasal passages.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Soft Diet Initially: Begin with soft, bland foods that do not require excessive chewing to minimize facial movement and discomfort.
  • Hydration: Drink plenty of fluids to stay hydrated, especially if breathing through your mouth due to congestion.
  • Avoid Hot Liquids: Refrain from consuming very hot liquids or foods for the first 24-48 hours, as heat can increase swelling and bleeding.
  • No Straws: Avoid using straws, as the suction can create pressure in the nasal passages and potentially trigger bleeding.
  • Alcohol Restriction: Do not consume alcohol for at least one week post-surgery, as it can thin the blood and interfere with medication.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact: Ensure the patient has immediate access to emergency contact numbers for the surgeon and hospital.
  • Medication Management: Assist the patient in adhering to the prescribed medication schedule, especially for pain relief and antibiotics.
  • Bleeding Management: Instruct the patient to sit upright, lean forward slightly, and apply gentle pressure to the sides of the nose if minor bleeding occurs. Seek immediate medical attention for uncontrolled bleeding.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Be vigilant for signs of infection (fever, increased pain, foul discharge) or excessive swelling and report them promptly to the medical team.
  • Transportation: Ensure safe transportation home post-discharge and assist with daily activities as needed during the initial recovery period.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Follow-up Appointments: It is crucial to attend all scheduled post-operative appointments for removal of packing/splints and assessment of healing.
  • Medication Compliance: Strict adherence to antibiotic and pain medication regimens is vital for preventing complications and managing discomfort.
  • Nasal Hygiene: Gentle saline rinses, if recommended, are important for clearing crusts and promoting healing, but avoid forceful irrigation.
  • Long-term Outcomes: While immediate improvement in breathing may be noticed, full benefits and stabilization of the septum can take several months.
  • When to Call: Contact my office immediately for severe pain, uncontrolled bleeding, fever, vision changes, or any concerns about your recovery.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Focus: Emphasize activity restrictions, proper nasal care (no blowing, gentle saline), and signs/symptoms of complications.
  • Pain Assessment & Management: Regularly assess pain levels using a validated scale and administer analgesics as prescribed, evaluating their effectiveness.
  • Bleeding Monitoring: Monitor for excessive epistaxis, assess the amount and type of discharge, and reinforce proper head positioning.
  • Infection Prevention: Educate on antibiotic adherence and monitor for signs of infection, including vital signs and wound assessment.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient anxieties regarding recovery, appearance, and potential discomfort, providing reassurance and clear instructions.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Septal Hematoma: A collection of blood under the septal mucosa, a potential complication requiring prompt drainage to prevent cartilage necrosis.
  • Septal Perforation: A hole in the nasal septum, a rare but possible complication, often due to trauma or infection.
  • Synechiae: Adhesions or scar tissue forming between the septum and lateral nasal wall, potentially obstructing airflow.
  • Turbinate Hypertrophy: Enlargement of the turbinates, sometimes co-occurring with septal deviation, which may also contribute to nasal obstruction.
  • Anterior Rhinoscopy: Clinical examination technique used to visualize the nasal septum and internal structures post-operatively.
