Septoplasty

🏠 Home / Ear, nose, and throat / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Septoplasty is a surgical procedure performed to correct a deviated nasal septum, which is the wall between the nostrils.
  • Primary Goal: The main objective is to straighten the septum, thereby improving airflow through the nose and alleviating symptoms caused by the deviation.
  • Common Indications: This surgery is typically recommended for individuals experiencing chronic nasal obstruction, recurrent nosebleeds (epistaxis), frequent sinus infections, or sleep disturbances due to a deviated septum.
  • Recovery Expectation: While the surgery itself is relatively quick, full recovery and optimal breathing improvement can take several weeks to months.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Chronic Nasal Obstruction: Persistent difficulty breathing through one or both nostrils, often worse on one side.
  • Recurrent Epistaxis: Frequent or severe nosebleeds, especially if originating from the deviated side of the septum.
  • Frequent Sinus Infections: Impaired sinus drainage due to septal deviation can lead to recurrent sinusitis.
  • Facial Pain or Pressure: Sometimes associated with chronic nasal obstruction or sinus issues related to the deviation.
  • Snoring and Sleep Disturbances: Difficulty breathing through the nose at night can contribute to snoring, mouth breathing, and disturbed sleep patterns.
  • Reduced Sense of Smell: In some cases, severe obstruction can impact olfactory function.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Review: Discontinue blood-thinning medications (e.g., aspirin, NSAIDs, warfarin) at least 7-10 days prior to surgery, or as specifically instructed by your surgeon.
  • Smoking Cessation: Avoid smoking for several weeks before and after surgery to promote healing and reduce complication risks.
  • Pre-operative Instructions: Strictly follow all fasting guidelines and other preparatory instructions provided by your surgical team.
  • Post-operative Activity: Avoid strenuous activities, heavy lifting, bending over, and forceful nose blowing for several weeks post-surgery.
  • Nasal Protection: Protect your nose from trauma; avoid wearing glasses that rest heavily on the bridge of the nose if splints are in place.
  • Avoid Irritants: Steer clear of dusty environments, smoke, and strong fumes that could irritate nasal passages during recovery.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nothing by mouth) guidelines, typically 6-8 hours before surgery, to prevent aspiration during anesthesia.
  • Initial Post-operative Diet: Begin with clear liquids, then progress to soft, bland foods that require minimal chewing for the first 24-48 hours.
  • Avoid Hot Liquids: Refrain from consuming very hot beverages or foods immediately after surgery, as they can increase the risk of bleeding.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration with cool, non-acidic fluids to prevent dryness and aid in recovery.
  • Avoid Irritating Foods: Steer clear of spicy, acidic, or crunchy foods that could irritate the nasal passages or require vigorous chewing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Expected Discharge: Most septoplasty procedures are outpatient, meaning discharge occurs on the same day. Arrange for a responsible adult to drive you home.
  • Post-operative Bleeding: A small amount of bloody discharge from the nose is normal. Report any excessive, bright red bleeding that does not subside with gentle pressure.
  • Pain Management: Take prescribed pain medication as directed. Avoid over-the-counter pain relievers that contain aspirin or NSAIDs unless specifically approved by your surgeon.
  • Nasal Packing/Splints: If nasal packing or splints are placed, do not attempt to remove them yourself. Follow your surgeon's instructions for their care and removal.
  • Signs of Infection: Monitor for signs of infection such as fever (above 101°F or 38.3°C), increased pain, foul-smelling discharge, or worsening redness/swelling. Contact your surgeon immediately if these occur.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Adhere to all scheduled post-operative appointments for wound check and removal of any internal splints or sutures.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Realistic Expectations: While septoplasty significantly improves nasal breathing for most patients, complete resolution of all symptoms may not always be achieved, especially if other conditions (e.g., allergies, turbinate hypertrophy) are present.
  • Combined Procedures: Septoplasty is often combined with other procedures, such as turbinate reduction or sinus surgery, to optimize outcomes for complex cases.
  • Long-term Benefits: Successful septoplasty can lead to long-term improvements in quality of life, including better sleep, reduced sinus infections, and enhanced physical activity tolerance.
  • Potential Complications: While rare, potential complications include bleeding, infection, septal hematoma, septal perforation, or recurrence of deviation. Discuss these risks thoroughly with your surgeon.
  • Adherence to Care: Strict adherence to pre- and post-operative instructions is crucial for optimal healing and minimizing complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Assessment: Conduct a thorough nursing assessment including patient history, current medications (especially anticoagulants), allergies, and baseline vital signs. Assess nasal patency and patient's understanding of the procedure.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear, concise education on pre-operative fasting, what to expect during and after surgery, pain management, and signs of complications. Reinforce discharge instructions.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, assess for signs of bleeding (e.g., frequent swallowing, excessive drainage), pain level, and airway patency.
  • Wound Care: Educate patient on proper care of external dressings (if any) and management of nasal drip pads. Advise against forceful nose blowing.
  • Discharge Planning: Ensure patient has a responsible adult for transport, understands medication regimen, activity restrictions, and knows when to seek emergency care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • CPT Code: Common CPT code for septoplasty is 30520 (Septoplasty or submucous resection, with or without turbinate, with or without ethmoidectomy).
  • Anesthesia: Typically performed under general anesthesia, though local anesthesia with sedation may be used in select cases.
  • Surgical Approach: Usually performed endonasally (through the nostrils) with an incision made inside the nose, avoiding external scars.
  • Instrumentation: Key instruments include nasal speculum, septal elevator, osteotomes, various forceps (e.g., Takahashi, Blakesley), and suction.
  • Pathophysiology: A deviated septum can result from congenital factors, developmental abnormalities, or trauma, leading to structural obstruction of nasal airflow.
  • Post-operative Management: May involve nasal packing (absorbable or non-absorbable) or silicone splints to support the septum during initial healing.
