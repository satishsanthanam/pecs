# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Purpose of Screening:** Tuberculosis (TB) screening tests are crucial for identifying individuals with latent TB infection (LTBI) or active TB disease, even before symptoms appear.
* **Common Tests:** The two primary methods are the Tuberculin Skin Test (TST), also known as the Mantoux test, and Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRAs), such as QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus or T-SPOT.TB.
* **Latent vs. Active TB:** Screening helps differentiate between LTBI, where the bacteria are present but inactive, and active TB disease, which is contagious and requires immediate treatment.
* **High-Risk Populations:** Screening is particularly recommended for individuals with close contact to active TB cases, those born in or who frequently travel to high TB burden countries, healthcare workers, and immunocompromised individuals.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Persistent Cough:** A cough lasting three weeks or longer, often producing sputum, is a key indicator for active pulmonary TB.
* **Fever and Chills:** Unexplained low-grade fever, particularly in the afternoon, accompanied by chills, can prompt TB investigation.
* **Night Sweats:** Drenching sweats occurring during sleep are a classic systemic symptom associated with active TB.
* **Unexplained Weight Loss:** Significant and unintentional loss of body weight is a common constitutional symptom of active TB disease.
* **Fatigue and Weakness:** Persistent feelings of tiredness, malaise, and general weakness can be indicative of an underlying infection like TB.
* **Chest Pain:** Pain in the chest, especially when breathing or coughing, may suggest pleural involvement or extensive lung disease.
* **Hemoptysis:** Coughing up blood or blood-streaked sputum is a serious symptom that warrants immediate medical evaluation for active TB.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **TST Reading Window:** For Tuberculin Skin Tests (TST), ensure the patient returns for the reading within 48 to 72 hours after the injection to accurately assess the reaction.
* **Injection Site Care (TST):** Advise patients not to scratch, rub, or cover the injection site with a bandage. Normal bathing is permissible.
* **Prior BCG Vaccination:** Inform the healthcare provider if the patient has received the Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine, as it can cause a false-positive TST result. IGRAs are generally preferred in BCG-vaccinated individuals.
* **Immunosuppression Disclosure:** Patients must disclose any conditions or medications that suppress the immune system (e.g., HIV, organ transplant, corticosteroids), as these can lead to a false-negative TST result.
* **Blood Sample Integrity (IGRA):** For Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRAs), ensure proper blood collection technique and timely processing to maintain sample integrity and accurate results.
* **Avoid Repeat TST Too Soon:** If a TST is negative, a second TST (two-step TST) may be performed 1-3 weeks later for certain populations to detect boosted reactions, but generally, avoid frequent, unnecessary repeat testing.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **No Specific Restrictions:** Tuberculosis screening tests (TST and IGRA) generally do not require any specific dietary restrictions or fasting prior to the procedure.
* **Normal Hydration:** Patients should maintain their usual hydration levels unless otherwise instructed for other concurrent medical reasons.
* **Medication Intake:** Continue all prescribed medications as usual, unless specifically advised by a healthcare provider, as they typically do not interfere with the test results.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Positive TST/IGRA Interpretation:** A positive result indicates exposure to TB bacteria and the presence of either latent TB infection or active TB disease, requiring further diagnostic evaluation.
* **Further Diagnostic Steps:** A positive screening test necessitates additional tests, such as a chest X-ray, sputum smear, and culture, to rule out active TB disease.
* **False Negative Possibility:** Be aware that false-negative results can occur, particularly in immunocompromised individuals, very young children, or those with recent TB infection.
* **False Positive Possibility (TST):** False-positive TST results can occur in individuals who have received the BCG vaccine or have been infected with non-tuberculous mycobacteria. IGRAs are less affected by BCG vaccination.
* **Contact Tracing:** If active TB is diagnosed, public health guidelines require contact tracing to identify and screen individuals who may have been exposed.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Clinical Context is Key:** Interpretation of TB screening results must always be done within the context of the patient's clinical history, risk factors, and epidemiological exposure.
* **Differentiating LTBI from Active TB:** A positive screening test alone is insufficient for diagnosing active TB; a thorough clinical evaluation, including imaging and microbiological studies, is essential.
* **Treatment for LTBI:** For individuals diagnosed with latent TB infection, preventive therapy is crucial to reduce the risk of progression to active disease, especially in high-risk groups.
* **Public Health Imperative:** Effective TB screening and management are vital components of global TB control strategies, aiming to reduce transmission and incidence.
* **Guideline Adherence:** Physicians should adhere to national and international guidelines for TB screening, diagnosis, and treatment to ensure optimal patient outcomes and public health safety.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **TST Administration Technique:** Master the Mantoux method for intradermal injection, ensuring a precise 0.1 mL dose of PPD to create a distinct wheal (6-10 mm in diameter).
* **Accurate TST Reading:** Learn to palpate and measure the diameter of induration (firm, raised area), not erythema (redness), in millimeters, within the specified 48-72 hour window.
* **Patient Education on Results:** Provide clear, concise explanations to patients about what a positive or negative result means and the necessary follow-up steps.
* **IGRA Blood Draw Protocol:** Understand the specific requirements for IGRA blood collection, including specialized tubes and timely transport to the laboratory to prevent degradation.
* **Infection Control Principles:** Reinforce strict infection control measures when caring for patients with suspected or confirmed active TB, including airborne precautions.
* **Documentation:** Meticulously document all aspects of the screening process, including test type, date, time, results, and patient education provided.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Tuberculin Skin Test (TST):** A delayed-type hypersensitivity reaction to purified protein derivative (PPD) of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, indicating prior exposure.
* **Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRAs):** *In vitro* blood tests that measure the release of interferon-gamma by T-cells in response to specific *M. tuberculosis* antigens (ESAT-6, CFP-10), which are absent from BCG and most non-tuberculous mycobacteria.
* **Induration Measurement:** The critical parameter for TST interpretation, with specific cut-off points (e.g., &#8805;5 mm, &#8805;10 mm, &#8805;15 mm) depending on risk factors.
* **Two-Step TST:** Used for initial screening of individuals who will be tested periodically (e.g., healthcare workers) to detect boosted reactions from remote infection.
* **Sensitivity and Specificity:** IGRAs generally offer higher specificity than TST, especially in BCG-vaccinated populations, and comparable sensitivity.
* **CDC and WHO Guidelines:** Refer to current Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines for comprehensive recommendations on TB screening and management.
