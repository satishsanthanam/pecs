Complete blood count (CBC)

🏠 Home / Laboratory and diagnostic tests / Laboratory tests

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Comprehensive Overview: A Complete Blood Count (CBC) is a common blood test providing a detailed snapshot of the cells in your blood, including red blood cells (RBCs), white blood cells (WBCs), and platelets.
  • Key Measurements: It measures the number of these cells, their size, shape, and other characteristics, along with hemoglobin and hematocrit levels.
  • Diagnostic Utility: Used to screen for, diagnose, and monitor a wide range of conditions such as anemia, infection, inflammation, and bleeding disorders.
  • Routine Screening: Often part of a routine physical examination to assess overall health and detect potential issues early.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Fatigue & Weakness: May indicate anemia (low RBCs or hemoglobin).
  • Frequent Infections & Fever: Could suggest issues with white blood cell counts, either too high or too low.
  • Easy Bruising & Prolonged Bleeding: Potentially points to platelet abnormalities, affecting blood clotting.
  • Dizziness & Shortness of Breath: Can be symptoms of reduced oxygen-carrying capacity due due to low red blood cell counts.
  • Unexplained Weight Loss or Swollen Lymph Nodes: May prompt a CBC to investigate underlying systemic conditions.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Disclosure: Inform your healthcare provider about all medications, supplements, and herbal remedies you are taking, as some can affect blood cell counts.
  • Anticoagulant Awareness: If you are on blood thinners (e.g., warfarin, aspirin), notify the phlebotomist prior to the blood draw to ensure appropriate post-procedure care to prevent excessive bruising.
  • Hydration Status: Ensure adequate hydration before the test to facilitate easier vein access and a smoother blood draw.
  • Post-Procedure Care: Apply pressure to the venipuncture site for several minutes after the draw to minimize bruising and hematoma formation. Avoid heavy lifting with the arm for a few hours.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Fasting Generally Not Required: For a standalone CBC, fasting is typically not necessary, allowing you to eat and drink normally before the test.
  • Concurrent Test Requirements: If your CBC is ordered alongside other blood tests (e.g., glucose, lipid panel) that require fasting, you will need to follow those specific fasting instructions (usually 8-12 hours without food or caloric beverages).
  • Hydration Encouragement: Drinking water is encouraged before the test to help keep veins plump and make the blood draw easier.
  • Alcohol Avoidance: Refrain from alcohol consumption for at least 24 hours prior to the test, as it can potentially influence certain blood parameters, though not typically the primary CBC components.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Patient Identification: Always confirm patient identity using at least two identifiers (e.g., name and date of birth) before specimen collection.
  • Specimen Labeling: Ensure all blood tubes are correctly labeled immediately after collection with the patient's full name, date of birth, and collection date/time.
  • Venipuncture Site Monitoring: Observe the venipuncture site for any signs of excessive bleeding, hematoma, or patient discomfort during and immediately after the procedure.
  • Patient Education: Instruct the patient on post-procedure care, including keeping the bandage on for a specified time and reporting any unusual pain, swelling, or redness.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Holistic Interpretation: CBC results are always interpreted in the context of the patient's clinical history, physical examination findings, and other diagnostic tests.
  • Trend Analysis: Serial CBCs are invaluable for monitoring disease progression, response to treatment (e.g., chemotherapy, iron supplementation), and detecting potential complications.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Abnormalities in specific cell lines (e.g., elevated neutrophils, decreased platelets) guide the physician towards a differential diagnosis, prompting further targeted investigations.
  • Risk Stratification: Certain CBC parameters, such as very low platelet counts or severe anemia, can indicate a need for urgent intervention or transfusion.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Preparation & Education: Nurses are crucial in explaining the CBC procedure, addressing patient anxieties, and ensuring adherence to any pre-test instructions.
  • Proficient Venipuncture: Mastering sterile venipuncture technique is essential for accurate sample collection and minimizing patient discomfort and complications.
  • Specimen Handling & Transport: Correct tube selection (e.g., lavender-top EDTA tube), proper mixing, and timely transport to the lab are critical for maintaining specimen integrity.
  • Recognizing Critical Values: Nurses must be aware of critical CBC values (e.g., extremely low hemoglobin, very high or low WBCs) and understand the protocol for immediate notification of the physician.
  • Post-Procedure Monitoring: Assessing the venipuncture site for hematoma, infection, or nerve injury and providing appropriate patient education on self-care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Red Blood Cell (RBC) Count: Measures the number of oxygen-carrying cells. Reference range: ~4.5-5.5 million/µL.
  • Hemoglobin (Hgb): Protein in RBCs that carries oxygen. Reference range: ~13-17 g/dL (men), ~12-15 g/dL (women).
  • Hematocrit (Hct): Percentage of blood volume occupied by RBCs. Reference range: ~40-50% (men), ~36-45% (women).
  • White Blood Cell (WBC) Count: Measures immune system cells. Reference range: ~4,500-11,000/µL.
  • WBC Differential: Breakdown of specific WBC types (Neutrophils, Lymphocytes, Monocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils) indicating specific immune responses.
  • Platelet Count: Cells involved in blood clotting. Reference range: ~150,000-450,000/µL.
  • Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV): Average size of RBCs, used to classify anemia (microcytic, normocytic, macrocytic).
  • Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW): Measures variation in RBC size, useful in differentiating types of anemia.
