Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus

🏠 Home / Adult general health / Travel health

💡 What You Need to Know

  • About MERS-CoV: Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV) is a viral respiratory illness caused by a novel coronavirus.
  • Origin and Spread: First identified in Saudi Arabia in 2012, it is primarily transmitted from dromedary camels to humans.
  • Human-to-Human Transmission: Can occur, especially in healthcare settings, but is less efficient than zoonotic transmission.
  • High-Risk Populations: Older adults, individuals with weakened immune systems, and those with chronic medical conditions are at higher risk for severe disease.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Common Presentation: Patients typically develop fever, cough, and shortness of breath.
  • Gastrointestinal Manifestations: Diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting may also be present in some cases.
  • Severe Illness Indicators: Progression to pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and kidney failure is common in severe infections.
  • Asymptomatic Cases: Some individuals, particularly healthcare workers or close contacts, may be infected without showing symptoms.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Camel Contact Avoidance: Travelers to MERS-CoV endemic regions should avoid close contact with dromedary camels.
  • Food Safety Practices: Do not consume raw camel milk or undercooked camel meat. Ensure all animal products are thoroughly cooked.
  • Hand Hygiene Protocol: Practice frequent hand washing with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer.
  • Respiratory Etiquette: Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue, then dispose of it, or use the inside of your elbow.
  • Healthcare Setting Vigilance: Healthcare professionals must adhere to strict infection control measures, including contact and airborne precautions, when caring for suspected or confirmed cases.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Camel Product Restriction: Strictly avoid consuming raw camel milk, camel urine, or undercooked camel meat due to the risk of zoonotic transmission.
  • Hydration Management: Maintain adequate fluid intake, especially if experiencing fever or gastrointestinal symptoms, to prevent dehydration.
  • Nutritional Support During Illness: Focus on easily digestible, nutrient-rich foods to support the immune system and recovery process if symptomatic.
  • General Food Safety: Ensure all food, particularly meat and dairy products, is prepared and cooked to safe internal temperatures to prevent foodborne illnesses.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Patient Isolation: Suspected or confirmed MERS-CoV patients require immediate isolation in a single-patient room with negative pressure airflow.
  • Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Attendants must consistently use N95 respirators, gowns, gloves, and eye protection when entering the patient's room.
  • Visitor Management: Restrict visitors to essential personnel only, ensuring they are educated on proper PPE use and hand hygiene.
  • Environmental Disinfection: Implement rigorous cleaning and disinfection protocols for all surfaces and equipment in the patient's environment.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Suspicion Criteria: Consider MERS-CoV in patients presenting with acute respiratory illness and a history of travel to or residence in MERS-CoV endemic areas, or contact with camels/MERS patients.
  • Diagnostic Confirmation: Utilize real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) on respiratory specimens (e.g., nasopharyngeal swab, sputum) for definitive diagnosis.
  • Treatment Approach: Management is primarily supportive, focusing on respiratory support, fluid and electrolyte balance, and addressing complications such as pneumonia or kidney failure.
  • Public Health Notification: Promptly report all suspected and confirmed MERS-CoV cases to local and national public health authorities for surveillance and contact tracing.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Infection Control Mastery: Understand and meticulously apply contact and airborne precautions for MERS-CoV patients to prevent nosocomial transmission.
  • Patient and Family Education: Educate patients and their families on the importance of hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, and avoiding high-risk exposures.
  • PPE Competency: Demonstrate proficiency in the correct donning and doffing procedures for all required personal protective equipment.
  • Travel History Assessment: Emphasize thorough patient history taking, including recent travel and animal exposures, to aid in early identification of potential cases.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiologic Agent: Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV), a beta-coronavirus.
  • Primary Zoonotic Reservoir: Dromedary camels are the main animal source of infection for humans.
  • Incubation Period Range: Typically ranges from 2 to 14 days, with a median of 5-6 days.
  • Case Fatality Rate (CFR): Approximately 35%, with higher rates observed in older individuals and those with underlying comorbidities.
  • Laboratory Diagnosis Standard: Real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) is the gold standard for MERS-CoV detection from respiratory samples.
