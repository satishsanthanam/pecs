# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Life-Threatening Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) are severe, potentially fatal mucocutaneous reactions.
* **Epidermal Detachment:** Characterized by widespread blistering and detachment of the epidermis, resembling severe burns.
* **Drug-Induced:** Most commonly triggered by medications, including certain antibiotics, anticonvulsants, NSAIDs, and allopurinol.
* **Spectrum of Severity:** SJS involves less than 10% body surface area (BSA) detachment, TEN involves more than 30% BSA, and overlap syndrome is between 10-30%.
* **Medical Emergency:** Requires immediate hospitalization, often in a burn unit or intensive care unit, for specialized care.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Prodromal Phase:** Often begins with fever, malaise, headache, cough, and flu-like symptoms 1-3 weeks after drug exposure.
* **Skin Lesions:** Painful, rapidly spreading erythematous macules and atypical target lesions that progress to flaccid blisters and extensive epidermal sloughing.
* **Mucosal Involvement:** Severe erosions and crusting affecting at least two mucosal sites (oral, ocular, anogenital), leading to difficulty eating, drinking, and vision impairment.
* **Nikolsky's Sign:** Positive, meaning the epidermis can be easily detached from the dermis with gentle lateral pressure.
* **Systemic Manifestations:** May include sepsis, acute kidney injury, respiratory distress, gastrointestinal bleeding, and liver dysfunction.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Immediate Drug Withdrawal:** Promptly discontinue all suspected causative medications upon diagnosis to halt disease progression.
* **Avoid Re-exposure:** Patients must be educated to permanently avoid the identified culprit drug and chemically related compounds.
* **Genetic Screening:** Consider HLA-B*1502 screening before prescribing carbamazepine to patients of Asian descent to reduce SJS/TEN risk.
* **Meticulous Skin Care:** Implement sterile wound care techniques to prevent secondary infections, similar to burn management.
* **Fluid and Electrolyte Balance:** Aggressive fluid resuscitation and electrolyte monitoring are critical due to extensive skin loss.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Nutritional Support:** Due to severe oral and esophageal erosions, patients often require enteral (nasogastric or gastrostomy tube) or parenteral nutrition.
* **Intravenous Hydration:** Maintain adequate hydration through intravenous fluids, especially in the acute phase, to compensate for significant fluid loss from denuded skin.
* **Soft, Bland Diet:** Once oral intake is tolerated, introduce a soft, bland, non-acidic, and non-spicy diet to minimize irritation to healing mucosal lesions.
* **Avoid Irritants:** Strictly avoid acidic, spicy, rough, or very hot foods and beverages that can exacerbate oral and esophageal discomfort.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Specialized Unit Care:** Patients should ideally be managed in a burn unit or intensive care unit with expertise in severe dermatological conditions.
* **Infection Prevention:** Strict adherence to infection control protocols, including sterile dressing changes and systemic antibiotics for confirmed infections, is paramount.
* **Pain Management:** Administer aggressive pain relief, often with opioids, to manage the severe pain associated with skin and mucosal lesions.
* **Ocular Care:** Regular ophthalmology consultations and meticulous eye care (e.g., lubricating eye drops, topical corticosteroids) are essential to prevent long-term ocular complications.
* **Temperature Regulation:** Monitor and manage body temperature carefully due to impaired thermoregulation from extensive skin loss.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Early Diagnosis is Key:** Rapid identification of SJS/TEN and immediate withdrawal of the offending drug are the most crucial interventions to improve prognosis.
* **Supportive Care Focus:** Management is primarily supportive, concentrating on fluid and electrolyte balance, nutritional support, pain control, and prevention of infection.
* **Immunomodulatory Therapies:** Systemic corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), or cyclosporine may be considered, but their efficacy remains debated and timing is critical.
* **Prognostic Scoring:** Utilize the SCORTEN score to assess mortality risk and guide management decisions.
* **Long-Term Follow-up:** Patients require ongoing monitoring for potential sequelae, including chronic eye disease, skin scarring, pulmonary fibrosis, and psychological impact.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Comprehensive Assessment:** Perform daily assessments of skin detachment percentage, mucosal involvement, and signs of infection.
* **Wound Management:** Apply non-adherent dressings, perform gentle debridement of necrotic skin, and administer topical antimicrobials as prescribed.
* **Fluid Balance Monitoring:** Accurately track intake and output, recognizing significant insensible fluid losses from denuded skin.
* **Patient Education:** Educate patients and families about the causative drug, the importance of lifelong avoidance, and potential long-term complications.
* **Psychological Support:** Provide empathetic care and psychological support to patients and families coping with a severe, disfiguring, and life-threatening condition.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pathophysiology:** Immune-mediated cytotoxic reaction leading to widespread keratinocyte apoptosis, often involving Fas-FasL pathway activation.
* **Common Causative Drugs:** Sulfonamides, anticonvulsants (carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin), allopurinol, NSAIDs (oxicams), nevirapine.
* **Histopathology:** Full-thickness epidermal necrosis with a sparse lymphocytic infiltrate at the dermo-epidermal junction.
* **Differential Diagnosis:** Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome (SSSS), generalized bullous fixed drug eruption, erythema multiforme major, acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP).
* **SCORTEN Score Parameters:** Age >40 years, presence of malignancy, heart rate >120 bpm, initial BSA detachment >10%, serum BUN >28 mg/dL, serum glucose >252 mg/dL, serum bicarbonate <20 mEq/L.
