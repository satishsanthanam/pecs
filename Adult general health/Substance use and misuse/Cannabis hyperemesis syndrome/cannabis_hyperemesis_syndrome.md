# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Cannabis Hyperemesis Syndrome (CHS) is a condition affecting chronic cannabis users, characterized by cyclical episodes of severe nausea, intractable vomiting, and abdominal pain.
* **Key Feature:** Symptoms typically resolve completely and permanently with sustained cessation of all cannabis products.
* **Misdiagnosis Risk:** Often mistaken for Cyclic Vomiting Syndrome (CVS) or other gastrointestinal disorders due to similar symptom presentation.
* **Prevalence:** Increasing with the rising potency and frequency of cannabis use.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Prodromal Phase:** Early morning nausea, abdominal discomfort, and a fear of vomiting, often leading to increased cannabis use in an attempt to alleviate symptoms.
* **Hyperemetic Phase:** Intense, persistent nausea, severe and often intractable vomiting, and diffuse abdominal pain.
* **Compulsive Hot Bathing/Showering:** A hallmark symptom where patients find temporary relief from nausea and pain by taking frequent hot baths or showers.
* **Dehydration:** Significant fluid loss due to persistent vomiting, potentially leading to electrolyte imbalances.
* **Weight Loss:** Chronic nausea and vomiting can lead to unintentional weight loss over time.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Cannabis Cessation:** The most critical precaution and the only definitive treatment; complete abstinence from all cannabis products (THC, CBD, edibles, concentrates) is essential.
* **Avoid Triggers:** Any reintroduction of cannabis, even after a period of abstinence, can trigger a relapse of symptoms.
* **Hydration Management:** Vigilant monitoring and management of hydration status during acute episodes to prevent severe dehydration and electrolyte disturbances.
* **Medical Evaluation:** Seek prompt medical attention for severe vomiting, abdominal pain, or signs of dehydration to rule out other serious conditions.
* **Complication Awareness:** Be aware of potential complications such as esophageal tears (Mallory-Weiss syndrome), acute kidney injury, and electrolyte abnormalities.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Acute Phase:** Initially, maintain NPO (nil per os) status to rest the gastrointestinal tract, then gradually reintroduce clear liquids.
* **Bland Diet Progression:** As tolerated, advance to a bland, low-fat diet, avoiding foods that may irritate the stomach.
* **Avoid Irritants:** Strictly avoid spicy, fatty, acidic foods, caffeine, alcohol, and any other known gastrointestinal irritants.
* **Small, Frequent Meals:** Once able to tolerate solids, consume small, frequent meals to minimize gastric distension and nausea.
* **Oral Rehydration:** Utilize oral rehydration solutions to replenish fluids and electrolytes once vomiting subsides.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Patient Education:** Provide clear and empathetic education linking chronic cannabis use to the patient's symptoms and emphasizing the necessity of abstinence.
* **Symptom Management:** Administer antiemetics (often ineffective), benzodiazepines (for anxiety and nausea), and consider topical capsaicin cream for abdominal pain relief.
* **Electrolyte Monitoring:** Closely monitor serum electrolytes, especially potassium, sodium, and chloride, and correct imbalances promptly.
* **Support for Cessation:** Offer resources and referrals for cannabis use disorder treatment and psychological support to aid in sustained abstinence.
* **Environmental Control:** Create a calm, quiet environment for the patient during acute episodes to minimize sensory stimulation.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Comprehensive History:** A detailed history of cannabis use, including frequency, duration, and type, is paramount for accurate diagnosis.
* **Differential Diagnosis:** Carefully differentiate CHS from other causes of cyclic vomiting, such as Cyclic Vomiting Syndrome, gastroparesis, and other gastrointestinal pathologies.
* **Treatment Strategy:** The cornerstone of treatment is complete cannabis cessation, supported by symptomatic management during acute phases.
* **Pharmacological Approach:** While traditional antiemetics are often ineffective, benzodiazepines, haloperidol, and topical capsaicin have shown some utility in managing acute symptoms.
* **Long-term Management:** Emphasize the importance of long-term abstinence and consider referral to addiction specialists for ongoing support and relapse prevention.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Assessment Focus:** Conduct a thorough assessment including a detailed substance use history, symptom characteristics (onset, duration, relieving factors like hot showers), and hydration status.
* **Nursing Interventions:** Administer IV fluids as prescribed, monitor intake and output, provide antiemetics and pain relief, and ensure a safe, comfortable environment.
* **Patient Teaching:** Educate patients on the direct link between cannabis and their symptoms, the importance of abstinence, and strategies for managing withdrawal symptoms.
* **Psychosocial Support:** Recognize the potential for underlying substance use disorder and provide empathetic support, offering resources for counseling and support groups.
* **Electrolyte Balance:** Closely monitor laboratory results for electrolyte imbalances and report critical values to the physician promptly.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pathophysiology:** The exact mechanism is unclear but is thought to involve dysregulation of cannabinoid receptors (CB1, CB2) in the central nervous system and enteric nervous system, affecting thermoregulation and emetic centers.
* **Diagnostic Criteria:** Clinical diagnosis based on chronic cannabis use, cyclical episodes of nausea and vomiting, abdominal pain, compulsive hot bathing, and resolution of symptoms with cannabis cessation.
* **Pharmacology:** Antiemetics (e.g., ondansetron, metoclopramide) are often ineffective. Benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam) can help with anxiety and nausea. Haloperidol has shown efficacy in refractory cases. Topical capsaicin cream may provide localized relief.
* **Epidemiology:** Predominantly affects long-term, heavy cannabis users, with an increasing incidence observed in emergency departments.
* **Differential Diagnosis:** Key conditions to rule out include Cyclic Vomiting Syndrome, gastroparesis, peptic ulcer disease, gastroenteritis, and acute abdomen.
