# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Life-Saving Intervention:** Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) is an emergency procedure performed when the heart stops beating.
* **Purpose:** It combines chest compressions and artificial ventilation to manually pump blood to the brain and other vital organs until medical help arrives or the heart restarts.
* **Time-Sensitive:** Immediate CPR can double or triple a person's chance of survival from cardiac arrest.
* **Cardiac Arrest:** This occurs when the heart suddenly stops pumping blood effectively, leading to loss of consciousness and cessation of breathing.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Unresponsiveness:** The individual does not respond to verbal commands or gentle shaking.
* **No Breathing or Abnormal Breathing:** The person is not breathing, or is only gasping (agonal breathing), which is not effective.
* **Absence of Pulse:** No palpable pulse can be found in major arteries (e.g., carotid artery in the neck).
* **Sudden Collapse:** Often, the person collapses suddenly without warning.
* **Loss of Consciousness:** The individual is unconscious and cannot be roused.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Scene Safety First:** Always ensure the environment is safe for both the rescuer and the patient before approaching.
* **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Use barrier devices (e.g., pocket mask) for ventilations if available, to prevent direct contact with bodily fluids.
* **Proper Hand Placement:** Ensure correct hand position (center of the chest, lower half of the sternum) to maximize effectiveness and minimize injury.
* **Avoid Excessive Force:** While compressions should be firm and deep, avoid unnecessary roughness that could cause additional trauma.
* **Confirm Need for CPR:** Rapidly assess for unresponsiveness and absence of normal breathing/pulse before initiating compressions.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Airway Clearance:** Ensure the patient's airway is clear of any food, fluids, or foreign objects before attempting ventilations to prevent aspiration.
* **Potential for Regurgitation:** Be aware that regurgitation of stomach contents can occur during CPR; turn the head to the side if vomiting occurs to prevent aspiration.
* **Post-Resuscitation NPO:** Following successful resuscitation, the patient will typically be kept 'nil per os' (NPO) until a full medical evaluation and airway protection assessment are completed.
* **Hydration Status:** While not directly related to CPR performance, underlying dehydration or electrolyte imbalances can contribute to cardiac events, highlighting the importance of overall patient health.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Call Emergency Services:** Immediately call 911 (or your local emergency number) and activate the emergency response system.
* **Initiate Compressions Promptly:** Begin chest compressions without delay if the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally.
* **Locate AED:** If an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) is available, retrieve it and apply it as soon as possible.
* **Continuous High-Quality CPR:** Continue compressions and ventilations until emergency medical services (EMS) arrive, an AED is ready for use, or the person shows signs of life.
* **Minimize Interruptions:** Limit pauses in chest compressions to less than 10 seconds.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Early Recognition is Key:** Prompt identification of cardiac arrest and immediate initiation of CPR are critical determinants of patient outcome.
* **High-Quality Compressions:** Focus on adequate depth (2-2.4 inches or 5-6 cm), rate (100-120/min), and full chest recoil between compressions.
* **Defibrillation Importance:** For shockable rhythms (Ventricular Fibrillation/Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia), early defibrillation significantly increases survival rates.
* **Post-Resuscitation Care:** Comprehensive post-cardiac arrest care, including targeted temperature management and hemodynamic support, is vital for neurological recovery.
* **Training and Certification:** Encourage all healthcare providers and the public to obtain and maintain current CPR certification.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Systematic Assessment:** Utilize the 'CAB' approach (Circulation, Airway, Breathing) for rapid assessment in an emergency.
* **Team Dynamics:** Understand roles and responsibilities within a resuscitation team to ensure efficient and coordinated care.
* **Ventilation Technique:** Practice effective bag-mask ventilation or mouth-to-mouth techniques, ensuring a good seal and visible chest rise.
* **Documentation:** Accurately record the time CPR was initiated, interventions performed, medications administered, and patient response.
* **Ethical Considerations:** Be aware of advanced directives (e.g., DNR orders) and ethical considerations surrounding resuscitation efforts.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Compression Depth (Adult):** At least 2 inches (5 cm) but no more than 2.4 inches (6 cm).
* **Compression Rate (Adult):** 100 to 120 compressions per minute.
* **Compression-to-Ventilation Ratio (Adult, Single Rescuer):** 30 compressions to 2 breaths.
* **Compression-to-Ventilation Ratio (Adult, Healthcare Provider, Advanced Airway):** Continuous compressions (100-120/min) with 1 breath every 6 seconds (10 breaths/min) asynchronously.
* **Chest Recoil:** Allow complete chest recoil after each compression to ensure adequate cardiac filling.
* **AED Use:** Follow AED prompts; deliver a shock if advised, then immediately resume CPR.
