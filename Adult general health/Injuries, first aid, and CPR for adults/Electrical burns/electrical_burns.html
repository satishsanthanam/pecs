Topic

Electrical burns

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Nature of Injury: Electrical burns occur when an electric current passes through the body, causing tissue damage.
  • Internal Damage Risk: The most significant danger is internal damage to organs, muscles, and nerves, which may not be immediately visible on the skin surface.
  • Severity Factors: Severity depends on voltage, current type (AC/DC), duration of contact, and the pathway of the current through the body.
  • Cardiac Impact: High voltage or alternating current can disrupt the heart's electrical rhythm, leading to cardiac arrest or arrhythmias.
  • Entry and Exit Wounds: Electrical current typically creates an entry point and an exit point on the body, both of which require medical attention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Visible Burns: Skin burns at the point of contact (entry and exit wounds), which may appear charred, leathery, or blistered.
  • Muscle Pain & Spasms: Severe muscle contractions can occur, potentially leading to fractures or dislocations.
  • Numbness & Tingling: Nerve damage can cause altered sensation, weakness, or paralysis.
  • Cardiac Arrhythmias: Irregular heartbeat, chest pain, or sudden cardiac arrest.
  • Respiratory Distress: Difficulty breathing, especially if the current pathway involved the chest muscles or brainstem.
  • Loss of Consciousness: May occur immediately upon contact, indicating significant neurological impact.
  • Kidney Dysfunction: Dark urine (rhabdomyolysis) due to muscle breakdown products, potentially leading to acute kidney injury.
  • Headaches & Seizures: Neurological symptoms can manifest immediately or be delayed.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Ensure Safety First: NEVER touch a person who is still in contact with an electrical source. Turn off the power source immediately if possible.
  • Call Emergency Services: Dial emergency services (e.g., 911) immediately, even if the burn appears minor, as internal damage can be severe.
  • Assess ABCs: Once safe, check for Airway, Breathing, and Circulation. Begin CPR if the person is not breathing and has no pulse.
  • Cover Burns: Loosely cover burned areas with a sterile, non-adhesive dressing or a clean cloth. Do not use blankets or towels that might shed fibers.
  • Prevent Hypothermia: Keep the person warm, especially if they are in shock, by covering them with a clean sheet or blanket.
  • Spinal Immobilization: If the person fell or was thrown by the electrical current, assume a potential spinal injury and avoid moving them unless absolutely necessary.
  • Monitor for Shock: Watch for signs of shock, such as pale, clammy skin, rapid pulse, and shallow breathing.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Initial NPO Status: In acute, severe cases, the patient will likely be NPO (nothing by mouth) due to potential for intubation, aspiration risk, or immediate surgical needs.
  • Intravenous Fluids: Aggressive intravenous fluid resuscitation is critical to prevent shock and manage potential kidney damage from rhabdomyolysis.
  • High Protein & Calorie Diet: Once stable and able to tolerate oral intake, a diet rich in protein and calories is essential to support wound healing and combat hypermetabolism.
  • Vitamin & Mineral Supplementation: Specific micronutrients like Vitamin C, Zinc, and Vitamin A are crucial for tissue repair and immune function in burn patients.
  • Hydration: Continuous adequate hydration is vital to support kidney function and overall recovery.
  • Avoid Irritants: Initially, avoid foods that may cause gastrointestinal upset or are difficult to digest, especially if there is any suspicion of internal abdominal injury.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Do Not Touch: Emphasize again: Do not touch the victim until the power source is confirmed to be off. Use a non-conductive object (e.g., wooden stick) to move the source if necessary and safe.
  • Do Not Remove Clothing: Do not attempt to remove clothing that is stuck to the burned skin; cut around it if necessary.
  • No Ice or Home Remedies: Do not apply ice, butter, ointments, or any other home remedies to the burn. This can worsen the injury or increase infection risk.
  • Monitor for Delayed Symptoms: Be aware that internal injuries and complications (e.g., cardiac arrhythmias, kidney failure) can manifest hours or days after the initial incident.
  • Seek Professional Help: Always transport the person to the nearest emergency department, even if they appear fine, for a thorough medical evaluation.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough evaluation for internal injuries, including cardiac, neurological, renal, and musculoskeletal systems, is paramount.
  • Cardiac Monitoring: Continuous ECG monitoring is essential for at least 24-48 hours due to the risk of delayed arrhythmias.
  • Fluid Resuscitation: Aggressive fluid resuscitation is critical to maintain organ perfusion and prevent acute kidney injury, often guided by urine output.
  • Pain Management: Effective pain control is crucial, often requiring intravenous analgesics.
  • Wound Care & Infection Control: Meticulous burn wound care, debridement of necrotic tissue, and infection prevention are vital for healing.
  • Compartment Syndrome Watch: Monitor for signs of compartment syndrome, especially in affected limbs, which may require fasciotomy.
  • Rehabilitation Planning: Early involvement of physical and occupational therapy is important for functional recovery, especially with nerve or muscle damage.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Prioritize Safety & ABCs: The immediate nursing priority is ensuring scene safety, then assessing and managing the patient's airway, breathing, and circulation.
  • Burn Assessment: Accurately assess burn depth and total body surface area (TBSA) using tools like the Rule of Nines or Lund-Browder chart.
  • Fluid Management: Administer intravenous fluids as prescribed, often following formulas like Parkland, and meticulously monitor urine output and vital signs.
  • Neurological Monitoring: Perform frequent neurological assessments to detect changes indicative of brain or nerve damage.
  • Pain Assessment & Intervention: Regularly assess pain levels and administer analgesia, evaluating its effectiveness.
  • Wound Care Principles: Adhere to sterile technique for wound care, applying appropriate dressings and monitoring for signs of infection.
  • Patient & Family Education: Educate on the importance of follow-up care, signs of complications, and electrical safety prevention.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Rhabdomyolysis: Breakdown of muscle tissue releasing myoglobin into the bloodstream, potentially causing acute kidney injury.
  • Ventricular Fibrillation: A life-threatening cardiac arrhythmia where the ventricles quiver uselessly instead of pumping blood.
  • Compartment Syndrome: A condition where increased pressure within a muscle compartment compromises circulation and nerve function.
  • Acute Kidney Injury (AKI): Sudden and often reversible loss of kidney function, frequently a complication of severe electrical burns.
  • Peripheral Neuropathy: Damage to peripheral nerves, leading to weakness, numbness, and pain, often a delayed complication.
  • Burn Shock: A form of hypovolemic shock resulting from massive fluid shifts out of the intravascular space due to burn injury.
  • Electrolyte Imbalance: Disturbances in sodium, potassium, calcium, and other electrolytes, common in severe burn patients.
