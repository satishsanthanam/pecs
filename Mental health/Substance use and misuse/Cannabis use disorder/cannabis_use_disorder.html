Cannabis use disorder

🏠 Home / Mental health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Cannabis Use Disorder (CUD) is a problematic pattern of cannabis use leading to clinically significant impairment or distress, characterized by symptoms such as impaired control, social impairment, risky use, and pharmacological criteria (tolerance and withdrawal).
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Diagnosis is based on the presence of at least two out of 11 criteria over a 12-month period, as outlined in the DSM-5, including craving, tolerance, withdrawal, and continued use despite negative consequences.
  • Prevalence: CUD is a significant public health concern, particularly among adolescents and young adults, with rates varying based on cannabis availability and societal perceptions.
  • Risk Factors: Key risk factors include early initiation of cannabis use, frequent and high-potency use, co-occurring mental health conditions (e.g., anxiety, depression, psychosis), and a family history of substance use disorders.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Impaired Control: Persistent desire or unsuccessful efforts to cut down or control cannabis use, often spending a great deal of time obtaining, using, or recovering from its effects.
  • Social Impairment: Recurrent cannabis use resulting in a failure to fulfill major role obligations at work, school, or home, or continued use despite persistent or recurrent social or interpersonal problems.
  • Risky Use: Recurrent cannabis use in situations in which it is physically hazardous (e.g., driving a car or operating machinery while impaired).
  • Tolerance Development: A need for markedly increased amounts of cannabis to achieve intoxication or desired effect, or a markedly diminished effect with continued use of the same amount of cannabis.
  • Withdrawal Syndrome: Development of a characteristic withdrawal syndrome (e.g., irritability, anxiety, sleep disturbance, decreased appetite, physical discomfort) when cannabis use is stopped or reduced.
  • Craving: A strong desire or urge to use cannabis, often leading to preoccupation with obtaining and using the substance.
  • Neglect of Activities: Important social, occupational, or recreational activities are given up or reduced because of cannabis use.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoidance of Triggers: Patients should identify and actively avoid environmental, social, and emotional triggers that prompt cannabis use.
  • Mental Health Screening: Comprehensive screening for co-occurring mental health disorders (e.g., anxiety, depression, psychosis) is essential, as these can exacerbate CUD and complicate treatment.
  • Driving & Operating Machinery: Strictly advise against driving or operating any heavy machinery while under the influence of cannabis due to impaired judgment and motor skills.
  • Pregnancy & Breastfeeding: Strongly recommend abstinence from cannabis during pregnancy and breastfeeding due to potential adverse effects on fetal and infant development.
  • Adolescent Brain Development: Caution adolescents and their guardians about the potential long-term negative impacts of cannabis use on the developing brain, including cognitive impairment and increased risk of psychiatric disorders.
  • Relapse Prevention Planning: Develop and regularly review a personalized relapse prevention plan, incorporating coping strategies, support systems, and emergency contacts.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration Management: Encourage adequate fluid intake, especially water, to manage symptoms like dry mouth and support overall physiological function during withdrawal.
  • Nutrient-Rich Diet: Promote a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains to support physical and mental recovery and address potential nutritional deficiencies.
  • Avoidance of Stimulants: Advise limiting or avoiding caffeine and other stimulants, which can exacerbate anxiety, restlessness, and sleep disturbances common during cannabis withdrawal.
  • Blood Sugar Stabilization: Recommend regular, balanced meals and snacks to help stabilize blood sugar levels, which can mitigate irritability and cravings often experienced during recovery.
  • Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome (CHS): For patients with suspected CHS, strict and complete abstinence from cannabis is the primary treatment; a bland diet and antiemetics may be used for symptomatic relief.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Seek Professional Evaluation: Individuals experiencing symptoms of CUD should seek prompt evaluation by a healthcare professional for accurate diagnosis and tailored treatment planning.
  • Engage in Support Systems: Encourage active participation in support groups (e.g., Marijuana Anonymous) and leverage family or social support networks to foster recovery.
  • Medication Adherence: If prescribed medications for co-occurring mental health conditions or withdrawal management, strict adherence to the regimen is crucial for treatment efficacy.
  • Honest Communication with Providers: Maintain open and honest communication with all healthcare providers regarding cannabis use patterns, challenges, and progress to ensure optimal care.
  • Safety Planning for Cravings: Develop a proactive safety plan to manage intense cravings, including distraction techniques, contacting support, and avoiding high-risk situations.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough clinical assessment is paramount, encompassing detailed substance use history, mental health status, physical health, and social determinants of health.
  • Evidence-Based Interventions: Recommend and facilitate access to evidence-based psychosocial treatments such as Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Motivational Enhancement Therapy (MET), and Contingency Management (CM).
  • Pharmacological Considerations: While no FDA-approved medications specifically treat CUD, consider pharmacological interventions for managing co-occurring mental health disorders or severe withdrawal symptoms (e.g., nabilone for sleep disturbance).
  • Harm Reduction Strategies: For patients not ready for complete abstinence, discuss harm reduction strategies to minimize negative consequences associated with cannabis use.
  • Long-Term Management: Emphasize that CUD is a chronic, relapsing condition requiring ongoing support, monitoring, and adjustment of treatment strategies to sustain recovery.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Screening & Assessment: Utilize validated screening tools (e.g., ASSIST, CUDIT-R) to identify individuals at risk for or experiencing CUD in various clinical settings.
  • Withdrawal Symptom Management: Monitor patients for cannabis withdrawal symptoms (irritability, anxiety, insomnia, decreased appetite, physical discomfort) and provide supportive care, including comfort measures and psychoeducation.
  • Patient Education & Counseling: Educate patients on the health risks of cannabis use, available treatment options, and effective coping strategies for cravings and triggers.
  • Motivational Interviewing: Employ motivational interviewing techniques to engage patients, explore their readiness for change, and enhance their intrinsic motivation for recovery.
  • Referral Pathways: Be proficient in identifying and facilitating appropriate referrals to specialized addiction treatment programs, mental health services, and community support groups.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria for CUD: Refer to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, for the official diagnostic criteria and severity specifiers for Cannabis Use Disorder.
  • Endocannabinoid System (ECS): Understand the neurobiological mechanisms of cannabis on the ECS, including cannabinoid receptors (CB1, CB2) and their role in reward, mood, and cognition.
  • Pharmacokinetics of THC: Review the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and its metabolites to understand its effects and duration of action.
  • Comorbidity with Psychiatric Disorders: Recognize the high rates of co-occurrence between CUD and other mental health conditions, such as anxiety disorders, depressive disorders, and psychotic disorders.
  • Evidence-Based Psychosocial Interventions: Familiarize with the efficacy and implementation of key psychosocial therapies for CUD, including Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Motivational Enhancement Therapy (MET), and Contingency Management (CM).
