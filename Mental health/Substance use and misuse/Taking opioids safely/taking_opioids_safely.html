Taking opioids safely

🏠 Home / Mental health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Prescription Adherence: Always take opioids exactly as prescribed by your healthcare provider. Do not alter the dose, frequency, or method of administration.
  • High Risk of Dependence: Opioids carry a significant risk of physical dependence and addiction, even when taken as prescribed. Understand these risks before starting treatment.
  • Never Share: It is illegal and extremely dangerous to share your opioid medication with anyone else, as it can lead to overdose or death for others.
  • Purpose of Use: Opioids are typically prescribed for severe acute pain or chronic pain when other treatments have failed. Discuss the duration of treatment with your doctor.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Signs of Overdose: Pinpoint pupils, slowed or stopped breathing, blue lips or fingernails, unresponsiveness, gurgling sounds, or limp body. Seek emergency help immediately.
  • Withdrawal Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, muscle aches, sweating, chills, anxiety, and agitation can occur if medication is stopped abruptly after dependence.
  • Common Side Effects: Constipation, drowsiness, nausea, vomiting, and dizziness are frequent. Report persistent or severe side effects to your doctor.
  • Increased Tolerance: Needing higher doses of the medication to achieve the same pain relief can indicate developing tolerance or dependence.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Secure Storage: Store opioids in a locked cabinet or secure location, out of sight and reach of children, pets, and visitors.
  • Avoid Interactions: Do not consume alcohol or take other central nervous system depressants (e.g., benzodiazepines, muscle relaxants) while on opioids, as this significantly increases overdose risk.
  • Proper Disposal: Dispose of unused or expired opioids safely. Utilize drug take-back programs or follow FDA guidelines for flushing certain medications if no other option is available.
  • Naloxone Availability: Discuss obtaining a naloxone prescription with your doctor, especially if you are at high risk for overdose or live with someone who is. Learn how to use it.
  • Driving and Operating Machinery: Avoid driving or operating heavy machinery until you know how opioids affect you, as they can cause drowsiness and impaired judgment.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Combat Constipation: Opioids commonly cause constipation. Increase dietary fiber intake (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) and ensure adequate fluid intake. Stool softeners or laxatives may be necessary as prescribed.
  • Manage Nausea: If experiencing nausea, try eating small, bland meals. Avoid fatty, spicy, or highly acidic foods. Stay hydrated with clear fluids.
  • Avoid Alcohol: Strictly avoid alcohol consumption while taking opioids, as it can dangerously enhance the sedative effects and increase the risk of respiratory depression and overdose.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Do Not Crush or Break: Never crush, chew, or break extended-release opioid tablets, as this can lead to a rapid release of the entire dose and cause a fatal overdose.
  • Report All Medications: Inform your healthcare provider about all other medications, supplements, and herbal remedies you are taking to prevent dangerous drug interactions.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Ensure family members or caregivers know the signs of an opioid overdose and how to administer naloxone if available.
  • Risk of Respiratory Depression: Be aware that opioids can slow or stop your breathing, especially at higher doses or when combined with other depressants.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Pain Plan: Opioids are one tool in pain management. We aim for a comprehensive plan that may include non-opioid medications, physical therapy, and other interventions.
  • Regular Reassessment: Your treatment plan will be regularly reviewed, including pain levels, functional improvement, and potential side effects, to ensure continued appropriateness.
  • Open Communication: It is crucial to have open and honest discussions about your pain, any side effects, concerns about dependence, or difficulties adhering to the prescription.
  • Exit Strategy: For chronic pain, we will work together on a tapering plan when appropriate to minimize withdrawal symptoms and transition to alternative pain management.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients and families on safe opioid use, storage, disposal, and overdose recognition, including naloxone administration.
  • Pain Assessment: Conduct thorough and regular pain assessments using appropriate scales, alongside monitoring for sedation levels and respiratory depression.
  • Bowel Regimen: Proactively implement bowel regimens (e.g., stool softeners, laxatives) for patients on opioids to prevent and manage opioid-induced constipation.
  • Withdrawal Monitoring: Be vigilant for signs of opioid withdrawal, especially if doses are missed or reduced, and report findings promptly to the prescribing physician.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Mu-Opioid Receptor Agonists: Opioids primarily exert their analgesic effects by binding to and activating mu-opioid receptors in the central nervous system.
  • CDC Guidelines for Prescribing Opioids: Refer to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for prescribing opioids for chronic pain for best practices and risk mitigation strategies.
  • Opioid Risk Assessment Tools: Utilize validated tools such as the Opioid Risk Tool (ORT) or Screener and Opioid Assessment for Patients with Pain (SOAPP) to identify patients at higher risk for opioid misuse.
  • Naloxone Mechanism of Action: Naloxone is an opioid antagonist that rapidly reverses opioid effects by competitively binding to opioid receptors, displacing opioid agonists.
