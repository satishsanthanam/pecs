Medical cannabis

🏠 Home / Mental health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Medical cannabis refers to the use of the cannabis plant or its synthetic derivatives to treat specific medical conditions or symptoms under medical supervision.
  • Active Compounds: Contains cannabinoids, primarily Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and Cannabidiol (CBD), which interact with the body's endocannabinoid system.
  • Mental Health Context: While not a first-line treatment, it is sometimes considered for severe, refractory symptoms of certain mental health conditions like PTSD, chronic anxiety, or insomnia, where conventional treatments have failed.
  • Legal Status: The legality and availability of medical cannabis vary significantly by jurisdiction, requiring patients to be aware of local regulations.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Indications for Use: Chronic anxiety, severe insomnia, PTSD-related nightmares or hypervigilance, chronic pain contributing to mental distress, and certain seizure disorders.
  • Potential Side Effects (Acute): Dizziness, dry mouth, impaired coordination, increased heart rate, paranoia, acute anxiety, and cognitive impairment.
  • Potential Side Effects (Chronic/Misuse): Development of cannabis use disorder, worsening of pre-existing psychotic disorders, impaired memory and attention, and respiratory issues with inhaled forms.
  • Withdrawal Symptoms: Irritability, anxiety, sleep disturbances, decreased appetite, and physical discomfort upon cessation in dependent individuals.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Contraindications: History of psychosis or schizophrenia, severe cardiovascular disease, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and unstable psychiatric conditions.
  • Drug Interactions: Potential interactions with central nervous system depressants (e.g., benzodiazepines, opioids), anticoagulants, and certain antidepressants (e.g., SSRIs, tricyclics).
  • Impairment Risk: Patients must be advised against driving or operating heavy machinery while under the influence of medical cannabis due to impaired cognitive and motor function.
  • Dosage Adherence: Strict adherence to prescribed dosage and administration route is critical to minimize adverse effects and prevent misuse.
  • Monitoring: Regular clinical monitoring for efficacy, adverse effects, and signs of cannabis use disorder is essential.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Edible Forms: When consuming edibles, be aware of delayed onset (30 minutes to 2 hours) and prolonged effects (up to 8 hours or more). Start with a very low dose and wait for full effects before considering more.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration, as dry mouth is a common side effect of cannabis use.
  • Alcohol Avoidance: Concurrent use of alcohol with medical cannabis can significantly amplify impairment and increase the risk of adverse effects.
  • Food Interactions: While no specific food restrictions exist, consuming edibles with fatty foods may enhance absorption and potency.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Secure Storage: Store medical cannabis in its original, child-resistant packaging, in a secure location inaccessible to children, pets, and unauthorized individuals.
  • Proper Disposal: Dispose of unused or expired medical cannabis according to local regulations, often involving mixing with undesirable substances (e.g., coffee grounds) and sealing in a bag before discarding.
  • Reporting Adverse Events: Immediately report any severe or unexpected side effects, worsening mental health symptoms, or signs of dependence to your prescribing physician.
  • Legal Compliance: Patients and caregivers must be fully aware of and comply with all local, state, and federal laws regarding the possession, use, and transportation of medical cannabis.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough medical and psychiatric evaluation is paramount before considering medical cannabis, especially in patients with a history of substance use disorder or psychotic illness.
  • Risk-Benefit Analysis: Physicians must conduct a careful risk-benefit analysis, weighing potential symptomatic relief against the risks of adverse effects, dependence, and interactions.
  • Treatment Plan Integration: Medical cannabis should be integrated into a broader, individualized treatment plan, often alongside psychotherapy and other evidence-based interventions.
  • Patient Education: Extensive patient education on product types, routes of administration, expected effects, potential side effects, and signs of problematic use is crucial.
  • Ongoing Reassessment: Regular follow-up appointments are necessary to monitor treatment efficacy, adjust dosages, and screen for any emerging concerns or complications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Role: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients on safe administration, potential side effects, storage, and the importance of adhering to prescribed dosages.
  • Assessment for Misuse: Be vigilant for signs of cannabis use disorder, including increased tolerance, withdrawal symptoms, unsuccessful attempts to cut down, and continued use despite negative consequences.
  • Monitoring Parameters: Monitor vital signs, mental status, pain levels, sleep patterns, and overall functional status. Observe for changes in mood, cognition, or behavior.
  • Advocacy and Support: Provide non-judgmental support and advocate for patients, addressing stigma and ensuring access to appropriate resources for both medical use and potential misuse.
  • Interdisciplinary Collaboration: Collaborate with physicians, pharmacists, and mental health professionals to ensure coordinated and safe patient care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Endocannabinoid System (ECS): A complex cell-signaling system involved in regulating a range of functions and processes, including mood, memory, appetite, sleep, and pain.
  • Cannabinoid Receptors: Primarily CB1 (found in the CNS) and CB2 (found in peripheral tissues and immune cells), which mediate the effects of cannabinoids.
  • Phytocannabinoids: Cannabinoids derived from the cannabis plant, such as THC (psychoactive) and CBD (non-psychoactive, anxiolytic, anti-inflammatory).
  • Cannabis Use Disorder (CUD): A problematic pattern of cannabis use leading to clinically significant impairment or distress, characterized by criteria such as tolerance, withdrawal, and craving.
  • Terpenes: Aromatic compounds found in cannabis that contribute to its flavor and aroma, and may modulate the effects of cannabinoids through the 'entourage effect'.
