# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Cannabis Hyperemesis Syndrome (CHS) is a condition characterized by recurrent episodes of severe nausea, intractable vomiting, and abdominal pain in chronic, heavy cannabis users.
* **Key Feature:** Patients often find temporary relief from symptoms by taking hot showers or baths.
* **Causation:** Directly linked to long-term, frequent use of cannabis and cannabinoid products.
* **Resolution:** Complete and sustained cessation of all cannabis use is the only definitive cure for CHS.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Prodromal Phase:** Early morning nausea, abdominal discomfort, fear of vomiting, and generalized malaise.
* **Hyperemetic Phase:** Intense, cyclical nausea and vomiting that can last for hours or days, severe diffuse abdominal pain, dehydration, and significant weight loss.
* **Compulsive Hot Bathing:** A hallmark symptom where individuals seek prolonged hot showers or baths for temporary symptom relief.
* **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Due to persistent vomiting, patients are at high risk for hypokalemia, hyponatremia, and metabolic alkalosis.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Cannabis Cessation:** Advise immediate and complete discontinuation of all cannabis and cannabinoid products to prevent recurrence and progression of symptoms.
* **Hydration Management:** Monitor closely for signs of dehydration and electrolyte disturbances, initiating intravenous fluid replacement as needed.
* **Avoid Triggers:** Educate patients that continued cannabis use, even in small amounts, will perpetuate the syndrome and negate treatment efforts.
* **Differential Diagnosis:** Ensure other causes of cyclical vomiting (e.g., cyclic vomiting syndrome, gastroparesis, Addison's disease) are considered and ruled out.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Acute Phase:** Maintain NPO (nothing by mouth) status during severe emesis to prevent aspiration and allow the gastrointestinal tract to rest.
* **Fluid Reintroduction:** Once vomiting subsides, gradually introduce small sips of clear liquids, oral rehydration solutions, or ice chips.
* **Bland Diet Progression:** Advance to a bland, low-fat diet (e.g., toast, crackers, rice, bananas) in small, frequent portions as tolerated.
* **Absolute Restriction:** Strictly avoid all forms of cannabis, including edibles, oils, and synthetic cannabinoids, as re-exposure will trigger symptoms.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Emergency Care:** Seek immediate medical attention for severe dehydration, intractable vomiting, altered mental status, or signs of electrolyte imbalance.
* **Symptomatic Relief:** While antiemetics are often ineffective, consider benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam) for anxiety and agitation, and topical capsaicin cream for abdominal pain relief.
* **Withdrawal Management:** Be prepared to manage potential cannabis withdrawal symptoms (e.g., irritability, insomnia, anxiety) upon cessation.
* **Psychological Support:** Refer patients to substance use counseling and support groups to aid in long-term cannabis abstinence.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Clinical Diagnosis:** CHS is primarily a clinical diagnosis based on a history of chronic cannabis use, cyclical vomiting, and resolution of symptoms with cannabis cessation.
* **Treatment Focus:** The cornerstone of treatment is complete cannabis abstinence, supported by aggressive symptomatic management during acute episodes.
* **Patient Education:** Emphasize the direct link between cannabis use and symptoms to foster patient understanding and compliance with cessation.
* **Long-term Management:** Address underlying substance use disorder with appropriate referrals and follow-up to prevent relapse.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Comprehensive Assessment:** Obtain a detailed history of cannabis use (frequency, quantity, duration, type) and associated symptoms, including hot bathing habits.
* **Fluid & Electrolyte Monitoring:** Closely monitor vital signs, intake/output, and laboratory results for signs of dehydration and electrolyte derangements.
* **Symptom Management:** Administer prescribed antiemetics, IV fluids, and pain relief, noting the effectiveness of interventions.
* **Patient Education & Support:** Educate patients on CHS, the importance of abstinence, and provide resources for addiction support and relapse prevention.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pathophysiology:** Proposed mechanisms involve dysregulation of the endocannabinoid system, particularly cannabinoid receptor type 1 (CB1) and transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1) receptors, affecting gastrointestinal motility and thermoregulation.
* **Pharmacological Interventions:** Antiemetics (e.g., ondansetron, metoclopramide) often have limited efficacy. Haloperidol and benzodiazepines have shown promise in refractory cases.
* **Diagnostic Criteria:** While no universally accepted criteria exist, key features include chronic cannabis use, cyclical vomiting, abdominal pain, and resolution with abstinence.
* **Epidemiology:** Incidence is increasing, particularly in regions with cannabis legalization, due to increased potency and accessibility of cannabinoid products.
