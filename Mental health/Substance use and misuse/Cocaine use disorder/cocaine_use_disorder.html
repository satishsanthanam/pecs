Cocaine use disorder

🏠 Home / Mental health / Substance use and misuse

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Chronic Brain Disease: Cocaine use disorder is a chronic, relapsing brain disease characterized by compulsive cocaine seeking and use despite harmful consequences.
  • High Addictive Potential: Cocaine is highly addictive due to its potent effects on the brain's reward system, particularly by increasing dopamine levels.
  • Rapid Onset & Short Duration: Effects are typically rapid in onset and short-lived, leading to a pattern of frequent, escalating use to maintain the desired high.
  • Varied Administration Routes: Can be snorted, injected, smoked (crack cocaine), or ingested, each influencing the speed and intensity of effects and associated risks.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Behavioral Changes: Increased risk-taking, secretive behavior, neglecting responsibilities, financial difficulties, legal problems, and social isolation.
  • Psychological Manifestations: Paranoia, hallucinations, delusions, severe anxiety, depression, irritability, mood swings, and anhedonia (inability to feel pleasure).
  • Physical Signs: Dilated pupils, increased heart rate and blood pressure, tremors, nosebleeds (if snorting), track marks (if injecting), significant weight loss, and dental erosion (crack cocaine).
  • Withdrawal Syndrome: Intense cravings, fatigue, dysphoria, increased appetite, psychomotor agitation or retardation, vivid unpleasant dreams, and suicidal ideation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Overdose Risk: High risk of overdose leading to cardiac arrest, stroke, seizures, or respiratory failure, especially with polysubstance use (e.g., with opioids or alcohol).
  • Mental Health Crises: Acute psychosis, severe depression, and suicidal ideation are common and require immediate psychiatric intervention.
  • Infectious Disease Transmission: Increased risk of HIV, hepatitis B and C through shared needles (injection use) or engaging in risky sexual behaviors.
  • Cardiovascular Complications: Long-term use can lead to cardiomyopathy, hypertension, arrhythmias, and increased risk of myocardial infarction and stroke.
  • Pregnancy Risks: Cocaine use during pregnancy is associated with premature birth, low birth weight, placental abruption, and developmental issues in the infant.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutritional Repletion: Focus on restoring nutritional status, as cocaine use often leads to poor dietary intake, dehydration, and significant weight loss.
  • Consistent Hydration: Encourage regular fluid intake to combat dehydration, which is common during acute intoxication and withdrawal.
  • Balanced Meal Planning: Promote regular, nutrient-dense meals to support physical recovery, stabilize blood sugar, and aid in restoring brain function.
  • Avoidance of Stimulants: Advise against excessive caffeine or other stimulants that can mimic or exacerbate cocaine's effects and potentially trigger cravings or anxiety.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prioritize Safety: Ensure the safety of both the individual and caregiver, particularly during acute intoxication or withdrawal where agitation, paranoia, or aggression may occur.
  • Immediate Medical Attention: Seek emergency medical and psychiatric evaluation for signs of overdose, severe withdrawal symptoms, or acute mental health crises.
  • Non-Judgmental Support: Provide empathetic, non-judgmental support, recognizing addiction as a complex disease requiring professional treatment.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Be aware of overdose signs (e.g., seizures, unresponsiveness, severe chest pain) and know how to promptly contact emergency services.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Assessment: A thorough medical and psychiatric evaluation is crucial to identify co-occurring mental health disorders and tailor an individualized treatment plan.
  • Behavioral Therapies: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Contingency Management (CM), and Motivational Interviewing (MI) are cornerstone treatments for cocaine use disorder.
  • Pharmacological Management: While no FDA-approved medications specifically treat cocaine use disorder, medications may be used to manage withdrawal symptoms or co-occurring conditions (e.g., antidepressants for depression).
  • Relapse Prevention: Long-term support, including individual and group therapy, peer support groups (e.g., Narcotics Anonymous), and ongoing monitoring, is vital for sustained recovery.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Withdrawal Monitoring: Nurses play a critical role in assessing and monitoring withdrawal symptoms, administering prescribed medications, and providing supportive care.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on the neurobiology of addiction, available treatment options, harm reduction strategies, and early warning signs of relapse.
  • Therapeutic Communication: Utilize strong therapeutic communication skills to build trust, address stigma, and encourage patient engagement and adherence to treatment.
  • Interdisciplinary Collaboration: Collaborate effectively with physicians, social workers, therapists, and other healthcare professionals to ensure holistic and coordinated patient care.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Diagnosis is based on specific criteria for Stimulant Use Disorder, moderate to severe, as outlined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition (DSM-5).
  • Neurotransmitter Impact: Primarily affects the dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin systems, leading to intense euphoria, increased energy, and subsequent dysphoria.
  • Pharmacokinetics: Characterized by rapid absorption and metabolism, resulting in a short half-life and intense, but brief, effects, which contributes to compulsive redosing.
  • High Comorbidity: Frequently co-occurs with other mental health disorders (e.g., depression, anxiety disorders, PTSD) and other substance use disorders.
