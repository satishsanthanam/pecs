Anorexia nervosa

🏠 Home / Mental health / Eating disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Core Definition: Anorexia nervosa is a severe eating disorder characterized by an intense fear of gaining weight, a distorted perception of body weight or shape, and extreme restriction of food intake.
  • Key Features: Individuals maintain a body weight significantly below what is minimally normal for their age and height.
  • Types: It presents in two main forms: the restricting type (weight loss achieved through dieting, fasting, and/or excessive exercise) and the binge-eating/purging type (recurrent episodes of binge eating or purging behavior, such as self-induced vomiting or misuse of laxatives, diuretics, or enemas).
  • Serious Health Risks: It carries the highest mortality rate of any psychiatric illness, often due to medical complications or suicide.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Physical Manifestations: Significant weight loss, fatigue, dizziness or fainting, brittle nails, thinning hair, dry skin, amenorrhea (absence of menstruation in females), lanugo (fine, downy hair growth on the body), cold intolerance, and constipation.
  • Behavioral Indicators: Restrictive eating patterns, excessive exercise, preoccupation with food, calories, and weight, frequent weighing, denial of hunger, ritualistic eating behaviors, and social withdrawal.
  • Psychological & Emotional Signs: Intense fear of gaining weight or becoming fat, distorted body image, irritability, depression, anxiety, obsessive thoughts about food, and perfectionism.
  • Cognitive Impairment: Difficulty concentrating, poor memory, and impaired judgment dueating to malnutrition.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Early Intervention: Prompt diagnosis and treatment are critical to prevent severe medical complications and improve long-term outcomes.
  • Medical Monitoring: Close medical supervision is essential to manage and prevent life-threatening complications such as electrolyte imbalances, cardiac arrhythmias, and refeeding syndrome.
  • Avoid Enabling Behaviors: Family and caregivers must avoid inadvertently supporting restrictive eating or excessive exercise; instead, foster a supportive environment for recovery.
  • Safe Exercise Guidelines: Exercise should be carefully monitored and restricted during initial recovery phases to prevent further weight loss and cardiac strain.
  • Psychological Safety: Ensure the patient is in a safe environment that minimizes triggers for disordered eating and promotes mental well-being.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Structured Meal Plans: Essential for weight restoration and normalizing eating patterns, often starting with a prescribed caloric intake and gradually increasing.
  • Refeeding Syndrome Prevention: Initial nutritional rehabilitation must be carefully managed with slow reintroduction of nutrients and close monitoring of electrolytes (potassium, phosphate, magnesium) to prevent this potentially fatal complication.
  • Nutrient-Dense Foods: Focus on providing calorically dense and nutritionally complete foods to support weight gain and address deficiencies.
  • Avoid Restrictive Food Rules: Post-recovery, encourage a balanced, varied diet without rigid food rules or 'good/bad' food labels to prevent relapse.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate fluid intake, especially during refeeding, to support metabolic processes and prevent dehydration.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Immediate Medical Attention: Seek emergency care for unstable vital signs (e.g., bradycardia, hypotension), severe electrolyte disturbances, or rapid, uncontrolled weight loss.
  • Multidisciplinary Team: Treatment requires a coordinated effort from physicians, psychiatrists, dietitians, and therapists specializing in eating disorders.
  • Family Involvement: Family-Based Treatment (FBT) is often recommended for adolescents, empowering parents to play an active role in their child's refeeding and recovery.
  • Psychiatric Evaluation: Assess for co-occurring mental health conditions such as depression, anxiety, or obsessive-compulsive disorder, which are common.
  • Avoid Judgment: Approach individuals with empathy and understanding, recognizing that anorexia nervosa is a serious mental illness, not a choice.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Prioritize Medical Stabilization: The initial focus is on restoring physiological stability and addressing life-threatening medical complications.
  • Weight Restoration: Achieving a healthy weight is a primary goal, as it is crucial for cognitive function and overall physical health recovery.
  • Psychotherapy is Key: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT-E), Family-Based Therapy (FBT), and other evidence-based psychotherapies are the cornerstone of long-term recovery.
  • Pharmacological Adjuncts: While no medication is FDA-approved specifically for anorexia nervosa, medications may be used to treat co-occurring conditions like depression or anxiety.
  • Long-Term Follow-up: Anorexia nervosa is a chronic condition requiring ongoing support and monitoring to prevent relapse.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct thorough physical, psychological, and nutritional assessments, including vital signs, weight, height, BMI, and mental status.
  • Refeeding Monitoring: Closely monitor patients for signs of refeeding syndrome, including changes in electrolytes, cardiac rhythm, and fluid balance.
  • Therapeutic Communication: Establish trust and rapport, validate patient feelings, and gently challenge distorted thoughts about food and body image.
  • Meal Supervision: Provide supervision during and after meals to ensure adequate intake and prevent compensatory behaviors (e.g., purging, excessive exercise).
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on the medical complications of anorexia nervosa, the importance of nutrition, and coping strategies for anxiety and body image concerns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, outlines the specific diagnostic criteria for Anorexia Nervosa.
  • SCOFF Questionnaire: A five-question screening tool used to identify potential eating disorders.
  • Electrolyte Panel: Frequent monitoring of serum potassium, phosphate, magnesium, and calcium levels, especially during refeeding.
  • ECG Monitoring: Electrocardiogram to assess for cardiac arrhythmias, particularly bradycardia and QT prolongation.
  • DEXA Scan: Dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry to assess bone mineral density due to increased risk of osteopenia and osteoporosis.
