Suicide prevention

🏠 Home / Mental health / Depression

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Recognize Warning Signs: Be aware of direct verbal threats, preoccupation with death, giving away possessions, increased substance use, or sudden mood improvements after a period of severe depression.
  • Understand Risk Factors: Key factors include a history of mental health disorders (especially depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia), previous suicide attempts, substance abuse, chronic physical pain, trauma, and significant life stressors.
  • Suicide is Preventable: Most individuals contemplating suicide do not truly want to die but rather want to end their pain. Intervention, support, and professional help can save lives.
  • Importance of Open Communication: Creating a safe space for individuals to discuss their feelings without judgment is crucial for early identification and intervention.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Sadness & Hopelessness: A pervasive feeling of despair, emptiness, or a belief that things will never get better.
  • Loss of Interest or Pleasure (Anhedonia): Diminished engagement in activities once enjoyed, including hobbies, social interactions, or work.
  • Changes in Sleep Patterns: Significant insomnia (difficulty falling or staying asleep) or hypersomnia (excessive sleeping).
  • Appetite & Weight Changes: Noticeable increase or decrease in appetite, leading to significant weight gain or loss.
  • Fatigue & Low Energy: Persistent tiredness, lack of energy, and a feeling of being slowed down.
  • Feelings of Worthlessness or Guilt: Excessive self-blame, feelings of inadequacy, or preoccupation with past failures.
  • Irritability & Agitation: Increased restlessness, anxiety, or an inability to sit still.
  • Difficulty Concentrating: Problems with focus, memory, and decision-making.
  • Social Withdrawal: Isolating oneself from friends, family, and social activities.
  • Reckless or Impulsive Behavior: Engaging in risky activities without regard for consequences.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Remove Lethal Means: Secure or remove access to firearms, dangerous medications, sharp objects, and other potential means of self-harm from the environment of an at-risk individual.
  • Do Not Leave Alone: If an individual expresses immediate suicidal intent, do not leave them unsupervised. Stay with them and seek immediate professional help.
  • Seek Emergency Assistance: Contact a crisis hotline, emergency services (911 or local equivalent), or take the individual to the nearest emergency department.
  • Follow Safety Planning: Collaborate with the individual and mental health professionals to develop a safety plan that outlines steps to take when suicidal thoughts arise, including coping strategies and contact information for support.
  • Monitor Medication Adherence: Ensure the individual takes prescribed psychiatric medications as directed, as non-adherence can lead to symptom relapse and increased risk.
  • Avoid Judgment & Stigma: Approach the individual with empathy and understanding, avoiding language or attitudes that could increase feelings of shame or isolation.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Substance Avoidance: Strictly avoid alcohol and illicit drugs, as they can exacerbate mood disorders, impair judgment, and significantly increase impulsivity and suicide risk.
  • Medication Interactions: Be aware of potential food-drug interactions with psychiatric medications (e.g., tyramine-rich foods with MAOIs, grapefruit with certain benzodiazepines or antidepressants). Consult a pharmacist or physician.
  • Hydration Status: Maintain adequate hydration, as dehydration can impact cognitive function, mood stability, and overall physical well-being, potentially worsening mental health symptoms.
  • Nutrient Support: While not a primary treatment, some evidence suggests a role for certain nutrients (e.g., Omega-3 fatty acids, Vitamin D, B vitamins) in mood regulation. Discuss with a healthcare provider before supplementing, especially if on medication.
  • Structured Eating Patterns: Encourage regular, balanced meals to help stabilize blood sugar and energy levels, which can positively impact mood and reduce irritability.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Listen Actively & Empathetically: Provide a non-judgmental space for the individual to express their feelings. Validate their pain without necessarily agreeing with their desire to end their life.
  • Take All Threats Seriously: Never dismiss or minimize any talk of suicide, even if it seems like a cry for attention. Always assume the person is serious and in distress.
  • Do Not Promise Secrecy: Explain that you cannot keep suicidal ideation a secret and that you will need to involve professionals to ensure their safety.
  • Encourage Professional Help: Guide the individual towards mental health professionals, crisis hotlines, or emergency services. Offer to help them make the call or accompany them.
  • Create a Safety Plan Together: Work with the individual to identify triggers, coping strategies, and a list of contacts for support during a crisis.
  • Practice Self-Care: Supporting someone at risk can be emotionally taxing. Ensure you have your own support system and seek professional help if needed.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Comprehensive Risk Assessment: Conduct a thorough evaluation of an individual's suicide risk, including current ideation, past attempts, access to means, protective factors, and underlying mental health conditions.
  • Pharmacotherapy: Prescribe appropriate medications (e.g., antidepressants, mood stabilizers, antipsychotics) to manage underlying mental health disorders, carefully monitoring for side effects and treatment response.
  • Psychotherapeutic Interventions: Recommend evidence-based therapies such as Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), or Collaborative Assessment and Management of Suicidality (CAMS).
  • Safety Planning & Crisis Management: Develop individualized safety plans with patients, outlining steps to take during a crisis, including emergency contacts and coping mechanisms.
  • Hospitalization Criteria: Consider inpatient psychiatric hospitalization for individuals at high risk, especially those with acute suicidal intent, lack of protective factors, or inability to maintain safety in an outpatient setting.
  • Long-Term Follow-up: Emphasize the importance of continuous care, regular follow-up appointments, and adherence to treatment plans to reduce relapse risk.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Suicide Risk Assessment Tools: Utilize standardized tools like the Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) or the SAD PERSONS scale to systematically assess risk factors and ideation.
  • Therapeutic Communication Techniques: Employ active listening, empathy, and non-judgmental communication to build rapport and encourage disclosure of suicidal thoughts.
  • Environmental Safety Protocols: Implement and maintain suicide precautions in clinical settings, including removing potential ligatures, sharp objects, and ensuring constant observation as indicated.
  • Crisis Intervention Skills: Be prepared to de-escalate crisis situations, provide immediate support, and facilitate access to higher levels of care or emergency services.
  • Patient & Family Education: Educate patients and their families on warning signs, the importance of adherence to treatment, and how to access crisis resources.
  • Documentation: Meticulously document all risk assessments, interventions, safety plans, and communications with patients and their support systems.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria for Major Depressive Disorder: Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, outlining criteria for depression, a primary risk factor for suicide.
  • ICD-10 Codes: Relevant codes for suicidal ideation (R45.851), intentional self-harm (X60-X84), and specific mental and behavioral disorders (F00-F99) associated with suicide risk.
  • Evidence-Based Psychotherapies: Specific modalities like Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) for chronic suicidality and Borderline Personality Disorder, and Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for depression.
  • National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: A 24/7, free, and confidential support line for people in distress, prevention and crisis resources for you or your loved ones (e.g., 988 in the U.S.).
  • Zero Suicide Initiative: A commitment to suicide prevention in health and behavioral health care systems, aiming for continuous quality improvement in care.
  • Safety Planning Intervention (SPI): A brief, evidence-based intervention designed to help individuals at risk for suicide develop a plan to stay safe.
