Panic attack – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Mental health / Anxiety disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Panic Attacks: A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear or discomfort that reaches a peak within minutes, accompanied by physical and cognitive symptoms.
  • Not Life-Threatening: While symptoms can be terrifying and mimic serious medical conditions (like a heart attack), a panic attack itself is not life-threatening.
  • Common Triggers: Panic attacks can occur unexpectedly or be triggered by specific situations, stress, or even certain substances. Identifying potential triggers can be a part of management.
  • Importance of Follow-Up: This episode requires follow-up with a healthcare provider to rule out other conditions and develop a long-term management plan.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Physical Manifestations: Patients often experience palpitations, pounding heart, accelerated heart rate, sweating, trembling or shaking, and sensations of shortness of breath or smothering.
  • Chest Discomfort: Feelings of chest pain or discomfort are common, leading many to fear a heart attack.
  • Neurological Symptoms: Dizziness, lightheadedness, faintness, chills or hot flashes, and paresthesias (numbness or tingling sensations) may occur.
  • Psychological Distress: Intense fear of losing control, "going crazy," or dying, along with feelings of unreality (derealization) or being detached from oneself (depersonalization).
  • Post-Attack Fatigue: After an attack, individuals may feel exhausted, drained, or anxious about having another attack.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Grounding Techniques: During an attack, focus on sensory details in your environment (e.g., 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, 1 thing you can taste) to help anchor yourself to the present.
  • Diaphragmatic Breathing: Practice slow, deep breathing from your diaphragm. Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 counts, hold for 2, exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 counts. This helps regulate your nervous system.
  • Medication Adherence: If prescribed an anxiolytic for acute relief, take it as directed. Understand its onset of action and potential side effects. Do not discontinue prescribed long-term medications without consulting your doctor.
  • Avoidance of Stimulants: Limit or avoid caffeine, nicotine, and excessive sugar, as these can exacerbate anxiety symptoms and trigger panic.
  • When to Seek Emergency Care: Return to the emergency department if symptoms worsen, new severe symptoms develop, or if you experience suicidal ideation or intent.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Caffeine Restriction: Strictly limit or eliminate caffeine intake from coffee, tea, energy drinks, and certain sodas, as it is a known stimulant that can trigger or worsen anxiety and panic symptoms.
  • Alcohol Moderation: Avoid alcohol, especially as a coping mechanism. While it may initially seem to reduce anxiety, it can disrupt sleep and worsen anxiety symptoms during withdrawal or rebound.
  • Nicotine Avoidance: Refrain from smoking or using nicotine products, as nicotine is a stimulant that can increase heart rate and contribute to anxiety.
  • Balanced Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration by drinking water throughout the day. Dehydration can sometimes mimic or exacerbate feelings of lightheadedness or fatigue.
  • Regular, Balanced Meals: Eat regular, balanced meals to prevent significant drops in blood sugar, which can sometimes trigger physical symptoms that mimic anxiety.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Remain Calm and Reassuring: If you are with someone experiencing a panic attack, stay calm. Speak in short, clear, and reassuring sentences. Avoid overwhelming them with questions.
  • Offer a Safe Space: Help the individual find a quiet, safe place away from excessive stimulation.
  • Encourage Breathing: Gently guide them to focus on their breathing. You can breathe with them to model slow, deep breaths.
  • Validate Their Experience: Acknowledge their fear and discomfort without dismissing it. Remind them that the attack will pass and they are safe.
  • Know When to Seek Help: Be aware of their emergency contact information and when to call for medical assistance if symptoms are severe, prolonged, or if they express self-harm ideation.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Treatable Condition: Panic attacks and panic disorder are highly treatable conditions. Early intervention and consistent management significantly improve outcomes.
  • Comprehensive Evaluation: A thorough medical evaluation is crucial to rule out underlying physical conditions that may mimic panic symptoms.
  • Referral to Mental Health Specialists: Follow-up with a psychiatrist or therapist specializing in anxiety disorders is strongly recommended for ongoing treatment, which may include psychotherapy (e.g., CBT) and/or pharmacotherapy.
  • Medication Management: If medication is prescribed, adhere strictly to the dosage and schedule. Discuss any side effects or concerns with your prescribing physician.
  • Prognosis: With appropriate treatment, individuals can learn to manage their symptoms, reduce the frequency and intensity of panic attacks, and improve their quality of life.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education on Coping: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients on immediate coping strategies such as diaphragmatic breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, and grounding techniques.
  • Trigger Identification: Assist patients in identifying potential triggers for their panic attacks and developing avoidance or management strategies.
  • Medication Teaching: Provide clear instructions on prescribed medications, including purpose, dosage, administration, potential side effects, and warning signs for adverse reactions.
  • Safety Assessment: Continuously assess for safety concerns, including suicidal ideation or self-harm risk, especially during and after an acute panic episode.
  • Referral Facilitation: Facilitate timely referrals to mental health professionals (psychiatrists, psychologists, social workers) for ongoing therapy and support.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Diagnosis of Panic Disorder requires recurrent unexpected panic attacks and at least one month of persistent concern about additional panic attacks or their consequences, or a significant maladaptive change in behavior related to the attacks.
  • Pharmacological Interventions: First-line agents often include Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) and Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs) for long-term management. Benzodiazepines may be used for acute, short-term symptom relief.
  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT): A highly effective psychotherapy for panic disorder, focusing on identifying and challenging distorted thoughts, interoceptive exposure, and relaxation techniques.
  • Differential Diagnoses: Important to differentiate panic attacks from cardiac events (e.g., myocardial infarction, arrhythmias), respiratory conditions (e.g., asthma, COPD exacerbation), thyroid dysfunction (hyperthyroidism), pheochromocytoma, and other anxiety disorders.
  • Interoceptive Exposure: A CBT technique where patients intentionally induce physical sensations associated with panic (e.g., hyperventilating to induce dizziness) to habituate to them and reduce fear.
