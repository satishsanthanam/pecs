Post-traumatic stress disorder

🏠 Home / Mental health / Anxiety disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a mental health condition triggered by experiencing or witnessing a terrifying event.
  • Core Symptoms: Characterized by intrusive memories, avoidance of reminders, negative changes in thinking and mood, and altered physical and emotional reactions.
  • Common Triggers: Can develop after combat exposure, natural disasters, serious accidents, physical or sexual assault, or other life-threatening events.
  • Impact: Significantly affects daily functioning, relationships, and overall quality of life if left untreated.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Intrusive Thoughts: Recurrent, involuntary, and distressing memories of the traumatic event, flashbacks, or nightmares.
  • Avoidance Behaviors: Persistent efforts to avoid thoughts, feelings, conversations, places, activities, or people associated with the trauma.
  • Negative Alterations in Cognitions and Mood: Inability to remember key aspects of the trauma, persistent negative beliefs about oneself or the world, distorted blame, diminished interest in activities, feelings of detachment, or inability to experience positive emotions.
  • Alterations in Arousal and Reactivity: Irritability, angry outbursts, reckless or self-destructive behavior, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, problems with concentration, and sleep disturbance.
  • Dissociative Symptoms: May include depersonalization (feeling detached from one's body or mental processes) or derealization (feeling that the world is unreal, dreamlike, distant, or distorted).

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Risk of Self-Harm: Closely monitor for suicidal ideation, plans, or self-injurious behaviors, especially during periods of acute distress or symptom exacerbation.
  • Substance Use: Be vigilant for co-occurring substance use disorders, as individuals may use substances to self-medicate or cope with PTSD symptoms.
  • Comorbidity: Recognize the high comorbidity with other mental health conditions such as major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, and panic disorder, which can complicate treatment.
  • Trigger Identification: Work with individuals to identify and develop strategies for managing personal trauma triggers to prevent symptom escalation.
  • Therapeutic Environment: Ensure a safe, predictable, and supportive therapeutic environment to foster trust and reduce re-traumatization.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Balanced Nutrition: Encourage a diet rich in whole foods, lean proteins, fruits, and vegetables to support overall brain health and mood stability.
  • Omega-3 Fatty Acids: Recommend sources like fatty fish, flaxseeds, and walnuts, which are linked to improved mood regulation and reduced inflammation.
  • Blood Sugar Regulation: Advise consistent meal times and complex carbohydrates to prevent blood sugar fluctuations that can exacerbate anxiety and irritability.
  • Caffeine and Alcohol: Counsel on limiting or avoiding caffeine and alcohol, as they can disrupt sleep, increase anxiety, and interfere with medication efficacy.
  • Hydration: Emphasize adequate water intake, as dehydration can negatively impact cognitive function and mood.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Validate Experiences: Acknowledge and validate the individual's feelings and experiences without judgment, even if they seem irrational.
  • Maintain Predictability: Strive to create a stable and predictable environment to enhance feelings of safety and control.
  • Recognize Triggers: Learn to identify and understand the individual's specific trauma triggers and help them develop coping mechanisms.
  • Encourage Professional Help: Strongly support and encourage adherence to professional mental health treatment plans, including therapy and medication.
  • Practice Self-Care: Attendants and caregivers must prioritize their own mental and emotional well-being to avoid burnout and compassion fatigue.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Intervention: Emphasize the critical importance of early assessment and intervention following trauma exposure to mitigate long-term effects.
  • Evidence-Based Treatments: Recommend psychotherapy modalities such as Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR), and Prolonged Exposure (PE).
  • Pharmacotherapy: Consider selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) as first-line pharmacological treatments for symptom management.
  • Holistic Care: Advocate for a comprehensive treatment plan that integrates psychotherapy, pharmacotherapy, and supportive lifestyle interventions.
  • Ongoing Monitoring: Stress the need for continuous monitoring of symptoms, treatment response, and potential side effects of medications.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Trauma-Informed Care: Integrate principles of trauma-informed care into all patient interactions, recognizing the widespread impact of trauma and promoting safety, trustworthiness, peer support, collaboration, empowerment, and cultural sensitivity.
  • Screening and Assessment: Utilize validated screening tools (e.g., PC-PTSD-5, PCL-5) to identify individuals at risk for or experiencing PTSD symptoms.
  • Psychoeducation: Provide comprehensive education to patients and families regarding PTSD symptoms, treatment options, and coping strategies.
  • Crisis Management: Be prepared to manage acute distress, flashbacks, and suicidal ideation, implementing safety protocols and facilitating immediate access to mental health support.
  • Referral Pathways: Understand and facilitate appropriate referrals to mental health specialists, support groups, and community resources.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • DSM-5 Criteria: Refer to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition, for the official diagnostic criteria for Post-traumatic Stress Disorder.
  • Neurobiological Basis: Involves dysregulation of the fear circuitry, including the amygdala, hippocampus, and prefrontal cortex, leading to exaggerated fear responses and impaired emotional regulation.
  • Pharmacological Agents: Key medications include SSRIs (e.g., Sertraline, Paroxetine) and SNRIs (e.g., Venlafaxine) for core symptoms, and Prazosin for nightmares.
  • Psychotherapeutic Modalities: Core evidence-based therapies include Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT), Prolonged Exposure (PE), Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR), and Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT).
  • Comorbidity Index: Frequently co-occurs with major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, substance use disorders, and dissociative disorders.
