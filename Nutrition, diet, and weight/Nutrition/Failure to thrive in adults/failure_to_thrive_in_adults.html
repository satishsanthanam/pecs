Failure to thrive in adults

🏠 Home / Nutrition, diet, and weight / Nutrition

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Failure to thrive (FTT) in adults is a complex geriatric syndrome characterized by weight loss, decreased appetite, poor nutrition, and inactivity, often accompanied by dehydration, depression, and impaired immune function.
  • Multifactorial Etiology: It is not a specific disease but a syndrome resulting from the interplay of physical, psychological, and social factors.
  • Common in Older Adults: Predominantly affects older adults, leading to significant morbidity and increased mortality.
  • Progressive Decline: Represents a state of progressive functional and cognitive decline, often signaling underlying health issues.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Unintentional Weight Loss: Significant and unexplained loss of body weight over a period of time.
  • Anorexia/Decreased Appetite: Persistent lack of desire to eat or reduced food intake.
  • Dehydration: Signs include dry mucous membranes, decreased skin turgor, and reduced urine output.
  • Fatigue and Weakness: Profound tiredness, lack of energy, and muscle weakness leading to reduced physical activity.
  • Cognitive Impairment: New or worsening confusion, memory problems, or difficulty concentrating.
  • Depression: Symptoms such as sadness, loss of interest, social withdrawal, and feelings of hopelessness.
  • Recurrent Infections: Increased susceptibility to infections due to compromised immune function.
  • Functional Decline: Difficulty performing activities of daily living (ADLs) like bathing, dressing, or eating.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Early Identification: Prompt recognition of subtle changes in weight, appetite, or functional status is critical for timely intervention.
  • Comprehensive Assessment: Conduct a thorough medical, psychological, and social evaluation to identify and address reversible causes.
  • Nutritional Support: Implement strategies to prevent further nutritional decline, including oral supplements, and consider enteral or parenteral nutrition if indicated.
  • Hydration Management: Closely monitor fluid intake and output to prevent and manage dehydration.
  • Medication Review: Regularly assess all medications for polypharmacy, adverse effects, or drug-nutrient interactions that may contribute to FTT.
  • Psychosocial Intervention: Address underlying depression, anxiety, social isolation, and cognitive deficits with appropriate support and therapy.
  • Fall Prevention: Implement measures to reduce fall risk due to weakness, deconditioning, and cognitive impairment.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • High-Calorie, High-Protein Diet: Encourage nutrient-dense foods to maximize caloric and protein intake in small volumes.
  • Frequent Small Meals: Offer 6-8 small, appealing meals and snacks throughout the day, as large meals may be overwhelming.
  • Oral Nutritional Supplements: Utilize commercially available high-calorie, high-protein drinks or puddings between meals.
  • Adequate Hydration: Provide fluids regularly, including water, broths, and diluted juices, to prevent dehydration.
  • Texture Modification: Adapt food textures (e.g., soft, pureed) for individuals with dysphagia or dental issues.
  • Appetite Stimulants: Discuss with the physician the potential use of appetite-stimulating medications if appropriate and indicated.
  • Avoid Empty Calories: Focus on nutrient-rich foods rather than those providing only calories without significant nutritional value.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Regular Weight Monitoring: Weigh the patient consistently (e.g., weekly) to track trends and assess the effectiveness of interventions.
  • Aspiration Precautions: Observe for signs of dysphagia and implement appropriate measures to prevent aspiration during meals.
  • Monitor for Dehydration: Continuously assess for signs of dehydration, such as dry mouth, decreased skin turgor, and concentrated urine.
  • Skin Integrity Assessment: Regularly inspect skin for pressure injuries, especially over bony prominences, due to poor nutrition and immobility.
  • Report Acute Changes: Immediately report any sudden decline in functional status, new symptoms, or refusal to eat or drink.
  • Safe Environment: Ensure the patient's environment is safe, well-lit, and free of obstacles to minimize fall risk.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Multidisciplinary Approach: FTT necessitates a collaborative effort involving physicians, nurses, dietitians, social workers, and physical therapists.
  • Thorough Diagnostic Workup: A comprehensive investigation is crucial to identify and treat any underlying medical conditions contributing to FTT, such as malignancy, chronic organ failure, or endocrine disorders.
  • Individualized Care Plan: Develop a patient-centered care plan that addresses specific needs, preferences, and goals, focusing on improving quality of life.
  • Prognosis Discussion: Engage in open and honest discussions with patients and their families regarding the complex nature, potential outcomes, and realistic expectations for FTT.
  • Palliative Care Integration: Consider integrating palliative care services early, especially for advanced cases, to manage symptoms and support patient and family well-being.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment: Master the skills for conducting a holistic assessment, including functional, cognitive, nutritional, and psychosocial domains.
  • Nutritional Screening Tools: Learn to utilize validated tools such as the Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) or Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST) for early detection.
  • Fluid Balance Monitoring: Accurately track intake and output to assess hydration status and guide fluid management.
  • Medication Reconciliation: Develop expertise in reviewing medication lists for potential adverse effects, interactions, and polypharmacy.
  • Communication Strategies: Practice effective communication techniques with older adults, especially those with cognitive impairments, to build rapport and gather essential information.
  • Family Education and Support: Provide education and emotional support to families, empowering them to participate in care and understand the complexities of FTT.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: R64 (Cachexia), R63.4 (Unintentional weight loss), F32.9 (Major depressive disorder, unspecified), G31.84 (Mild cognitive impairment).
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Clinical diagnosis based on a constellation of symptoms including weight loss (>5% in 6-12 months), decreased appetite, functional decline, and often dehydration/depression.
  • Key Biomarkers: Albumin, prealbumin, C-reactive protein (CRP), complete blood count (CBC), thyroid function tests, electrolytes, vitamin B12, folate.
  • Intervention Strategies: Oral nutritional supplements, enteral nutrition (nasogastric, gastrostomy), parenteral nutrition, appetite stimulants (e.g., megestrol acetate, dronabinol), physical therapy, occupational therapy, psychological counseling.
  • Ethical Considerations: Patient autonomy in refusal of care, informed consent for interventions, and end-of-life discussions regarding goals of care.
