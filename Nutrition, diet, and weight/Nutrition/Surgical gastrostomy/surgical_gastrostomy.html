Surgical gastrostomy

🏠 Home / Nutrition, diet, and weight / Nutrition

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Definition: Surgical gastrostomy involves creating an artificial opening (stoma) into the stomach from the abdominal wall to place a feeding tube.
  • Primary Purpose: To provide long-term enteral nutrition when a patient is unable to safely or adequately consume food orally.
  • Indications: Commonly performed for individuals with severe dysphagia, neurological conditions, head and neck cancers, or critical illnesses requiring nutritional support.
  • Tube Types: Various types of gastrostomy tubes exist, including percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) tubes, surgically placed tubes, and low-profile button devices.
  • Nutritional Support: Enables direct delivery of liquid nutrition, fluids, and medications into the stomach, bypassing the mouth and esophagus.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Dysphagia: Persistent difficulty or inability to swallow safely, leading to high risk of aspiration.
  • Significant Weight Loss: Unintentional and progressive weight loss due to inadequate oral intake and malnutrition.
  • Recurrent Aspiration Pneumonia: Repeated lung infections resulting from food or liquid entering the airway during swallowing.
  • Neurological Impairment: Conditions like stroke, ALS, Parkinson's disease, or severe brain injury affecting swallowing reflexes.
  • Head and Neck Cancer: Obstruction, pain, or surgical alterations that prevent adequate oral nutrition.
  • Prolonged Unconsciousness: Patients in a coma or persistent vegetative state requiring long-term feeding.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Fasting: Strict adherence to NPO (nil per os) guidelines for solids and liquids before surgery to prevent aspiration.
  • Anticoagulant Management: Careful review and temporary discontinuation or bridging of anticoagulant medications to minimize bleeding risk during the procedure.
  • Infection Prophylaxis: Administration of prophylactic antibiotics as per protocol to reduce the risk of surgical site infection.
  • Stoma Site Care: Meticulous post-operative care of the gastrostomy site to prevent infection, skin irritation, and leakage.
  • Tube Dislodgement: Secure the tube properly and educate patient/caregivers on preventing accidental dislodgement and immediate actions if it occurs.
  • Patient Positioning: Maintain an elevated head-of-bed position (at least 30-45 degrees) during and for 30-60 minutes after feeds to prevent reflux and aspiration.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-procedure Fasting: Absolutely no food or drink for at least 6-8 hours prior to the surgical gastrostomy, as instructed by the medical team.
  • Post-operative Initiation: Initial feeds typically begin with sterile water or clear liquids, gradually advancing to prescribed enteral formulas once bowel sounds return and tolerance is established.
  • Formula Selection: Use only physician-prescribed enteral formulas tailored to the patient's specific nutritional needs, caloric requirements, and medical conditions.
  • Feeding Schedule: Adhere strictly to the prescribed bolus or continuous feeding schedule, including rate and volume, to prevent gastric distension or dumping syndrome.
  • Water Flushes: Flush the gastrostomy tube with appropriate amounts of water before and after each feed and medication administration to prevent clogging and maintain hydration.
  • Temperature of Feeds: Administer formulas at room temperature to minimize gastrointestinal discomfort.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Infection: Regularly inspect the gastrostomy site for signs of infection such as redness, swelling, warmth, pain, or purulent drainage.
  • Prevent Tube Obstruction: Ensure thorough flushing of the tube with water before and after feeds and medications to prevent clogging.
  • Manage Leakage: Address any leakage around the stoma promptly to prevent skin excoriation and breakdown; use appropriate dressings.
  • Aspiration Risk: Always ensure the patient's head is elevated during and after feeding to minimize the risk of aspiration.
  • Gastrointestinal Complications: Monitor for nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, or abdominal distension, which may indicate intolerance to feeds or other issues.
  • Electrolyte Imbalance: Be vigilant for signs of electrolyte disturbances, especially in malnourished patients or those experiencing significant fluid shifts.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Careful Patient Selection: Gastrostomy is indicated for long-term nutritional support when oral intake is unsafe or inadequate, not for short-term needs.
  • Multidisciplinary Team: Collaboration with dietitians, speech therapists, and nursing staff is crucial for optimal patient outcomes and complication prevention.
  • Risk-Benefit Assessment: Thoroughly discuss the risks of surgery (e.g., infection, bleeding, tube dislodgement) against the benefits of improved nutrition and quality of life.
  • Long-term Management Plan: Establish a clear plan for tube care, feeding regimen adjustments, and regular follow-up to monitor nutritional status and address complications.
  • Patient and Family Education: Provide comprehensive education on tube care, feeding techniques, medication administration, and recognition of potential complications to empower caregivers.
  • Ethical Considerations: Engage in open discussions regarding the patient's wishes and goals of care, especially in cases of advanced illness.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Educate patients and families on the procedure, expected post-operative course, and the importance of NPO status.
  • Post-operative Assessment: Monitor vital signs, pain levels, abdominal assessment (bowel sounds, distension), and meticulous gastrostomy site assessment for drainage or signs of infection.
  • Feeding Tube Competency: Demonstrate proficiency in administering enteral feeds, flushing the tube, and administering medications safely via the gastrostomy tube.
  • Skin Integrity Management: Implement strategies to prevent peristomal skin breakdown, including proper cleaning, drying, and application of barrier creams or dressings.
  • Patient Teaching: Provide detailed instructions to patients and caregivers on daily tube care, feeding schedules, troubleshooting common issues, and when to seek medical attention.
  • Documentation: Accurately document all aspects of gastrostomy care, including site assessment, feeding volumes, flushes, medication administration, and any observed complications.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • PEG (Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy): A common minimally invasive technique for gastrostomy tube placement using an endoscope.
  • Open Gastrostomy: Surgical creation of a gastrostomy stoma, typically performed when endoscopic placement is contraindicated or unsuccessful.
  • Refeeding Syndrome: A potentially fatal metabolic complication that can occur in severely malnourished patients upon reintroduction of nutrition; requires careful monitoring of electrolytes.
  • Gastric Residual Volume (GRV): Measurement of stomach contents to assess gastric emptying, though routine checking is increasingly debated in current practice guidelines.
  • Stoma Maturation: The process by which the gastrostomy tract heals and becomes established, typically taking 2-4 weeks.
  • Tube Replacement: Procedures for replacing gastrostomy tubes, which may be done by a trained nurse or physician depending on the tube type and stoma maturity.
