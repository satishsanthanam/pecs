Fundoplication – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Acid reflux

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Understanding Your Recovery: Fundoplication surgery strengthens the lower esophageal sphincter to prevent acid reflux. Full recovery typically takes 4-6 weeks, with gradual return to normal activities.
  • Expected Post-Operative Sensations: You may experience mild to moderate pain at the incision sites, a sore throat from the breathing tube, and some abdominal discomfort or bloating due to gas used during surgery.
  • Importance of Adherence: Strictly follow all discharge instructions regarding diet, activity, and medications to ensure proper healing and prevent complications.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Normal Post-Operative Discomfort: Mild incisional pain, shoulder pain (referred pain from gas), sore throat, and a sensation of fullness or difficulty belching are common and usually resolve within days to weeks.
  • Signs Requiring Immediate Medical Attention: Report any fever over 101°F (38.3°C), severe or worsening abdominal pain, persistent nausea or vomiting, difficulty breathing, chest pain, inability to swallow liquids, or signs of wound infection (redness, swelling, pus, warmth).
  • Persistent Dysphagia: While some initial difficulty swallowing (dysphagia) is expected, severe or persistent inability to swallow food or liquids warrants medical evaluation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Incision Care: Keep your surgical incisions clean and dry. Follow specific instructions for showering and dressing changes. Avoid soaking in baths or swimming until cleared by your surgeon.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting (nothing over 10-15 pounds) or strenuous activities for at least 4-6 weeks to prevent strain on the surgical repair. Gradually increase walking and light activities as tolerated.
  • Driving: Do not drive while taking narcotic pain medication or if your reaction time is impaired. Ensure you can safely operate a vehicle, including emergency braking, before driving.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications as directed, including pain relievers, anti-nausea drugs, and any acid-reducing medications if still prescribed.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Gradual Diet Progression: Begin with a clear liquid diet for the first 24-48 hours, then advance to a full liquid diet, followed by a soft, pureed diet for several weeks. Avoid solid foods initially.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Eat small portions frequently throughout the day rather than large meals to prevent discomfort and pressure on the surgical site.
  • Thorough Chewing and Hydration: Chew food very thoroughly and take small bites. Drink plenty of fluids between meals, but avoid drinking large amounts with meals to prevent bloating.
  • Foods to Avoid: Initially, avoid gas-producing foods (e.g., beans, broccoli, carbonated beverages), tough meats, bread, and sticky foods (e.g., peanut butter) that can be difficult to swallow.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency Contact Information: Ensure the patient and caregiver have contact numbers for the surgical team and emergency services readily available.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Caregivers should be vigilant for signs of post-operative complications such as high fever, severe pain, persistent vomiting, difficulty breathing, or changes in mental status.
  • Medication Assistance: Assist the patient with medication schedules and ensure proper dosage and administration, especially for pain management.
  • Activity Support: Help the patient with light activities and ensure they adhere to lifting and activity restrictions to prevent injury or strain.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Follow-Up Appointments: Attend all scheduled post-operative follow-up appointments to monitor your recovery and address any concerns.
  • Long-Term Lifestyle Modifications: Continue to practice healthy eating habits, maintain a healthy weight, and avoid triggers that previously worsened reflux, even after surgery.
  • When to Call Your Doctor: Do not hesitate to contact your surgeon's office if you experience any concerning symptoms, have questions about your recovery, or feel your pain is not adequately controlled.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pain Assessment and Management: Regularly assess the patient's pain level using a validated scale and administer analgesics as prescribed, evaluating their effectiveness.
  • Wound Care Education: Provide clear, step-by-step instructions on incision care, including signs of infection to watch for and when to seek medical attention.
  • Dietary Progression Monitoring: Educate the patient on the importance of gradual diet advancement and monitor their tolerance to different food textures, addressing any dysphagia concerns.
  • Patient Education Reinforcement: Reinforce all discharge instructions, particularly regarding activity restrictions, medication adherence, and recognizing signs of complications.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Fundoplication: A surgical procedure that involves wrapping the upper part of the stomach (fundus) around the lower esophagus to strengthen the lower esophageal sphincter and prevent acid reflux.
  • Nissen Fundoplication: The most common type of fundoplication, involving a 360-degree wrap of the stomach around the esophagus.
  • Gas-Bloat Syndrome: A common post-fundoplication side effect where patients experience difficulty belching or vomiting, leading to abdominal distension and discomfort.
  • Dysphagia: Difficulty swallowing, which can be a temporary post-operative symptom due to swelling or a more persistent issue requiring further evaluation.
  • GERD (Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease): The chronic condition that fundoplication aims to treat, characterized by frequent acid reflux.
