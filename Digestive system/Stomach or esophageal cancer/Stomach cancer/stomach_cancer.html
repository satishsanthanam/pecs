Stomach cancer

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Stomach or esophageal cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Stomach cancer, also known as gastric cancer, is a disease in which malignant cells form in the lining of the stomach.
  • Prevalence: It is one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths worldwide, though incidence varies geographically.
  • Risk Factors: Key risk factors include chronic Helicobacter pylori infection, chronic gastritis, pernicious anemia, smoking, high intake of salted/smoked foods, and a family history of stomach cancer.
  • Early Detection Challenge: Early-stage stomach cancer often presents with non-specific symptoms, making early diagnosis challenging.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Early Symptoms: Often vague and include indigestion, heartburn, mild abdominal discomfort, and a feeling of fullness after small meals.
  • Advanced Symptoms: May include persistent abdominal pain, unexplained weight loss, nausea and vomiting, dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), melena (black, tarry stools), and anemia due to chronic blood loss.
  • Gastric Outlet Obstruction: In later stages, tumors can block the stomach outlet, leading to severe vomiting and inability to eat.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • H. pylori Eradication: Timely diagnosis and treatment of Helicobacter pylori infection can significantly reduce the risk of gastric cancer.
  • Dietary Modifications: Limiting intake of highly salted, smoked, and processed foods, and increasing consumption of fresh fruits and vegetables.
  • Smoking Cessation: Avoiding tobacco products is crucial as smoking is a significant risk factor.
  • Regular Screening: Individuals with high-risk factors or family history may benefit from regular endoscopic surveillance, especially in high-incidence regions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Post-Gastrectomy Diet: After surgical removal of part or all of the stomach, patients require small, frequent meals, avoiding large portions to prevent dumping syndrome.
  • Nutrient Absorption: Focus on nutrient-dense foods and consider vitamin B12 supplementation due to impaired intrinsic factor production.
  • Foods to Limit: Avoid highly fatty, sugary, and spicy foods that can exacerbate symptoms or trigger dumping syndrome.
  • Hydration: Drink fluids between meals rather than with meals to prevent early satiety and aid digestion.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Symptom Monitoring: Patients and caregivers must be vigilant for worsening symptoms such as persistent vomiting, severe pain, or significant weight loss, and report them promptly.
  • Medication Adherence: Strict adherence to prescribed medications, including pain management and antiemetics, is vital for symptom control.
  • Nutritional Support: Monitor for signs of malnutrition and ensure adequate nutritional intake, potentially requiring enteral or parenteral support in advanced cases.
  • Psychological Support: Provide emotional and psychological support, as a cancer diagnosis can be overwhelming for both patients and their families.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Approach: Diagnosis typically involves endoscopy with biopsy, followed by imaging studies (CT, PET scans) for staging.
  • Treatment Modalities: Treatment depends on the stage and location of the cancer and may include surgery (gastrectomy), chemotherapy, radiation therapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: A team approach involving oncologists, surgeons, gastroenterologists, radiologists, and dietitians is essential for optimal patient management.
  • Prognosis: Prognosis is highly dependent on the stage at diagnosis, with earlier detection significantly improving survival rates.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Education: Educate patients on surgical procedures, potential complications, and post-operative care expectations.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Closely monitor vital signs, wound healing, drainage, pain levels, and signs of anastomotic leak or infection.
  • Nutritional Counseling: Provide detailed dietary guidance for post-gastrectomy patients, including managing dumping syndrome and ensuring adequate caloric intake.
  • Symptom Management: Administer medications for pain, nausea, and other symptoms, and educate patients on self-management strategies.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Histopathology: Adenocarcinoma is the most common type of gastric cancer, accounting for over 90% of cases.
  • Staging Systems: TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) staging system is used to classify the extent of the cancer and guide treatment decisions.
  • Molecular Markers: HER2 status, PD-L1 expression, and microsatellite instability (MSI) are important molecular markers guiding targeted and immunotherapeutic approaches.
  • Surgical Techniques: Procedures include subtotal gastrectomy, total gastrectomy, and lymphadenectomy, often performed laparoscopically or robotically.
