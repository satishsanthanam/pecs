Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) in adults

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG): A medical procedure involving the placement of a feeding tube directly into the stomach through the abdominal wall.
  • Primary Purpose: To provide long-term nutritional support, hydration, and medication administration for individuals unable to swallow safely or adequately.
  • Indications for PEG: Commonly performed for patients with functional gastrointestinal tracts but impaired oral intake due to neurological conditions, head and neck cancers, or severe dysphagia.
  • Procedure Overview: Typically performed endoscopically under sedation, involving a small incision in the abdomen to guide and secure the tube into the stomach.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Dysphagia: Persistent difficulty or inability to swallow, leading to a high risk of aspiration pneumonia.
  • Malnutrition or Dehydration: Documented evidence of inadequate oral intake resulting in significant weight loss, nutrient deficiencies, or fluid imbalance.
  • Neurological Impairments: Conditions such as stroke, Parkinson's disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), or severe dementia affecting the swallowing reflex.
  • Head and Neck Malignancies: Tumors or treatment-related side effects (e.g., radiation mucositis) that obstruct the upper digestive tract or cause severe pain during swallowing.
  • Prolonged Unconsciousness: Patients requiring long-term enteral feeding due to extended coma or vegetative states.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-procedure Fasting: Strict adherence to nothing by mouth (NPO) for at least 6-8 hours prior to the procedure to minimize the risk of aspiration during sedation.
  • Anticoagulant Management: Thorough review and potential temporary discontinuation of antiplatelet agents or anticoagulants as per physician's orders to reduce bleeding risk.
  • Prophylactic Antibiotics: Administration of intravenous antibiotics before the procedure is often recommended to decrease the incidence of peristomal infection.
  • Contraindications Assessment: Careful evaluation for absolute contraindications such as severe coagulopathy, peritonitis, gastric outlet obstruction, or significant ascites.
  • Sedation Monitoring: Continuous monitoring of vital signs, oxygen saturation, and respiratory status during and immediately after the procedure due to sedation.
  • Post-procedure Site Care: Meticulous daily cleaning and dressing changes around the stoma site to prevent infection, skin irritation, and leakage.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-procedure Fasting: Absolutely no food, drink, or oral medications for a minimum of 6-8 hours before PEG insertion.
  • Initial Post-procedure Feeding: Feeding typically commences 4-6 hours post-procedure, starting with sterile water flushes, then clear liquids, and gradually advancing to prescribed enteral formula.
  • Formula Administration: Administer prescribed liquid enteral formula slowly via gravity or pump, ensuring the patient is in an upright or semi-Fowler's position to prevent reflux and aspiration.
  • Medication Administration: Only liquid medications or finely crushed pills (if appropriate and approved) mixed with water should be administered through the PEG tube, flushing before and after each dose.
  • Hydration Maintenance: Regular flushing of the PEG tube with water is essential to maintain patency and ensure adequate patient hydration.
  • Oral Intake Restrictions: Depending on the underlying condition, patients may remain NPO or on a highly restricted oral diet, with all primary nutrition provided via the PEG tube.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Stoma Site Vigilance: Attendants must regularly inspect the PEG insertion site for any signs of infection (redness, swelling, warmth, pus), excessive leakage, or skin breakdown.
  • Tube Dislodgement Protocol: In the event of accidental tube dislodgement, do not attempt reinsertion; immediately contact a healthcare professional or emergency services.
  • Blockage Prevention: Always flush the PEG tube with water before and after each feeding and medication administration to prevent lumen occlusion.
  • Emergency Indicators: Seek immediate medical attention for severe abdominal pain, persistent fever, continuous vomiting, significant bleeding from the stoma, or signs of peritonitis.
  • Proper Patient Positioning: Ensure the patient remains in an upright or semi-Fowler's position during all feedings and for at least 30-60 minutes afterward to minimize aspiration risk.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Careful Patient Selection: Thoroughly evaluate the patient's overall prognosis, quality of life, and potential benefits from enteral feeding before recommending PEG placement.
  • Shared Decision-Making: Engage patients and their families in comprehensive discussions regarding the indications, risks, benefits, alternatives, and long-term implications of PEG.
  • Regular Reassessment: Periodically reassess the ongoing need for PEG feeding, especially in patients with progressive diseases, to ensure alignment with their evolving goals of care.
  • Proactive Complication Management: Be vigilant for potential complications such as peristomal infection, leakage, buried bumper syndrome, or tube occlusion, and initiate prompt management strategies.
  • Multidisciplinary Collaboration: Work closely with dietitians, nurses, speech therapists, and other specialists to optimize nutritional support and provide holistic patient care.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Stoma Assessment: Systematically assess the PEG insertion site for signs of infection, skin integrity, leakage, and proper external bumper positioning during every shift.
  • Adherence to Feeding Protocols: Strictly follow prescribed feeding schedules, formula types, and administration rates to prevent complications like refeeding syndrome, diarrhea, or gastric distension.
  • Safe Medication Administration: Master the principles of safe medication delivery via PEG tube, including appropriate crushing techniques, thorough flushing, and awareness of medication incompatibilities.
  • Patient and Caregiver Education: Provide detailed, hands-on education to patients and their caregivers on daily PEG tube care, feeding techniques, complication recognition, and when to seek medical assistance.
  • Troubleshooting Tube Issues: Develop proficiency in identifying and managing common PEG tube problems such as blockages, minor leakage, or skin irritation around the stoma.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Procedure Name: Percutaneous Endoscopic Gastrostomy (PEG) tube insertion.
  • Primary Indications: Severe dysphagia, head and neck malignancy, neurological disorders (e.g., stroke, ALS), prolonged unconsciousness requiring long-term enteral access.
  • Common Complications: Peristomal infection, leakage, tube dislodgement, buried bumper syndrome, aspiration pneumonia, hemorrhage, gastric perforation.
  • Key Equipment: Endoscope, gastrostomy tube kit (e.g., pull-method or push-method), local anesthetic, guidewire, external and internal retention devices.
  • Post-procedure Care Focus: Daily stoma care, flushing protocols, monitoring for complications, initiation and management of enteral nutritional support.
  • Related Terminology: Enteral nutrition, stoma, bolus feeding, continuous feeding, aspiration, gastric lumen, external bumper, internal bumper.
