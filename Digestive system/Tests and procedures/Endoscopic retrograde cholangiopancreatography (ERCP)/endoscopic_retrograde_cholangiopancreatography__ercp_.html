Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) is a specialized endoscopic technique used to diagnose and treat problems in the bile and pancreatic ducts.
  • Diagnostic and Therapeutic: It combines upper gastrointestinal endoscopy with X-ray imaging to visualize and often treat conditions such as gallstones, strictures, leaks, or tumors in these ducts.
  • Common Indications: Often performed for obstructive jaundice, unexplained abdominal pain, or recurrent pancreatitis when other imaging suggests a ductal issue.
  • Sedation Required: Patients typically receive conscious sedation or general anesthesia to ensure comfort and minimize movement during the procedure.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Jaundice: Yellowing of the skin and eyes, often indicating bile duct obstruction.
  • Upper Abdominal Pain: Persistent or severe pain in the upper right quadrant or epigastric region, potentially radiating to the back, suggestive of gallstones or pancreatitis.
  • Dark Urine and Pale Stools: Clinical signs of impaired bile flow from the liver to the intestine.
  • Unexplained Weight Loss: May be a symptom of pancreatic or biliary malignancy.
  • Recurrent Pancreatitis: Especially when a structural cause like a stone or stricture in the ducts is suspected.
  • Cholangitis: Fever, chills, and right upper quadrant pain indicating an infection of the bile ducts.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Medication Review: Inform your physician about all medications, especially blood thinners (anticoagulants like warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel) and insulin, as adjustments may be necessary.
  • Allergy Disclosure: Crucially disclose any allergies to contrast dye, iodine, or sedative medications.
  • NPO Status: Strict adherence to NPO (nothing by mouth) for at least 6-8 hours prior to the procedure to prevent aspiration.
  • Pregnancy Status: ERCP involves X-ray radiation; women of childbearing age must inform their doctor if they are pregnant or suspect they might be.
  • Cardiac/Pulmonary Conditions: Patients with significant heart or lung conditions require thorough pre-procedure evaluation due to sedation risks.
  • Informed Consent: Ensure you fully understand the procedure, potential risks (e.g., pancreatitis, bleeding, perforation), and alternatives before signing the consent form.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Absolutely no food or drink (including water) for at least 6-8 hours before the scheduled ERCP to minimize aspiration risk during sedation.
  • Medication with Sips: If essential medications must be taken, discuss with your doctor; they may be allowed with a minimal sip of water several hours before the procedure.
  • Post-Procedure Reintroduction: Begin with clear liquids once fully awake and gag reflex returns, gradually advancing to a light diet as tolerated, avoiding heavy or fatty foods initially.
  • Hydration: Ensure adequate hydration post-procedure, especially if fasting was prolonged.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Transportation Requirement: The patient will be sedated and unable to drive or operate machinery for at least 24 hours post-procedure; an adult must be present to drive them home.
  • Post-Procedure Observation: Closely monitor the patient for signs of complications such as severe abdominal pain, fever, chills, persistent nausea/vomiting, or dark, tarry stools.
  • Medication Management: Assist the patient in understanding and adhering to any post-procedure medication instructions.
  • Decision-Making Impairment: Be aware that the patient's judgment may be impaired for up to 24 hours; avoid important decision-making during this period.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Risk-Benefit Assessment: ERCP is a powerful tool but carries specific risks, notably post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP), bleeding, and perforation; a careful risk-benefit discussion is paramount.
  • Alternative Diagnostic Modalities: Consider non-invasive alternatives like MRCP (Magnetic Resonance Cholangiopancreatography) or EUS (Endoscopic Ultrasound) for diagnosis before proceeding with therapeutic ERCP.
  • Patient Selection: Meticulous patient selection is crucial, ensuring the procedure is indicated and the patient is medically optimized to minimize complications.
  • Post-Procedure Management: Emphasize close monitoring for complications and clear instructions for follow-up care, including symptom recognition and when to seek immediate medical attention.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-Procedure Assessment: Verify NPO status, allergies, current medications, vital signs, and confirm informed consent is signed and understood.
  • Intra-Procedure Monitoring: Continuously monitor vital signs, oxygen saturation, cardiac rhythm, and level of sedation; assist with patient positioning and equipment management.
  • Post-Procedure Care: Assess for return of gag reflex, pain level, abdominal distension, and signs of bleeding or perforation; monitor for fever and chills indicative of infection.
  • Patient Education: Provide clear discharge instructions regarding diet, activity restrictions, medication schedule, and specific warning signs requiring immediate medical attention.
  • Fluid and Electrolyte Balance: Monitor for potential imbalances, especially if the patient experiences post-procedure nausea or vomiting.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Indications: Choledocholithiasis, cholangitis, biliary strictures (benign/malignant), pancreatic duct strictures, sphincter of Oddi dysfunction, bile leaks, recurrent acute pancreatitis of biliary origin.
  • Contraindications: Unstable patient, recent myocardial infarction, severe coagulopathy (relative), perforated viscus, uncorrectable severe cardiopulmonary disease.
  • Key Complications: Post-ERCP pancreatitis (PEP) (most common), perforation (duodenal or ductal), bleeding (especially after sphincterotomy), cholangitis, cholecystitis, sedation-related adverse events.
  • Therapeutic Interventions: Sphincterotomy, stone extraction (basket/balloon), stent placement (plastic/metal), balloon dilation of strictures, brush cytology for suspected malignancy.
  • Anatomical Focus: Ampulla of Vater, common bile duct, pancreatic duct, duodenum.
