Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) – Discharge instructions

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Tests and procedures

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Sedation Effects: Expect drowsiness, dizziness, and impaired judgment for at least 24 hours due to the sedatives administered during the procedure.
  • Throat Discomfort: A mild sore throat or hoarseness is common and usually resolves within 1-2 days.
  • Gas and Bloating: Air introduced during the ERCP may cause temporary abdominal bloating and gas pains. Walking can help alleviate this.
  • Recovery Period: Plan for a quiet day of rest at home. Avoid making important decisions or signing legal documents.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Severe Abdominal Pain: Worsening or persistent severe abdominal pain, especially in the upper abdomen, could indicate pancreatitis or perforation.
  • Fever and Chills: A temperature above 100.4°F (38°C) accompanied by chills may signal an infection (e.g., cholangitis).
  • Persistent Nausea/Vomiting: Inability to keep fluids down or persistent vomiting beyond the initial recovery period.
  • Dark Urine or Jaundice: New or worsening yellowing of the skin or eyes, or dark urine, may indicate bile duct obstruction or liver issues.
  • Bloody Stool or Black, Tarry Stools: Any signs of gastrointestinal bleeding require immediate medical evaluation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • No Driving/Operating Machinery: Absolutely no driving, operating heavy machinery, or engaging in activities requiring full alertness for 24 hours post-procedure.
  • Avoid Alcohol and Illicit Drugs: Refrain from consuming alcohol or illicit drugs for at least 24 hours, as they can interact with residual sedatives and impair recovery.
  • Medication Adherence: Take all prescribed medications (e.g., pain relievers, antibiotics) exactly as directed. Do not stop without consulting your physician.
  • Strenuous Activity Restriction: Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, or activities that increase abdominal pressure for at least 24-48 hours, or as advised by your doctor.
  • Hydration: Maintain adequate hydration by drinking plenty of fluids unless otherwise instructed.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Initial Fluid Intake: Begin with clear liquids (water, clear broth, apple juice) once fully awake and able to swallow without difficulty.
  • Gradual Food Reintroduction: If clear liquids are tolerated, slowly advance to a light, bland diet (e.g., toast, crackers, plain rice, boiled chicken) over the next 24 hours.
  • Avoid Irritating Foods: For the first few days, avoid fatty, greasy, spicy, or gas-producing foods that can irritate the digestive system.
  • No Alcohol: Strictly avoid alcoholic beverages for at least 24 hours, and preferably longer, to prevent irritation and potential medication interactions.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Patient Supervision: Ensure the patient has a responsible adult to drive them home and stay with them for at least 24 hours post-procedure.
  • Medication Assistance: Help the patient remember and take prescribed medications as directed.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for any signs of complications such as severe pain, fever, vomiting, or changes in consciousness, and know when to contact emergency services.
  • Safe Environment: Ensure the patient is in a safe, quiet environment conducive to rest and recovery, free from hazards.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Instructions: Strict adherence to all discharge instructions is paramount for a safe and uneventful recovery.
  • Prompt Reporting of Symptoms: Do not hesitate to contact your healthcare provider or seek emergency care if any concerning symptoms develop. Early intervention is crucial for managing potential complications.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Ensure you attend all scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor your recovery and discuss further management.
  • Understanding Results: Discuss the ERCP findings and any subsequent treatment plans thoroughly with your physician.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Key Discharge Teaching Points: Emphasize signs and symptoms of post-ERCP pancreatitis, cholangitis, and perforation. Provide clear contact information for emergencies.
  • Sedation Assessment: Educate on the duration of sedative effects and the importance of a responsible adult escort and supervision for 24 hours.
  • Pain Management: Instruct on appropriate use of prescribed analgesics and when to escalate pain concerns.
  • Dietary Progression: Explain the rationale for gradual dietary reintroduction and avoidance of irritating foods.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Post-ERCP Pancreatitis (PEP): The most common serious complication, typically presenting with severe epigastric pain radiating to the back, elevated amylase/lipase. Risk factors include difficult cannulation, sphincterotomy, and history of PEP.
  • Perforation: Rare but serious complication, often presenting with sudden, severe abdominal pain, fever, and signs of peritonitis. Can occur in the duodenum or bile duct.
  • Cholangitis/Sepsis: Infection of the bile ducts, presenting with Charcot's triad (fever, jaundice, right upper quadrant pain). Requires prompt antibiotic therapy.
  • Bleeding: Most common after sphincterotomy, can be immediate or delayed. Signs include melena, hematochezia, or significant drop in hemoglobin.
  • Sedative Reversal Agents: Awareness of flumazenil (for benzodiazepines) and naloxone (for opioids) in case of over-sedation, though rarely needed post-discharge.
