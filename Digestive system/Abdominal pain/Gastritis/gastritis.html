Gastritis

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Abdominal pain

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Gastritis Definition: Gastritis is an inflammation of the stomach lining, which can be acute (sudden onset) or chronic (gradual onset and persistent).
  • Common Causes: Frequently caused by Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection, regular use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), excessive alcohol consumption, stress, autoimmune reactions, or bile reflux.
  • Protective Barrier Compromise: The stomach lining normally has a protective mucous barrier; gastritis occurs when this barrier is weakened, allowing digestive acids to damage the underlying tissue.
  • Potential Complications: Untreated chronic gastritis can lead to stomach ulcers, bleeding, and in rare cases, an increased risk of stomach cancer.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Epigastric Pain: A gnawing, burning, or aching pain in the upper abdomen, often worse or better with eating.
  • Nausea and Vomiting: Feelings of sickness and the expulsion of stomach contents, sometimes containing blood (hematemesis).
  • Bloating and Fullness: A sensation of distension or feeling uncomfortably full after only a small amount of food.
  • Loss of Appetite: Reduced desire to eat, potentially leading to unintentional weight loss.
  • Dark Stools (Melena): Black, tarry stools indicating upper gastrointestinal bleeding, a serious symptom requiring immediate medical attention.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • NSAID Avoidance: Minimize or avoid the use of NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen) if you have a history of gastritis or are at risk, opting for alternative pain relief when possible.
  • Alcohol and Caffeine Restriction: Limit or eliminate alcohol and caffeine intake, as these substances can irritate the stomach lining and exacerbate inflammation.
  • Stress Management: Implement stress-reduction techniques such as mindfulness, meditation, or regular exercise, as stress can worsen gastritis symptoms.
  • Identify Trigger Foods: Pay attention to foods that worsen your symptoms and avoid them; common triggers include spicy foods, acidic foods (citrus, tomatoes), fatty foods, and highly processed items.
  • Prompt Medical Evaluation: Seek immediate medical attention for severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, vomiting blood, or black, tarry stools, as these may indicate complications.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Bland Food Choices: Focus on easily digestible, bland foods such as plain rice, boiled potatoes, lean proteins (chicken, fish), and cooked vegetables.
  • Avoid Irritants: Strictly avoid spicy foods, highly acidic foods (e.g., citrus fruits, tomatoes, vinegar), fried foods, high-fat foods, and carbonated beverages.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Consume smaller meals more frequently throughout the day rather than large, heavy meals to reduce the burden on the stomach.
  • Adequate Hydration: Drink plenty of water, but avoid drinking large amounts during meals, which can dilute digestive enzymes. Herbal teas (e.g., ginger, chamomile) may be soothing.
  • Limit Dairy and Gluten: Some individuals may find relief by temporarily limiting dairy products or gluten-containing foods if they suspect an intolerance contributes to their symptoms.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Medication Adherence: Strictly follow the prescribed medication regimen, which may include proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), H2 blockers, or antibiotics for H. pylori eradication.
  • Monitor for Bleeding: Be vigilant for signs of gastrointestinal bleeding, such as black, tarry stools (melena) or vomiting blood (hematemesis), and report them immediately.
  • Avoid Self-Medication: Do not self-medicate with over-the-counter antacids or other remedies without consulting a healthcare provider, as this can mask symptoms or interfere with prescribed treatments.
  • Regular Follow-up: Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments to monitor treatment effectiveness and assess for any complications or recurrence.
  • Lifestyle Modifications: Understand that lifestyle and dietary changes are crucial components of long-term management and symptom control, not just temporary measures.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Diagnostic Approach: A definitive diagnosis often requires an upper endoscopy with biopsy to visualize the stomach lining and identify the cause, such as H. pylori infection or specific types of inflammation.
  • Treatment Pillars: Treatment typically involves addressing the underlying cause (e.g., H. pylori eradication therapy), reducing stomach acid production with PPIs or H2 blockers, and implementing significant lifestyle and dietary modifications.
  • H. pylori Eradication: For H. pylori-positive gastritis, a combination of antibiotics and acid suppressants is crucial to prevent recurrence and reduce the risk of peptic ulcers and gastric cancer.
  • Long-term Management: Chronic gastritis often requires ongoing management, including dietary adjustments and careful monitoring, especially in cases of autoimmune gastritis or persistent symptoms.
  • When to Refer: Patients with refractory symptoms, signs of bleeding, significant weight loss, or alarm features should be referred for gastroenterology consultation and further investigation.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education on Medications: Educate patients on the purpose, dosage, administration, and potential side effects of prescribed medications (e.g., taking PPIs 30-60 minutes before a meal).
  • Dietary Counseling: Provide detailed dietary guidance, emphasizing foods to avoid and foods to incorporate, and the importance of small, frequent meals.
  • Monitoring for Complications: Regularly assess patients for signs of gastrointestinal bleeding (e.g., vital signs, pallor, melena, hematemesis) and report changes promptly.
  • Pain Assessment and Management: Assess the nature, location, and severity of abdominal pain, and educate patients on non-pharmacological pain relief strategies and when to seek help for worsening pain.
  • Understanding Etiologies: Differentiate between various causes of gastritis (e.g., H. pylori, NSAID-induced, autoimmune) to tailor patient education and care plans effectively.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: K29.0 (Acute gastritis), K29.3 (Chronic superficial gastritis), K29.5 (Unspecified chronic gastritis), K29.7 (Gastritis, unspecified).
  • Common Medications: Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) such as Omeprazole, Pantoprazole, Esomeprazole; H2 Receptor Blockers such as Famotidine, Ranitidine (historical); Antibiotics for H. pylori (e.g., Clarithromycin, Amoxicillin, Metronidazole, Tetracycline, Bismuth subsalicylate).
  • Diagnostic Procedures: Upper Endoscopy (Esophagogastroduodenoscopy - EGD) with biopsy, Urea Breath Test, Stool Antigen Test for H. pylori, Blood tests for H. pylori antibodies (less common for active infection).
  • Pathophysiology: Inflammation of the gastric mucosa, characterized by infiltration of inflammatory cells (neutrophils for acute, lymphocytes/plasma cells for chronic) leading to mucosal damage.
  • Key Risk Factors: H. pylori infection, chronic NSAID use, excessive alcohol, smoking, severe stress, autoimmune diseases (e.g., pernicious anemia), Crohn's disease, bile reflux.
