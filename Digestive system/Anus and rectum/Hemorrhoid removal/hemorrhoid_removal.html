Hemorrhoid removal

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Anus and rectum

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Procedure Overview: Hemorrhoid removal refers to various surgical or non-surgical interventions aimed at treating symptomatic hemorrhoids that have not responded to conservative management.
  • Common Indications: Procedures are typically performed for persistent pain, bleeding, prolapse, or thrombosis of hemorrhoids.
  • Types of Procedures: Options range from minimally invasive office procedures like rubber band ligation or sclerotherapy to surgical hemorrhoidectomy for more severe cases.
  • Goal of Treatment: The primary objective is to alleviate symptoms, improve quality of life, and prevent recurrence of severe hemorrhoidal disease.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Rectal Bleeding: Bright red blood observed during or after bowel movements, often coating the stool or dripping into the toilet bowl.
  • Anal Pain or Discomfort: Aching, throbbing, or sharp pain in the anal region, especially during defecation or when sitting.
  • Pruritus Ani: Persistent itching, irritation, or burning sensation around the anus.
  • Prolapse: A feeling of tissue protruding from the anus, which may require manual reduction or remain permanently outside.
  • Swelling or Lump: A palpable mass or swelling around the anal opening, often associated with external or thrombosed hemorrhoids.
  • Mucus Discharge: Secretion of mucus from the anus, which can lead to skin irritation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-Procedure Medication Review: Inform your physician about all medications, especially anticoagulants (blood thinners) or antiplatelet agents, as these may need to be stopped prior to the procedure.
  • Bowel Preparation: Follow specific instructions for bowel cleansing, which may include enemas or laxatives, to ensure a clean surgical field.
  • Post-Operative Pain Management: Discuss pain relief strategies with your care team, as post-hemorrhoidectomy pain can be significant.
  • Activity Restrictions: Avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise, and prolonged sitting or standing for several weeks post-procedure to prevent complications and aid healing.
  • Hygiene: Maintain meticulous perianal hygiene, especially after bowel movements, using soft wipes or gentle washing to prevent infection.
  • Avoid Straining: Prevent constipation and straining during bowel movements, as this can disrupt the healing site and cause pain.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: Adhere strictly to NPO (nil per os) guidelines, typically involving no food or drink for a specified period before the procedure, if general anesthesia is used.
  • Post-Procedure Hydration: Increase fluid intake significantly to help soften stools and prevent constipation, which is crucial for comfortable bowel movements.
  • High-Fiber Diet: Gradually reintroduce a diet rich in fiber (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) to promote soft, bulky stools and reduce straining.
  • Avoid Irritants: Temporarily restrict foods that may irritate the bowel or cause constipation, such as highly processed foods, excessive caffeine, and very spicy items.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Initially, opt for smaller, more frequent meals to ease digestion and minimize gastrointestinal discomfort.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor for Complications: Watch for signs of excessive bleeding (more than a few drops), fever, severe pain not relieved by medication, difficulty urinating, or pus/foul odor from the surgical site.
  • Medication Adherence: Take prescribed pain relievers, stool softeners, and any other medications exactly as directed to manage discomfort and promote healing.
  • Sitz Baths: Utilize warm sitz baths 2-3 times daily, especially after bowel movements, to soothe the area, reduce swelling, and promote cleanliness.
  • Follow-up Appointments: Attend all scheduled post-operative follow-up appointments to ensure proper healing and address any concerns.
  • Avoid Prolonged Sitting: Use a donut cushion or avoid sitting for extended periods to reduce pressure on the surgical area.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Realistic Expectations: Understand that recovery can be uncomfortable, especially after surgical hemorrhoidectomy, but symptoms will gradually improve.
  • Compliance is Key: Strict adherence to post-operative instructions regarding diet, activity, and wound care is paramount for optimal healing and preventing complications.
  • Long-Term Prevention: Discuss lifestyle modifications such as increased fiber intake, adequate hydration, and regular exercise to prevent recurrence of hemorrhoids.
  • When to Seek Help: Do not hesitate to contact your surgeon immediately for any signs of infection, uncontrolled pain, significant bleeding, or inability to pass urine.
  • Individualized Care: Each patient's recovery journey is unique; follow personalized advice provided by your healthcare team.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Focus: Prioritize teaching on pain management strategies, importance of stool softeners, sitz bath technique, and signs of complications.
  • Pain Assessment: Utilize appropriate pain scales to accurately assess and manage post-operative pain, which can be severe due to the rich nerve supply of the anal region.
  • Urinary Retention Monitoring: Be vigilant for post-operative urinary retention, a common complication, especially after regional anesthesia or significant pain.
  • Bowel Regimen: Educate patients on the critical role of a high-fiber diet and adequate fluid intake to prevent constipation and straining.
  • Wound Care Principles: Instruct on gentle perianal hygiene, avoiding harsh wiping, and the use of soft, absorbent dressings if needed.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Indications for Intervention: Symptomatic internal hemorrhoids (Grade II-IV), thrombosed external hemorrhoids, or mixed hemorrhoids unresponsive to conservative measures.
  • Surgical Techniques: Milligan-Morgan (open) and Ferguson (closed) hemorrhoidectomy, stapled hemorrhoidopexy (PPH), Doppler-guided hemorrhoidal artery ligation (DG-HAL).
  • Non-Surgical Techniques: Rubber band ligation, sclerotherapy, infrared coagulation, cryotherapy.
  • Anesthesia Considerations: Procedures can be performed under local, regional (spinal/epidural), or general anesthesia depending on complexity and patient factors.
  • Potential Complications: Post-operative pain, bleeding, infection, urinary retention, fecal impaction, anal stricture, and rarely, fecal incontinence.
  • Pathophysiology: Hemorrhoids result from the downward displacement and engorgement of the anal cushions, leading to venous dilation and prolapse.
