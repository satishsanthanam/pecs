Enteric (typhoid and paratyphoid) fever

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Food poisoning

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Causative Agents: Enteric fever is caused by specific bacteria, primarily Salmonella enterica serovar Typhi (typhoid fever) and Salmonella enterica serovars Paratyphi A, B, or C (paratyphoid fever).
  • Transmission Route: The infection spreads through the fecal-oral route, typically by consuming food or water contaminated with the feces of an infected person or carrier.
  • Global Impact: It remains a significant public health concern globally, particularly in regions with inadequate sanitation and unsafe drinking water.
  • Clinical Spectrum: While often grouped, typhoid and paratyphoid fevers can present with similar symptoms, though paratyphoid is generally milder.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Sustained High Fever: A hallmark symptom, often gradually increasing over several days, reaching 103-104°F (39-40°C).
  • Headache and Malaise: Persistent frontal headache, generalized body aches, and profound fatigue are common.
  • Abdominal Discomfort: Diffuse abdominal pain, tenderness, and sometimes distension.
  • Gastrointestinal Changes: Early stages may present with constipation, later potentially progressing to 'pea-soup' diarrhea.
  • Rose Spots: Faint, salmon-colored maculopapular rash, typically appearing on the trunk and chest, often transient.
  • Bradycardia Relative to Fever: A pulse rate that is slower than expected for the degree of fever (Faget's sign).
  • Splenomegaly and Hepatomegaly: Enlargement of the spleen and liver may be observed upon physical examination.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Safe Food and Water Consumption: Strictly adhere to 'boil it, cook it, peel it, or forget it' guidelines, especially when traveling to endemic areas. Avoid raw or undercooked foods, unpasteurized dairy, and untreated water.
  • Hand Hygiene: Frequent and thorough handwashing with soap and water, particularly before eating or preparing food and after using the restroom.
  • Vaccination: Consider typhoid vaccination if traveling to high-risk areas or living in endemic regions. Note that vaccines do not cover paratyphoid fever.
  • Avoid Contaminated Sources: Be vigilant about street vendors and food prepared in unhygienic conditions.
  • Carrier Identification: Public health measures include identifying and treating asymptomatic carriers to prevent further spread.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Hydration: Maintain adequate fluid intake with safe, boiled water, oral rehydration solutions, or clear broths to prevent dehydration, especially with diarrhea.
  • Easily Digestible Foods: During acute illness, consume soft, bland, low-fiber foods such as rice porridge, boiled potatoes, well-cooked vegetables, and lean protein sources.
  • Avoid Irritants: Strictly avoid spicy, fatty, fried foods, raw fruits and vegetables (unless peeled by the patient), whole grains, and dairy products if lactose intolerant, as these can exacerbate gastrointestinal symptoms.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Opt for smaller, more frequent meals to ease digestion and nutrient absorption.
  • Post-Recovery Diet: Gradually reintroduce a normal diet, monitoring for any digestive intolerance.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Prompt Medical Attention: Seek immediate medical evaluation for persistent fever, especially after travel to endemic areas, as early diagnosis is crucial for effective treatment.
  • Complete Antibiotic Course: Adhere strictly to the prescribed antibiotic regimen, completing the entire course even if symptoms improve, to prevent relapse and antibiotic resistance.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for signs of severe complications such as intestinal hemorrhage (bloody stools, sudden weakness) or perforation (sudden severe abdominal pain, rigidity), which require emergency medical intervention.
  • Isolation and Hygiene: Implement strict personal hygiene and sanitation measures for infected individuals to prevent transmission to household contacts.
  • Public Health Reporting: Enteric fever is a reportable disease; healthcare providers must notify public health authorities to facilitate contact tracing and outbreak control.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: Clinical suspicion combined with confirmatory laboratory tests (blood, stool, or bone marrow cultures) is vital for timely intervention.
  • Antibiotic Stewardship: Treatment primarily involves appropriate antibiotics, chosen based on local resistance patterns. Resistance to common antibiotics is a growing concern.
  • Supportive Care: Alongside antibiotics, supportive care including fluid and electrolyte management, nutritional support, and fever control is essential.
  • Vaccination for Prevention: Advise at-risk individuals and travelers about available typhoid vaccines, emphasizing that they are not 100% protective and do not cover paratyphoid.
  • Follow-up and Carrier State: Ensure follow-up cultures to confirm eradication, especially in food handlers, to prevent the development of a chronic carrier state.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Assessment: Conduct thorough assessments for fever patterns, abdominal tenderness, rash, and signs of dehydration or complications.
  • Fluid and Electrolyte Management: Monitor intake and output, administer IV fluids as prescribed, and educate patients on oral rehydration.
  • Medication Administration: Administer prescribed antibiotics on schedule and monitor for adverse effects. Educate patients on the importance of adherence.
  • Hygiene and Infection Control: Implement strict enteric precautions, including proper hand hygiene, safe disposal of bodily fluids, and educating patients and families on preventing spread.
  • Nutritional Support: Collaborate with dietitians to provide appropriate, easily digestible meals and monitor nutritional status.
  • Health Education: Educate patients and caregivers on disease transmission, prevention strategies (safe food/water), symptom recognition, and the importance of completing treatment.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Causative Organisms: Salmonella enterica serovar Typhi (Typhoid), Salmonella enterica serovars Paratyphi A, B, C (Paratyphoid).
  • Diagnostic Methods: Blood culture (most reliable in early stages), stool culture, urine culture, bone marrow culture (highest sensitivity), Widal test (limited utility due to low sensitivity and specificity).
  • First-line Antibiotics (Empiric): Fluoroquinolones (e.g., ciprofloxacin), macrolides (e.g., azithromycin), third-generation cephalosporins (e.g., ceftriaxone), depending on local resistance patterns.
  • Mechanism of Action: Bacteria invade the small intestine, enter the bloodstream, and disseminate to organs like the liver, spleen, and bone marrow.
  • Complications: Intestinal hemorrhage, intestinal perforation, encephalopathy, myocarditis, cholecystitis, osteomyelitis.
  • Vaccine Types: Live attenuated oral vaccine (Ty21a) and inactivated injectable polysaccharide vaccine (ViCPS).
