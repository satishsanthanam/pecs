Eosinophilic esophagitis

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Esophagus

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Chronic Immune Condition: Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory disease of the esophagus.
  • Eosinophil Infiltration: Characterized by a significant accumulation of eosinophils, a type of white blood cell, in the lining of the esophagus.
  • Allergy Association: Often triggered by specific food allergens or environmental allergens, leading to inflammation.
  • Progressive Damage: Untreated, chronic inflammation can lead to esophageal dysfunction, strictures, and narrowing.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Dysphagia: Difficulty swallowing, especially solid foods, is a hallmark symptom in adults.
  • Food Impaction: Food getting stuck in the esophagus, requiring emergency medical intervention.
  • Chest Pain: Non-cardiac chest pain, often described as heartburn-like but unresponsive to antacids.
  • Vomiting & Regurgitation: Frequent episodes of vomiting or regurgitation, particularly after meals.
  • Abdominal Pain: More common in children, presenting as recurrent abdominal pain.
  • Growth Failure: In infants and young children, poor weight gain or failure to thrive can be a key indicator.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Avoid Known Triggers: Strictly adhere to dietary restrictions if specific food allergens have been identified through testing or elimination diets.
  • Careful Eating Habits: Chew food thoroughly, take small bites, and drink liquids with meals to minimize the risk of food impaction.
  • Medication Adherence: Consistently take prescribed medications, such as swallowed topical corticosteroids or proton pump inhibitors, as directed by your physician.
  • Monitor for Complications: Be vigilant for worsening dysphagia, recurrent food impaction, or signs of esophageal narrowing, and report them promptly.
  • Regular Follow-up: Maintain scheduled appointments with your gastroenterologist for ongoing monitoring and management of the condition.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Elimination Diets: Common approaches include the 6-food elimination diet (milk, wheat, soy, egg, peanut/tree nuts, fish/shellfish) or 4-food elimination, followed by reintroduction.
  • Elemental Diet: In severe or refractory cases, an elemental diet consisting of amino acid-based formulas may be prescribed to completely remove food antigens.
  • Nutritional Support: Work with a dietitian to ensure adequate nutrient intake, especially during restrictive elimination phases, to prevent deficiencies.
  • Gradual Reintroduction: Reintroduce foods one at a time under medical supervision to identify specific triggers, often with repeat endoscopy and biopsy.
  • Hydration: Maintain good hydration, particularly when experiencing dysphagia, to aid in food passage and prevent dehydration.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Emergency for Food Impaction: Seek immediate medical attention if food becomes lodged in the esophagus and cannot be cleared, as this may require endoscopic removal.
  • Do Not Self-Adjust Medications: Never stop or alter your EoE medications without consulting your healthcare provider, even if symptoms improve.
  • Biopsy is Essential: Diagnosis of EoE requires esophageal biopsies during endoscopy, as visual findings alone are not sufficient.
  • Long-term Management: EoE is a chronic condition that typically requires ongoing management to prevent relapse and complications.
  • Avoid Forceful Swallowing: Do not attempt to force down food that feels stuck, as this can increase the risk of esophageal injury or perforation.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Early Diagnosis is Key: Prompt diagnosis and initiation of treatment are crucial to prevent long-term complications such as esophageal strictures and remodeling.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Effective management often involves a collaborative approach with gastroenterologists, allergists, and registered dietitians.
  • Individualized Treatment Plans: Treatment strategies are tailored to each patient, considering their age, symptoms, endoscopic findings, and identified triggers.
  • Monitoring Treatment Response: Regular endoscopic evaluations with biopsies are necessary to assess the effectiveness of treatment and guide therapeutic adjustments.
  • Patient Education: Empowering patients with a thorough understanding of their condition, dietary restrictions, and medication regimen is vital for successful long-term outcomes.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education on Medication: Instruct patients on the correct technique for administering swallowed topical steroids (e.g., puffing into the mouth and swallowing, or mixing budesonide with a thickener).
  • Dietary Compliance Support: Provide practical advice and resources for adhering to elimination diets, including label reading and meal planning.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Educate patients and caregivers on recognizing and reporting key symptoms like dysphagia, food impaction, and signs of poor nutrition.
  • Pre- and Post-Procedure Care: Prepare patients for endoscopy with biopsy, including NPO status, and monitor for post-procedure complications, especially if esophageal dilation was performed.
  • Nutritional Assessment: Conduct thorough nutritional assessments, particularly in pediatric patients, to identify and address any growth or weight concerns.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Pathophysiology: Characterized by a T-helper 2 (Th2) inflammatory response, often involving IL-5, IL-13, and eotaxin-3, leading to eosinophil recruitment and activation.
  • Diagnostic Criteria: Clinical symptoms of esophageal dysfunction AND ≥15 eosinophils per high-power field (HPF) on esophageal biopsy AND exclusion of other causes of esophageal eosinophilia (e.g., GERD unresponsive to PPIs).
  • Endoscopic Findings: May include esophageal rings (trachealization), linear furrows, white exudates (eosinophilic microabscesses), strictures, and decreased vascularity.
  • Treatment Modalities: Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), swallowed topical corticosteroids (e.g., fluticasone propionate, budesonide slurry), and dietary elimination are first-line therapies.
  • Complications: Potential complications include esophageal strictures, food impaction, and, rarely, esophageal perforation during endoscopy or dilation.
