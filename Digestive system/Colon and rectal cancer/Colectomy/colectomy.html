Colectomy

🏠 Home / Digestive system / Colon and rectal cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Surgical Procedure: Colectomy is the surgical removal of all or part of the colon (large intestine).
  • Primary Indications: Commonly performed for colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis), severe diverticulitis, large polyps, or bowel obstruction.
  • Types of Colectomy: Can range from partial removal (hemicolectomy, segmental colectomy) to total removal (total colectomy, proctocolectomy), depending on the extent of the disease.
  • Ostomy Consideration: May involve creating a temporary or permanent colostomy or ileostomy, where a portion of the intestine is brought through the abdominal wall to divert waste.
  • Surgical Approaches: Can be performed via open surgery (laparotomy) or minimally invasive techniques (laparoscopic or robotic-assisted surgery).

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Rectal Bleeding: Presence of blood in stool, which can be bright red or dark/tarry.
  • Changes in Bowel Habits: Persistent diarrhea, constipation, or alternating patterns, often accompanied by a change in stool consistency or caliber.
  • Abdominal Pain or Cramping: Chronic or severe discomfort in the abdomen, often localized.
  • Unexplained Weight Loss: Significant and unintentional decrease in body weight.
  • Persistent Fatigue: Chronic tiredness often linked to anemia from blood loss.
  • Iron Deficiency Anemia: Low red blood cell count due to chronic blood loss from the gastrointestinal tract.
  • Feeling of Incomplete Evacuation: Sensation that the bowel has not emptied completely after a bowel movement.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Pre-operative Assessment: Thorough medical evaluation including cardiac, pulmonary, and renal function tests to ensure surgical fitness.
  • Medication Review: Discontinue blood thinners (anticoagulants, antiplatelets) as advised by the surgeon, typically 5-7 days prior to surgery, to minimize bleeding risk.
  • Bowel Preparation: Adhere strictly to prescribed bowel cleansing regimen (laxatives, enemas) to clear the colon, reducing infection risk.
  • NPO Status: Maintain nothing by mouth (NPO) status for the specified duration before surgery to prevent aspiration.
  • Infection Prevention: Administer prophylactic antibiotics as directed before incision to reduce surgical site infection risk.
  • DVT Prophylaxis: Implement measures such as compression stockings and subcutaneous heparin to prevent deep vein thrombosis.
  • Post-operative Monitoring: Vigilantly monitor for signs of complications like anastomotic leak (fever, severe abdominal pain, tachycardia), bleeding, or infection.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-operative Clear Liquid Diet: Follow a clear liquid diet for 1-2 days prior to surgery, as instructed, to aid bowel preparation.
  • NPO Before Surgery: Strictly adhere to nothing by mouth (NPO) guidelines for food and drink for several hours before the procedure.
  • Post-operative Gradual Reintroduction: Progress from clear liquids to full liquids, then soft, low-residue foods as tolerated, typically starting within 1-3 days post-surgery.
  • Avoid Gas-Producing Foods: Initially avoid foods known to cause gas (e.g., beans, broccoli, carbonated beverages) to prevent discomfort and distension.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Consume smaller, more frequent meals to aid digestion and reduce the burden on the healing bowel.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake to prevent dehydration, especially important if an ostomy is present.
  • Low-Residue Diet: Follow a low-fiber, low-residue diet for several weeks post-surgery to allow the bowel to heal, gradually reintroducing fiber as advised.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Early Ambulation: Encourage and assist with early and frequent walking post-surgery to prevent complications like DVT and promote bowel function.
  • Pain Management Adherence: Administer prescribed pain medication regularly to ensure comfort and facilitate mobility and deep breathing exercises.
  • Incentive Spirometry: Instruct and encourage regular use of an incentive spirometer to prevent pulmonary complications like atelectasis and pneumonia.
  • Wound Care: Provide meticulous wound care, monitoring for signs of infection (redness, swelling, discharge, increased pain) and ensuring dressing changes as ordered.
  • Stoma Care Education: If an ostomy is created, provide comprehensive education on stoma care, appliance management, and skin protection to the patient and caregivers.
  • Monitor Bowel Function: Closely observe for return of bowel sounds, passage of flatus, and first bowel movement, indicating resolution of post-operative ileus.
  • Discharge Instructions: Ensure the patient and family understand all discharge instructions, including medication schedules, activity restrictions, dietary guidelines, and signs of complications requiring immediate medical attention.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Shared Decision-Making: Engage patients in a comprehensive discussion regarding surgical options, potential risks, benefits, and expected outcomes, aligning with their values and preferences.
  • Multidisciplinary Approach: Emphasize the importance of a collaborative team including surgeons, oncologists, gastroenterologists, stoma nurses, and dietitians for optimal patient care.
  • Realistic Expectations: Counsel patients on the anticipated recovery timeline, potential for temporary or permanent ostomy, and necessary lifestyle adjustments post-colectomy.
  • Oncologic Principles: For cancer cases, ensure adherence to oncologic principles, including adequate margin assessment and lymph node dissection, to optimize long-term survival.
  • Post-operative Surveillance: Stress the critical need for long-term follow-up, including imaging and colonoscopies, to monitor for disease recurrence or new pathology, especially in cancer or IBD patients.
  • ERAS Protocols: Advocate for the implementation of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols to accelerate recovery, reduce complications, and shorten hospital stays.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Pre-operative Teaching: Educate patients on the surgical process, pain management strategies, importance of early ambulation, and use of incentive spirometry.
  • Post-operative Assessment: Conduct frequent assessments of vital signs, pain level, abdominal distension, bowel sounds, wound integrity, and urine output.
  • Fluid and Electrolyte Balance: Monitor intravenous fluid administration and electrolyte levels, especially in patients with high ostomy output or prolonged ileus.
  • Stoma Site Care: Provide meticulous care for new stomas, including assessing viability, managing effluent, and preventing skin irritation.
  • Patient Education for Discharge: Reinforce dietary guidelines, activity restrictions, medication regimen, wound care, and signs/symptoms to report to the healthcare provider.
  • Psychosocial Support: Address patient concerns regarding body image changes, particularly with ostomy creation, and provide resources for support groups.
  • Pain Management: Administer analgesia as prescribed, utilizing a multimodal approach, and assess effectiveness regularly.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: Relevant codes include C18 (Malignant neoplasm of colon), K51 (Ulcerative colitis), K50 (Crohn's disease), K57 (Diverticular disease of intestine).
  • CPT Codes: Common codes include 44140 (Colectomy, partial; with anastomosis), 44150 (Colectomy, total, abdominal, without proctectomy), 44160 (Colectomy, partial; with removal of terminal ileum and ileocolostomy).
  • Anastomotic Techniques: Surgical rejoining of bowel segments can be hand-sewn or stapled, with various configurations (end-to-end, side-to-side, end-to-side).
  • Surgical Approaches: Open colectomy, laparoscopic colectomy, robotic-assisted colectomy, each with distinct advantages and recovery profiles.
  • Pathology Reporting: Critical for cancer staging (TNM classification), margin assessment, lymph node yield, and identification of specific tumor characteristics.
  • Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS): A multimodal perioperative care pathway designed to achieve early recovery for patients undergoing major surgery.
  • Potential Complications: Anastomotic leak, surgical site infection, ileus, bowel obstruction, bleeding, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, stoma complications (if applicable).
