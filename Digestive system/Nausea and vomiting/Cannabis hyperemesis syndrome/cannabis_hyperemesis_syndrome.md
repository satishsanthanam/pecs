# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Definition:** Cannabis Hyperemesis Syndrome (CHS) is a condition characterized by recurrent episodes of severe nausea, intractable vomiting, and abdominal pain in chronic, heavy cannabis users.
* **Key Feature:** Symptoms are often temporarily relieved by hot showers or baths.
* **Causation:** Directly linked to long-term, frequent cannabis use, including THC, CBD, and synthetic cannabinoids.
* **Resolution:** Complete and sustained cessation of all cannabis products is the only definitive cure for CHS.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Prodromal Phase:** Early morning nausea, abdominal discomfort, and a fear of vomiting.
* **Hyperemetic Phase:** Intense, persistent nausea, intractable vomiting (often multiple times per hour), diffuse abdominal pain, dehydration, and significant weight loss.
* **Compulsive Hot Bathing:** A characteristic behavior where individuals seek prolonged hot showers or baths for temporary symptom relief.
* **Recovery Phase:** Resolution of symptoms typically occurs within days to weeks after complete cannabis cessation.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Cannabis Abstinence:** Emphasize the absolute necessity of avoiding all forms of cannabis to prevent recurrence and achieve symptom resolution.
* **Hydration Monitoring:** Closely monitor for signs of dehydration (e.g., dry mucous membranes, decreased urine output, dizziness) and administer intravenous fluids as needed during acute episodes.
* **Electrolyte Management:** Be vigilant for electrolyte imbalances, particularly hypokalemia and hyponatremia, which can result from prolonged vomiting.
* **Avoid Triggers:** Advise patients to identify and avoid other potential triggers such as stress, certain foods (e.g., caffeine, chocolate, spicy foods), or alcohol that may exacerbate symptoms.
* **Medication Caution:** Use caution with opioid analgesics, as they can sometimes worsen nausea or contribute to rebound hyperalgesia; consider alternative pain management strategies.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Acute Phase:** Maintain NPO (nil per os) or allow only sips of clear liquids if tolerated to rest the gastrointestinal tract and prevent further vomiting.
* **Gradual Reintroduction:** Once vomiting subsides, slowly reintroduce bland, easily digestible foods in small, frequent portions (e.g., crackers, toast, rice, bananas, applesauce).
* **Avoid Irritants:** Restrict spicy, fatty, acidic, highly processed, or gas-producing foods that can trigger nausea or gastric irritation.
* **Hydration Focus:** Encourage oral rehydration with electrolyte-rich fluids (e.g., oral rehydration solutions, diluted fruit juices) once the patient can tolerate liquids.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Emergency Care:** Seek immediate medical attention for severe, intractable vomiting, signs of severe dehydration (e.g., altered mental status, syncope), or persistent abdominal pain.
* **Misdiagnosis Risk:** Be aware that CHS is frequently misdiagnosed as cyclic vomiting syndrome, gastroparesis, or other GI disorders; a thorough cannabis use history is paramount.
* **Withdrawal Symptoms:** Patients attempting cannabis cessation may experience withdrawal symptoms; provide appropriate support and management strategies.
* **Psychological Support:** Offer counseling and support for cannabis cessation, as psychological dependence is common and can hinder recovery.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Diagnostic Challenge:** Diagnosis of CHS is primarily clinical, relying on a detailed history of chronic cannabis use, characteristic symptoms, and resolution upon cessation, often after ruling out other causes.
* **Acute Management:** Focus on aggressive hydration with intravenous fluids, antiemetics (though often less effective than in other conditions), and sedatives (e.g., benzodiazepines) for agitation. Topical capsaicin cream may offer some relief.
* **Patient Education:** Crucially educate patients about the direct causal link between cannabis use and their symptoms, as many are unaware or resistant to this connection.
* **Long-term Strategy:** Emphasize that sustained cannabis abstinence is the only definitive treatment and requires ongoing support and follow-up.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Assessment Priority:** Conduct a comprehensive assessment of hydration status, electrolyte balance, pain level, and a non-judgmental history of cannabis use.
* **Intervention Focus:** Administer prescribed antiemetics and IV fluids. Facilitate access to hot showers/baths for symptom relief as tolerated and safe.
* **Patient Teaching:** Educate patients on the importance of cannabis cessation, dietary modifications, and recognizing signs of dehydration or electrolyte imbalance.
* **Empathy & Support:** Provide empathetic care, acknowledging the severe distress caused by intractable vomiting and addressing potential stigma associated with cannabis use.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Pathophysiology:** Proposed mechanisms include cannabinoid receptor dysregulation in the central and enteric nervous systems, affecting thermoregulation, gastric motility, and visceral pain perception.
* **Receptors Involved:** Primarily involves CB1 receptors, with potential roles for CB2 and TRPV1 receptors.
* **Differential Diagnosis:** Includes cyclic vomiting syndrome, gastroparesis, acute pancreatitis, peptic ulcer disease, gastroenteritis, and other causes of recurrent vomiting.
* **Pharmacological Interventions (Acute):** Haloperidol and benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam) have shown more efficacy than traditional antiemetics (e.g., ondansetron, metoclopramide) in acute CHS episodes. Topical capsaicin cream can be applied to the abdomen.
