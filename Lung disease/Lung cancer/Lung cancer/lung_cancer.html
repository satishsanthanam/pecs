Lung cancer

🏠 Home / Lung disease / Lung cancer

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Lung cancer is a malignant tumor characterized by uncontrolled cell growth in the lung tissues.
  • Primary Types: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounts for about 85% of cases, and small cell lung cancer (SCLC), which is more aggressive.
  • Major Risk Factors: Smoking (including secondhand smoke), exposure to radon gas, asbestos, certain chemicals, air pollution, and a family history of lung cancer.
  • Prognosis: Highly dependent on the stage at diagnosis, type of cancer, and overall patient health. Early detection significantly improves outcomes.
  • Prevention: The most effective prevention is avoiding all tobacco products and minimizing exposure to environmental carcinogens.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Cough: A new cough that does not go away or a chronic cough that worsens over time.
  • Hemoptysis: Coughing up blood or rust-colored sputum.
  • Dyspnea: Shortness of breath, especially during routine activities or exertion.
  • Chest Pain: Persistent chest pain that may worsen with deep breathing, coughing, or laughing.
  • Unexplained Weight Loss: Significant and unintentional loss of body weight.
  • Fatigue: Profound and persistent tiredness that is not relieved by rest.
  • Hoarseness: A change in voice that lasts for several weeks.
  • Recurrent Infections: Frequent bouts of pneumonia or bronchitis that do not resolve.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Smoking Cessation: Immediately cease all forms of tobacco use, including cigarettes, cigars, and vaping products.
  • Avoid Secondhand Smoke: Minimize exposure to environments where others are smoking.
  • Radon Testing: Test homes for radon gas, especially in basements, and mitigate if levels are high.
  • Occupational Safety: Use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) if working with known carcinogens like asbestos or silica.
  • Screening Eligibility: Discuss low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) screening with a physician if you meet high-risk criteria (e.g., current or former heavy smoker).
  • Symptom Awareness: Promptly report any new or worsening respiratory symptoms to a healthcare provider for evaluation.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Nutrient-Dense Diet: Focus on a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains to support overall health and immune function during treatment.
  • Adequate Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake, especially during chemotherapy or radiation, to help manage side effects like dry mouth or constipation.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: May help manage nausea, early satiety, or loss of appetite often associated with cancer treatments.
  • Soft, Bland Foods: Recommended if experiencing mouth sores (mucositis) or difficulty swallowing (dysphagia).
  • Avoid Irritants: Limit very spicy, acidic, or extremely hot/cold foods if oral or esophageal irritation is present.
  • Protein Intake: Ensure sufficient protein intake to help maintain muscle mass and support tissue repair.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Early Diagnosis: Lung cancer symptoms can be subtle; early and accurate diagnosis is paramount for effective treatment.
  • Treatment Adherence: Strictly follow the prescribed treatment regimen, which may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy.
  • Side Effect Management: Communicate all treatment-related side effects to the healthcare team for prompt intervention and symptom management.
  • Psychosocial Support: Seek support from family, friends, support groups, or mental health professionals to cope with the emotional challenges of a cancer diagnosis.
  • Regular Follow-up: Adhere to all scheduled follow-up appointments and surveillance imaging to monitor for recurrence or new primary cancers.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Risk Assessment: Thoroughly assess patient risk factors, particularly smoking history, to determine eligibility for screening and guide preventative counseling.
  • Diagnostic Pathway: Utilize a combination of imaging (CT, PET), bronchoscopy, biopsy, and molecular testing for precise diagnosis and staging.
  • Multidisciplinary Care: Collaborate with a team of specialists including pulmonologists, oncologists, radiation oncologists, thoracic surgeons, and palliative care providers.
  • Personalized Treatment: Tailor treatment plans based on the specific type and stage of lung cancer, molecular markers, and individual patient health status.
  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education on the disease, treatment options, potential side effects, and the critical importance of smoking cessation.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Respiratory Assessment: Master comprehensive respiratory assessment skills, including auscultation, percussion, and recognizing signs of respiratory distress.
  • Symptom Monitoring: Closely monitor patients for changes in cough, dyspnea, pain, and signs of infection or treatment-related complications.
  • Chemotherapy/Radiation Care: Understand safe administration, patient education, and management of side effects for oncology treatments.
  • Pain Management: Implement effective pain assessment and management strategies, including pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions.
  • Palliative Care: Recognize the role of palliative care in improving quality of life for patients with lung cancer at all stages of the disease.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on disease progression, treatment goals, self-care strategies, and available support resources.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • ICD-10 Codes: C34.9 (Malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung, unspecified), with specific codes for different anatomical sites and histological types.
  • Diagnostic Modalities: Low-dose CT (LDCT) screening, chest X-ray, CT scan, PET scan, MRI, bronchoscopy with biopsy, fine-needle aspiration (FNA), mediastinoscopy.
  • Staging Systems: TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) staging for NSCLC; limited stage vs. extensive stage for SCLC.
  • Treatment Modalities: Surgery (lobectomy, pneumonectomy, segmentectomy), radiation therapy (external beam, SBRT), chemotherapy, targeted therapy (e.g., EGFR, ALK, ROS1 inhibitors), immunotherapy (e.g., PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors).
  • Biomarkers: Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) mutations, Anaplastic Lymphoma Kinase (ALK) rearrangements, ROS1 rearrangements, BRAF mutations, PD-L1 expression.
