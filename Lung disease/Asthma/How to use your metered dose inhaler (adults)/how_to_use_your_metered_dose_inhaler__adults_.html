How to use your metered dose inhaler (adults)

🏠 Home / Lung disease / Asthma

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose of MDI: Metered Dose Inhalers deliver medication directly into your lungs to manage respiratory conditions like asthma.
  • Reliever vs. Preventer: Understand if your inhaler is a quick-relief medication (for immediate symptom relief) or a daily preventer (for long-term control).
  • Correct Technique is Key: Proper technique ensures the medication reaches your airways effectively, maximizing its benefit and minimizing side effects.
  • Spacer Device: A spacer is often recommended, especially for steroid inhalers, to improve drug delivery to the lungs and reduce medication deposition in the mouth and throat.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Wheezing: A whistling sound during breathing, often indicating narrowed airways.
  • Shortness of Breath: Difficulty breathing or feeling like you can't get enough air.
  • Chest Tightness: A feeling of pressure or constriction in the chest.
  • Persistent Cough: Especially a cough that is worse at night or with exercise, which can be a sign of airway inflammation.
  • Asthma Exacerbation: Acute worsening of these symptoms, requiring prompt use of a reliever inhaler.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Shake Before Use: Always shake your MDI vigorously for 5 seconds before each puff to ensure the medication is evenly mixed.
  • Prime Your Inhaler: If your inhaler is new or hasn't been used for several days, prime it by spraying a few puffs into the air away from your face.
  • Exhale Fully: Before taking a puff, breathe out completely to empty your lungs as much as comfortable.
  • Slow, Deep Inhalation: When activating the inhaler, breathe in slowly and deeply through your mouth to draw the medication into your lungs.
  • Hold Your Breath: After inhaling, hold your breath for 5-10 seconds (or as long as comfortable) to allow the medication to settle in your airways.
  • Wait Between Puffs: If multiple puffs are prescribed, wait at least 30-60 seconds between each puff to allow the first dose to penetrate deeper.
  • Rinse Mouth (Steroid Inhalers): Always rinse your mouth thoroughly with water and spit it out after using a corticosteroid inhaler to prevent oral thrush.
  • Clean Your Inhaler: Regularly clean the plastic casing and mouthpiece of your inhaler as per manufacturer instructions.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Post-Inhaler Oral Hygiene: After using a steroid-containing MDI, rinse your mouth with water and spit to minimize the risk of oral candidiasis (thrush). Do not swallow the rinse water.
  • Hydration for Mucus Clearance: While not a direct MDI instruction, maintaining good hydration can help thin respiratory secretions, potentially aiding in clearer airways.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Monitor Inhaler Counter: Keep track of the dose counter on your inhaler to ensure you have enough medication, especially for preventer inhalers.
  • Emergency Situations: If your reliever inhaler does not alleviate severe shortness of breath, wheezing, or chest tightness, seek immediate medical attention.
  • Avoid Overuse of Relievers: Frequent use of your reliever inhaler (more than twice a week, excluding exercise-induced symptoms) indicates poorly controlled asthma and requires a medical review.
  • Potential Side Effects: Be aware of potential side effects such as tremor, increased heart rate (from relievers), or hoarseness and oral thrush (from steroid preventers). Report persistent or severe side effects to your healthcare provider.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Adherence to Treatment Plan: Consistent and correct use of your prescribed inhalers, especially preventers, is crucial for effective asthma control and preventing exacerbations.
  • Personalized Asthma Action Plan: Work with your doctor to develop and understand your individualized asthma action plan, which guides when and how to adjust your medication based on symptoms.
  • Regular Technique Review: Schedule regular appointments to have your inhaler technique checked by a healthcare professional to ensure optimal medication delivery.
  • Spacer Recommendation: For most adult patients, especially those using corticosteroid inhalers, the use of a spacer device is strongly recommended to enhance lung deposition and reduce local side effects.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education Priority: Nurses play a vital role in educating patients on the correct MDI technique, including demonstration and return demonstration.
  • Assessment of Technique: Systematically assess patient's ability to use their MDI, identifying common errors such as poor coordination, insufficient breath-hold, or incorrect inhalation speed.
  • Spacer Benefits Explanation: Educate patients on how spacers improve drug delivery, reduce oropharyngeal deposition, and minimize systemic absorption of corticosteroids.
  • Reinforce Adherence: Emphasize the importance of consistent preventer inhaler use and the dangers of reliever inhaler overuse as indicators of uncontrolled asthma.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Aerosol Delivery: MDIs deliver medication as a fine aerosol mist, requiring precise coordination between actuation and inhalation for optimal lung deposition.
  • Pharmacodynamics: Medications like beta-agonists (e.g., salbutamol/albuterol) act on beta-2 adrenergic receptors in the bronchial smooth muscle, causing bronchodilation. Corticosteroids (e.g., fluticasone, budesonide) reduce airway inflammation.
  • Particle Size: The optimal particle size for deep lung penetration is typically 1-5 micrometers. Larger particles tend to deposit in the oropharynx.
  • Inspiratory Flow Rate: A slow, deep inspiratory flow rate (approximately 30-60 L/min) is generally recommended for MDIs to maximize drug delivery to the lower airways.
  • Breath-Hold Duration: A breath-hold of 5-10 seconds post-inhalation allows for gravitational sedimentation of aerosol particles, enhancing drug deposition in the lungs.
