# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Procedure Overview:** Angiography is an invasive imaging technique that uses X-rays and a special contrast dye to visualize blood vessels, particularly in the lungs for suspected pulmonary embolism (PE).
* **Purpose:** It is considered the gold standard for definitively diagnosing pulmonary embolism by identifying blockages or abnormalities within the pulmonary arteries.
* **Method:** A thin, flexible catheter is inserted, typically into a vein in the groin (femoral vein), and carefully guided through the circulatory system to the pulmonary arteries.
* **Contrast Injection:** Once positioned, contrast dye is injected, and a series of X-ray images are taken to highlight the blood flow and detect any obstructions.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Sudden Dyspnea:** Acute onset of shortness of breath, often without exertion.
* **Pleuritic Chest Pain:** Sharp, stabbing chest pain that worsens with deep breaths or coughing.
* **Tachycardia:** An abnormally rapid heart rate, often a compensatory mechanism.
* **Hemoptysis:** Coughing up blood or blood-streaked sputum.
* **Syncope or Presyncope:** Fainting or feeling lightheaded and on the verge of fainting.
* **Unilateral Leg Swelling/Pain:** Symptoms indicative of Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT), which is a common source of pulmonary emboli.
* **Hypoxia:** Low blood oxygen saturation levels, often detected by pulse oximetry.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Contrast Allergy History:** Thoroughly assess for any prior allergic reactions to iodine-based contrast dyes; pre-medication may be necessary.
* **Renal Impairment:** Evaluate kidney function (creatinine, GFR) as contrast dye can be nephrotoxic; adequate hydration is crucial.
* **Anticoagulant Therapy:** Patients on blood thinners may have an increased risk of bleeding at the catheter insertion site; medication management plans must be established.
* **Pregnancy Status:** Angiography involves radiation exposure; alternative imaging modalities or strict shielding protocols are necessary for pregnant patients.
* **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Monitor for and manage potential cardiac rhythm disturbances that can occur during catheter manipulation.
* **Diabetes Mellitus:** Special consideration for patients on metformin, as it may need to be held before and after the procedure to prevent lactic acidosis in the context of renal impairment.
* **Hydration Status:** Ensure the patient is well-hydrated before the procedure to help protect kidney function.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **Pre-Procedure Fasting:** Patients are typically required to be NPO (nil per os) for 4-6 hours prior to the procedure to minimize the risk of aspiration, especially if sedation is administered.
* **Post-Procedure Hydration:** Encourage ample oral fluid intake after the procedure, unless medically contraindicated, to aid in flushing the contrast dye from the kidneys.
* **Medication Adjustments:** Discuss with the physician regarding the timing of insulin or oral hypoglycemic medications if fasting is required.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **Informed Consent:** Ensure the patient and/or their legal guardian fully understands the procedure, potential risks, benefits, and alternative options before obtaining signed consent.
* **Continuous Monitoring:** Maintain vigilant monitoring of vital signs, cardiac rhythm, and oxygen saturation throughout the procedure and during the recovery period.
* **Access Site Care:** Apply firm pressure to the catheter insertion site immediately post-procedure and meticulously observe for signs of bleeding, hematoma formation, or pseudoaneurysm.
* **Allergic Reaction Preparedness:** Have emergency medications and equipment readily available to manage potential anaphylactic reactions to contrast dye.
* **Post-Procedure Mobility Restrictions:** Instruct the patient to keep the limb used for vascular access straight and immobile for several hours to prevent complications at the insertion site.
* **Pain Assessment:** Regularly assess the patient for any discomfort or pain and administer analgesia as prescribed.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Definitive Diagnosis:** Pulmonary angiography provides the most definitive diagnosis of pulmonary embolism, particularly in cases where CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA) is inconclusive or contraindicated.
* **Risk-Benefit Analysis:** Carefully weigh the diagnostic accuracy against the inherent risks of an invasive procedure, radiation exposure, and potential contrast-induced nephropathy.
* **Alternative Modalities:** Consider CTPA or Ventilation-Perfusion (V/Q) scans as less invasive first-line diagnostic tools in appropriate clinical settings.
* **Treatment Guidance:** The precise localization and extent of emboli identified by angiography directly inform critical treatment decisions, including anticoagulation, thrombolysis, or embolectomy.
* **Patient Selection:** Meticulous patient selection is paramount, considering clinical probability, comorbidities, renal function, and overall patient stability.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Pre-Procedure Assessment:** Conduct a comprehensive assessment including allergy history, NPO status, baseline vital signs, renal function tests (BUN, creatinine), and coagulation studies (PT/INR, PTT).
* **Patient Education:** Provide clear explanations of the procedure, including expected sensations (e.g., warm flush from contrast), and detailed post-procedure care instructions.
* **Post-Procedure Monitoring:** Closely monitor the access site for bleeding, hematoma, and distal pulses. Assess for neurological changes and signs of delayed allergic reactions.
* **Fluid Management:** Administer intravenous fluids as ordered and encourage oral fluid intake post-procedure to support renal clearance of contrast.
* **Medication Reconciliation:** Ensure accurate restart of all home medications, especially anticoagulants, following physician orders.
* **Documentation:** Maintain meticulous and accurate documentation of all assessments, interventions, patient responses, and any complications.


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **Seldinger Technique:** The standard method used for percutaneous vascular access during angiography.
* **Contrast-Induced Nephropathy (CIN):** A potential complication characterized by acute kidney injury following exposure to iodinated contrast media.
* **Radiation Dose:** The amount of ionizing radiation absorbed by the patient during the procedure, a factor in cumulative lifetime exposure.
* **Catheterization Risks:** Potential complications include cardiac arrhythmias, vessel dissection, perforation, and air embolism.
* **Pulmonary Embolism (PE):** A blockage in one of the pulmonary arteries in the lungs, most often caused by blood clots that travel from the legs (DVT).
* **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT):** A blood clot that forms in a deep vein, typically in the leg or pelvis, which can dislodge and travel to the lungs.
* **D-dimer:** A blood test that measures a protein fragment produced when a blood clot dissolves; elevated levels can indicate the presence of a clot but are not specific to PE.
