# 🩺 Medical Content

## 💡 What You Need to Know
* **Purpose of Screening**: Tuberculosis (TB) screening tests are crucial for identifying individuals with latent TB infection (LTBI) or active TB disease, even before symptoms appear.
* **Target Populations**: Screening is particularly recommended for close contacts of active TB cases, healthcare workers, immunocompromised individuals, and those born in or who have traveled to high TB prevalence countries.
* **Primary Tests Available**: The two main types of TB screening tests are the Tuberculin Skin Test (TST), also known as the Mantoux test, and Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRAs), such as QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus or T-SPOT.TB.
* **Importance of Early Detection**: Early detection allows for timely treatment of LTBI to prevent progression to active disease, and prompt treatment of active TB to prevent further transmission.


## 🤒 Associated Symptoms
* **Persistent Cough**: A cough lasting more than three weeks, which may or may not produce sputum.
* **Unexplained Fever and Chills**: Often low-grade fever, particularly in the afternoon or evening, accompanied by chills.
* **Night Sweats**: Profuse sweating during sleep, often soaking clothes and bedding.
* **Unintentional Weight Loss**: Significant loss of body weight without changes in diet or exercise.
* **Fatigue and Malaise**: Persistent tiredness, weakness, and a general feeling of being unwell.
* **Loss of Appetite**: A noticeable decrease in desire to eat.
* **Hemoptysis**: Coughing up blood or blood-streaked sputum, indicating more advanced disease.


## 🛡️ Crucial Precautions
* **Prior BCG Vaccination**: Individuals who have received the Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine may have a false-positive TST result; IGRAs are generally preferred in these cases.
* **Immunosuppression**: Patients with weakened immune systems (e.g., HIV, organ transplant recipients, those on immunosuppressive medications) may have a false-negative TST due to anergy; IGRAs may be more reliable.
* **Recent Live-Virus Vaccination**: TST should be delayed for 4-6 weeks after administration of live-virus vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella) to avoid a false-negative reaction.
* **Proper TST Administration and Reading**: The TST must be administered by trained personnel using the correct intradermal technique and read accurately within 48-72 hours by a healthcare professional.
* **Two-Step TST**: For individuals who will be periodically retested (e.g., healthcare workers), a two-step TST may be recommended to establish a baseline and avoid a 'booster phenomenon'.


## 🍽️ Dietary Directions & Restrictions
* **No Fasting Required**: Neither the Tuberculin Skin Test (TST) nor Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRAs) require any specific dietary restrictions or fasting prior to their administration.
* **Normal Hydration**: Patients should maintain their usual fluid intake unless otherwise advised for other concurrent medical conditions or procedures.
* **Medication Adherence**: Continue all prescribed medications as usual, unless specifically instructed by a healthcare provider.
* **General Well-being**: While not a restriction, maintaining a balanced diet and good hydration supports overall health, which can be beneficial for immune response, though not directly impacting test accuracy.


## ⚠️ Attendant Guidelines
* **TST Reading Appointment**: It is critical to return to the clinic within 48 to 72 hours after the TST injection for the test site to be accurately read by a healthcare professional. Failure to do so will require retesting.
* **Avoid Manipulation of TST Site**: Do not scratch, rub, or apply creams, ointments, or bandages to the TST injection site, as this can interfere with the test result.
* **IGRA Blood Draw Procedure**: For IGRA tests, a standard blood draw will be performed, similar to other routine blood tests. Minimal discomfort is expected.
* **Positive Result Follow-up**: A positive screening test (TST or IGRA) does not confirm active TB disease. Further diagnostic tests, such as a chest X-ray and sputum analysis, will be required to differentiate between latent TB infection and active disease.
* **Inform Healthcare Provider**: Always inform your healthcare provider about any prior BCG vaccination, history of TB exposure, or immunosuppressive conditions, as these factors influence test interpretation.


## 🩺 Physician's Perspective
* **Comprehensive Risk Assessment**: Always integrate individual patient risk factors, including exposure history, comorbidities, travel history, and immune status, when selecting and interpreting TB screening tests.
* **Test Selection Rationale**: Choose between TST and IGRA based on patient-specific factors (e.g., BCG vaccination history, likelihood of follow-up, immunosuppression) and adherence to local public health guidelines.
* **Confirmation of Active Disease**: Emphasize that a positive screening test necessitates further diagnostic evaluation (e.g., chest radiography, sputum microscopy, culture, NAAT) to definitively diagnose active TB disease.
* **Latent TB Infection Management**: Discuss the benefits and risks of treatment for latent TB infection (LTBI) with high-risk individuals to prevent progression to active disease, considering drug interactions and patient adherence.
* **Public Health Reporting**: Active TB is a reportable disease; ensure prompt notification to public health authorities for contact investigation and disease surveillance.


## 🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner
* **Patient Education Mastery**: Develop comprehensive patient education skills to explain the purpose, procedure, potential results, and critical follow-up requirements for both TST and IGRA tests.
* **TST Administration Proficiency**: Practice and master the precise intradermal injection technique for TST, ensuring the correct dose and formation of a distinct wheal.
* **Accurate TST Reading and Documentation**: Learn to accurately measure the induration (not erythema) in millimeters at the appropriate 48-72 hour window and meticulously document the findings.
* **IGRA Specimen Handling**: Understand and adhere to proper blood collection, handling, and transport protocols for IGRA samples to ensure test integrity and accurate results.
* **Adverse Reaction Monitoring**: Be prepared to identify and manage any rare adverse reactions to the TST (e.g., severe local reactions) or blood draw (e.g., vasovagal syncope).


## 🔬 Clinical Reference Index
* **TST Induration Interpretation Criteria**:
* **≥5 mm induration**: Positive for close contacts of active TB cases, immunocompromised individuals (e.g., HIV-positive), recent TB converters, and persons with fibrotic changes on chest X-ray consistent with prior TB.
* **≥10 mm induration**: Positive for recent immigrants from high-prevalence countries, intravenous drug users, residents/employees of high-risk congregate settings (e.g., prisons, nursing homes), children <4 years of age, and persons with clinical conditions that increase TB risk (e.g., diabetes, silicosis).
* **≥15 mm induration**: Positive for persons with no known risk factors for TB.
* **Interferon-Gamma Release Assays (IGRAs)**: Blood tests that measure the immune response to specific TB antigens (ESAT-6, CFP-10). Results are reported as positive, negative, or indeterminate.
* **Mycobacterium tuberculosis**: The slow-growing, acid-fast bacillus that is the primary causative agent of tuberculosis.
* **Latent Tuberculosis Infection (LTBI)**: A state where TB bacteria are present in the body but are inactive and cause no symptoms. Individuals with LTBI are not infectious but can progress to active TB disease.
* **CDC Guidelines**: Refer to the latest Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for comprehensive recommendations on TB screening, diagnosis, and treatment.
