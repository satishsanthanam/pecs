Acute bronchitis in adults

🏠 Home / Lung disease / Respiratory tract infections

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Definition: Acute bronchitis is an inflammation of the bronchial tubes, often caused by a viral infection, leading to a cough.
  • Duration: Symptoms typically last for 1 to 3 weeks, though the cough may persist longer.
  • Distinction: It is distinct from chronic bronchitis, which is a long-term condition often associated with smoking.
  • Transmission: Primarily spread through respiratory droplets when an infected person coughs or sneezes.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Persistent Cough: The hallmark symptom, which can be dry or productive (with mucus).
  • Sputum Production: Mucus may be clear, white, yellow, or green.
  • Chest Discomfort: Mild soreness or tightness in the chest due to persistent coughing.
  • Fatigue: General tiredness and malaise are common.
  • Mild Fever: Low-grade fever may be present, especially in the initial stages.
  • Sore Throat & Body Aches: Often accompany the onset, similar to a common cold.
  • Wheezing: Some individuals may experience wheezing or shortness of breath, particularly with exertion.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Hand Hygiene: Frequent and thorough hand washing with soap and water or using an alcohol-based hand sanitizer.
  • Cough Etiquette: Cover mouth and nose with a tissue or the elbow when coughing or sneezing to prevent spread.
  • Avoid Irritants: Steer clear of tobacco smoke, vaping, air pollution, and other respiratory irritants.
  • Adequate Rest: Ensure sufficient sleep to support the body's immune response and recovery.
  • Hydration: Maintain good fluid intake to help thin mucus and soothe the throat.
  • Monitor Symptoms: Watch for worsening symptoms such as high fever, difficulty breathing, or bloody sputum, which warrant medical attention.

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Increased Fluid Intake: Encourage water, clear broths, herbal teas, and warm lemon-honey water to soothe the throat and aid in mucus clearance.
  • Avoid Dehydrating Beverages: Limit consumption of caffeine and alcohol, which can contribute to dehydration.
  • Soothing Foods: Opt for soft, easy-to-swallow foods like soups, smoothies, mashed potatoes, and yogurt.
  • Irritant Avoidance: Temporarily avoid very spicy, acidic, or overly cold foods that might irritate a sensitive throat or trigger coughing.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Symptom Observation: Closely monitor for signs of secondary bacterial infection, such as persistent high fever, chills, or worsening shortness of breath.
  • Medication Adherence: Administer prescribed symptomatic treatments (e.g., cough suppressants, bronchodilators) as directed.
  • Environmental Control: Ensure a humidified environment if possible, and avoid exposure to known respiratory irritants.
  • Infection Control: Advise minimizing close contact with vulnerable individuals (e.g., infants, elderly, immunocompromised) to prevent transmission.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Clinical Diagnosis: Diagnosis is primarily based on patient history and physical examination; chest X-rays are usually not needed unless pneumonia is suspected.
  • Antibiotic Use: Antibiotics are generally not prescribed as acute bronchitis is predominantly viral in origin.
  • Symptomatic Management: Treatment focuses on relieving symptoms with over-the-counter pain relievers, cough suppressants, and bronchodilators if indicated.
  • Red Flag Awareness: Educate patients on warning signs that necessitate prompt re-evaluation, such as persistent fever, increasing dyspnea, or hemoptysis.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Patient Education: Provide comprehensive education on self-care measures, infection prevention, and when to seek further medical attention.
  • Respiratory Assessment: Perform thorough lung auscultation to identify adventitious sounds like wheezing or rhonchi.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Understand how to differentiate acute bronchitis from other respiratory conditions such as pneumonia, asthma exacerbation, or the common cold.
  • Supportive Care: Emphasize the importance of rest, hydration, and avoiding irritants in the recovery process.

🔬 Clinical Reference Index

  • Etiology: Most commonly caused by respiratory viruses (e.g., influenza, parainfluenza, RSV, adenovirus, rhinovirus).
  • Pathophysiology: Viral infection leads to inflammation and edema of the bronchial mucosa, increased mucus production, and impaired mucociliary clearance.
  • Complications: Rarely, acute bronchitis can lead to secondary bacterial pneumonia, especially in individuals with underlying lung conditions or weakened immune systems.
  • ICD-10 Code: J20.9 (Acute bronchitis, unspecified).
