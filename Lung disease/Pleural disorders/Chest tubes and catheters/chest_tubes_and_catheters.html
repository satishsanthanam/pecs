Chest tubes and catheters

🏠 Home / Lung disease / Pleural disorders

💡 What You Need to Know

  • Purpose: Chest tubes and catheters are medical devices inserted into the pleural space to drain air (pneumothorax), fluid (pleural effusion), blood (hemothorax), or pus (empyema) to re-establish normal lung function.
  • Indications: Commonly used for spontaneous or traumatic pneumothorax, post-surgical drainage (e.g., thoracotomy, cardiac surgery), malignant pleural effusions, and empyema.
  • Procedure: Involves inserting a sterile tube through the chest wall into the pleural space, typically under local anesthesia, and connecting it to a closed drainage system.
  • Function: The drainage system maintains negative pressure in the pleural space, allowing the lung to re-expand and facilitating the removal of unwanted substances.

🤒 Associated Symptoms

  • Dyspnea: Sudden or worsening shortness of breath, often a primary indicator of pleural space compromise.
  • Pleuritic Chest Pain: Sharp, localized chest pain that intensifies with breathing, coughing, or movement.
  • Tachypnea: Abnormally rapid breathing rate, reflecting respiratory distress.
  • Hypoxia: Decreased oxygen saturation levels, indicating impaired gas exchange.
  • Cough: Persistent cough, which may be dry or productive depending on the underlying cause.
  • Asymmetrical Chest Expansion: Unequal movement of the chest wall during respiration, suggesting lung collapse or fluid accumulation on one side.
  • Diminished Breath Sounds: Reduced or absent breath sounds over the affected lung field upon auscultation.

🛡 Crucial Precautions

  • Maintain Closed System: Always ensure the drainage system remains sealed to prevent air from entering the pleural space, which could lead to a tension pneumothorax.
  • Secure Dressing: Regularly inspect and reinforce the sterile occlusive dressing at the insertion site to prevent infection and dislodgement.
  • Monitor Drainage: Closely observe the amount, color, and consistency of drainage, reporting any sudden changes or excessive output immediately.
  • Water Seal Integrity: Keep the drainage unit upright and below the patient's chest level at all times to maintain the water seal and prevent backflow.
  • Tubing Patency: Ensure all tubing is free from kinks, loops, or clots that could impede drainage.
  • Pain Management: Administer prescribed analgesics to manage pain effectively, as adequate pain control promotes deep breathing and coughing, reducing complications.
  • Infection Prevention: Adhere strictly to aseptic technique during dressing changes and when handling the drainage system to minimize the risk of infection.
  • Emergency Equipment: Always have sterile clamps, sterile water, and occlusive dressings readily available at the bedside for emergency situations (e.g., tube dislodgement).

🍽 Dietary Directions & Restrictions

  • Pre-Procedure Fasting: If the chest tube insertion involves sedation, adhere strictly to NPO (nothing by mouth) guidelines for the specified period before the procedure.
  • Post-Procedure Hydration: Encourage oral fluid intake once stable and alert, unless contraindicated, to maintain hydration and aid recovery.
  • Nutritional Support: Ensure adequate caloric and protein intake to support healing and recovery, especially for patients with prolonged drainage or underlying chronic conditions.
  • Avoid Constipation: Encourage a diet rich in fiber and adequate fluid intake to prevent constipation, as straining can increase intrathoracic pressure and cause discomfort.
  • Small, Frequent Meals: Patients may tolerate smaller, more frequent meals better, especially if experiencing discomfort or dyspnea affecting appetite.

⚠️ Attendant Guidelines

  • Report Sudden Changes: Immediately notify medical staff of sudden onset dyspnea, severe chest pain, excessive bubbling in the water seal chamber, or significant changes in drainage volume or character.
  • Never Routinely Clamp: Do not clamp the chest tube without a specific physician's order, as this can lead to a dangerous tension pneumothorax. Clamping is typically only done for brief periods during tube changes or trials of removal.
  • Maintain Drainage System Position: Ensure the drainage system is always kept below the level of the patient's chest to facilitate gravity drainage and prevent fluid backflow into the pleural space.
  • Monitor for Air Leaks: Observe the water seal chamber for continuous bubbling, which indicates an ongoing air leak from the lung or a leak in the system. Intermittent bubbling with respirations is normal for some conditions.
  • Encourage Mobility: Assist the patient with ambulation and position changes as tolerated, ensuring the drainage system is properly managed and secured during movement.

🩺 Physician's Perspective

  • Precise Placement: Accurate placement of the chest tube is critical, confirmed by imaging (e.g., chest X-ray) post-insertion, to ensure optimal drainage and prevent complications.
  • Individualized Management: Treatment protocols, including suction levels and duration of drainage, are tailored to the specific etiology and patient response.
  • Pain Control Strategy: A robust pain management plan is essential, often involving multimodal analgesia, to facilitate patient comfort, deep breathing, and early mobilization.
  • Complication Vigilance: Close monitoring for potential complications such as infection, bleeding, re-expansion pulmonary edema, or tube dislodgement is paramount.
  • Timing of Removal: Decision for chest tube removal is based on clinical improvement, cessation of air leak, minimal drainage output, and radiographic evidence of lung re-expansion.

🎓 Academic & Nursing Corner

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Regularly assess respiratory status, pain level, insertion site integrity, and patency of the drainage system.
  • Drainage System Expertise: Understand the principles of water seal, suction control, and collection chambers, and be proficient in troubleshooting common issues like air leaks or decreased drainage.
  • Patient Education: Educate patients and families on the purpose of the chest tube, signs to report, deep breathing exercises, and safe mobility with the drainage system.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Meticulously document drainage characteristics (amount, color, consistency), presence of air leaks, patient's respiratory status, pain, and any interventions performed.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Be prepared for emergency situations such as tube dislodgement or accidental disconnection, knowing the immediate steps to take.
